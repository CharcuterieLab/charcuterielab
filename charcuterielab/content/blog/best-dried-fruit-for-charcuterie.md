# The Best Dried Fruit for a Charcuterie Board

> **Quick Answer:** Start with **Mariani Mediterranean (Turkish) Apricots** and **Sun-Maid California Mission Figs** (about $4 for 7 oz). Add **Craisins** for color, and for a showpiece, **raisins on the vine**. For a big party, **Kirkland's unsweetened dried mango** is just mango, no sugar or sulfites.

<h2 id="how-to-choose">Which dried fruit goes with which cheese</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Figs</strong> with blue cheese, goat cheese and salty cured meats.</li><li><strong>Apricots</strong> with brie, triple crème and young Manchego.</li><li><strong>Tart cherries</strong> with aged cheddar and aged Gouda.</li><li><strong>Raisins on the vine</strong> with any cheese course, as the showpiece. <strong>Mango</strong> with spicy salami and pepper Jack.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> pick two or three kinds and use them to fill the gaps; about 1 to 2 oz per guest in total is plenty.</li></ul>

## Dried fruit questions

**What dried fruit goes on a charcuterie board?**

Apricots, Mission or Calimyrna figs, tart cherries, cranberries, raisins on the vine and mango all work. Pick two or three for color and contrast.

**Turkish or California dried apricots?**

Turkish apricots are dried whole, so they're plump, juicy and sweeter. California apricots are halved before drying, so they're thinner, chewier and more intensely apricot. Both work on a board.

**Sulfured or unsulfured apricots?**

Sulfur dioxide keeps apricots bright orange; unsulfured ones turn brown and taste a little sweeter. Variety matters more than sulfuring, but skip sulfured fruit for guests sensitive to sulfites.

**How much dried fruit per person?**

Use it to fill gaps rather than as a main item: two or three kinds, about 1 to 2 oz per guest in total.

**What dried fruit goes with which cheese?**

Figs with blue cheese and goat cheese, apricots with brie and triple crème, tart cherries with aged cheddar and Gouda, and dates with Parmesan.
