A glass dome does two jobs. On the table, it keeps cheese from drying out and keeps flies and fingers off while it comes to room temperature. In the fridge, an airtight keeper stops cheese from picking up other smells. A dome isn't long-term storage for a good wedge, though; for that, wrap it in cheese paper.

<h2 id="how-to-choose">When a cheese dome helps</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>On the table:</strong> cover cheese while it warms up for an hour before guests arrive, so the cut faces don't dry out.</li><li><strong>Outdoors:</strong> keeps bugs off soft cheeses.</li><li><strong>In the fridge:</strong> use an airtight keeper for strong cheeses, with each wedge wrapped in cheese paper inside.</li><li><strong>Not for weeks:</strong> a dome on the counter is for hours, not days; perishable cheese follows the two-hour rule.</li><li><strong>Size:</strong> measure your largest cheese; a whole wheel of brie needs a base about 9 inches wide or more.</li></ul>

## Cheese dome questions

**Do cheese domes keep cheese fresh?**

For a few hours on the table, yes; they stop cut faces drying out. For days, wrap cheese in cheese paper and refrigerate it.

**Can you put a cheese dome in the fridge?**

Glass domes usually can, if they fit. An airtight plastic keeper is more practical for the fridge.

**Is a marble or wood base better?**

Marble stays cool, which helps on a warm day. Wood is lighter and warmer looking but needs oiling.

**How long can cheese sit under a dome?**

Treat it like any cheese on a board: about two hours at room temperature for soft cheeses.

**What is a cheese cloche?**

Another name for a cheese dome: a glass or wire cover set over cheese on a plate or board.
