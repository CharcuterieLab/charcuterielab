# 15 Holiday Charcuterie Boards from Halloween to New Year's

> **Quick Answer:** Plan one board per party from Halloween to New Year's Day: three Halloween boards, a fall harvest board, Friendsgiving, Thanksgiving and a leftovers board, a dairy Hanukkah board, a Christmas tree, a wreath, Christmas morning, a cookie board, a hot cocoa board, New Year's Eve and a New Year's Day brunch. Use 2, 3 or 4 oz of meat and cheese per guest.

Save this now. Between late October and the first of January most of us host or bring food to five or six gatherings, and every one of them goes better with a board. Here are fifteen, in calendar order, with what goes on each and one thing to do ahead.

Every board below follows the same amounts: meat and cheese each at **2 oz per guest** before a meal, **3 oz** when the board is the party food and **4 oz** when it is dinner. The [Party Planner](/party-planner/) turns that into a shopping list for any guest count.

## Halloween

### 1. The pumpkin-shaped board

Arrange cubed aged cheddar, orange bell pepper strips and dried apricots in a round pumpkin shape, then add a "stem" of pretzel rods and a leaf of sage. Fill the gaps with salami folded into quarters. It photographs well from above, which is why it gets shared. More ideas are in the [Halloween charcuterie board](/blog/halloween-charcuterie-board/) guide.

### 2. The black and orange board

This is the grown-up version. Use Mimolette or aged Gouda for orange, black olives, blackberries, dark rye crisps and black olive tapenade. A small bowl of blood-orange marmalade ties it together. Serve with a dry red.

### 3. The kids' monster board

Cut string cheese into "fingers" with an almond slice for a nail, add candy eyes to cheese rounds and use grapes for eyeballs. Cut grapes in half lengthwise for small children and leave whole nuts off the board if young kids are coming.

## November

### 4. The fall harvest board

Build around apples and pears (toss slices in lemon juice so they do not brown), aged cheddar, a wedge of blue, candied pecans, fig jam and walnut bread. This is the board that suits a Pinot Noir.

### 5. The Friendsgiving board

Friendsgiving boards travel, so use jars and small containers: whipped feta, cranberry relish, mustard, pickles. Hard cheeses and cured meats hold up best on the drive. Put everything on the board once you arrive.

### 6. The Thanksgiving board

Keep it light because dinner follows. Two oz per guest of meat and cheese is plenty. Lean on fresh fruit, cranberries, crisp crackers and one soft cheese. The full plan is in the [Thanksgiving charcuterie board](/blog/thanksgiving-charcuterie-board/) post.

### 7. The leftovers board

The day after, slice cold turkey, add the leftover cranberry sauce, stuffing bites crisped in a pan, the cheese ends from yesterday's board and some pickles. Nobody cooks, everybody eats.

## December

### 8. The dairy Hanukkah board

Many families keep Hanukkah food dairy and skip pork, so this board is built on cheese, smoked salmon, cream cheese, latke bites, applesauce, sour cream, olives and rugelach. If guests keep kosher, ask what they need and buy certified products.

### 9. The Christmas tree board

Lay cubes of cheddar and Havarti in a triangle, tuck salami and rosemary sprigs between the rows and top it with a star cut from a slice of cheese. It is the easiest holiday board on this list. See the [Christmas charcuterie board](/blog/christmas-charcuterie-board/) for more shapes.

![Christmas tree charcuterie board made of cheddar and Havarti cubes with salami, rosemary and a cheese star](/images/halloween-to-new-years-charcuterie-boards-tree.webp)

### 10. The wreath board

Place a small bowl in the center of a round board, then ring it with rosemary, salami roses, mozzarella pearls, cherry tomatoes and green olives. Remove the bowl before serving so the ring reads as a wreath.

![Wreath charcuterie board with rosemary, salami roses, mozzarella pearls and cranberry sauce in the center](/images/halloween-to-new-years-charcuterie-boards-wreath.webp)

### 11. The Christmas morning board

Mini muffins, croissants, soft scrambled egg cups, bacon, fruit, yogurt and a wedge of brie. Set it out while the gifts are opened and let people graze.

### 12. The cookie and dessert board

Mix three or four kinds of cookies with dark chocolate, clementines, candied nuts and a small wedge of aged cheddar. The cheese keeps it from tasting like one long sugar rush.

### 13. The hot cocoa board

Cocoa in a thermos, then jars of marshmallows, crushed peppermint, whipped cream, cinnamon sticks and shortbread. This one is for movie nights and kids.

## New Year's

### 14. The New Year's Eve board

This is the night to spend a little more: a triple-crème, Parmigiano-Reggiano, prosciutto, Marcona almonds, honeycomb and something bubbly. Guests are often grazing for hours, so plan 3 oz per guest and refill instead of putting everything out at once. The [New Year's Eve board](/blog/charcuterie-board-new-years-eve/) post has the full list.

### 15. The New Year's Day brunch board

Bagels, smoked salmon, cream cheese, soft-boiled eggs, fruit, and a pot of coffee. It is a slow, easy start to the year.

## Keep every board safe

Perishable food should sit out no more than **2 hours**, or 1 hour above 90°F, per USDA guidance. For long parties, build two smaller boards and swap them. Take hard cheese out of the fridge 45 to 60 minutes before serving and soft cheese 20 to 30 minutes before.
