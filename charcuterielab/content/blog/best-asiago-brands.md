<h2 id="how-to-choose">Fresh vs aged Asiago, and how to serve it</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Fresh Asiago</strong> (pressato or fresco) is mild, springy and sliceable: good for kids and sandwiches.</li><li><strong>Aged Asiago</strong> (d'allevo, and most Wisconsin wedges) is firm, nutty and sharp: cube it or break it into chunks.</li><li><strong>Pair it with</strong> soppressata, salami, olives, fig jam and pears.</li><li><strong>Drink:</strong> Valpolicella or Pinot Grigio, both from the Veneto like the cheese.</li><li><strong>Serve it</strong> at room temperature, out of the fridge 30 to 45 minutes ahead. About 1 oz per person.</li></ul>

## Asiago questions

**What is the best Asiago cheese brand?**

Sartori Classic Asiago, a 2022 World Championship gold medalist. BelGioioso is the budget pick; Asiago d'Allevo DOP is the Italian original.

**Is Asiago good on a charcuterie board?**

Yes. Aged Asiago cubes neatly and goes with salami, olives and fig jam; fresh Asiago slices well for kids.

**What's the difference between fresh and aged Asiago?**

Fresh Asiago is young, mild and soft. Aged Asiago is firm, nutty and sharp, and the oldest grades grate like Parmesan.

**Is Asiago like Parmesan?**

Aged Asiago is a common, cheaper stand-in for Parmesan; it's tangier and a little softer.

**How much Asiago per person?**

About 1 oz per person as one of several cheeses.
