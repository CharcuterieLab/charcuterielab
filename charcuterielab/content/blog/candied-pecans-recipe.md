# Candied Pecans Recipe: 10 Minutes on the Stove (Sweet or Spicy)

> **Quick Answer:** Melt **2 tablespoons butter** with **1/4 cup brown sugar**, a splash of water, cinnamon and salt. Add **2 cups pecans** and stir over medium heat for **4 to 5 minutes**. Spread on parchment, separate and cool **15 minutes**. They keep **2 weeks** in an airtight jar.

The fastest homemade thing you can put on a [cheese board](/blog/best-cheese-charcuterie-board/), and cheaper than most store-bought bags. Short on time? See [the best store-bought candied pecans](/blog/best-candied-pecans/).

## Stovetop candied pecans

**Makes:** 2 cups · **Serves:** about 8 on a board · **Prep:** 2 minutes · **Cook:** 8 minutes · **Cool:** 15 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 2 cups pecan halves
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1/4 cup packed light brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon water
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
- Flaky sea salt, to finish (optional)

**Steps**
1. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
2. Melt the butter in a large nonstick skillet over medium heat. Stir in the brown sugar, water, cinnamon and salt, and cook for about 1 minute, until bubbly.
3. Add the pecans and stir constantly for 4 to 5 minutes, until the syrup thickens, coats the nuts and smells toasty.
4. Tip the pecans onto the parchment, spread them in one layer and separate any clumps with two forks while hot. Sprinkle with flaky salt if you like.
5. Cool for at least 15 minutes, until the coating is hard, then store in an airtight container.

**Three tips that make them better**
- **Use halves, not pieces.** Small pieces burn before the sugar sets and clump into brittle.
- **Keep stirring.** Sugar goes from caramel to burnt in under a minute. If it smells sharp or smoky, pull the pan off the heat.
- **Cool completely before storing.** Warm nuts sealed in a jar steam and turn sticky.

## 4 variations

| Version | Change |
|---|---|
| **Sweet and spicy** | Add 1/4 teaspoon cayenne and 1/4 teaspoon smoked paprika with the sugar |
| **Maple** | Swap the brown sugar and water for 3 tablespoons pure maple syrup; cook 1 to 2 minutes longer |
| **Rosemary and sea salt** | Skip the cinnamon; stir in 1 tablespoon finely chopped fresh rosemary at the end and finish with flaky salt |
| **Chai spice** | Add 1/4 teaspoon each ground ginger and cardamom and a pinch of cloves |

## Oven method (for a bigger batch)

Whisk 1 egg white with 1 tablespoon water until frothy. Toss with 4 cups pecans, then with 1/2 cup brown sugar, 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/2 teaspoon salt. Spread on a parchment-lined sheet and bake at **300°F for 30 minutes**, stirring every 10 minutes. Cool completely on the pan. The coating is lighter and crunchier than the stovetop version, and it's easier for 4 cups or more.

## What they go with on a board

Sweet, crunchy and a little salty, candied pecans fix the two things a cheese board often lacks: crunch and sweetness. Put a small pile next to:

- [Blue cheese](/ingredients/gorgonzola/) or [Stilton](/ingredients/stilton/): sweet against salty funk
- [Brie](/ingredients/brie/) and [baked brie](/blog/baked-brie-recipe/): crunch on soft cream
- [Fresh goat cheese](/ingredients/fresh-chevre/) with a drizzle of honey
- [Aged Gouda](/ingredients/aged-gouda/): butterscotch on both sides
- [Pears](/ingredients/pears/) and apples, for a sweet-tart bite

The spicy version is the one to put beside sharp cheddar and a glass of bourbon.

## How much to make

About **1/4 cup per guest** if they're one of several nuts and sweets on a board; 2 cups covers 8. For gifts, one batch fills two 8 oz jars.

## Storage and make-ahead

- **Room temperature:** up to 2 weeks in an airtight container. Keep them away from the stove and the dishwasher, where steam softens them.
- **Freezer:** up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature in the closed container so condensation doesn't form on the nuts.
- **Re-crisp:** if they go soft, spread them on a sheet and bake at 300°F for 5 minutes, then cool.

## FAQ

**How do you make candied pecans on the stove?**

Melt 2 tablespoons butter in a skillet over medium heat, stir in 1/4 cup brown sugar, a tablespoon of water, salt and cinnamon until bubbly, then add 2 cups pecans. Stir for 4 to 5 minutes until the syrup coats the nuts, then spread them on parchment and separate them to cool.

**Why are my candied pecans sticky?**

Usually the sugar didn't cook long enough, or they were stored before they cooled completely. Cook until the syrup thickens and clings, spread the nuts in one layer and let them cool for at least 15 minutes before storing. Humid weather can also soften them.

**How long do candied pecans last?**

About 2 weeks in an airtight container at room temperature, or up to 3 months in the freezer. Keep them away from heat and humidity so they stay crisp.

**What cheese goes with candied pecans?**

Blue cheese and brie first, then goat cheese, aged Gouda and sharp cheddar. Sweet, crunchy pecans balance salty, creamy and funky cheese.

**Can I use walnuts instead of pecans?**

Yes. Walnut halves work with the same recipe and timing. Almonds and cashews work too but may need a minute or two longer.
