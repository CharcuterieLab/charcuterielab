<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to make a restaurant-style dipping plate</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Start with extra virgin.</strong> "Pure" and "light" olive oils are refined and taste flat with bread.</li><li><strong>Season the plate, not the bottle:</strong> a pinch of flaky salt, cracked pepper, red pepper flakes, chopped rosemary and a little grated Parmesan.</li><li><strong>Add balsamic</strong> in the center of the oil for the classic Italian look. Our <a href="/blog/best-balsamic-glaze/">best balsamic glaze</a> picks work well.</li><li><strong>Pick the bread:</strong> warm focaccia, ciabatta or a crusty baguette. See <a href="/blog/what-bread-for-charcuterie-board/">the best bread for a charcuterie board</a>.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> about 1 tablespoon of oil per guest on a shared plate.</li><li><strong>Store it right:</strong> keep oil away from the stove and use an opened bottle within a couple of months, while it's fresh.</li></ul>

## Olive oil for dipping questions

**What is the best olive oil for dipping bread?**

Sciabica's Mediterranean Medley, Tasting Table's top dipping oil, made with herbs pressed into the oil. For a plain extra virgin, Lucini Premium Select won The Takeout's 13-oil test.

**What's the best cheap olive oil for dipping?**

Bertolli Extra Virgin, about $9, was Delish's budget surprise: rich and fruity. Skip Bertolli's other, non-extra-virgin oils.

**Should dipping oil be extra virgin?**

Yes. Extra virgin is unrefined, so it keeps the fruity, grassy, peppery flavor that makes bread dipping worth it.

**What do you put in olive oil for dipping bread?**

Flaky salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes, chopped rosemary or garlic, a little Parmesan, and a splash of balsamic.

**Why does good olive oil taste peppery?**

The peppery catch at the back of the throat comes from natural compounds in fresh, high-quality extra virgin oil.
