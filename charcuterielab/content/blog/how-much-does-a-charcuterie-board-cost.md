# How Much Does a Charcuterie Board Cost? (DIY vs Catered, by Guest Count)

> **Quick Answer:** Made at home as an appetizer before a meal, a charcuterie board costs about **$3 to $5 per guest on a budget**, **$5 to $8 for a standard board** and **$12 to $25 for a luxury board**. Plan about 1½ times that when the board is the party food. That's roughly **$40 to $65 for a standard board for 8**. Bought from a small business, boards typically run **$11 to $16 per guest**, and grazing tables **$12 to $15 per guest** and up, plus setup.

The price of a charcuterie board depends on three things: how many people it feeds, whether it comes before a meal or is the meal, and how much of it is imported. This guide gives you the per-guest cost at three levels, a chart from 4 to 50 guests, what businesses charge, and where the money actually goes.

## Cost per guest at three levels

![Three charcuterie boards side by side: a budget board, a standard board and a luxury board](/images/charcuterie-board-cost-three-levels.jpg)

| Level | Per guest (before a meal) | Per guest (board is the party food) | What's on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | $3 to $5 | $5 to $7 | Store-brand cheddar, a soft cheese, salami, crackers, seasonal fruit, pickles |
| Standard | $5 to $8 | $8 to $12 | Three cheeses including one good one, two meats, two crackers, fruit, nuts, a jam |
| Luxury | $12 to $25+ | $18 to $35+ | Imported cheeses like Parmigiano-Reggiano, prosciutto di Parma, truffle honey, Marcona almonds |

These ranges come from the boards we've priced on this site: the [$20 board for four](/blog/20-dollar-charcuterie-board-challenge/), the [$25 budget board for 6 to 8](/boards/25-dollar-budget-charcuterie-board/), the [Friendsgiving board](/blog/friendsgiving-charcuterie-board/) at $4.50 to $7 a guest, the [Costco board for 20](/blog/costco-charcuterie-board/) at $9.50 to $11.50 a guest as the party food, and the [luxury board](/boards/luxury-charcuterie-board/) at $150 to $250 for 8 to 12.

"Before a meal" uses the Lab standard of **2 oz of meat and 2 oz of cheese per guest**. "Party food" uses **3 oz of each**. See [how much charcuterie per person](/blog/how-much-charcuterie-per-person/) for the full formula.

## Price chart by number of guests

Homemade, served as an appetizer before a meal:

| Guests | Budget | Standard | Luxury |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | $12 to $20 | $20 to $32 | $50 to $100 |
| 8 | $25 to $40 | $40 to $65 | $95 to $200 |
| 12 | $35 to $60 | $60 to $95 | $145 to $300 |
| 20 | $60 to $100 | $100 to $160 | $240 to $500 |
| 50 | $150 to $250 | $250 to $400 | $600 to $1,250 |

If the board is the whole party, multiply by about 1½. For 20 or more guests, buying at a warehouse store pulls you toward the low end of each range.

## Where the money goes

Cheese and cured meat are most of the bill. Everything else is cheap by comparison, which is why the fastest way to change the total is to change the meat and cheese, not the crackers.

| Item | Typical price | Cost per guest at 2 oz |
|---|---|---|
| Store-brand cheddar, block | $5 to $8 per lb | About $0.65 to $1 |
| Domestic brie | $8 to $14 per lb | About $1 to $1.75 |
| Manchego or aged gouda | $14 to $22 per lb | About $1.75 to $2.75 |
| Parmigiano-Reggiano | $18 to $26 per lb | About $2.25 to $3.25 |
| Salami, whole or sliced | $10 to $18 per lb | About $1.25 to $2.25 |
| Prosciutto, pre-sliced packs | $25 to $45 per lb | About $3 to $5.50 |
| Crackers | $3 to $5 a box | About $0.40 to $0.75 |
| Grapes and seasonal fruit | $2 to $4 per lb | About $0.50 to $1 |

*Prices are typical US grocery prices seen in fall 2026. They vary a lot by store and region, so check yours before you shop.*

The biggest single swing is prosciutto. A board with pre-sliced imported prosciutto as its only meat can cost twice as much per guest as one built on salami. For the full breakdown of where to spend and where to save, see [the myth of the expensive charcuterie board](/blog/expensive-board/).

## What it costs to buy one

Small charcuterie businesses price by the guest, and prices have settled into a fairly narrow range:

- **Boards:** a [fall 2026 survey of small sellers by Homegrown](https://findhomegrown.com/blog/how-much-to-sell-charcuterie-boards-for) found most boards cost **$11 to $16 per guest** at their largest listed size, with small boards for 1 to 3 people closer to **$20 per guest**.
- **Individual grazing boxes:** about **$10 to $14** each in the same survey.
- **Grazing tables:** about **$12 to $15 per guest** for 25 or more, plus a setup fee. Pricing guidance for caterers from [CaterCamp](https://catercamp.com/blog/grazing-table-catering) (March 2026) puts basic tables at $12 to $18 per guest, premium at $20 to $35 and luxury at $35 to $55.

That makes a homemade standard board about **half the price** of a bought one. The difference pays for the maker's time, design, packaging and delivery, which is worth it for a wedding or a big work event. For a party at home, building it yourself saves the most. If you're feeding a crowd, the [grazing table guide](/blog/grazing-table/) walks through the build.

## Costs people forget

- **The board itself:** $20 to $50 for a good wooden board, once. See [wood vs slate vs marble](/blog/best-charcuterie-boards/).
- **Small bowls and knives:** for jam, olives and honey, and one knife per cheese.
- **Garnish:** a bunch of rosemary or thyme costs $2 to $3 and makes the board look finished.
- **Extra crackers:** they go first, so buy one more box than you think you need.

## How to spend less without it looking cheap

1. **Buy blocks and whole salami, not pre-cut trays.** You pay a lot for slicing.
2. **Spend on one anchor.** One good cheese (an aged gouda or a real Parmigiano-Reggiano) and everything else store brand.
3. **Use salami as the main meat.** Save prosciutto for a [prosciutto rose](/blog/prosciutto-rose/) or skip it.
4. **Fill with cheap color.** Grapes, apples, clementines, cornichons and olives cover space for very little.
5. **Shop where it's cheapest for your size.** [Aldi](/blog/best-aldi-charcuterie-items/) and [Trader Joe's](/blog/trader-joes-charcuterie-board/) for small boards, [Costco](/blog/costco-charcuterie-board/) for 15 guests or more.
6. **Don't overbuy.** Use the [Party Planner](/party-planner/) to get exact amounts for your guest count.
7. **Make it look full.** A smaller board packed tight looks more generous than a big board with gaps. See [the $25 board that looks like $75](/blog/budget-charcuterie-board/).

## If you're selling boards

If you're pricing boards to sell, start from your ingredient cost, then add packaging, your time and delivery. In Homegrown's example, a single grazing box cost **about $6 to make and sold for $14**. Check what local makers charge, because prices vary a lot by city, and check your local rules for selling food made at home before you take orders.

## FAQ

**How much does a charcuterie board cost per person?**
Making it yourself as an appetizer before a meal costs about $3 to $5 per guest on a budget, $5 to $8 for a standard board and $12 to $25 for a luxury board. When the board is the party food, plan about 1½ times that. Small charcuterie businesses typically charge about $11 to $16 per guest.

**How much does a charcuterie board for 10 people cost?**
A homemade standard board for 10 before a meal costs about $50 to $80, a budget board about $30 to $50 and a luxury board $120 to $250 or more. A catered board for 10 usually runs about $110 to $160.

**Is it cheaper to make or buy a charcuterie board?**
Making it is usually about half the price of buying one. A standard homemade board runs about $5 to $8 per guest, while small businesses charge about $11 to $16 per guest for boards and $12 to $15 or more per guest for grazing tables. The extra covers their labor, design, packaging and delivery.

**Why are charcuterie boards so expensive?**
Cheese and cured meat cost $10 to $40 or more a pound, and they make up most of the bill. Pre-sliced imported meats like prosciutto cost the most per ounce. Buying blocks and whole salami, using one splurge cheese and filling the rest with fruit, crackers and pickles brings the cost down.

**How much should I charge for a charcuterie board?**
Small businesses surveyed in fall 2026 mostly charged $11 to $16 per guest at their largest sizes, with small boards for 1 to 3 people closer to $20 per guest. Start from your ingredient cost, then add packaging, your time and delivery, and check what local makers charge.
