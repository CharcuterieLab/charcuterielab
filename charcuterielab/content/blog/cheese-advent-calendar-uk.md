### <a id="crafty-herd"></a>The cheap one: The Crafty Herd, £12

This is the supermarket calendar, and at £12 it is a tenth of the price of the cheesemonger boxes. It is made by [The Ilchester Cheese Co](https://www.thecraftyherd.co.uk/) in Somerset, the company behind the original 24-door cheese calendar that started the whole category in 2017.

You get 25 individually wrapped mini truckles of 16 g each, 400 g in total, in a folding cardboard calendar built to sit in the fridge door. It splits in half once you have opened the first twelve, which is a genuinely good idea in late December when fridge space is the scarcest thing in the house. The flavours lean novelty rather than cheeseboard: cheddar with sriracha and honey, truffle cheddar, Wensleydale with chocolate and orange, Wensleydale with panettone fruits, mature cheddar with pineapple.

Two things to know. Ocado has a **£40 minimum spend**, so this works as something you add to a normal food shop rather than order on its own. And 16 g is two crackers' worth, so treat it as a daily nibble, not a cheeseboard.

### <a id="courtyard-dairy"></a>The safest premium pick: The Courtyard Dairy, £135

Of the cheesemonger calendars this is the one with real stock: **119 when we checked**, against roughly 30 at George & Joseph and "limited" at Paxton & Whitfield.

It is 24 different cheeses at 80 g a day, a minimum of 2.1 kg, which is enough for two people each evening rather than one. The selection leans British Isles farmhouse and includes World Cheese Award and British Cheese Award champions, plus one cheese that has never been sold before. Each parcel comes with tasting notes, and the box includes drink pairings and a booklet on British Isles cheese history.

It arrives in two deliveries of twelve, on **30 November and 11 December**. The shop asks you to eat each half within two weeks of delivery, which is the honest instruction for cheese of this quality.

### <a id="george-joseph"></a>Order this one first: George & Joseph, £150

The Leeds cheesemonger makes **about 30** of these, and the product page was already warning on stock when we checked. If you want it, today is better than next week.

Twenty-four artisan cheeses, roughly 75 g each, about 1.8 kg in total, no repeats, with the line-up kept secret. It comes as two boxes of twelve so the second half arrives fresh: box one is dispatched **Thursday 26 November** for Friday 27 November, box two on Thursday 10 December for Friday 11 December. Local delivery and click-and-collect run on the same two Thursdays.

### <a id="paxton"></a>The prestige one: Paxton & Whitfield, £175

Paxton & Whitfield have been selling cheese in London since 1797, and this is the fifth year of their calendar. It has **sold out every year since it launched**, which is the thing to weigh against the price.

Twenty-four cheeses, each 75 g or more, hand-cut and hand-wrapped, with whole small cheeses used where a whole one is better than a wedge. The £175 includes UK delivery across three chilled drops: **27 November, 4 December and 11 December**. You pick the first date at checkout and the other two are arranged for you.

## Make your own

This is the fallback that always works, and in the UK it is easy because every supermarket has a decent cheese counter.

Buy **six cheeses** and cut each into two pieces of about 30 to 40 g. Wrap and number the twelve parcels, and keep them in the fridge in a box. For a full 24 nights, buy twelve cheeses and do the same. Aim for a spread rather than six cheddars: something soft, something blue, a hard sheep's cheese, a washed rind, a goat's cheese and one wildcard.

A rough British shopping list for twelve nights, all of it from a big supermarket: mature cheddar, Red Leicester, Wensleydale with cranberries, a Somerset brie, Stilton, and a goat's log. That lands around £25 to £30, holds more cheese than the £12 calendar, and the pieces are big enough to eat with something.
