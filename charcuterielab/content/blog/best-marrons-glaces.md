Marrons glacés are whole chestnuts candied over days in vanilla syrup until they're sweet, tender and almost creamy inside. They're the classic French and Italian Christmas luxury, and Smithsonian notes they run about $4 a piece, because they take days to make and are rarely made in the US. A single foil-wrapped chestnut on a holiday board is a showstopper.

## Marrons glacés questions

**What are marrons glacés?**

Whole chestnuts candied slowly in vanilla sugar syrup, then glazed. They're a French and Italian Christmas tradition.

**Why are marrons glacés so expensive?**

They take days to make, many chestnuts break along the way, and most are imported. Smithsonian put them at about $4 a piece in New York.

**What is the best brand of marrons glacés?**

Clément Faugier is the classic French maker, in business since 1882. Corsiglia and Agrimontana are also highly regarded.

**How do you serve marrons glacés?**

Whole on a small plate in their foil, with mascarpone, Comté or dark chocolate and a glass of Champagne or Sauternes.

**Are broken marrons glacés worth buying?**

Yes. Broken pieces from a good maker cost about half as much and are perfect over ice cream or in whipped cream.
