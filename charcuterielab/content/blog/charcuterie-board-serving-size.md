# Charcuterie Board Serving Size: Exactly How Much Per Person

> **Quick Answer:** Plan 2 oz each of cheese and cured meat per person when a meal follows, 3 oz each when the board is the party food, and 4 oz each when it's dinner. Add 6–8 crackers per person (10–12 as the party food) and 10% extra from 20 guests up.

> **For your exact guest count**, the [Party Planner](/party-planner/) works out meat, cheese, crackers, cost and a timeline for 4 to 100 guests.
## The Per-Person Formula

The confusion around charcuterie board quantities usually comes from not distinguishing between two very different use cases. An appetizer board and a meal board require completely different quantities — and getting it wrong in either direction is a problem: a too-small appetizer board runs out awkwardly, while a meal-sized board for an appetizer leaves you with a lot of expensive leftovers.

**Formula for any board:**

**(Number of guests) × (oz of cheese per person) + (oz of charcuterie per person) = base quantity to purchase**

Then add accompaniments — fruit, crackers, condiments, nuts — which don't require the same per-person math because they're supporting elements rather than primary ones.

---

## Appetizer Board (30–60 Minutes, Before or Alongside Dinner)

This is the most common use case: a board served while guests arrive, with dinner to follow. Guests are not relying on the board for a meal — it's a welcome snack and a social lubricant.

**Per person:**
- Cheese: 2 oz total (across all cheeses on the board)
- Charcuterie: 2 oz total (across all meats)
- Accompaniments: fruit cluster, handful of crackers, a few olives/cornichons per person — don't over-portion these

**Why this range:** Guests at a cocktail-hour board are snacking, not eating. They'll have a few bites and move on. If you plan 3 oz or more per person for a pre-dinner board, you'll have significant leftovers and guests won't be hungry for dinner.

**Scaling for the appetizer board:**

| Guests | Total cheese | Total charcuterie |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 8 oz | 8 oz |
| 6 | 1 lb | 12 oz |
| 8 | 1 lb | 1 lb |
| 10 | 1½ lb | 1¼ lb |
| 12 | 1½ lb | 1½ lb |

**Practical note on cheese distribution:** If you're using three cheeses, split unevenly. The semi-firm cheese (Manchego, Comté, Gruyère) should be the largest portion — it's the most versatile and guests return to it. The soft cheese (Brie) and aged hard (Parmigiano) should be smaller — they're more intense and guests take smaller pieces.

A reasonable split for 8 guests (16 oz total): 7 oz semi-firm, 5 oz soft, 4 oz aged hard.

## Party-Food Board (2+ Hours, No Meal)

This is a grazing board meant to carry guests through a party with no dinner: holiday entertaining where the board is the main attraction, or a wine-and-charcuterie evening. If the board is dinner itself, plan 4 oz each of cheese and charcuterie.

**Per person:**
- Cheese: 3 oz total
- Charcuterie: 3 oz total
- Accompaniments: more fruit, more crackers (guests will cycle through the board multiple times)

**Scaling for the party-food board:**

| Guests | Total cheese | Total charcuterie |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1 lb | 12 oz |
| 6 | 1½ lb | 1¼ lb |
| 8 | 1½ lb | 1½ lb |
| 10 | 2 lb | 2 lb |
| 12 | 2½ lb | 2¼ lb |

Cheese is rounded up to the nearest half pound, the way it's sold.

## The Budget Calculation

Once you have the quantities, you can work out the budget. Quality varies significantly in price, but rough guidelines:

**Cheese (per pound):**
- Brie, Camembert, Havarti: $10–18/lb
- Manchego, Gruyère, Comté: $18–25/lb
- Parmigiano-Reggiano (DOP, 24+ months): $20–30/lb
- Gorgonzola, Stilton: $15–22/lb

**Charcuterie (per pound):**
- Genoa salami, soppressata: $12–18/lb
- Prosciutto di Parma (deli counter): $20–30/lb
- Coppa, bresaola, speck: $18–28/lb
- Jamón Serrano: $15–22/lb

**Quick budget estimate:** For an appetizer board for 8 guests, budget $35–55 for ingredients (cheese + charcuterie + accompaniments), depending on quality tier. For a main-event board for 8, budget $65–100.

## Accompaniment Quantities

Accompaniments don't require the same per-person math as cheese and charcuterie, but here are useful reference points:

| Accompaniment | Per person (appetizer) | Per person (party food) |
|---|---|---|
| Crackers | 6–8 pieces | 10–12 pieces |
| Grapes | 6–8 grapes | 10–14 grapes |
| Cornichons | 2–3 | 4–5 |
| Nuts (marcona almonds) | 4–5 | 6–8 |
| Honey | 1–2 tsp | 2–3 tsp |
| Fig jam/condiments | 1 tbsp total per person | 2 tbsp |

## What to Buy More Of

**If you're uncertain, buy more cheese than you think.** Cheese is the primary draw for most guests. Running out of cheese when charcuterie remains is more awkward than the reverse. Leftover cheese stores well (parchment-wrapped, refrigerated) and can be used for meals all week.

**The one thing to buy less of:** Crackers. Most boards put out too many crackers at once, which then soften and get discarded. Buy 6–8 per person for an appetizer board, put out half, and refill.

---

## Scaling Up for Large Groups (20+ Guests)

For very large groups, a few adjustments:

**Use larger blocks or whole wedges** rather than pre-sliced. Cheese in a block stays fresh longer than sliced, and for large groups you'll be cutting throughout the evening anyway.

**Two boards instead of one large board.** For groups of 20+, two separate boards (perhaps positioned at different tables or areas) keeps the flow better than one massive board. Guests can access it from multiple sides and the board doesn't get depleted unevenly.

**Increase the budget per person slightly.** Large boards require more visual abundance to look full. The per-serving math still applies, but the visual floor is higher — a large board that's technically adequate per person may look sparse. Add 10% to your quantity estimates from 20 guests up.

## FAQ

**How much food do you need per person for a charcuterie board?**
As an appetizer before a meal: 2 oz cured meat and 2 oz cheese per person. As the party food with no meal: 3 oz of each. As dinner: 4 oz of each. When in doubt, err toward more — a generous board is always better than a depleted one.

**How do you calculate charcuterie portions for a large group?**
Multiply per-person quantities by the number of guests, then add 10% from 20 guests up. For 20 guests with a board as an appetizer: 20 × 2 oz = 40 oz (2½ lb) each of meat and cheese. Add 10%: about 2¾ lb of meat and 3 lb of cheese. Divide cheese across 3–4 varieties and meat across 2–3 types.

**Does the occasion change how much charcuterie you need?**
Yes. Before a meal: 2 oz each of meat and cheese per person. Game day or a party with no dinner (3–4 hours of grazing): 3 oz of each. A grazing table that is the meal: 4 oz of each. More food out for longer periods = more consumption per person.

**How much charcuterie do you need for 10 people?**
For a cocktail board for 10: about 1¼ lb cured meat and 1½ lb cheese. For a party spread where it's the main food for 10: about 2 lb of each, plus crackers, fruit, nuts and spreads. See the full [plan for 10](/party-planner/charcuterie-board-for-10-people/) for amounts and a cost estimate.

**What is the formula for calculating charcuterie for a crowd?**
Use the per-person formula and multiply: (2 oz meat × guests), plus 10% from 20 guests up = total meat. (2 oz cheese × guests), plus 10% from 20 guests up = total cheese. Divide cheese total across 3–4 types, meat total across 2–3 types. Add crackers (6–8 per person), and accompaniments to taste. Always round up rather than down when buying.
