# Can You Eat Charcuterie While Pregnant? A Pregnancy-Safe Board Guide

> **Quick Answer:** Not as usually served. Skip **cold cured meat** (unless heated until steaming), **raw-milk cheese**, **refrigerated pâté** and **smoked salmon**. Swap in **prosciutto crisps, pepperoni chips and baked brie**, and use **pasteurized** cheese.

Most of a board is already fine, and the rest has easy swaps. Here's what the CDC and NHS say.

*This is general food-safety information, not medical advice. Guidance differs by country and changes over time, so check with your own doctor or midwife.*

## Why charcuterie is on the pregnancy list

There are two concerns, and both are about bacteria or parasites in food that isn't cooked:

- **Listeria.** It can grow at fridge temperature and is found in ready-to-eat foods such as deli meats, refrigerated pâté, refrigerated smoked fish and some soft cheeses. Pregnant people are much more likely to get sick from it, and it can cause miscarriage or stillbirth. This is the main reason behind the [CDC's guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html).
- **Toxoplasmosis.** The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/) also notes that cold cured meats carry a small risk of toxoplasmosis, a parasite that can harm a baby.

Heat kills both. That's why the rule for meat is "steaming hot," not "never."

<h2 id="safe">What's safe and what to swap</h2>
:::

| Food | US (CDC) | UK (NHS) | Pregnancy-safe swap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salami, pepperoni, chorizo and other dry sausages | Avoid unless heated to 165°F or steaming hot | Cook until steaming hot | Pepperoni chips, pan-crisped salami or chorizo |
| Prosciutto, coppa, ham and other cured or deli meats | Avoid unless heated to 165°F or steaming hot | Cook until steaming hot | Prosciutto crisps |
| Refrigerated pâté or meat spreads | Avoid; shelf-stable cans and jars are fine | Listed as a be-careful food | Shelf-stable pâté, or skip it |
| Refrigerated smoked salmon | Avoid unless cooked; shelf-stable is fine | Cook until steaming hot | Shelf-stable smoked fish, or a hot smoked-salmon dip |
| Hard cheeses (cheddar, gouda, parmesan) | Fine if pasteurized; avoid raw-milk cheese | Fine, pasteurized or not | Pasteurized cheddar and gouda |
| Brie, camembert and white-rind goat cheese | Not named; avoid if made with raw milk | Cook thoroughly | Baked brie, served hot |
| Soft blue cheese (gorgonzola, Roquefort) | Avoid if made with raw milk | Cook thoroughly | Skip it, or bake it into something hot |
| Queso fresco, panela and similar fresh cheeses | Avoid unless heated, even if pasteurized | Fine if pasteurized | Skip on a cold board |
| Mozzarella, feta, cream cheese, cottage cheese | Fine if pasteurized | Fine if pasteurized | Fresh mozzarella, whipped feta, Boursin |

Where the two disagree, the safest board follows whichever rule is stricter. That means pasteurized cheese, cooked meat, and soft white-rind or blue cheese only when it's baked.

:::html
<h2 id="raw-milk">Check the label: raw-milk cheeses</h2>
:::

US guidance says to avoid any cheese made with raw (unpasteurized) milk, and a few of the most popular board cheeses are made that way by tradition. Look for **"made with pasteurized milk"** on the label.

- **[Parmigiano-Reggiano](/ingredients/parmigiano-reggiano/)** is always made with raw milk. [Grana Padano](/ingredients/grana-padano/) is also raw-milk; a pasteurized American parmesan is the swap.
- **[Comté](/ingredients/comte/)** and traditional **[Gruyère](/ingredients/gruyere/)** are made with raw milk.
- **[Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/)** comes both ways, so read the label.
- **[Roquefort](/ingredients/roquefort/)** is made with raw sheep's milk.

Most supermarket cheddar, gouda, mozzarella, feta, cream cheese and Boursin in the US are pasteurized, but checking takes two seconds.

<h2 id="swaps">The swaps that make it still feel like charcuterie</h2>
:::

![Prosciutto crisps, crisp pepperoni chips and a small baked brie with honey on a parchment-lined baking sheet](/images/pregnancy-safe-swaps.jpg)

### Prosciutto crisps

Lay [prosciutto](/ingredients/prosciutto-di-parma/) slices flat on a parchment-lined baking sheet and bake at **400°F for 10 to 12 minutes**, until they shrink and turn deep red and crisp. They firm up more as they cool. Break them into shards and stand them in a small glass or crumble them over whipped feta.

### Pepperoni chips

Spread [pepperoni](/ingredients/pepperoni/) slices on a parchment-lined sheet and bake at **400°F for 6 to 8 minutes**, or microwave them between paper towels for 45 to 60 seconds, until they're sizzling and crisp. Blot off the fat. They eat like a salty chip and go fast.

### Pan-crisped salami or chorizo

Cook thin slices of [Genoa salami](/ingredients/genoa-salami/) or sliced [chorizo](/ingredients/chorizo/) in a dry pan over medium heat for a minute or two per side until they sizzle and curl. Serve them warm.

### Baked brie

Score the top of a small pasteurized [brie](/ingredients/brie/), bake it at **350°F for 10 to 15 minutes** until the center is soft and steaming, and top it with [honey](/ingredients/honey/) or [fig jam](/ingredients/fig-jam/) and walnuts. Put it on the board hot. It's the centerpiece most guests go for first.

A note on cooked meat: once it's cooked, treat it like any other board food. Make it the same day and keep it out **no more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F**.

<h2 id="hosting">Hosting a pregnant guest</h2>
:::

- **Make a separate small plate** if the main board has cold cured meat on it. Shared knives and meat touching cheese can carry bacteria from one food to another.
- **Label the board.** A small card that says "all cheeses pasteurized, all meat cooked" saves the guest from having to ask.
- **Skip the wine pairing for her.** Sparkling apple cider, black iced tea and kombucha follow the same pairing rules. See [zero-proof pairings](/pairings/zero-proof/).

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**Can you eat salami while pregnant?**

Not cold. The CDC advises pregnant people to avoid unheated deli meats and fermented or dry sausages such as salami and pepperoni, and to eat them only reheated to 165°F or until steaming hot. Baked pepperoni chips and pan-crisped salami are easy ways to keep them on the board.

**Is prosciutto safe to eat when pregnant?**

Cold prosciutto is a cured, ready-to-eat meat, so US and UK guidance both say to heat it until steaming hot first. Baking slices into prosciutto crisps at 400°F for 10 to 12 minutes is the simplest way to serve it.

**Can pregnant women eat brie?**

Guidance differs. The CDC focuses on avoiding cheese made with raw milk and queso fresco-type cheeses, and pasteurized brie is widely sold in the US. The NHS says brie, camembert and other white-rind cheeses should be cooked thoroughly. Baked brie served steaming hot meets both, and it is worth asking your own doctor or midwife.

**What cheeses are safe during pregnancy?**

Pasteurized hard and semi-firm cheeses such as cheddar and gouda, and pasteurized cream cheese, mozzarella, feta, string cheese and cottage cheese. Check the label for made with pasteurized milk, because Parmigiano-Reggiano, Comté, traditional Gruyère and some Manchego are made with raw milk.

**What can a pregnant person eat on a charcuterie board?**
- **Cheese:** pasteurized cheddar, Gouda, mozzarella and whipped feta, plus baked brie
- **Meat:** prosciutto crisps and pepperoni chips, cooked until hot
- **Everything else:** washed fruit, crackers, nuts, honey, olives, pickles, hummus and dark chocolate

Keep any cold cured meat on a separate plate with its own utensils.
