# The Best Capicola (Coppa) for a Charcuterie Board: What to Buy

> **Quick Answer:** For a charcuterie board, buy dry-cured coppa sliced thin, such as Volpi Sliced Coppa. At the deli counter, Boar's Head Hot Capocollo is the easy pick: Tasting Table ranked it 8th of 20 Boar's Head meats and called it delicious. No published taste test has ranked capicola brands side by side, so this guide names what to buy without star scores.

Capicola, capocollo, coppa and gabagool are all names for the same cut: the muscle that runs from a pig's neck into the shoulder. It's marbled, so each slice has a pretty pattern of fat, and it tastes richer than prosciutto. The catch is that two very different products share the name. Dry-cured coppa is aged like salami and belongs on a board. Deli "capicola" is often cooked, more like a spiced ham, and works better in sandwiches.

*How we picked these: no published side-by-side taste test of store-bought capicola exists yet, so this guide is not scored with stars like our other buying guides. Instead it names the widely sold options, what each is like and where to find it, using award results where they exist, the few published tests that include them and how this kind of cured meat behaves on a board. Sources are listed at the end, and our method is on the [buying guides hub](/blog/best-charcuterie-brands/#how-we-rank). Stock last checked October 2026.*

### Volpi Sliced Coppa

**Best overall** · Best for: Dry-cured coppa, ready to serve · $$ mid-range

Volpi sells sliced cured coppa in 3 oz packs, in sweet and hot versions ([d'Italia](https://www.ditalia.com/products/volpi-coppa-sweet-hot-spicy)), and some grocery chains stock its Sliced Traditional Coppa ([H-E-B](https://heb.com/product-detail/volpi-sliced-traditional-coppa/5124518)). Dry-cured coppa is the kind to buy for a board: firm, marbled and full-flavored.

- **Why we like it:** Real dry-cured coppa, already sliced thin.
- **Keep in mind:** Small packs, so plan on one per 3 or 4 guests. Hot is coated in red pepper.
- **Where to buy:** Some grocery deli cases (H-E-B and others), Italian markets and online.

### Boar's Head Hot Capocollo

**Easiest to find** · Best for: Deli-counter capicola · $ budget

[Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1035671/ranking-boars-head-deli-meat-from-worst-to-best/) ranked Boar's Head Hot Uncured Capocollo 8th of 20 Boar's Head deli meats and called it delicious. Ask the deli to slice it paper-thin for a board.

- **Why we like it:** Sold at most Boar's Head deli counters, sliced to order.
- **Keep in mind:** A deli-style capicola, milder and softer than dry-cured coppa.
- **Where to buy:** Supermarket deli counters that carry Boar's Head.

### Trader Joe's Spicy Uncured Charcuterie Collection

**Best value** · Best for: Three spicy meats in one pack · $ budget

A 9 oz pack of coppa, sopressa and cooked capocolla. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2203603/best-trader-joes-meats-charcuterie-board/) included it in its July 2026 list of the best Trader Joe's meats for a board, noting that some people find it too spicy.

- **Why we like it:** One pack covers the cured-meat side of a small board.
- **Keep in mind:** Spicy, and it only comes in this mix.
- **Where to buy:** Trader Joe's.

### Columbus Coppa (mild or hot)

**Also good** · Best for: A second easy-to-find coppa · $$ mid-range

Columbus, the San Francisco salami maker behind our [top-ranked mortadella](/blog/best-mortadella-brands/), makes Italian-style coppa in mild and hot versions ([Cured & Cultivated](https://curedandcultivated.com/products/italian-mild-coppa-columbus)).

- **Why we like it:** From a brand many stores already carry.
- **Keep in mind:** Less common than Columbus salami, so call ahead.
- **Where to buy:** Grocery deli cases that stock Columbus, and online.

## Cured coppa or cooked capicola?

- **Dry-cured coppa (capocollo):** salted, seasoned and air-dried for months, like salami. Firm, deep red with white marbling, and safe to eat as is. This is the one for a charcuterie board.
- **Cooked capicola ("capicola ham" or "cotto"):** cooked rather than dried, so it's softer and paler, more like spiced ham. Good in an Italian sub, softer on a board.
- **Hot or sweet:** hot is rubbed with red pepper or paprika; sweet (dolce) is not. For a mixed crowd, buy sweet, or one of each.

For an example of the cooked kind, [Olympia Provisions](https://www.olympiaprovisions.com/products/capicola) sells a capicola that's dry-cured for ten days, then coated in spices and slow-roasted into a tender shoulder ham. Delicious, but it's a different thing from aged coppa.

## What to look for on the label

**Label check:** "Dry cured" or "dry-cured coppa" on the label for a board. Look for a deep red slice with clear white marbling.

**Skip:** Thick deli slices. Coppa should be sliced paper-thin, or it turns chewy. And not every store-brand capicola is a winner: [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2259740/publix-deli-meats-ranked/) ranked Publix Deli Hot Capicola 11th of 13 Publix deli meats.

**How much to buy:** about 1 oz of coppa per guest when it's one of three meats.
**How to serve it:** fold or loosely ruffle the slices so the marbling shows. It pairs well with Manchego, provolone, figs, melon and a Chianti or Lambrusco.

## Capicola and coppa questions

**Is capicola the same as coppa?**

Yes, they're the same cut from the pig's neck and shoulder. Capocollo and coppa are the Italian names, capicola is the American name, and gabagool is New Jersey slang.

**What is the best capicola for a charcuterie board?**

A dry-cured coppa sliced thin, such as Volpi. At the deli counter, Boar's Head Hot Capocollo is the easy choice.

**Is capicola cooked or cured?**

It can be either. Dry-cured coppa is aged like salami and is the better choice for a board. Some deli capicola is cooked, which makes it softer, more like ham.

**Is capicola spicy?**

Hot capicola is coated in red pepper or paprika. Sweet capicola isn't spicy.
