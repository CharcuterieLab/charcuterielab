# Types of Cured Meat: 100 Kinds Explained, With a Chart

> **Quick Answer:** There are hundreds of cured meats, but almost all of them fit into **8 families**. Five sit on a line from **cooked** deli meats (moist and mild) through **semi-dry** sausages, **whole-muscle** meats like prosciutto and **salami** to **jerky** (dry and intense). The other three are **cured fat** like lardo, **pâtés and spreads**, and **cured fish**. Know the family and you know roughly how it will taste, and whether you need to cook it.

<h2 id="how-made">How cured meat is made, in 4 steps</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">All cured meat starts with salt. What happens next decides which family it lands in. Here's <a href="/blog/why-cured-meat-doesnt-spoil/">the science of why it doesn't spoil</a>.</p>
<ol class="tc-make"><li><span class="tc-mk-n">1</span><b>Salt it</b><p>Salt pulls water out of the meat, and with less water, spoilage bacteria can't grow. Most cures also use a little nitrite, which guards against botulism and keeps the meat pink.</p><p class="tc-mk-fx">Prosciutto di Parma is the famous exception: salt is its only cure. All <a href="#whole" class="tc-mk-fam" style="--fc:#8e3b46">whole-muscle meats</a> start here.</p></li><li><span class="tc-mk-n">2</span><b>Grind and ferment (sausages only)</b><p>For salami, meat and fat are chopped, seasoned and stuffed into a casing. Friendly bacteria then turn a little sugar into lactic acid, which adds tang and makes the meat safer.</p><p class="tc-mk-fx">Long fermenting and drying makes <a href="#salami" class="tc-mk-fam" style="--fc:#6e2a35">salami</a>; a short one plus smoke makes <a href="#semidry" class="tc-mk-fam" style="--fc:#8a5a2b">semi-dry sausages</a>.</p></li><li><span class="tc-mk-n">3</span><b>Dry and age</b><p>The meat hangs in cool, humid rooms for weeks, months or years while it loses water and its flavor deepens.</p><p class="tc-mk-fx">Prosciutto di Parma must age at least 14 months; <a href="#dried" class="tc-mk-fam" style="--fc:#5b3a2a">jerky</a> is dried fast with heat.</p></li><li><span class="tc-mk-n">4</span><b>Or cook and smoke it</b><p>Instead of drying, some cured meats are cooked or hot-smoked, which makes them moist, mild and ready to eat in days.</p><p class="tc-mk-fx">That's the whole <a href="#cooked" class="tc-mk-fam" style="--fc:#a8503f">deli counter</a>: ham, mortadella, pastrami, smoked turkey.</p></li></ol>
:::

<h2 id="safety">Is it raw? Is it safe? What does "uncured" mean?</h2>
<div class="tm-safe">
<div><b>Is cured meat raw?</b><p>Many of the best ones were never cooked. Prosciutto and salami are made safe by salt, drying and (for salami) fermentation, and they're sold ready to eat. A few are cured but still raw and need cooking: <a href="/ingredients/pancetta/">pancetta</a>, <a href="/ingredients/guanciale/">guanciale</a>, bacon and <a href="/ingredients/lap-cheong/">lap cheong</a>. The label will say "cook before eating".</p></div>
<div><b>Pregnant?</b><p>The CDC advises people who are pregnant, 65 or older, or have a weakened immune system to heat deli meats to 165°F, or until steaming hot, before eating. Cooked-on-a-pizza prosciutto and salami are fine. More in <a href="/blog/can-you-eat-prosciutto-while-pregnant/">can you eat prosciutto while pregnant</a> and our <a href="/blog/pregnancy-safe-charcuterie-board/">pregnancy-safe board</a>.</p></div>
<div><b>"Uncured" and "no nitrates added"</b><p>In the US, these words usually mean the maker cured the meat with celery powder or another natural source of nitrite instead of sodium nitrite. The meat is still cured. USDA has said it plans to change these label rules.</p></div>
<div><b>How long does it keep?</b><p>An uncut dry salami keeps for weeks (check the label). Sliced deli meat is the most perishable: eat an opened pack within 3 to 5 days. On the table, perishable food shouldn't sit out more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F).</p></div>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**How many types of cured meat are there?**

There's no official count. Almost every region of Italy, Spain, France and Germany has its own hams and sausages, so the true number runs into the hundreds. This guide covers 100, and nearly all cured meats fit into 8 families: cooked cold cuts, semi-dry sausages, whole-muscle meats, salami, jerky, cured fat, spreads and cured fish.

**What are the main types of cured meat?**

Cooked and smoked cold cuts (ham, mortadella, pastrami), semi-dry sausages (summer sausage, meat sticks), whole-muscle cured meats (prosciutto, jamón, coppa, bresaola), dry-cured salami (Genoa, soppressata, chorizo), jerky and dried meats (beef jerky, biltong), cured fat (lardo, pancetta), spreads (pâté, rillettes, 'nduja) and cured fish (smoked salmon, anchovies).

**What is the difference between salami and prosciutto?**

Prosciutto is a whole pork leg that's salted and air-dried for many months, often more than a year, then sliced paper-thin. Salami is chopped meat and fat mixed with spices, stuffed into a casing, fermented and dried. Prosciutto tastes sweet and silky; salami is firmer, tangier and usually more seasoned.

**Is cured meat raw?**

Many dry-cured meats, like prosciutto and salami, are never cooked. Salt, drying and fermentation make them safe, and they're sold ready to eat. Some cured meats are still raw and must be cooked, including pancetta, guanciale, bacon and Chinese sausage. Check the label for "cook before eating".

**What does uncured mean on a meat label?**

In the US, "uncured" usually means the meat was cured with a natural source of nitrite, such as celery powder, instead of sodium nitrite. It is still cured and still contains nitrite. USDA has said it plans to update these labeling rules.

**What is the difference between charcuterie and salumi?**

Charcuterie is the French word for prepared meats (from chair cuite, "cooked meat") and is now used for any cured meat board. Salumi is the Italian word for cured meats. Salami is only one kind of salumi.

**What are the best cured meats for a charcuterie board?**

Pick three from different families: one whole-muscle meat like prosciutto, one salami like Genoa or soppressata, and one surprise, like a spicy 'nduja, a pâté or bresaola for guests who don't eat pork. Plan about 2 ounces of meat per person when there's a meal and 3 to 4 when the board is the meal.
