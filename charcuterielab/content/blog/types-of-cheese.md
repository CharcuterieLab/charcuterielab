# Types of Cheese: 100 Cheeses Explained, With a Chart

> **Quick Answer:** There are more than 1,800 cheeses, but almost all of them fit into **8 families**. Five sit on a line from **fresh** (wet, young, mild) through **soft-ripened**, **semi-soft** and **firm** to **hard** (dry, old, strong). The other three get an extra step: **blue** (mold grown inside), **washed-rind** (the stinky ones) and **flavored**. Know the family and you know roughly how a cheese will taste.

|---|---|
| **Rind** | The outside of the cheese. Bloomy and washed rinds are edible. Skip wax, cloth and very hard rinds. |

| **Bloomy rind** | A white, fuzzy mold rind, like on [Brie](/ingredients/brie/). |

| **Pasta filata** | "Stretched curd": the curd is heated and stretched. Mozzarella, provolone, Oaxaca, string cheese. |
| **Raw milk** | Unpasteurized. In the US, raw-milk cheese has to be aged at least 60 days. |
| **PDO / DOP / AOP** | A protected name. It must be made in one region, by set rules. [Parmigiano-Reggiano](/ingredients/parmigiano-reggiano/), [Roquefort](/ingredients/roquefort/) and [Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/) are examples. |
| **Crystals** | Crunchy white bits in aged cheese: harmless crystals of amino acids or calcium lactate that form with age. Not mold. |

## Good to know

- **Pregnant?** In the US, most cheese made with pasteurized milk is fine, but be careful with soft Latin-style cheeses like queso fresco. See [which cheeses are safe during pregnancy](/blog/cheese-safe-during-pregnancy/).
- **Lactose:** Long-aged hard cheeses like [Parmigiano-Reggiano](/ingredients/parmigiano-reggiano/), aged cheddar and [Aged Gouda](/ingredients/aged-gouda/) have very little lactose. Most of it drains off with the whey, and aging breaks down what's left.
- **Serving:** Take hard cheese out of the fridge 45 to 60 minutes before serving and soft cheese 20 to 30 minutes before. Cold cheese tastes muted. Here's [how to cut each shape](/blog/how-to-cut-cheese-charcuterie-board/).
- **Storing:** Wrap it in cheese paper or wax paper, not tight plastic. See [how to store cheese](/blog/cheese-storage/) and [whether you can freeze it](/blog/can-you-freeze-cheese/).

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**How many types of cheese are there?**

There's no official count. By some estimates there are more than 1,800, according to Wisconsin Cheese. Nearly all of them fit into 8 families: fresh, soft-ripened, semi-soft, firm, hard, blue, washed-rind and flavored. Once you know the family, you can roughly guess how a new cheese will taste.

**What are the main types of cheese?**

Fresh (ricotta, mozzarella, feta), soft-ripened (Brie, Camembert), semi-soft (Havarti, Monterey Jack), firm (cheddar, Gruyère, Manchego), hard (Parmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino Romano), blue (Gorgonzola, Stilton), washed-rind (Taleggio, Époisses) and flavored or specialty (smoked Gouda, truffle cheese).

**What is the most popular cheese in the US?**

Mozzarella. Americans ate about 12.5 pounds of mozzarella per person in 2024, compared with 10.9 pounds of cheddar, according to USDA data reported by Cheese Reporter. Pizza is a big reason why.

**What is the difference between soft and hard cheese?**

Mostly water and time. Soft cheeses keep more moisture and are eaten young, so they taste milky and mild. Hard cheeses are pressed to remove whey and aged for months or years, so they're drier, saltier and more intense, and they keep much longer.

**What are the mildest cheeses?**

Fresh mozzarella, ricotta, Havarti, young Gouda, Colby Jack, Monterey Jack, Baby Swiss and Brie are the mildest common cheeses. They're the safest picks for kids and picky eaters.

**What cheese melts best?**

Gruyère, Fontina, Raclette, Monterey Jack, Havarti, low-moisture mozzarella, Oaxaca and American cheese melt the smoothest. Halloumi, paneer and queso blanco barely melt at all, which is why you can grill or fry them.

**Which cheeses smell strong but taste mild?**

Washed-rind cheeses. Taleggio, Limburger and Pont-l'Évêque smell much stronger than they taste. The smell comes from bacteria on the rind, and the inside is usually buttery and mild.
