<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to pick ricotta, and how to serve it on a board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Whole milk</strong> for a board; part skim is fine for lighter cooking.</li><li><strong>Short ingredient list:</strong> milk or whey, an acid and salt. Gums make it stiffer.</li><li><strong>Watery or grainy?</strong> Drain it in a mesh strainer for 30 minutes, stirring now and then. Milk Street found that brought supermarket ricotta close to the better brands.</li><li><strong>Whipped ricotta:</strong> blend 1 cup ricotta with 1 to 2 tbsp olive oil, a pinch of salt and lemon zest until smooth. Top with honey, hot honey or chopped pistachios.</li><li><strong>On the board:</strong> a bowl of it with crostini, figs, prosciutto or a spoonful of &rsquo;nduja. About 2 oz per person.</li><li><strong>Use it fast:</strong> fresh ricotta only lasts a few days once opened.</li></ul>

## Ricotta questions

**What is the best ricotta brand?**

BelGioioso Ricotta con Latte won America's Test Kitchen's tasting; Organic Valley won Sporked's. For whipped ricotta, Galbani.

**Whole milk or part skim for whipped ricotta?**

Whole milk. It whips smoother and tastes richer.

**Why is my ricotta watery or grainy?**

Some brands have a loose, curdy texture. Drain it in a strainer for 30 minutes, then whip it.

**Can you put ricotta on a charcuterie board?**

Yes: whip it and serve it in a bowl with honey, crostini and figs.

**Is ricotta the same as cottage cheese?**

No. Ricotta has finer curds and a sweeter taste, and is traditionally made from whey.
