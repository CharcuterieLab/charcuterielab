<h2 id="how-to-choose">Grazing table checklist</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Table and paper:</strong> one caterer sizes tables at about 4 feet for 30 guests and 7 feet for 50; cover it end to end in butcher paper.</li><li><strong>Height:</strong> two or three risers and one footed stand so the eye travels up and down the table.</li><li><strong>Bowls:</strong> one small bowl for every wet item (olives, honey, jam, dips).</li><li><strong>Tools:</strong> small tongs for meat, cheese knives at every hard cheese, cocktail picks in a cup.</li><li><strong>Labels:</strong> write cheese names on the paper with a marker, and flag nuts and gluten.</li><li><strong>Food safety:</strong> perishable food comes off after about two hours; set out half and refill from the fridge.</li></ul>

## Grazing table supply questions

**What do you put down on a grazing table?**

Food-safe butcher paper. It protects the table, lets you label foods on it, and makes cleanup easy.

**How do you add height to a grazing table?**

With wood risers, footed cake stands, overturned crates and stacked boards.

**How big a table do I need for a grazing table?**

One caterer plans about 4 feet of table for 30 guests, 5 feet for 40 and 7 feet for 50.

**Is butcher paper food safe?**

Food-grade butcher paper is made for wrapping food. Avoid craft paper with coatings or dyes not meant for food.

**How long can a grazing table sit out?**

About two hours for perishable foods. Put out half and refill from the fridge.
