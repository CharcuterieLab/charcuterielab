# Feast of the Seven Fishes Board: A No-Cook Christmas Eve Spread

> **Quick Answer:** Build the Feast of the Seven Fishes on one board with seven seafoods you can buy ready to eat: **smoked salmon, white anchovies, sardines, shrimp, a salt cod or smoked trout spread, marinated octopus or calamari, and bottarga**. Add crusty bread, lemon, olives and Prosecco. Plan 3 to 4 ounces of seafood per person if it's the meal.

A full Feast of the Seven Fishes means hours at the stove on Christmas Eve. This version keeps the spirit (seven seafoods, no meat, a long table and good bread) but every fish comes from a tin, a jar, the deli case or the smoked-fish fridge. You arrange it; nobody fries.

:::html
<h2 id="what-it-is">What the Feast of the Seven Fishes is</h2>
:::

- **An Italian-American Christmas Eve dinner.** Britannica describes it as a meatless Christmas Eve meal of seven different fish dishes, and notes the name itself is believed to be Italian-American.
- **Rooted in southern Italy's La Vigilia.** In Italy the night is simply "the Vigil", and the meal is the dinner of the Vigil. The tradition comes from the south, where the custom of eating fish on Christmas Eve has no fixed count of seven.
- **The number is flexible.** Some families serve 7 dishes, some 9, 11 or 13, and some count every fish on the table. There's no official list.
- **The classics:** baccalà (salt cod), calamari, eel and sardines, the affordable fish of southern Italy, plus shrimp, clams, mussels, anchovies and smelts.

:::html
<h2 id="the-seven">The seven fishes for a no-cook board</h2>
:::

Each pick is ready to eat as bought, covers a different texture, and has an easy swap.

| # | Fish | How it goes on the board | Swap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Smoked salmon](/ingredients/smoked-salmon/) | Loose folds or ribbons, with lemon and capers | [Gravlax](/ingredients/gravlax/) or [smoked trout](/ingredients/smoked-trout/) |
| 2 | [White anchovies (boquerones)](/ingredients/boquerones/) | Laid flat on crostini or in a small dish with their oil | Salt-cured [anchovies](/ingredients/anchovies/) on buttered toast |
| 3 | [Sardines](/ingredients/sardines/) | A good tin, opened on the board with a fork | Mackerel in olive oil |
| 4 | [Shrimp](/ingredients/cocktail-shrimp/) | Chilled, peeled, tails on, around a lemony aioli | Crab claws |
| 5 | Baccalà spread (whipped salt cod) | In a bowl with toasted bread, the traditional dish | Smoked trout dip |
| 6 | [Marinated octopus](/ingredients/tinned-octopus/) or calamari | Deli-counter seafood salad, or tinned octopus with lemon | Marinated mussels |
| 7 | [Bottarga](/ingredients/bottarga/) | Shaved thin over buttered crostini | Smoked oysters, or a little [caviar](/ingredients/caviar/) |

**Short on time?** Make it seven with all-tinned fish: sardines, anchovies, mackerel, tuna belly, octopus, mussels and smoked oysters. Our [best tinned fish](/blog/best-tinned-fish/) guide has the tins worth buying.

<h2 id="how-much">How much to buy</h2>
:::

Plan **3 to 4 ounces of seafood per person** when the board is dinner, and **2 ounces** when a cooked meal follows. Split it unevenly: more of the crowd-pleasers (salmon and shrimp), less of the bold ones (anchovies and bottarga).

| Guests | Seafood total (as the meal) | Smoked salmon | Shrimp | Each tin or jar | Bread and crackers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 guests | 1 lb | 4 oz | 8 large | 1 of each | 1 baguette |
| 8 guests | 2 lb | 8 oz | 16 large | 1 to 2 of each | 2 baguettes + 1 box crackers |
| 12 guests | 3 lb | 12 oz | 24 large | 2 of each | 3 baguettes + 2 boxes crackers |

For guest counts beyond 12, use the [Party Planner](/party-planner/) and swap in seafood for the meats.

:::html
<h2 id="with-it">What goes with it</h2>
:::

- **Bread first.** [Crostini](/blog/crostini-recipe/), torn [focaccia](/ingredients/focaccia/), [grissini](/ingredients/grissini/) and [taralli](/ingredients/taralli/). Plain [water crackers](/ingredients/water-crackers/) keep the fish in charge.
- **Something briny.** [Castelvetrano olives](/ingredients/castelvetrano-olives/), [capers](/ingredients/capers/), [caperberries](/ingredients/caperberries/) and [giardiniera](/ingredients/giardiniera/).
- **Something creamy.** [Ricotta](/ingredients/ricotta/) or [burrata](/ingredients/burrata/) to cool the salt, or crème fraîche for the salmon.
- **Something fresh.** Lemon wedges, shaved fennel, [blood oranges](/ingredients/blood-oranges/) and parsley.
- **Leave off:** strong cheese and cured meat. The night is meatless, and big cheeses fight delicate fish.

:::html
<h2 id="build">How to build it</h2>
:::

1. **Start with bowls.** Place the baccalà spread, the aioli and the marinated seafood first, spread out across the board.
2. **Add the big fish.** Fold the smoked salmon into two loose piles, and fan the shrimp around the aioli.
3. **Open the tins.** Set two tins on the board, lids peeled back, with small forks.
4. **Make a crostini row.** Lay out crostini topped with anchovies and with bottarga, so guests see how to eat them.
5. **Fill with bread, olives and citrus.** Tuck lemon wedges next to every fish.
6. **Label the seven.** Small cards help guests find all seven, and it's half the fun.

For layout basics, see [how to make a charcuterie board](/blog/how-to-make-charcuterie-board/) and our [seafood charcuterie board](/boards/seafood-charcuterie-board/) plan.

<h2 id="drinks">What to drink</h2>
:::

- **Sparkling wine is the safest pour.** [Prosecco or Champagne](/pairings/wine/champagne-prosecco/) cuts through oily fish and salt, and it feels like Christmas Eve. See [why bubbles cut fat](/blog/why-bubbles-cut-fat/).
- **Crisp Italian whites.** Vermentino, Verdicchio, Soave or Pinot Grigio. A [Sauvignon Blanc](/pairings/wine/sauvignon-blanc/) also works.
- **Skip big reds.** Tannic reds like Cabernet can taste metallic with oily fish. If someone wants red, pour a light, chilled one.
- **No alcohol:** sparkling water with lemon, or a dry sparkling cider. More ideas in our [zero-proof pairing guide](/pairings/zero-proof/).

<h2 id="make-ahead">Make-ahead plan for Christmas Eve</h2>
:::

- **Dec 21–22:** buy the tins, jars, crackers, olives and wine.
- **Dec 23:** buy smoked salmon, shrimp and deli seafood. Make the aioli and the baccalà or trout spread.
- **Dec 24, afternoon:** bake the [crostini](/blog/crostini-recipe/), slice lemons, set out bowls and the empty board.
- **30 minutes before guests:** add the cold seafood, open the tins, fill the gaps.

:::html
<h2 id="safety">Keeping seafood safe</h2>
:::

- **Two hours, then back in the fridge.** Seafood shouldn't sit at room temperature longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. Put out half and refill from the fridge. More in [how long a charcuterie board can sit out](/blog/how-long-charcuterie-board-last/).
- **Keep it cold on the board.** Set the shrimp and salmon on a chilled plate or over a tray of ice.
- **Pregnant guests:** refrigerated smoked seafood like lox carries a listeria risk unless it's cooked. Tinned and shelf-stable fish are the safer picks. See our [pregnancy-safe board](/blog/pregnancy-safe-charcuterie-board/).
- **Shellfish allergies:** keep the shrimp and mussels on their own plate with a label.

<h2 id="faq">Feast of the Seven Fishes: FAQ</h2>
:::

**What are the seven fishes on Christmas Eve?**

There's no fixed list. Families choose their own, and some serve more than seven or fewer. Common choices are baccalà (salt cod), fried calamari, shrimp, clams, mussels, anchovies, sardines and smelts. For a no-cook board, use smoked salmon, white anchovies, sardines, shrimp, a salt cod or smoked trout spread, marinated octopus or calamari, and bottarga or smoked mussels.

**Is the Feast of the Seven Fishes Italian?**

It's Italian-American. Southern Italian families have long eaten a meatless fish dinner on Christmas Eve, called La Vigilia, but the name Feast of the Seven Fishes and the count of seven grew up in Italian-American homes in the 20th century.

**Why seven fishes?**

Nobody knows for sure. Common explanations are the seven sacraments, the seven days of creation and the seven hills of Rome, and some families serve 9, 11 or 13 dishes instead. The meal is meatless because Catholics traditionally abstained from meat on the eve of a feast day.

**How much seafood do I need for a Feast of the Seven Fishes board?**

About 3 to 4 ounces of seafood per person when the board is the main event, or 2 ounces when a cooked dinner follows. For 8 people as the meal, that's roughly 2 pounds of seafood across all seven, plus bread, crackers and sides.

**What wine goes with the Feast of the Seven Fishes?**

Sparkling wine is the safest pick: Prosecco, Champagne or Cava cuts through oily fish and salt. Crisp Italian whites like Vermentino, Verdicchio or Pinot Grigio also work. Skip big tannic reds, which can taste metallic with oily fish.

**Can I make a seafood board ahead of time?**

Partly. Make dips and marinated seafood the day before and keep them cold. Slice bread and set out the dry things in the afternoon. Put the fish on the board right before guests arrive, and don't leave it out more than 2 hours.
