<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to build a tinned fish board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Serve them in the tins.</strong> Peel the lids back, set the open tins on a board and let the oil do the work. That&#x27;s how Spanish and Portuguese bars serve conservas.</li><li><strong>Pick three styles:</strong> one mild (tuna or trout), one rich (sardines or mackerel) and one punchy (anchovies or mussels in escabeche).</li><li><strong>Add bread and acid:</strong> toasted baguette or crusty bread, good butter, lemon wedges, pickled shallots, cornichons, olives and potato chips.</li><li><strong>Pour something crisp:</strong> dry cava, Albariño, vinho verde, a dry fino sherry or a cold lager.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> one 3 to 4 oz tin serves 2 to 3 people as part of a board; plan 3 to 4 tins for 8 guests.</li></ul>

## Tinned fish questions

**What is the best tinned fish?**

For a luxury tin, Ortiz Bonito del Norte tuna. Sporked's best sardines are Nuri in olive oil, and Consumer Reports' best anchovies are Fishwife's Cantabrian anchovies.

**What is conservas?**

Spanish and Portuguese tinned seafood, usually packed in olive oil and served straight from the tin with bread.

**What do you serve with tinned fish?**

Crusty bread or toast, good butter, lemon, pickled shallots, olives, potato chips and a crisp white wine or cava.

**How many tins do I need for a party?**

About one tin for every 2 to 3 people as part of a board, so 3 to 4 tins for 8 guests.

**Does tinned fish go bad?**

Unopened tins keep for years; check the date. Once opened, refrigerate leftovers in a covered container and eat them within a day or two.
