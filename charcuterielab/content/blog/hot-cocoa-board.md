# Hot Cocoa Board: How to Set Up a Hot Chocolate Board (Serves 8 to 20)

> **Quick Answer:** Set out a pot of cocoa with **toppings** (marshmallows, whipped cream, chocolate chips, crushed peppermint) and **dippers** (cookies, pretzel rods, candy canes). Plan **12 oz per guest** and keep it warm in a slow cooker.

It's cheap, mostly pantry items, and takes 20 minutes. Great for kids and snow days.

## How much to make for 8, 12 or 20 guests

Plan 1½ mugs per guest. Most adults have one full mug, and kids come back for a second, smaller one. A standard mug holds 8 oz of hot chocolate with room for toppings.

| Item | 8 guests | 12 guests | 20 guests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole milk (for homemade cocoa) | 1 gallon | 1½ gallons | 2½ gallons |
| Mini marshmallows | 1 bag (10 oz) | 1½ bags | 2½ bags |
| Large or flavored marshmallows | 12 to 16 | 18 to 24 | 30 to 40 |
| Whipped cream | 1 can or 1 cup heavy cream, whipped | 2 cans | 3 cans |
| Chocolate chips and candies (combined) | 2 cups | 3 cups | 5 cups |
| Sauces (caramel, chocolate) | 1 jar each | 1 jar each | 2 jars each |
| Dippers (cookies, biscotti, pretzel rods) | 24 to 32 | 36 to 48 | 60 to 80 |
| Mini candy canes (stirrers) | 1 box (12) | 1 to 2 boxes | 2 boxes |

If the cocoa board is the only dessert, add 25% more dippers.

## Slow cooker hot chocolate (about 1 gallon, serves 8)

- 1 gallon whole milk
- 1 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1 to 1¼ cups sugar, to taste
- 1½ cups semisweet or dark chocolate chips
- 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
- ¼ teaspoon salt

1. Whisk the cocoa, sugar, salt and about 2 cups of the milk in the slow cooker until smooth.
2. Whisk in the rest of the milk and the chocolate chips.
3. Cook on high about 2 hours, or low 3 to 4 hours, whisking every 30 minutes, until steaming and melted.
4. Stir in the vanilla and switch to warm.

**On the stovetop:** the same recipe takes about 15 minutes over medium heat in a large pot. Don't let it boil.

For 12 guests make 1½ batches, and for 20 make 2½ batches in two slow cookers. A good [dark chocolate](/blog/best-dark-chocolate/) in place of the chips makes the cocoa noticeably richer.

## What to put on the board: 25 toppings and dippers

Pick 6 to 10 from this list. More than that crowds the board and slows the line.

| Group | Pick 2 to 4 |
|---|---|
| Marshmallows | Mini marshmallows, large marshmallows, peppermint or gingerbread marshmallows, marshmallow fluff |
| Creamy | Whipped cream, whipped cream cans in two flavors, chocolate whipped cream |
| Chocolate | Semisweet chips, white chocolate chips, chopped chocolate bars, mini peanut butter cups |
| Crunchy | Crushed peppermint, crushed toffee, crushed graham crackers, sprinkles, mini chocolate candies |
| Sauces and flavors | Caramel sauce, chocolate sauce, peppermint syrup, cinnamon, a pinch of flaky salt |
| Dippers | [Shortbread](/blog/best-shortbread/), [biscotti](/ingredients/biscotti/), pretzel rods, [pizzelle](/ingredients/pizzelle/), [speculoos](/ingredients/speculoos/), candy canes, wafer rolls |

**Flavor ideas to label:** peppermint mocha (peppermint syrup, crushed candy canes), salted caramel (caramel sauce, flaky salt), s'mores (graham crumbs, toasted marshmallow), and Mexican hot chocolate (cinnamon and a pinch of chili).

## How to lay out the board

1. **Pick a large tray or board.** An 18 inch board or a rimmed baking sheet holds toppings for 12. Use two for 20.
2. **Bowls first.** Put the small bowls of loose toppings (chips, sprinkles, crushed peppermint) on the board first, spaced apart. Loose, rolling items always go in bowls.
3. **Sauces in squeeze bottles or small jars** with a spoon, at one corner so drips stay in one place.
4. **Big items fill the gaps.** Large marshmallows, cookies, biscotti and pretzel rods go in piles between the bowls.
5. **Candy canes on the edge**, hooked over the side of the board or a mug.
6. **The cocoa beside the board**, with mugs stacked and a ladle, and the whipped cream in an ice-filled bowl at the end of the line.

Set it up in the order guests use it: mugs, cocoa, then toppings, then whipped cream and sauces last.

## Keeping it warm and safe

- Keep the cocoa on the **warm** setting and stir it every 30 minutes so a skin doesn't form.
- Whipped cream and milk-based toppings shouldn't sit out more than 2 hours. Keep the whipped cream on ice and refill from the fridge.
- With small kids, fill their mugs halfway and add a few marshmallows or a splash of cold milk to cool it down.
- Label anything with nuts or peanut butter, and keep it in its own bowl with its own spoon.

## The grown-up version

For an adults-only party, or one corner of a family party, add a small tray of liqueurs with a jigger: Irish cream, peppermint schnapps, coffee liqueur, amaretto or bourbon. About 1 oz per mug is plenty. Label the tray clearly and keep it at the far end of the table, away from the kids' side. Offer peppermint or caramel syrup as a zero-proof option on the same tray.

## Pair it with a savory board

A cocoa board is sweet and filling, so it works best after something savory. Serve a [Christmas charcuterie board](/blog/christmas-charcuterie-board/) or a [winter board](/blog/winter-charcuterie-board/) first, then bring out the cocoa for dessert. Aged cheddar and a few salted nuts on the side balance the sweetness.

## What it costs

A hot cocoa board for 12 costs about **$25 to $40** with homemade cocoa and store-brand toppings, around $2 to $3.50 per guest. Packets of instant cocoa cost more per mug than making it in a slow cooker. Seasonal flavored marshmallows and specialty whipped creams are the easiest places to save.

## FAQ

**What goes on a hot cocoa board?**
- **Toppings:** mini and large marshmallows, whipped cream, chocolate chips, crushed peppermint, caramel and chocolate sauce, sprinkles
- **Dippers:** cookies, biscotti, pretzel rods, candy canes
- **Setup:** hot chocolate in a slow cooker or carafe, plus mugs and spoons

**How much hot chocolate do I need per person?**

Plan 1½ mugs per guest, or about 12 oz of hot chocolate each. That is about 1 gallon of milk for 8 guests, 1½ gallons for 12 and 2½ gallons for 20, since kids usually come back for a second, smaller cup.

**How do you keep hot chocolate warm for a party?**

Make it in a slow cooker or move it there once it is hot, then keep it on the warm setting and stir every 30 minutes so a skin doesn't form. An insulated drink dispenser or a thermal carafe also holds it warm for a couple of hours.

**Can you make a hot cocoa board ahead of time?**

Yes. Fill the topping bowls and set out the dry dippers up to a few hours ahead and cover them. Start the hot chocolate in a slow cooker 2 to 3 hours ahead, or make it on the stove 30 minutes before guests arrive, and add whipped cream and any cold toppings last.

**How do you make an adult hot chocolate board?**

Set up the same board and add a small, clearly labeled adults-only tray with Irish cream, peppermint schnapps, coffee liqueur or bourbon, about 1 oz per mug. Keep it at the far end, away from the kids' side, and offer a non-alcoholic peppermint or caramel syrup too.
