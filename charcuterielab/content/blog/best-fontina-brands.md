<h2 id="how-to-choose">Real fontina vs the copies, and how to serve it</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Real Fontina Val d'Aosta</strong> has a stamped, brownish natural rind and an earthy, mushroomy taste.</li><li><strong>Red wax</strong> means Danish or Swedish style: milder, sweeter and softer.</li><li><strong>Fontal or fontinella</strong> are fontina-style cheeses made outside the Aosta Valley.</li><li><strong>Pair it with</strong> prosciutto, marinated mushrooms, walnuts, roasted grapes and honey.</li><li><strong>Drink:</strong> Barbera, Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir.</li><li><strong>Serve it</strong> at room temperature, out of the fridge 45 minutes ahead. About 1 oz per person; melt leftovers into grilled cheese.</li></ul>

## Fontina questions

**What is the best fontina cheese?**

Fontina Val d'Aosta DOP from Italy. At the supermarket, BelGioioso Aged Fontina; for a mild crowd-pleaser, Danish red-wax fontina.

**Is Danish fontina real fontina?**

It's a milder, sweeter cheese inspired by the Italian original. Only cheese from the Aosta Valley can be called Fontina Val d'Aosta.

**Is fontina good on a charcuterie board?**

Yes. Real fontina is earthy and nutty; the mild styles are buttery and kid-friendly.

**Fontina vs Gruyère?**

Both are nutty Alpine-style cheeses. Fontina is softer and milder; Gruyère is firmer and sharper.

**How can I tell real Fontina Val d'Aosta?**

Look for the DOP seal, the consortium stamp and a natural brownish rind, not red wax.
