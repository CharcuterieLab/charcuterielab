# Trader Joe's Charcuterie Board: Shopping List for 10 + Prices

> **Quick Answer:** For 10 guests where the board is the party food, buy **30 oz of cheese and 30 oz of meat** (just under 2 lb of each) at Trader Joe's: Unexpected Cheddar, sliced Manchego, Double Cream Brie and Creamy Toscano soaked in Syrah, plus salame, Calabrese and prosciutto. Add Fig and Olive Crisps, Savory Thins, a baguette, grapes, raspberries, Marcona almonds, cornichons and dark chocolate. At prices seen in 2026 it comes to about **$90 to $110, or $9 to $11 a guest**.

Trader Joe's is built for this. The cheese case is small but well chosen, the crackers are cheap, and the extras that make a board look finished (dried orange slices, Marcona almonds, dark chocolate) cost a couple of dollars each. The trick is buying the right amounts, because it's easy to come home with seven cheeses and no meat.

## How much to buy for 10

The Lab standard is 3 oz each of meat and cheese per guest when the board is the main food, and 2 oz each when a meal follows. Under 20 guests you don't need a buffer.

| Item | Board is the party food | Before a meal |
|---|---|---|
| Cheese | 30 oz | 20 oz |
| Cured meat | 30 oz | 20 oz |
| Crackers and bread | 100 to 120 pieces | 60 to 80 pieces |
| Fruit | 2 to 3 lb | 1½ to 2 lb |
| Nuts | 1½ cups | 1 cup |
| Olives and pickles | 1½ cups | 1 cup |

Trader Joe's pack sizes vary and change, so read the weight on each label and keep a running total until you hit the ounces above.

## Seasonal finds worth grabbing

Trader Joe's rotates seasonal items in and out, so these depend on when you shop.

- **Fall:** look for cranberry or pumpkin crackers and crisps, apple cider products and any cheese with cranberries. They fit a [fall board](/blog/fall-charcuterie-board/) or a [Friendsgiving board](/blog/friendsgiving-charcuterie-board/).
- **Holiday season:** cranberry goat cheese, festive cheese assortments and peppermint or chocolate extras for a dessert corner. See the [Christmas wreath board](/blog/christmas-wreath-charcuterie-board/).
- **Year-round staples:** the cheeses, salame and crackers above.

If a seasonal item is gone, swap in the year-round version. A [cranberry goat cheese](/ingredients/cranberry-goat-cheese/) can be made at home by rolling a plain goat log in chopped dried cranberries.

## Build it in 20 minutes

1. **1 hour before:** take out the Manchego and Unexpected Cheddar (45 to 60 minutes for aged cheese).
2. **30 minutes before:** take out the brie, Toscano and meat.
3. **Bowls first:** olives, cornichons, almonds.
4. **Cheese:** the brie wheel whole with a knife, the cheddar crumbled into rough chunks, the Manchego fanned.
5. **Meat:** salame rivers, prosciutto in loose piles, salame coins in a stack.
6. **Crackers** in two long runs, then grapes, raspberries, orange slices and chocolate in the gaps. Tuck the rosemary in last.

Keep the board out no more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.

## FAQ

**How much does a Trader Joe's charcuterie board cost?**
About $90 to $110 for a full board for 10 people where the charcuterie is the party food, or roughly $9 to $11 a guest, using prices seen at Trader Joe's in 2026. A smaller board for 4 to 6 can be built for about $25.

**What cheese should I get at Trader Joe's for a charcuterie board?**
Unexpected Cheddar, sliced Manchego, a Double Cream Brie and Creamy Toscano Cheese Soaked in Syrah give you a sharp, a nutty, a soft and a wine-washed cheese. Boursin and Port Salut are good swaps.

**What meat does Trader Joe's have for charcuterie?**
Trader Joe's usually carries sliced Italian dry salame and a salame stick, Calabrese, sliced prosciutto and a mixed Italian deli selection. Prosciutto-wrapped mozzarella is an easy extra.

**How much meat and cheese do I need for 10 people?**
When the board is the main party food, plan 3 oz each of meat and cheese per guest, which is 30 oz, just under 2 lb, of each for 10 people. If a meal follows, 2 oz each per guest, or 20 oz of each, is enough.

**What are the best Trader Joe's crackers for charcuterie?**
Fig and Olive Crisps for the sweet-salty cheeses, Savory Thins as a cheap neutral cracker, and a sliced baguette. In the fall, look for the seasonal cranberry and pumpkin crisps.
