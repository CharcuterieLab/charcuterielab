### 1. Appleton Farms Prosciutto

**★★★★☆ 4.2 out of 5** · Best overall · Best for: Aldi shoppers · $ budget

Aldi's store brand scored a perfect 10 at [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-prosciutto/), which called it practically as good as freshly sliced prosciutto that costs much more.

- **Why we like it:** Top taste-test score. Very cheap.
- **Keep in mind:** Only at Aldi.
- **Where to buy:** Aldi.

### 2. Creminelli Prosciutto

**★★★★☆ 4.0 out of 5** · Best splurge · Best for: Thin, delicate slices · $$$ premium

[Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-prosciutto/) scored it 9 out of 10 for its extremely thin cut and flavor crystals. American-made from humanely raised pork.

- **Why we like it:** Very thin. Excellent texture.
- **Keep in mind:** Expensive.
- **Where to buy:** Whole Foods, specialty grocers.

## What to look for on the label

**Label check:** For Italian prosciutto, a DOP mark and the ingredients pork and salt. Anything labeled only 'prosciutto' with no origin is usually a quick-cured domestic ham.

**Skip:** Thick-cut prosciutto for a board. It should be thin enough to drape.

## Prosciutto questions

**What is the best prosciutto for a charcuterie board?**

Parma or San Daniele DOP sliced to order. For a pre-sliced pack, Creminelli or La Quercia; Aldi's Appleton Farms is the best value.

**Is American prosciutto good?**

The best is. La Quercia and Creminelli make American prosciutto that holds its own against imported.

**How do I serve prosciutto on a board?**

Take it out about 20 minutes before serving and drape or fold it loosely so it stays tender.
