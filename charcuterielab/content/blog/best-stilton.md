<h2 id="how-to-choose">Buying and serving Stilton</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Label check:</strong> real Stilton carries the PDO name. It's made only in Derbyshire, Leicestershire and Nottinghamshire, from pasteurized milk, by a handful of licensed dairies.</li><li><strong>Serve it right:</strong> take it out an hour ahead and cut thin wedges from the center out. Don't scoop the middle and pour in port.</li><li><strong>Pair it with</strong> tawny or ruby port, walnuts, pears, dried figs, oatcakes and a drizzle of honey.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> about 1 oz per person when it's one of several cheeses.</li><li><strong>Skip:</strong> pre-crumbled Stilton for a board; it dries out and loses its creamy texture.</li></ul>

## Stilton questions

**What is the best Stilton to buy?**

Colston Bassett, from a specialty counter like Murray's. On a budget, Costco's Coombe Castle Blue Stilton is real PDO Stilton for about $14 a pound.

**Is Stilton pasteurized?**

Yes. Stilton must be made from pasteurized milk. Stichelton is the raw-milk version, which is why it can't use the name.

**Why is Stilton served with port?**

The sweet, rich wine balances Stilton's salt and tang. It's a long English tradition, especially at Christmas.

**What's white Stilton?**

Stilton made without the blue mold. It's mild and crumbly and usually sold mixed with fruit like cranberries, apricots or blueberries.

**How much Stilton per person?**

About 1 oz per person on a mixed board, or 2 oz if it's the main cheese after dinner.
