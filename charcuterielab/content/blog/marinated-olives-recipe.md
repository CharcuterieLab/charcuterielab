# Marinated Olives Recipe: Citrus, Garlic and Herbs in 10 Minutes

> **Quick Answer:** Warm **1/3 cup olive oil** with garlic, **orange and lemon peel**, rosemary, thyme, fennel seed and chili flakes for 3 to 5 minutes. Pour it over **2 cups mixed olives**, add lemon juice and marinate **1 hour**. Serve warm. Refrigerate leftovers and **eat within 4 days**.

Ten minutes of work turns a jar of plain olives into the best-smelling thing on the [board](/blog/charcuterie-board-olives/). The warm oil is half the point: put bread next to it.

## Citrus and herb marinated olives

**Makes:** 2 cups · **Serves:** 8 to 10 · **Prep:** 5 minutes · **Cook:** 5 minutes · **Marinate:** 1 hour

**Ingredients**
- 2 cups mixed olives with pits, such as [Castelvetrano](/ingredients/castelvetrano-olives/), [Kalamata](/ingredients/kalamata-olives/) and [Manzanilla](/ingredients/manzanilla-olives/), drained
- 1/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 3 strips orange peel and 2 strips lemon peel, no white pith
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1/2 teaspoon fennel seeds, lightly crushed
- 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice

**Steps**
1. Drain the olives well and put them in a heatproof bowl.
2. Put the olive oil, garlic, citrus peel, rosemary, thyme, fennel seeds and red pepper flakes in a small saucepan over low heat.
3. Warm gently for 3 to 5 minutes, until the garlic is fragrant and just starting to sizzle. Don't let it brown.
4. Pour the warm oil and aromatics over the olives, add the lemon juice and toss.
5. Marinate for at least 1 hour at room temperature, tossing once or twice. Serve warm or at room temperature.

**Three tips that make them better**
- **Buy olives with pits.** Pitted olives go soft and lose flavor faster. Put a small bowl out for the pits.
- **Use a vegetable peeler for the citrus.** Wide strips with no white pith flavor the oil without bitterness.
- **Warm, don't fry.** Gentle heat pulls the flavor into the oil. Browned garlic tastes bitter.

## 4 variations

| Version | Swap or add |
|---|---|
| **Spanish** | Smoked paprika, sherry vinegar instead of lemon juice, and [Marcona almonds](/ingredients/marcona-almonds/) tossed in at serving |
| **Greek** | All Kalamata, dried oregano instead of rosemary, red wine vinegar, and cubes of [feta](/ingredients/feta/) added just before serving |
| **Spicy Calabrian** | Double the chili flakes or add sliced Calabrian chilies |
| **Holiday** | Orange peel only, a cinnamon stick and a few cranberries for color |

## How to serve them on a board

- **Use a bowl, not the board.** The oil runs. A shallow bowl with a small spoon works best, plus a second small bowl for pits.
- **Serve warm in winter.** Reheat gently in a skillet for 2 to 3 minutes just before guests arrive.
- **Put bread or crostini next to it.** Guests will dip in the oil. See our [crostini recipe](/blog/crostini-recipe/).
- **Pair with salty meat and fresh cheese.** Olives sit well beside [Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/), feta, chorizo and salami.
- **How much:** about 1/4 cup per guest when olives are one of several briny items; 2 cups covers 8 to 10 people.

## Storage and food safety

Garlic and fresh herbs held in oil are low-acid and sealed from air, which is the kind of setting where *Clostridium botulinum* can grow if the mixture is kept too long. Extension food-safety guides advise **refrigerating vegetable-and-herb-in-oil mixtures and using them within 4 days, or freezing them**. Follow that rule here:

- Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of serving.
- **Eat within 4 days**, and discard the oil at the same time; don't reuse it.
- Don't store them at room temperature, and don't give them as a shelf-stable gift.
- Olive oil turns solid and cloudy in the fridge. Let it sit out about 30 minutes, or warm it gently, before serving.

## FAQ

**How long do marinated olives need to marinate?**

At least 1 hour at room temperature for good flavor, or overnight in the fridge for more. Let refrigerated olives sit out for about 30 minutes before serving so the oil turns liquid again.

**How long do homemade marinated olives last?**

Keep them in the fridge and eat them within 4 days, or freeze them. Garlic and fresh herbs in oil can support botulism if stored too long, and extension food-safety guides advise refrigerating these mixtures and using them within 4 days.

**What olives are best for marinating?**

A mix of colors and textures: buttery green Castelvetrano, briny purple Kalamata and a mild green like Manzanilla or Picholine. Buy them with pits for the best texture, and drain them well before marinating.

**Should marinated olives be served warm or cold?**

Warm or at room temperature. Cold oil goes cloudy and thick, and cold mutes the flavor. Warm olives are a nice touch at a winter party: heat them gently in a skillet for 2 to 3 minutes.

**Why did my olive oil turn solid in the fridge?**

That's normal. Olive oil thickens and clouds when cold. It turns clear and liquid again after about 30 minutes at room temperature, with no change in quality.
