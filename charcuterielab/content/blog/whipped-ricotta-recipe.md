# Whipped Ricotta With Hot Honey: 5-Minute Board Dip

> **Quick Answer:** Blend **2 cups whole-milk ricotta** with **2 tablespoons olive oil**, lemon zest and salt for **1 to 2 minutes** until silky. Spread in a shallow bowl, swirl, and finish with **hot honey**, flaky salt and thyme. Make the base up to 2 days ahead.

It's the easiest way to add something creamy and mild to a board full of salty meat, and the swirls of honey make it look like a restaurant dish. If you don't have hot honey, see [the best hot honey brands](/blog/best-hot-honey/) or use plain honey and a pinch of chili flakes.

## Whipped ricotta with hot honey

**Makes:** about 2 cups · **Serves:** 8 · **Prep:** 5 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 2 cups (15 to 16 oz) whole-milk [ricotta](/ingredients/ricotta/)
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, plus more to finish
- 1 teaspoon finely grated lemon zest
- 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
- Black pepper
- 2 tablespoons [hot honey](/ingredients/hot-honey/) or regular honey
- Flaky sea salt and fresh thyme leaves, to finish

**Steps**
1. Put the ricotta, olive oil, lemon zest, salt and a few grinds of pepper in a food processor.
2. Blend for 1 to 2 minutes, scraping down the sides once, until completely smooth and fluffy. Taste and add more salt if needed.
3. Spread the ricotta in a shallow bowl and make swirls in the top with the back of a spoon.
4. Just before serving, drizzle with hot honey and a little olive oil, then sprinkle with flaky salt and thyme.
5. Serve with crostini, crackers and fruit.

**Three tips that make it better**
- **Use whole-milk ricotta.** Part-skim stays grainy no matter how long you blend. See [the best ricotta brands](/blog/best-ricotta-brands/).
- **Drain watery ricotta.** If there's liquid on top, spoon it off or let the ricotta sit in a fine strainer for 15 minutes.
- **Top it at the last minute.** Honey soaks in and herbs wilt if they sit for hours.

## How to use it on a board

- **Put it next to the saltiest meats.** Creamy ricotta calms salty prosciutto and spicy salami. It also tames chili heat; see [spicy salami and wine](/blog/spicy-salami-and-wine/).
- **Give it a spoon or small spreader.** It's a dip, not a slicing cheese.
- **Add something sturdy to scoop with:** crostini, [pita chips](/ingredients/pita-chips/) or endive leaves.
- **How much:** 2 cups covers about 8 guests when it's one of several dips or cheeses.

## Make-ahead and storage

- **Make ahead:** blend the base up to 2 days ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Stir and let it sit out 15 minutes before topping.
- **Serving:** fresh cheese shouldn't sit out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.
- **Leftovers:** refrigerate within 2 hours and use within 2 to 3 days, spread on toast or stirred into pasta.

## FAQ

**How do you make whipped ricotta?**

Blend 2 cups whole-milk ricotta with 2 tablespoons olive oil, lemon zest and salt in a food processor for 1 to 2 minutes, scraping the sides once, until smooth and fluffy. Spread it in a shallow bowl and top with hot honey, flaky salt and herbs.

**Can you whip ricotta without a food processor?**

Yes. A stand mixer or hand mixer works, beaten for 2 to 3 minutes. It won't get quite as silky. A blender works too but needs scraping often.

**Why is my whipped ricotta grainy?**

Part-skim ricotta stays grainy. Use whole-milk ricotta, blend for the full 1 to 2 minutes, and add a spoon of cream cheese or mascarpone for an extra-smooth texture.

**How far ahead can you make whipped ricotta?**

Up to 2 days ahead, covered in the fridge. Stir it, let it sit out 15 minutes and add the honey and toppings just before serving. Serve within 2 hours of setting it out.

**What do you serve with whipped ricotta?**

Crostini or toasted baguette, crackers, prosciutto, fresh figs or pears, and roasted or raw vegetables. It's a creamy, mild counterpoint to salty cured meats on a charcuterie board.
