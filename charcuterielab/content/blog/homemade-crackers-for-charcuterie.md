# Homemade Crackers for Charcuterie: 3 Easy Recipes

> **Quick Answer:** Make **olive oil and sea salt crackers** as your everyday base (5 ingredients, 30 minutes), **fruit-and-nut crisps** when you want the fancy boxed kind for a fraction of the price, and **Parmesan crisps** for a gluten-free, low-carb option. Roll thin, bake until golden, and store airtight for up to a week.

<h2 id="olive-oil">1. Olive oil and sea salt crackers</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">Your neutral, everyday cracker. Thin, crisp and sturdy enough for soft cheese.</p>
:::

**Makes about 40 crackers. Active time 15 minutes; total 30 minutes.**

- 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) extra-virgin olive oil
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) water
- Flaky sea salt for the tops
- Optional: 1 tsp chopped fresh rosemary or cracked black pepper

1. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C).
2. Stir the flour and salt together, then add the oil and water and mix until a soft dough forms. Knead for a minute until smooth.
3. Divide the dough in half. Roll each half on parchment **as thin as you can**, about 1/16 inch.
4. Brush lightly with olive oil, sprinkle with flaky salt (and rosemary or pepper), and cut into squares or rectangles with a pizza cutter. Prick each a few times with a fork so they don't puff.
5. Slide the parchment onto a baking sheet and bake 10 to 14 minutes, until golden at the edges. Thin edge pieces brown first; pull them off early if needed.
6. Cool completely on the pan. They crisp as they cool.

:::html
<h2 id="crisps">2. Fruit-and-nut crisps</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">The thin, crunchy, studded crisps sold in small, pricey boxes. Bake a loaf, chill it, slice it thin, and bake again.</p>
:::

**Makes about 80 crisps. Active time 30 minutes; total about 3 hours including chilling.**

- 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tsp baking soda
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 cups (480 ml) buttermilk
- 1/4 cup (50 g) packed brown sugar
- 1/4 cup (85 g) honey
- 1 cup dried fruit: chopped figs, dried cranberries, apricots or raisins
- 1/2 cup chopped nuts: pecans, pistachios or walnuts
- 1/4 cup seeds: pumpkin, sunflower, sesame or flax
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary (optional)

1. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease two standard loaf pans, or four mini loaf pans.
2. Whisk the flour, baking soda and salt. Stir in the buttermilk, brown sugar and honey, then fold in the fruit, nuts, seeds and rosemary.
3. Divide between the pans and bake 35 to 45 minutes (25 to 30 for mini pans) until a toothpick comes out clean.
4. Cool, then wrap and **chill or freeze until firm**, at least 2 hours. Chilled bread slices thin without crumbling.
5. Heat the oven to 300°F (150°C). Slice the loaves **as thin as possible**, about 1/8 inch, with a serrated knife.
6. Lay the slices on parchment-lined sheets and bake 15 minutes, flip, and bake another 10 to 15 minutes until dry and golden. Cool completely.

**Make ahead:** wrap the baked loaves and freeze for up to a month. Slice and do the second bake the day before the party.

<h2 id="parmesan">3. Parmesan crisps</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">One ingredient, naturally gluten-free and low-carb. A lacy, savory crisp that's great with salami.</p>
:::

**Makes about 16 crisps. Total 10 minutes.**

- 1 1/2 cups (about 4 oz) finely grated Parmesan (grate it yourself; pre-grated cheese with anti-caking agents spreads less evenly)
- Optional: cracked black pepper, smoked paprika or chopped thyme

1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment.
2. Drop level tablespoons of cheese onto the sheet, 2 inches apart, and pat each into a thin round. Sprinkle with pepper or herbs.
3. Bake 5 to 7 minutes, until golden and lacy. Watch closely; they go from golden to bitter quickly.
4. Cool on the pan for 2 minutes, then move to a rack. They crisp as they cool.

**For gluten-free guests:** serve these on their own plate, away from wheat crackers, with their own knife. See our [gluten-free charcuterie board](https://charcuterielab.com/boards/gluten-free-charcuterie-board/) for the full spread.

<h2 id="tips">Tips for crisp crackers every time</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Roll thinner than you think.</strong> Thick crackers stay chewy in the middle.</li>
<li><strong>Roll on parchment.</strong> You can slide the whole sheet onto the pan without stretching the dough.</li>
<li><strong>Cool completely before storing.</strong> Warm crackers trap steam and go soft.</li>
<li><strong>Store airtight.</strong> A tin or sealed container at room temperature, up to a week for the first two recipes and two or three days for Parmesan crisps.</li>
<li><strong>Re-crisp soft crackers</strong> at 300°F for about 5 minutes, then cool on the pan.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Don't put crackers on the board too early.</strong> They soften from the moisture in cheese and fruit. Add them last, or serve them in a basket alongside.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**Are homemade crackers worth it for a charcuterie board?**

Yes, especially fruit-and-nut crisps, which cost a lot boxed and are easy to make in bulk. Homemade crackers also let you control thickness, salt and flavor, and a basket of them makes a board look more special.

**How far ahead can I make crackers?**

Olive oil crackers and fruit-and-nut crisps keep for about a week in an airtight container at room temperature. The fruit-and-nut loaf can be baked, wrapped and frozen for up to a month before slicing. Parmesan crisps are best within two or three days.

**How do I make crackers crispy again?**

Spread them on a baking sheet and bake at 300°F for about five minutes, then let them cool completely on the pan. They crisp up as they cool.

**What crackers are gluten-free for a charcuterie board?**

Parmesan crisps are naturally gluten-free and low-carb: just grated Parmesan baked until golden. For a gluten-free guest, keep them on their own plate away from wheat crackers.

**How thin should homemade crackers be?**

About 1/16 inch, or as thin as you can roll them. Thin crackers bake crisp all the way through. Thicker ones stay chewy in the middle and can taste doughy.
