<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to use tzatziki on a board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Mezze and Greek boards:</strong> next to feta, olives, cucumber, cherry tomatoes and warm pita. It's the cool dip that balances spicy or salty things.</li><li><strong>With meat:</strong> spoon it beside lamb meatballs, chicken skewers or gyro meat, the way it's served in Greece.</li><li><strong>Drain it first</strong> if it looks watery: tip off any liquid on top and stir. Thicker tzatziki stays put on a board.</li><li><strong>Keep it cold.</strong> It's a yogurt dip, so serve it in a chilled bowl and keep it out no more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.</li></ul>

## Tzatziki questions

**What is the best store-bought tzatziki?**

Boar's Head won The Daily Meal's blind test of seven brands for its balance. Karoun Tzatziki Labne was Sporked's top pick for a thicker, tangier dip.

**What goes with tzatziki on a charcuterie board?**

Warm pita, cucumber, cherry tomatoes, olives, feta, roasted red peppers, and lamb meatballs or chicken skewers.

**How long does tzatziki last once opened?**

Go by the label: most tubs give a number of days to use it after opening. Keep it in the fridge, and don't leave it out more than 2 hours.

**Is tzatziki gluten-free?**

Most store-bought tzatziki is, including Boar's Head and Joseph's, but check the label.

**What's the difference between tzatziki and labneh?**

Tzatziki is yogurt with cucumber, garlic and dill. Labneh is yogurt strained until it's thick like cream cheese. Karoun's version combines the two.
