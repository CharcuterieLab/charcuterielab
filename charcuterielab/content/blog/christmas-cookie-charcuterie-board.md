# Christmas Cookie Charcuterie Board: How to Build It (and How Many Cookies)

> **Quick Answer:** Use **5 or 6 kinds of cookies**, then fill with chocolate, candy, fruit, nuts and one dip. Plan **3 to 4 cookies per guest** (3 to 4 dozen for 12). Biggest cookies first, then fill every gap.

## Why a cookie board works

A plate of cookies disappears into the background. The same cookies laid out like a charcuterie board become the centerpiece of the dessert table, and guests can try a little of everything. It's also the best way to show off a cookie exchange haul or to stretch two batches of homemade cookies with some good store-bought ones.

## Pick 5 or 6 cookies with different roles

Variety is what makes the board look full and festive. Choose one or two from each line:

| Role | Homemade ideas | Store-bought shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Classic cutout | Iced sugar cookies, gingerbread people | Bakery sugar cookies |
| Buttery and plain | Shortbread, spritz | Walkers shortbread |
| Chocolate | Chocolate crinkles, double chocolate | Chocolate wafers, Milano cookies |
| Spiced | Gingersnaps, molasses cookies | Biscoff, gingersnaps |
| Jammy or nutty | Thumbprints, Linzer cookies, Mexican wedding cookies | Jam-filled butter cookies |
| Crunchy and long | Biscotti, pizzelles | Pirouline wafer rolls |

For the shortbread, see our ranking of [the best store-bought shortbread](/blog/best-shortbread/).

## Add the extras

- **Chocolate:** squares of good dark chocolate, chocolate-dipped pretzels or peppermint bark. Our [dark chocolate ranking](/blog/best-dark-chocolate/) names the best bars.
- **A little candy:** candy canes, Hershey's Kisses or chocolate truffles. Use candy as an accent, not the main event. For a full candy spread, see our [candy charcuterie board](/blog/candy-charcuterie-board/).
- **Fresh fruit:** strawberries, raspberries, clementine segments and pomegranate seeds for color and a break from all the sugar.
- **Nuts:** candied pecans or salted almonds.
- **One dip:** a cream cheese frosting dip, Nutella or salted caramel in a small bowl.
- **Garnish:** rosemary sprigs look like little Christmas trees and smell great.

## How many cookies you need

| Guests | Party dessert (3–4 each) | After a big dinner (2–3 each) |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 to 3 dozen | 1½ to 2 dozen |
| 12 | 3 to 4 dozen | 2 to 3 dozen |
| 20 | 5 to 7 dozen | 3½ to 5 dozen |
| 30 | 7½ to 10 dozen | 5 to 7½ dozen |

Smaller cookies stretch further. Cut big bakery cookies in half, and count two mini cookies as one.

## How to build it

1. **Pick the board.** A large wooden board, a tray or a clean baking sheet lined with parchment. Round boards make a nice wreath shape.
2. **Place the bowls first:** the dip and anything loose, like candy or nuts.
3. **Lay the largest cookies,** like decorated cutouts and gingerbread people, so they're easy to see.
4. **Fan or stack the rest in rows or wedges,** grouping each kind together. Lean cookies against each other at an angle so they don't lie flat.
5. **Tuck in the chocolate and fruit** to separate colors.
6. **Fill every gap** with candy, nuts and rosemary. A full board looks abundant; gaps look sparse.

**Shape ideas:** arrange the cookies in a ring for a wreath, or in a triangle for a Christmas tree like our [Christmas tree board](/blog/christmas-tree-charcuterie-board/).

## Make-ahead tips

- **Bake or buy cookies up to a week ahead** and keep them in airtight tins, with soft and crisp cookies stored separately so the crisp ones don't go soft.
- **Arrange the board a few hours ahead** and cover it loosely with plastic wrap.
- **Add fresh fruit, dips and anything that melts** just before serving.
- **Label cookies with nuts,** and keep a few nut-free cookies on a separate plate if any guest has an allergy.

## Want a sweet-and-savory board?

Cookies and cheese are a great match. Try aged cheddar with shortbread or gingersnaps, brie with ginger cookies, or a wedge of blue cheese next to chocolate or oatmeal cookies. A small cheese board next to the cookie board gives guests who don't love sweets something to nibble.

## FAQ

**What goes on a Christmas cookie board?**

Five or six kinds of cookies with different shapes, colors and textures (for example sugar cookies, gingerbread, shortbread, chocolate crinkles, thumbprints and biscotti), plus chocolate, a little candy, fresh fruit like strawberries or clementines, nuts and one dip.

**How many cookies do I need for a cookie board?**

About 3 to 4 cookies per guest when it's the dessert at a party, or 2 to 3 per guest after a big dinner. For 12 guests at a party, that's about 3 to 4 dozen.

**Can I make a cookie board ahead of time?**

Yes. Most cookies keep for several days in airtight tins. Arrange the board up to a few hours ahead, cover it loosely, and add fresh fruit and anything soft or melty just before serving.

**Can I use store-bought cookies?**

Yes. Mix one or two homemade kinds with good store-bought cookies like butter shortbread, gingersnaps, Pirouline wafers or Pepperidge Farm cookies. Nobody minds once they're arranged.

**What cheese goes with Christmas cookies?**

Aged cheddar with shortbread or gingersnaps, brie with ginger cookies, and a blue cheese with chocolate or oatmeal cookies. Add one cheese if you want a sweet-and-savory board.
