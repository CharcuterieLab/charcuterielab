### 1. Bertolli Balsamic Glaze

**★★★★½ 4.7 out of 5** · Best overall · Best for: Drizzling over everything · $ budget

[Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-balsamic-glaze/)'s best of the best, with 9.5 out of 10: syrupy, deep in color and "the perfect mix of sweet and sour." Both testers picked it first.

- **Why we like it:** The top score, and it's cheap and everywhere.
- **Keep in mind:** Thickened like most supermarket glazes, so check the label if that matters to you.

### 2. Monari Federzoni Balsamic Glaze

**★★★★☆ 3.9 out of 5** · Best for: Sweet, thick drizzles · $ budget

The thickest glaze in [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-balsamic-glaze/)'s test, scored 8.5 out of 10. It's mostly sweet, bordering on too sweet eaten straight.

- **Why we like it:** Holds a neat line on burrata or fruit.
- **Keep in mind:** Sweet more than tangy.
- **Where to buy:** Grocery stores and Walmart.

### 3. DeLallo Balsamic Glaze

**★★★★☆ 3.8 out of 5** · Least sweet · Best for: Guests who don't like sweet · $ budget

[Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-balsamic-glaze/) scored it 8 out of 10 and named it the best tart glaze: tangier and less sweet than the others, with a bright, acidic bite.

### 4. DiBruno Bros Balsamic Glaze

**★★★½☆ 3.6 out of 5** · Best for: Mild cheeses · $$ mid-range

Very thick and sweet, and [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-balsamic-glaze/)'s pick for the best cling, with 9 out of 10. Sporked said it works best with mild cheese and "doesn't taste a ton like balsamic vinegar."

- **Why we like it:** Clings to fresh mozzarella and burrata without running.
- **Keep in mind:** Pricier, and light on balsamic flavor.
- **Where to buy:** Di Bruno Bros shops, online and some grocers.

### 5. Modenaceti Balsamic Glaze

**★★★½☆ 3.6 out of 5** · Best for: Real balsamic flavor · $ budget

[Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-balsamic-glaze/) gave it 8 out of 10 for the best balsamic flavor: not very sweet, and it "really does taste like someone just concentrated the flavor of balsamic vinegar."

- **Why we like it:** Tastes most like the vinegar itself.

- **Where to buy:** Stop & Shop, ShopRite, H-E-B and other grocers.

## What to look for on the label

**Label check:** Look for "Aceto Balsamico di Modena IGP" (balsamic vinegar of Modena) near the top of the ingredient list. Many glazes are thickened with starch or sweetened with sugar. That's fine for drizzling, but the shorter the list, the more it tastes like balsamic.

**Skip:** Using glaze where you want aged balsamic. A few drops of a real aged balsamic on Parmigiano-Reggiano is a different, better thing. Glaze is for drizzling.

**Serving it:** drizzle glaze over burrata, fresh mozzarella, strawberries, figs or chunks of Parmesan just before guests arrive, or put it in a small bowl with a spoon so it doesn't run across the board. You can make your own by simmering a cheap balsamic vinegar until it coats a spoon.

## Balsamic glaze questions

**What is the best balsamic glaze brand?**

Bertolli, which scored 9.5 out of 10 in Sporked's taste test and is sold almost everywhere. DeLallo is the pick if you want something less sweet.

**Is balsamic glaze the same as balsamic reduction?**

Nearly. A reduction is balsamic vinegar simmered until thick. Store-bought glazes are often thickened and sweetened too.

**What does balsamic glaze go with on a charcuterie board?**

Burrata, fresh mozzarella, Parmesan, strawberries, figs and prosciutto-wrapped fruit.

**Does balsamic glaze need to be refrigerated?**

Check the bottle. Most can be stored in the pantry after opening.
