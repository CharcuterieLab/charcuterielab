# Charcuterie Cups: How to Make Them for a Party (12, 24 or 50 Cups)

> **Quick Answer:** A charcuterie cup is a single serving of board food in a 9 oz clear cup: a 6-inch skewer of folded salami, cheese cubes and a grape, 3 crackers or a breadstick standing up behind it, and a few olives, nuts or berries in the bottom. Each cup holds about **1 oz of meat and 1 oz of cheese**, so plan **one cup per guest** alongside other food, or **two per guest** if the cups are the whole appetizer. Fill them the morning of, keep them in the fridge, and add the crackers last.

Charcuterie cups solve the two problems a big board has at a party: nobody has to hover over a shared board, and nobody touches food someone else is about to eat. Guests pick one up and keep walking. They work for Halloween parties, showers, tailgates, office parties and anything where people stand more than they sit.

## What goes in one cup

Build every cup from the same five parts. Change the ingredients, keep the parts.

| Part | Example | Amount per cup |
|---|---|---|
| The base (bottom of the cup) | Marcona almonds, olives or blueberries | 1 small spoonful |
| The skewer | [Genoa salami](/ingredients/genoa-salami/), folded, plus cheese cubes and a grape | About 1 oz meat (4 thin slices) and 1 oz cheese (4 cubes, ¾ inch) |
| The crunch | Crackers, a breadstick or pretzel rod | 3 crackers or 1 breadstick |
| The fresh bite | Grapes, berries, a cornichon or a snap pea | 2 to 3 pieces |
| The finish | A rosemary sprig, a dried apricot, a square of dark chocolate | 1 piece |

The cheese works best when it's firm enough to skewer: [aged cheddar](/ingredients/aged-cheddar/), [Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/), Gouda, pepper jack or Havarti. Soft cheese like Brie can go in, but put it in the bottom of the cup as a wedge, not on the skewer.

## Shopping list for 12, 24 or 50 cups

These amounts follow the same standard as the rest of the Lab (2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest before a meal), with one cup counted as half a serving, and 10% extra from 20 cups up.

| Item | 12 cups | 24 cups | 50 cups |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salami or other cured meat | 12 oz | 1¾ lb | 3½ lb |
| Firm cheese (2 kinds) | 12 oz | 1¾ lb | 3½ lb |
| Crackers or breadsticks | 36 crackers (1 box) | 80 crackers (2 to 3 boxes) | 165 crackers (5 boxes) |
| Grapes | 1 lb | 2 lb | 4 lb |
| Olives, nuts or berries for the base | 1 cup | 2 cups | 4 cups |
| 9 oz clear cups | 12 | 24 | 50 |
| 6-inch skewers | 12 | 24 | 50 |

Buy the salami pre-sliced and the cheese as blocks you cube yourself. Blocks are cheaper and the cubes hold their shape on a skewer. ## How to build them (layer in this order)

The order matters. Wet things sit low and crackers go in last, so nothing gets soggy.

1. **Base first.** Put a spoonful of olives, nuts or berries in the bottom of each cup. Drain olives well and pat them dry.
2. **Build the skewers.** Fold each salami slice in half, then in half again, and thread it on. Alternate meat and cheese, and finish with a grape at the top.
3. **Skewer in.** Stand the skewer at the back of the cup, leaning on the rim.
4. **Fresh bite.** Tuck the grapes, a cornichon or a snap pea in front of the skewer.
5. **Chill.** Cover the tray with plastic wrap and refrigerate until about 30 minutes before guests arrive.
6. **Crackers last.** Stand 3 crackers or a breadstick behind the skewer just before serving, then add the finish (rosemary, chocolate, a dried apricot).

A half-sheet pan holds about 24 cups, which also makes them easy to carry to the fridge and back.

## Four themed versions

| Theme | Meat | Cheese | Crunch | Fresh and finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Halloween | Soppressata or salami | Orange cheddar, Mimolette | Charcoal crackers | Blackberries, a candy corn, an orange or black cup |
| Christmas | Genoa salami | Manchego, white cheddar | Rosemary crackers or grissini | Dried cranberries, a rosemary sprig as the skewer, a red ribbon |
| Game day | Pepperoni, summer sausage | Pepper jack, sharp cheddar | Pretzel rods | Pickle spear, a pickled jalapeño |
| Kids' party | Ham or turkey rolls | Mild cheddar, Colby Jack | Pretzel sticks, butter crackers | Grapes (quartered for little kids), strawberries |

For children under 5, skip the pointed skewers, cut grapes into quarters lengthwise, and leave out whole nuts and whole olives. Those are choking risks for young children.

## How far ahead to make them

- **2 days before:** buy everything. Cube the cheese and keep it in a sealed container in the fridge.
- **The night before:** build the skewers, lay them flat in a container, cover and chill. Wash and dry the fruit.
- **The morning of:** fill the cups with the base, skewer and fresh bite. Cover the tray and refrigerate.
- **Just before serving:** add the crackers and the finish.

Once the cups are out, follow the same rule as any board: **no more than 2 hours at room temperature, or 1 hour above 90°F**. For a long party, bring out half the tray at a time and keep the rest in the fridge.

## What charcuterie cups cost

With grocery-store ingredients, plan on roughly **$1.75 to $2.75 a cup**, including the cup and skewer. That breaks down to about $0.75 to $1.10 for the salami, $0.50 to $0.75 for the cheese, $0.30 to $0.50 for crackers, fruit and the base, and $0.10 to $0.20 for the cup and skewer. Pepperoni, a block of store-brand cheddar and grapes keep you at the low end. Prosciutto, Manchego and Marcona almonds push you to the top. Prices checked September 2026.

## Cups or a board?

| Choose cups when | Choose a board when |
|---|---|
| Guests are standing and moving | Guests are sitting around one table |
| The party is outdoors or at a venue | It's a small group of 4 to 10 |
| Kids or a big crowd are coming | You want a centerpiece |
| You want each portion the same | You want people to graze and mix |

You can do both: one board on the table and a tray of cups by the door. For a large group, see [How to Make a Charcuterie Board for a Large Group](/blog/charcuterie-board-for-large-group/).

## FAQ

**What goes in a charcuterie cup?**
A skewer of folded salami, cheese cubes and a grape or olive, 3 crackers or a breadstick, and a few nuts, olives or berries in the bottom, all in a 9 oz clear cup. Each cup holds about 1 oz of meat and 1 oz of cheese.

**How many charcuterie cups per person?**
One cup per guest when there is other food, two per guest when the cups are the only appetizer. Each cup is about half of the 2 oz each of meat and cheese we plan per guest before a meal.

**How far ahead can you make charcuterie cups?**
Fill them the morning of the party, cover and refrigerate, and add the crackers just before serving so they stay crisp. Once they are out, serve them within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.

**What size cup is best for charcuterie cups?**
A 9 oz clear plastic cup is the standard size. It holds a 6-inch skewer, three crackers and a layer of nuts or fruit, and about 24 fit on a half-sheet pan.

**How much does it cost to make charcuterie cups?**
About $1.75 to $2.75 a cup with grocery-store salami, cheddar, crackers and fruit, including the cup and skewer. Pepperoni and a block of cheese you cube yourself keep it at the low end.
