> **Quick Answer:** Best overall: **Citterio Bresaola**, 98% lean and sold pre-sliced. Best widely available: **Volpi Bresaola**. Most traditional: imported **Bresaola della Valtellina**. Plan about 1 oz per guest, dressed with lemon and olive oil.

<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to serve bresaola</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Dress it:</strong> the classic is a drizzle of olive oil, a squeeze of lemon, black pepper, arugula and shaved Parmigiano.</li><li><strong>Fold it loosely</strong> or roll it around a breadstick or a piece of arugula; it's lean, so it doesn't drape like prosciutto.</li><li><strong>About 1 oz per guest</strong> when it's one of three meats.</li><li><strong>For guests who skip pork,</strong> set it on its own side of the board with its own tongs.</li><li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> bresaola is cured, not cooked. ## Bresaola questions

**What is the best bresaola to buy?**

Citterio Bresaola for availability and quality, Volpi as the easiest to find, and imported Bresaola della Valtellina IGP for the traditional version.

**Is bresaola beef or pork?**

Beef. It's lean, air-dried beef, usually from the eye of round.

**Where can I buy bresaola?**

Pre-sliced at good deli cases (Citterio, Volpi) and at Italian markets or online for imported IGP bresaola.

**How do you eat bresaola?**

Thin slices with olive oil, lemon, arugula and Parmigiano, or rolled around breadsticks.

**How much bresaola per person?**

About 1 oz per guest when it's one of three meats.
