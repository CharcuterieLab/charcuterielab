<h2 id="what-to-look-for">What to look for</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for:</strong> Castelvetranos should be bright green, not olive-drab. Buy with pits if you can: pitted olives are softer and blander.</li><li class="sk-x"><strong>Skip:</strong> Canned black ripe olives. They are mild and soft and add little to a board.</li></ul>

<h2 id="faq">Olives questions</h2>
:::

**What are the best olives for a charcuterie board?**

Castelvetrano olives. Mezzetta is the easiest to find, and Partanna ranked higher in Tasting Table's test.

**Should olives on a board have pits?**

Pitted is easier for guests, but olives with pits have better texture. If you keep the pits, add a small bowl for them.

**How many kinds of olives should I serve?**

One is fine; two or three different colors and sizes look better.
