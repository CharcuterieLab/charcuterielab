# Jack-o'-Lantern Charcuterie Board: The Giant Pumpkin Board for 12 to 15

> **Quick Answer:** Outline a **24-inch pumpkin in pretzel sticks**, split it into curved ribs, and fill each with an **orange food** (cheddar, Gouda, apricots, soppressata). Make the face with **black olives and blackberries**. Serves 12–15, about 75 minutes.

From across the room it's a carved pumpkin; up close it's cheese and salami. It's one of the easiest boards in *Impossible Boards*.

## The trick: it's all in two details

**The face is just black food.** Outline two triangle eyes, a triangle nose and a jagged grin with pretzel sticks, then pack them with black olives and blackberries. Against all that orange, black reads instantly as a carved hole. You don't need to cut a single shape.

**The ribs make it round.** A flat circle of orange cheese looks like a plate of cheese. Divide the circle into curved bands that bow outward, like the ribs of a real pumpkin, and give each band a slightly different shade: bright orange cheddar, golden Colby, pale Muenster or Gouda, deep orange apricots and red soppressata. The change in color from band to band is what makes the pumpkin look three-dimensional.

## What to put on it

For 12 to 15 guests, plan on about **3 pounds of cured meat and 4 pounds of cheese** when the board is the main appetizer. That's in line with our [how much charcuterie per person](/blog/how-much-charcuterie-per-person/) guide.

**For the pumpkin**
- **Orange cheddar**, the brightest rib and the biggest share of the cheese
- **Colby or golden cheddar** for a second shade of orange
- **Muenster or Gouda** for a pale rib
- **Dried apricots**, fanned in a rib of their own
- **Soppressata**, overlapping along one edge for a red rib ([soppressata vs. salami](/blog/soppressata-vs-salami/) if you're choosing)
- **Thin pretzel sticks** for the outline and the rib lines

**For the face and stem**
- **Pitted black olives** and **blackberries**
- **Pretzel rods** tied with **kitchen twine**
- A sprig of **rosemary** as the vine

**For the outer ring**
- **Black grapes** and **charcoal or black-sesame crackers**, for the dark frame
- **Dry Spanish chorizo** or hot salami, and **pepperoni**
- **White cheddar and pepper jack cubes**, for contrast
- **Clementines**, set in little groups like a pumpkin patch

## How to build it

1. **Outline the pumpkin.** On a 36 to 40 inch round board, lay pretzel sticks in a slightly squat circle about 24 inches wide. Leave a ring of 6 to 8 inches around it.
2. **Draw the ribs.** Lay curved lines of pretzel sticks from top to bottom, bowing outward, to make six or seven bands.
3. **Make the face.** Outline the eyes, nose and a wide grin with two or three teeth, then pack them tightly with olives and blackberries so no board shows through.
4. **Fill the ribs,** one food per band, alternating light and dark shades of orange.
5. **Add the stem and vine** at the top: 10 to 12 pretzel rods tied in a bundle, with rosemary curled beside it.
6. **Build the dark ring** around the pumpkin: clusters of grapes, fans of crackers, folded chorizo and pepperoni, cheese cubes, and clementines in twos and threes.

## A smaller version for 6 to 8

The same design shrinks well. Use an **18 inch round board** and make the pumpkin about **12 inches** across with four or five ribs: orange cheddar, Colby, apricots and soppressata. Fill the face with olives only, use 5 or 6 pretzel rods for the stem, and keep the outer ring to grapes and crackers. You'll need roughly a third of the food.

## Make it your way

- **Easiest version:** skip the ribs and fill the whole pumpkin with orange cheddar cubes. Just do the black face. It still reads as a jack-o'-lantern.
- **On a budget:** cheddar in every rib, pepperoni for all the meat, and clementine segments instead of dried apricots.
- **Spookier:** set it on black slate in a dim room with battery tea lights around the edge (never real flames on a food board), and add a "spider" made from a black olive with olive-slice legs.
- **For kids:** skip the hot salami and pepper jack, and let them fill the face. For toddlers, cut the grapes and olives into quarters. More ideas are in our [charcuterie board for kids](/blog/charcuterie-board-for-kids/) guide.
- **Gluten-free:** outline it with carrot sticks, make the stem from a bundle of celery, and use gluten-free crackers.

## Make-ahead and food safety

The day before, cut all the cheese into cubes, slice the soppressata and store everything covered and separate in the fridge. About **2 hours before** guests arrive, lay the outline, the ribs and the face. **An hour before**, fill everything in and build the outer ring. Add the stem and rosemary in the **last 5 minutes**.

Once it's out, keep it out for **no more than 2 hours**. For a long party, build the pumpkin and keep a container of refills cold. Our guide to [making a board the night before](/blog/make-charcuterie-board-night-before/) has more on what can be done early.

**Did you know?** The first jack-o'-lanterns weren't pumpkins. In Ireland they were carved from turnips. Irish immigrants switched to the much bigger, easier-to-carve American pumpkin.

## FAQ

**How do you make a pumpkin-shaped charcuterie board?**
Outline a slightly squat circle with pretzel sticks on a round board, divide it into six or seven curved ribs with more pretzel sticks, and fill each rib with a different orange food: orange cheddar, Colby, Muenster or Gouda cubes, dried apricots and soppressata. Fill a carved face with black olives and blackberries, then add a stem of pretzel rods tied with twine.

**How much food do you need for a Halloween board for 12 to 15 people?**
When the board is the main appetizer, plan on about 3 pounds of cured meat and 4 pounds of cheese, plus fruit, crackers and olives. If it is one of several appetizers, cut everything by about a third.

**How far ahead can you make a jack-o'-lantern charcuterie board?**
Cut the cheese cubes and slice the meat the day before and keep them covered in the fridge. Lay the outline and the face about 2 hours before guests arrive, fill it in over the next hour, and add the stem last. Keep it out for no more than 2 hours.

**What size board do you need?**
For 12 to 15 guests, a 36 to 40 inch round board or a round table covered in butcher paper. The pumpkin itself is about 24 inches across, with a ring of grapes, crackers and meat around it. A small version for 6 to 8 fits on an 18 inch round board.

**Is a jack-o'-lantern board kid-friendly?**
Yes. Every bite can be picked up, the flavors are mild if you skip the hot salami and pepper jack, and kids can help fill the face with olives and blackberries.

**Can you make it gluten-free?**
Yes. Outline the pumpkin with carrot sticks instead of pretzels, make the stem from a bundle of celery, and serve gluten-free crackers. Check the labels on the cured meats.
