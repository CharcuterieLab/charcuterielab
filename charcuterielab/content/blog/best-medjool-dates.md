<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to choose</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for "Medjool" on the pack.</strong> A plain "pitted dates" bag is usually the smaller, drier Deglet Noor.</li><li><strong>Stuffing them?</strong> Buy pitted, then still check each one: even pit-free packs can hide a fragment.</li><li><strong>Shop the produce fridge.</strong> Medjools are fresh fruit. Plump, glossy and slightly wrinkled is right; dry, hard or spotted is not.</li><li><strong>How many?</strong> 1 to 2 per guest on a board, or 2 to 3 if they're stuffed and served as a bite.</li></ul><p class="sk-note">On the board: halve them and lay them cut side up near blue cheese, Manchego or aged Gouda, or stuff them with goat cheese and a Marcona almond. <a href="/ingredients/medjool-dates/">More on Medjool dates</a></p>

## Medjool date questions

**Medjool vs Deglet Noor: which is better for a charcuterie board?**

Medjool. Medjools are large, soft and very sweet, which suits a board and stuffing. Deglet Noors are smaller, drier and chewier, and they're better for baking.

**How should I store Medjool dates?**

In an airtight container in the fridge, where they keep for months. They also freeze well. Bring them to room temperature before serving so they're soft.

**Should I pit Medjool dates before putting them on a board?**

Yes, if you're stuffing them or your guests won't expect a pit. Slit one side with a paring knife, open it with your thumbs and pull out the pit. Check pit-free packs too.

**What can I stuff dates with for a charcuterie board?**

Goat cheese or blue cheese with a smoked or Marcona almond is the classic. Wrap them in half a slice of bacon and bake at 400°F for about 30 minutes for a warm bite.

**How many dates per person on a charcuterie board?**

1 to 2 per guest as one of several sweet things, or 2 to 3 if they're stuffed and served as an appetizer.
