# Dry January Charcuterie: Zero-Proof Pairings That Actually Work

> **Quick Answer:** Wine works with charcuterie because of **acid, bubbles and tannin**, not alcohol. Pick drinks that bring at least one of those: **unsweetened black iced tea** for salami and aged cheese, **ginger kombucha** for spicy meats, a **tart cherry spritz** for brie, and **sparkling water with citrus** for everything.

<h2 id="science">Why wine works, and how to copy it without alcohol</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">Alcohol isn't doing much of the pairing work. Three other things are, and you can get all of them in a glass with no alcohol at all.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Acid.</strong> Sourness makes your mouth water, and saliva rinses fat and salt off the tongue. This is the <a href="https://charcuterielab.com/blog/acid-rule/">acid rule</a>, and it's the most important one. Citrus, vinegar shrubs, kombucha and tart fruit juice all bring it.</li>
<li><strong>Bubbles.</strong> Carbonation physically scrubs the palate and adds a light bite of carbonic acid. It's why Champagne goes with almost everything, and why sparkling water does too.</li>
<li><strong>Tannin and bitterness.</strong> Tannins bind to fat and protein, which is why a red wine feels clean after a slice of salami. Black tea, cold brew coffee and tonic all have this grip.</li>
<li><strong>A little sweetness, not a lot.</strong> A touch of fruit sweetness triggers the <a href="https://charcuterielab.com/blog/salt-sweet-principle/">salt-sweet principle</a> with cured meat. Too much, and it coats the palate instead of clearing it.</li>
</ul>
:::

<h2 id="tea">Tea is the most underused pairing</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">If you only change one thing for Dry January, brew a pitcher of tea.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Brew it strong, chill it, and don't sweeten it.</strong> Sugar softens the tannin, and the tannin is the point.</li>
<li><strong>Black tea for meat-heavy boards.</strong> Assam or English breakfast iced tea is the zero-proof answer to "what red do I serve?"</li>
<li><strong>Green tea for light boards.</strong> Fresh cheeses, prosciutto, melon and cucumber.</li>
<li><strong>Smoked tea for winter boards.</strong> Lapsang souchong is polarizing on its own and excellent next to aged gouda.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Don't over-steep green tea.</strong> It turns harsh and bitter fast. Two to three minutes in water below boiling.</li>
</ul>
:::

<h2 id="recipes">Two easy zero-proof drinks for a board</h2>
:::

**Tart cherry rosemary spritz (serves 1)**

- 2 oz unsweetened tart cherry juice
- 1/2 oz lemon juice
- 1 tsp honey or simple syrup (optional)
- Sparkling water to top
- Rosemary sprig

Stir the juice, lemon and honey over ice, top with sparkling water, and slap the rosemary sprig between your hands before adding it. Serve with brie, pate or prosciutto.

**Quick apple cider vinegar shrub (makes about 8 servings)**

- 1 cup chopped apple or pear
- 1/2 cup sugar
- 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar

Toss the fruit with the sugar and refrigerate overnight, then stir in the vinegar, strain, and keep it in the fridge for up to two weeks. Use 1 oz shrub to 5 oz sparkling water over ice. Serve with the richest things on the board.

:::html
<h2 id="avoid">What to skip</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Very sweet sodas and straight juice.</strong> Sugar coats the palate and flattens cheese. Cut juice at least half and half with sparkling water.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Milky drinks.</strong> A latte adds more fat to an already rich board.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Heavily flavored seltzers.</strong> Artificial fruit flavors clash with delicate cheese. Plain or citrus is safer.</li>
</ul>
:::

:::html
<h2 id="hosting">Hosting tips</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Offer two zero-proof options, not one.</strong> One with tannin (tea) and one with bubbles (a spritz or sparkling water) covers almost every board.</li>
<li><strong>Serve them in real glassware.</strong> A stemmed glass makes a zero-proof drink feel like part of the spread, not an afterthought.</li>
<li><strong>Check the label if it matters.</strong> In the US, "non-alcoholic" drinks can contain up to 0.5% alcohol, and kombucha can carry trace amounts. For guests who avoid alcohol completely, choose 0.0% options or stick to tea, coffee and juice spritzes.</li>
<li><strong>Pregnant guest?</strong> Pair this with our <a href="https://charcuterielab.com/blog/pregnancy-safe-charcuterie-board/">pregnancy-safe charcuterie board guide</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**What non-alcoholic drink goes best with charcuterie?**

Something with acid, bubbles or gentle bitterness. Unsweetened black iced tea, ginger kombucha, sparkling water with citrus and a tart cherry or pomegranate spritz are the most reliable. They do the same job wine does: cut through fat and reset the palate between bites.

**Does tea pair well with cheese?**

Yes. Black tea has tannins, like red wine, that scrub fat from the tongue, so it works with aged cheddar and salami. Green and jasmine teas are lighter and suit fresh goat cheese, mozzarella and prosciutto. Smoky lapsang souchong is excellent with aged gouda and smoked meats.

**What is a shrub drink?**

A shrub is a drinking vinegar: fruit, sugar and vinegar steeped together, then topped with sparkling water. The vinegar gives it strong acidity, which makes it one of the best zero-proof partners for rich foods like brie and pâté.

**Is non-alcoholic wine completely alcohol free?**

Not always. In the US, drinks labeled non-alcoholic can contain up to 0.5% alcohol, and kombucha can carry trace amounts too. If a guest avoids alcohol entirely for health, pregnancy, recovery or religious reasons, choose drinks labeled 0.0% or stick to tea, coffee, juice spritzes and sparkling water.

**What drinks should you avoid with charcuterie?**

Very sweet sodas and juices, which coat the palate and flatten the flavor of cheese, and milky drinks, which add more fat to an already rich board. If you serve juice, cut it with sparkling water and a squeeze of citrus.
