<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to use fruit and nut crisps</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Use them as the second cracker,</strong> next to a plain water cracker for strong or delicate cheeses.</li><li><strong>Best partners:</strong> blue cheese, brie, triple crème, goat cheese and aged Comté. Skip them with spicy salami.</li><li><strong>Fan them out</strong> in a short row; they break if stacked.</li><li><strong>Skip:</strong> a Daily Meal reviewer found Aldi's raisin-rosemary Savoritz crisps unbalanced, with rosemary overpowering the raisins.</li><li><strong>Make your own:</strong> they're a quick-bread baked, chilled, sliced thin and baked again.</li></ul>

## Fruit and nut crisps questions

**What are the best Raincoast Crisps for a cheese board?**

Rosemary Raisin Pecan, Sporked's best sweet cracker for charcuterie. Cranberry Hazelnut is the pick with brie.

**Is there a cheaper Raincoast Crisps dupe?**

Trader Joe's Raisin Rosemary Crisps cost $3.99, about half the price. Cup of Jo's taste test called it a toss-up.

**What cheese goes with Raincoast Crisps?**

Brie, triple crème, blue cheese, goat cheese and aged Comté.

**Why are Raincoast Crisps so expensive?**

They're made in small batches with a lot of fruit, nuts and seeds, and twice-baked.

**How many boxes do I need?**

One box covers about 8 guests as one of two crackers.
