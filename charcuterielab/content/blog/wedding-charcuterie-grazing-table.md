# Wedding Charcuterie and Grazing Table: How Much for 50 to 200 Guests

> **Quick Answer:** Cocktail hour: **2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest**, plus 10%: about **14 lb of each for 100**. If it's the main food: **3 oz each** (21 lb for 100). Build 3–4 stations and refill before 2 hours.

The two big decisions: before dinner or instead of it, and how many stations. Amounts match the [Party Planner](/party-planner/).

## How much to buy: grazing reception (no full dinner)

Use this when the grazing table is the main food for a longer reception, or for a late-night spread with nothing else. It's 3 oz each of meat and cheese per guest, plus 10%.

| Guests | Cured meat | Cheese | Crackers and bread | Fruit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 10½ lb | 10½ lb | 500–600 pieces | 10½ lb |
| 75 | 15½ lb | 15½ lb | 750–900 pieces | 15½ lb |
| 100 | 20¾ lb | 21 lb | 1,000–1,200 pieces | 20¾ lb |
| 150 | 31 lb | 31 lb | 1,500–1,800 pieces | 31 lb |
| 200 | 41¼ lb | 41½ lb | 2,000–2,400 pieces | 41¼ lb |

If the grazing table is replacing dinner entirely, add something hot and filling (sliders, pasta, a carving station). Charcuterie alone doesn't hold guests through an evening of dancing.

## Stations, not one long table

One giant table makes a queue. Build identical stations instead, each with every cheese, meat, cracker and spread, and its own knives and tongs.

- **50 guests:** three stations, or one long grazing table worked from both sides.
- **75 to 200 guests:** four stations, refilled from the kitchen. For 150 or 200, give each station two sides so people can graze from both.
- **Keep 5 to 6 cheeses and 4 to 5 meats.** More variety than that slows the line without making anyone happier.
- **Put the crackers in several spots** along each station, not in one pile at the end.

## The timeline

1. **One week before:** order large pieces from a deli or warehouse store, and borrow or rent boards, risers and platters. Buy crackers, nuts, jams and anything shelf-stable.
2. **Two days before:** buy the cheese, meat and fruit.
3. **The day before:** cut the firm cheeses and portion everything into containers, one set per station plus one refill set. Cover and chill.
4. **One hour before:** take the aged and firm cheeses out to warm up. Leave soft cheeses like brie in the fridge.
5. **30 to 45 minutes before:** take out the soft cheeses and build the stations: cheese first, then meat, then bowls, crackers and fruit.
6. **During the reception:** swap in the fridge-cold refill before any tray has been out 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F, per [USDA guidance](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-the-2-Hour-Rule-with-leaving-food-out). An outdoor summer wedding needs the 1-hour rule.

If you're building it at home and driving it to the venue, see [how to transport a charcuterie board](/blog/how-to-transport-charcuterie-board/).

## Guests with special diets

- **Pregnant guests:** label the cheeses and keep cooked meats on one end. Our [pregnancy-safe board guide](/blog/pregnancy-safe-charcuterie-board/) lists what's off the table.
- **Gluten-free guests:** give them their own cracker bowl, away from the bread, with [gluten-free crackers](/blog/best-gluten-free-crackers/).
- **Nut allergies:** keep nuts in bowls rather than scattered, and leave one station nut-free.
- **Small cards** naming each cheese and meat save the bride's aunt from asking about every one.

## FAQ

**How much charcuterie do I need for 100 wedding guests?**

For a cocktail hour before dinner, about 14 lb of cured meat and 14 lb of cheese, with 600 to 800 crackers. If the grazing table replaces dinner appetizers and runs longer, plan about 20¾ lb of meat and 21 lb of cheese. Both include a 10% buffer for a crowd.

**How much does a wedding grazing table cost to make yourself?**

For 100 guests at cocktail hour, roughly $300 to $400 on a budget, $500 to $700 for a standard spread and $1,300 to $2,000 with splurge cheeses and meats. That's food only; boards, risers, linens and help are extra.

**How many grazing stations do I need for a wedding?**

Three stations for about 50 guests and four from 75 guests up. Each station gets every cheese, meat and cracker, so guests don't queue at one end.

**How far ahead can I prepare a wedding charcuterie table?**

Buy shelf-stable items a week ahead and the cheese, meat and fruit two days before. Cut and portion everything the day before, then build the stations 30 to 45 minutes before guests arrive.

**How long can a grazing table sit out at a wedding?**

No more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F, per USDA guidance. For a long reception, put out half and swap in a fridge-cold refill tray before the first one hits 2 hours.
