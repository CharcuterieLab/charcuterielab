# Charcuterie Board Sizes: Which Size for Which Occasion

> **Quick Answer:** For 2 people, use an 8–10 inch board. For 4–6, a 12–14 inch board. For 8–10 people, use a 16–18 inch board or two medium boards. The 80% coverage rule: food should cover most of the board surface with natural gaps — not stacked high. When in doubt, go one size larger.

**Why size matters:** too small and you end up stacking, so guests can't see or reach what's at the back. Too big and the board looks half-finished. Pick the size before you shop, then buy food to fill it.

:::html
<h2 id="guests">Size guide by guest count</h2>
:::

| Guests | Board | What fits |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 8–10 inch round or small rectangle | 2 cheeses, 1 meat, 3–4 extras. A 6-inch board only works for a single-cheese board. |
| 4–6 | 12–14 inch, or a 12x16 rectangle | 3 cheeses, 2 meats, crackers, fruit, 2 condiments. Most recipes are written for this size. |
| 8–10 | 16–18 inch, or two 12-inch boards | Two boards side by side are easier to place, and you can split them (one bold, one mild). |
| 12–20 | 3–4 medium boards | Set them around the table as stations. Refilling one small board beats reloading one huge one. |
| 20+ | One station per 8–10 guests | Each station gets its own cheese knife, condiment spoon and full set of items. Plan quantities ahead. |

:::html
<h2 id="coverage">The 80% coverage rule</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">Food should cover about 80% of the board, with small natural gaps between items.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Under 60%: too sparse.</strong> Items float in empty space. Use a smaller board, or fill in with crackers and fruit.</li>
<li><strong>75–85%: right.</strong> It looks full, and every item is still easy to see and reach.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Over 90%: overcrowded.</strong> Things overlap and stack, and guests can't see what's at the back.</li>
</ul>
<p>The rule works at any size: match the amount of food to the board, not the board to the food.</p>
:::

:::html
<h2 id="material">Board material</h2>
:::

| Material | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Walnut | Boards you use often: dense, resists knife marks, rich dark grain | Costs more |
| Acacia | Everyday use: lighter, pretty grain, affordable, easy to find | Shows knife marks sooner than walnut |
| Marble | Summer and soft cheese: stays cooler than wood | Heavy; hard to store and carry |
| Slate | A dark background for pale cheeses; holds cool like marble | Scratches; chalky surface |
| Softwood (pine) | Skip it | Absorbs odors and scars quickly under a knife |

:::html
<h2 id="shape">Shape</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Rectangle:</strong> the most practical. Most usable space per inch, fits most tables, easy to build left to right.</li>
<li><strong>Round:</strong> best for 2–4 people. Build from the center out, with the cheeses in the middle.</li>
<li><strong>Long and narrow (paddle):</strong> for 2 people, a narrow cocktail-party surface, or as an extra board next to the main one.</li>
</ul>
<p>Shape matters less than size: get the size right first.</p>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**What size charcuterie board do I need for 4 people?**
A 12–14 inch board works for 4 people. That gives you enough surface to place 3 cheeses, 2 meats, and a handful of accompaniments without overcrowding. The 80% coverage rule applies — food should cover most of the board with small natural gaps, not be stacked in layers to fit.

**What is the best material for a charcuterie board?**
Walnut and acacia wood are the most durable and visually suited to boards. Walnut is denser and resists knife scoring better; acacia is lighter and has attractive grain variation. Marble and slate are strong alternatives — they stay cooler than wood, which is useful in summer. Avoid softwoods like pine, which absorb food odors.

**Can I use a cutting board as a charcuterie board?**
Yes, if it's made from hardwood (walnut, maple, cherry) and is at least 12 inches in its longest dimension. The distinction is primarily aesthetic — a dedicated charcuterie board usually has a handle or decorative edge, but functionally any clean hardwood board works. Avoid plastic boards; they absorb stains and don't present as well.

**What shape charcuterie board is best?**
Rectangular boards are the most practical — they offer the most usable surface area per inch and fit more naturally on a table. Round boards are aesthetically striking for 2–4 person builds and intimate settings. Long and narrow boards (paddle-style) work well for 2 people or as a supplementary accent board. Shape is a secondary concern after size.

**How much food fits on a 12-inch charcuterie board?**
A 12-inch round or 12x16 rectangular board comfortably holds food for 4–6 people: 3 cheeses (3–4 oz each), 2–3 meats (3–4 oz each), plus crackers, fruit, and 2 condiments. Apply the 80% coverage rule — the board should look full and intentional, not sparse, but individual elements should still be identifiable.
