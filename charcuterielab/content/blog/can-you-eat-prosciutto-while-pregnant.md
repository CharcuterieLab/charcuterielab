# Can You Eat Prosciutto While Pregnant? What the CDC and NHS Say

> **Quick Answer:** **Not cold.** The CDC says to heat cured meats to 165°F or until steaming. Crispy baked prosciutto, prosciutto on hot pizza, or prosciutto roasted around something are all fine.

*This is general food-safety information, not medical advice. Ask your doctor or midwife about your own pregnancy.*

## Why cold prosciutto is on the list

Prosciutto is never cooked. It's a pork leg that's salted and air-dried for months, which makes it safe for most people to eat as is. During pregnancy, two small risks matter more:

- **Listeria.** Ready-to-eat cold meats can carry *Listeria*, and pregnant people are much more likely to get sick from it. Listeriosis can cause miscarriage or stillbirth even when the mother feels only mildly ill. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/))
- **Toxoplasmosis.** Raw and cured meat carries a small risk of the parasite that causes it. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/))

Heat kills both. That's why both the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/pdf/protect-your-pregnancy-h.pdf) and the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/) say the same thing: heat it until it's steaming hot, then eat it.

## Does it matter which prosciutto?

No. Prosciutto di Parma, San Daniele, domestic prosciutto, jamón serrano, speck and other dry-cured hams all fall under the same advice. Price and quality don't change it.

**Prosciutto cotto** is cooked ham, but it's still a cold deli meat once it's sliced and packed, so the CDC's advice to heat deli meat applies to it too. **Pancetta** is usually cooked in a recipe, so it's fine once it's cooked through.

## How to eat prosciutto safely while pregnant

| Way to eat it | How | Safe when |
|---|---|---|
| Prosciutto crisps | Lay slices flat on a lined sheet and bake at 375°F for 10 to 15 minutes | Fully crisp |
| On pizza or flatbread | Add before baking, not after | Hot all the way through |
| Wrapped and roasted | Around asparagus, dates or melon wedges, then roasted | Sizzling, not just warm |
| In pasta or eggs | Fry until crisp, then toss in | Steaming hot |

**Prosciutto crisps** are the best board swap. They keep the salty, savory flavor, they're sturdy enough to hold a smear of soft goat cheese or fig jam, and they can be made a day ahead and kept in an airtight container.

## Building a board a pregnant guest can enjoy

You don't have to ban prosciutto from the party. Give the pregnant guest options that are safe as they are, and label them:

- **Meat:** prosciutto crisps, cooked ham or roast turkey heated until steaming hot, or cooked chicken sausage.
- **Cheese:** pasteurized hard cheeses such as cheddar, Parmesan, Gruyère and Swiss, which the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/pdf/protect-your-pregnancy-h.pdf) lists as safer choices. Pasteurized mozzarella, feta and cream cheese work too.
- **Brie:** only baked until steaming hot. See [can you eat brie rind?](/blog/can-you-eat-brie-rind/)
- **Everything else:** crackers, fruit, nuts, olives, honey and jam are all fine.

## FAQ

**Can you eat prosciutto while pregnant?**

Not cold. The CDC advises heating deli meats and dry-cured meats to 165°F or until steaming hot during pregnancy, and the NHS says prosciutto should be cooked until steaming hot first.

**Is cooked prosciutto safe during pregnancy?**

Yes, when it's heated until steaming hot all the way through, such as crisped in the oven or baked on a pizza.

**Why can't you eat prosciutto when pregnant?**

Cold cured meats can carry listeria and, rarely, the parasite that causes toxoplasmosis. Both are more dangerous during pregnancy. Heat kills them.

**Is prosciutto di Parma safe in pregnancy?**

Only when cooked until steaming hot, the same as any other prosciutto.

**What can I eat instead of prosciutto on a charcuterie board?**

Prosciutto crisps, cooked ham or roast turkey heated until steaming hot, plus pasteurized hard cheeses.
