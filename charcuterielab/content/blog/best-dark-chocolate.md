<h2 id="what-to-look-for">What to look for</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for:</strong> Cacao between 60% and 75%, with cocoa beans or cocoa mass listed before sugar. &quot;Dark chocolate&quot; with no percentage is usually sweeter.</li><li class="sk-x"><strong>Skip:</strong> Bars over 85% for a mixed crowd. They&#x27;re bitter and chalky to most guests. Also skip anything with fillings that melt on a warm board.</li></ul>
:::

**Serving it:** break bars into rough 1-inch pieces, or set out wrapped squares, about 1 ounce for every 2 guests. Put the chocolate near aged cheddar, blue cheese, Parmesan or dried fruit, and keep it away from the sun so it doesn't soften.

<h2 id="faq">Dark chocolate questions</h2>
:::

**What is the best dark chocolate for a charcuterie board?**

Tony's Chocolonely 70% ranked at or near the top of two big taste tests. For easy serving, Ghirardelli's 72% squares and Godiva's wrapped sticks are already in bite-size pieces.

**What cheese goes with dark chocolate?**

Aged cheddar, blue cheese, Parmesan, aged Gouda and Manchego. Salty, nutty and sharp cheeses stand up to dark chocolate.

**What percentage of dark chocolate is best?**

60% to 75% cacao suits most guests. Above 85% tastes bitter to many people.

**Is dark chocolate vegan?**

Often, but not always. Some bars, including Aldi's Choceur, contain milk. Check the label.
