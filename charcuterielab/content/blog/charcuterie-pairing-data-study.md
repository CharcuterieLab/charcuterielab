# We Mapped 2,521 Charcuterie Pairings. Here's What Goes With Everything.

> **Quick Answer:** Across 375 charcuterie ingredients and 2,521 pairings, **honey** pairs with the most (98), then **goat cheese**, **aged cheddar**, **prosciutto** and **walnuts**. Cheese pairs with fruit almost three times as often as with cured meat, and fruit never pairs with pickles.

<h2 id="findings">Key findings</h2>
:::

- **Honey is the most versatile thing on a charcuterie board.** It pairs with 98 of our 375 ingredients, more than ten times the typical ingredient.
- **A handful of ingredients do most of the work.** Ten ingredients, under 3% of the list, appear in 29% of all pairings.
- **Five ingredients cover two-thirds of the board.** Honey, fresh goat cheese, aged cheddar, prosciutto and walnuts pair with 67% of everything we track.
- **Cheese loves fruit and nuts more than it loves meat.** Cheese and fruit pair at 8.0% of possible matches, cheese and nuts at 7.3%, and cheese and cured meat at only 2.9%, below the board average of 3.6%.
- **Cured meat's best friend is the pickle jar.** Meat pairs with pickles and olives (4.6%) more often than with cheese.
- **Fruit and pickles never pair.** Zero matches out of 1,120 possible.
- **Soft, fresh cheese is the most fragile thing on a board.** 107 ingredients, more than a quarter of the list, carry a warning to keep away from it.
- **Dry rosé and sparkling wine suit the most boards.** In our [charcuterie wine pairing chart](/blog/wine-and-charcuterie-board/), they work with 12 and 11 of 15 board types and clash with none.

:::html
<h2 id="method">How we built the data</h2>
:::

Every ingredient on Charcuterie Lab has its own page with a "what it pairs with, and why" table. This study counts those pairings.

- **375 ingredients** in 8 groups: cheese, cured meat and seafood, fruit, nuts, spreads and honey, crackers and bread, pickles and olives, and finishing touches.
- **2,521 pairings.** A pairing counts once, both ways: if the brie page lists fig jam, brie and fig jam are a pair.
- **More than 2,000 written reasons.** Most pairings come with a one-line reason (salt against sweet, fat against acid, crunch against cream).
- **Checked for contradictions.** In November 2026 we checked all 375 pages and removed any pairing that one page recommended and another said to skip.
- **What this is, and isn't.** It's editorial pairing data: what our guides recommend and why. It is not a lab taste test, and it's as of November 2026. As we add ingredients, the numbers will move.
- **Group-to-group rates** are the share of possible pairings between two groups that appear in the data. We leave out pairings within a group (cracker with cracker, for example), because those lists are mostly swaps.

<h2 id="versatile">The 25 most versatile things on a charcuterie board</h2>
:::

![Bar chart: the 25 most versatile charcuterie ingredients by number of pairings, led by honey with 98, fresh chèvre 89, aged cheddar 81, prosciutto 80 and walnuts 73](/images/pairing-study-most-versatile.webp)

Ten of the top 25 are cheeses. The most versatile are cheeses that are either **tangy and soft** (goat cheese, burrata) or **aged and salty** (aged cheddar, Manchego, aged Gouda). Both kinds have a strong, simple character that something sweet, crunchy or salty can play against.

The single most versatile item isn't a cheese at all. **Honey** is sweet, which balances salt (cheese, meat, olives), softens sharp flavors (blue cheese, aged cheddar) and makes crunchy things (nuts, crackers) feel like dessert. See [what goes with honey](/pairings/food/honey/).

The median ingredient pairs with **9** others. The top ten average **75**.

<h2 id="groups">Which parts of a board pair best with each other</h2>
:::

![Heat map of how often each group of charcuterie ingredients pairs with each other group: cheese and fruit 8.0%, cheese and nuts 7.3%, fruit and nuts 6.9%, cheese and cured meat 2.9%, fruit and pickles 0%](/images/pairing-study-category-map.webp)

The strongest links on a board, as a share of possible pairings:

| Groups | Pairing rate | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Cheese + fruit | 8.0% | Put fruit right beside every cheese |
| Cheese + nuts | 7.3% | Nuts are the easiest second partner for cheese |
| Fruit + nuts | 6.9% | Fruit and nuts can share a pile |
| Cheese + spreads and honey | 5.4% | One jam or honey per board, next to the cheese |
| Cured meat + pickles and olives | 4.6% | Cornichons and olives belong next to the salami |
| *Board average* | *3.6%* | |
| Cheese + cured meat | 2.9% | Don't count on meat and cheese alone; add a bridge |
| Fruit + crackers | 0.6% | Crackers go with cheese and spreads, not fruit |
| Fruit + pickles | 0.0% | Keep them apart |

**The surprise: meat and cheese are a weaker pair than people think.** A charcuterie board is built on cured meat and cheese, yet those two pair less often than the board average. Plenty of specific pairs work beautifully (prosciutto with Parmigiano, Manchego with chorizo), but many don't, and the clash usually comes from two strong flavors fighting. The fix is a **bridge**: fruit, honey or jam between them. That's why our [meat and cheese pairing chart](/pairings/meat-and-cheese-chart/) marks so many squares "with a bridge".

:::html
<h3 id="never">Fruit and pickles: zero for 1,120</h3>
:::

Not one of our 35 fruits pairs with any of our 32 pickles and olives. Sweet, juicy fruit and sour brine cancel each other out: the fruit tastes sour and the pickle tastes flat. On a board, put fruit on the cheese side and pickles and olives on the meat side.

<h2 id="fragile">The most fragile things on a board</h2>
:::

Many ingredient pages also say what to keep away. Counting those warnings shows what gets pushed around most easily:

| Keep away from… | Warned by |
|---|---|
| Soft, fresh cheese (burrata, fresh mozzarella, goat cheese, brie) | 107 ingredients |
| Blue cheese | 48 |
| Smoked cheese | 25 |
| Everything crackers | 19 |
| Strong cured meat | 7 |

**The goat cheese paradox.** Fresh goat cheese is the second most versatile ingredient in the study, and yet soft, fresh cheese is the most-warned group on the board. Both are true. Goat cheese pairs with almost anything sweet, crunchy or bright (honey, figs, pistachios, crackers), and gets bullied by anything loud: smoked meat, strong blue, spicy salami, tannic red wine. **Give fresh cheese its own corner, with gentle neighbors.**

**The specialists.** At the other end, some ingredients pair with only five others: black garlic, yuzu kosho, cured egg yolks, bacon jam and peppadew peppers among them. They're not bad, they're specialists. Use one per board, and set it right beside its partners.

<h2 id="rules">Six rules the data backs up</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Build around one or two connectors.</strong> Honey, a tangy goat cheese or an aged cheddar will suit almost anything else you add.</li>
<li><strong>Bridge meat and cheese with fruit or honey.</strong> Meat and cheese pair less often than you'd think; fruit, honey and jam pair well with both.</li>
<li><strong>Pickles go with meat, fruit goes with cheese.</strong> Keep the two on opposite sides of the board.</li>
<li><strong>Protect the fresh cheese.</strong> Give burrata and goat cheese a corner away from smoke, spice and blue cheese.</li>
<li><strong>Plain crackers carry more.</strong> One plain cracker or baguette suits far more than a flavored one.</li>
<li><strong>Pour rosé or sparkling if you only open one bottle.</strong> They suit the most and clash with nothing.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**What is the most versatile charcuterie ingredient?**

Honey. In our data it pairs with 98 of 375 charcuterie ingredients, more than any cheese, meat or fruit. Its sweetness balances salt, softens sharp cheese and makes nuts and crackers taste richer.

**What cheese goes with the most things on a charcuterie board?**

Fresh goat cheese (89 pairings), then aged cheddar (81), Manchego (68), aged Gouda, brie and burrata (66 each). Tangy soft cheeses and salty aged ones are the most flexible.

**Do meat and cheese pair well together?**

Less often than you'd expect. Cheese and cured meat pair at 2.9% of possible matches in our data, below the 3.6% board average, while cheese and fruit pair at 8.0%. Add fruit, honey or jam as a bridge between them.

**What should never go on a charcuterie board together?**

Fruit and pickles: none of our 35 fruits pairs with any of our 32 pickles and olives. Also keep soft, fresh cheese away from smoked meat, strong blue cheese and spicy salami.

**What wine goes with the most charcuterie?**

Dry rosé and sparkling wine. In our charcuterie wine chart they suit 12 and 11 of 15 board types and clash with none. Big reds suit 4 and clash with 7.

**How was this data collected?**

We counted the pairings listed, with written reasons, on Charcuterie Lab's 375 ingredient pages, counting each pair once in both directions after removing contradictions. It is editorial pairing data as of November 2026, not a lab taste test.
