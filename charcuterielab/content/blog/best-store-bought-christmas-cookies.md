<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to build a cookie board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Mix textures:</strong> one buttery (Danish butter cookies), one spiced and crisp (ginger thins), one chocolate (fudge-covered Oreos or Milanos), one soft (frosted sugar cookies).</li><li><strong>Keep crisp and soft apart</strong> so the crisp ones don't go soft, and keep chocolate-coated cookies away from candles and heat.</li><li><strong>Fill the gaps</strong> with peppermint bark, chocolate coins, candied nuts and a few clementines.</li><li><strong>Put out half and refill</strong> from sealed packages so the second round tastes as fresh as the first.</li></ul>

## Christmas cookie questions

**How many cookies per person for a cookie board?**

Plan on 3 to 4 cookies per guest when the board is the party dessert, or 2 to 3 after a big dinner. Mix four or five kinds so guests can try several.

**How far ahead can I buy store-bought holiday cookies?**

Buy limited holiday flavors as soon as you see them, because they sell out. Packaged cookies keep until the best-by date, so buying two or three weeks early is fine.

**How do I keep cookies fresh on a board?**

Build the board shortly before guests arrive, keep crisp and soft cookies apart, and refill from sealed packages instead of putting everything out at once.

**What goes on a Christmas cookie board besides cookies?**

Peppermint bark, chocolate coins, candy cane marshmallows, candied nuts, clementines and a few pretzels for salt.

**Can you freeze store-bought cookies?**

Sturdy ones, yes: freeze in a single layer, then store airtight with parchment between layers for up to three months. Skip frosted and delicate cookies.
