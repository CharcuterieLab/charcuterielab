# Prosciutto vs. Pancetta: What's the Difference?

> **Quick Answer:** They come from different parts of the pig and do different jobs. Prosciutto is the cured hind leg, salted and air-dried for more than a year, then sliced paper-thin and eaten as is. Pancetta is cured pork belly, the same cut as bacon but not smoked, and it's mostly used as a cooking ingredient. On a charcuterie board, use prosciutto. Pancetta belongs in the pan, crisped for pasta, salads or a warm topping.

## Are prosciutto and pancetta the same?

No. Both are Italian cured pork, but almost everything else is different:

- **Prosciutto** is the leg (ham). It's lean meat with a cap of fat on the outside.
- **Pancetta** is the belly. It's roughly half fat, layered through the meat.

## Prosciutto vs. pancetta at a glance

| | Prosciutto | Pancetta |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | Hind leg | Belly (same cut as bacon) |
| Fat | Lean, with an outer fat cap | About half fat, in layers |
| Cure | Salt, then air-dried | Salt, pepper and spices, then dried briefly |
| Aging | More than a year (14 months minimum for Prosciutto di Parma) | Weeks to a few months |
| Smoked? | No | No (smoked versions exist but aren't traditional) |
| Eaten | Uncooked, sliced paper-thin | Usually cooked |
| Comes as | Thin slices | Diced, flat slab (*tesa*) or rolled (*arrotolata*) |
| Texture | Silky, melts in the mouth | Soft and fatty raw; crisp when cooked |
| Flavor | Sweet, delicate, nutty | Rich, porky, peppery |
| Price | $$ | $ |

## How they're made

**Prosciutto** is salted, rested and hung in cool, dry air for well over a year. Prosciutto di Parma uses nothing but salt. The long, slow aging is what makes it safe to eat without cooking and gives it its sweet, nutty flavor.

**Pancetta** is rubbed with salt, black pepper and spices like juniper, nutmeg or garlic, then dried for a much shorter time. It comes **flat** (*tesa*) for cooking or **rolled** (*arrotolata*) into a spiral. Only properly aged rolled pancetta is ever served thinly sliced and uncooked, and most pancetta in US stores, especially the diced kind, is meant to be cooked.

## Which belongs on a charcuterie board?

**Prosciutto.** It's one of the classic board meats: drape it in loose folds, wrap it around melon or figs, or roll it into a [prosciutto rose](/blog/prosciutto-rose/). Pair it with burrata, fresh mozzarella, Parmigiano-Reggiano, melon, figs and Prosecco.

**Pancetta can join a board cooked.** Crisp thin slices in a pan or oven and serve them as "pancetta chips" next to soft cheese and honey, or scatter crisped pancetta over baked brie.

## How to cook with each

- **Pancetta:** the base of carbonara and amatriciana, and great in soups, beans and roasted vegetables. Start it in a cold pan so the fat renders slowly.
- **Prosciutto:** add it at the end, or crisp it quickly in the oven. Cooking it for long makes it tough and salty.

## Which should you buy?

- **For a board:** prosciutto, sliced to order. See [the best prosciutto brands](/blog/best-prosciutto-brands/).
- **For pasta and cooking:** pancetta, diced.
- **For a warm board topping:** a few slices of pancetta, crisped.
- **For pregnant guests:** the CDC advises heating deli and cured meats until steaming hot. Crisped pancetta or prosciutto is a good option.
