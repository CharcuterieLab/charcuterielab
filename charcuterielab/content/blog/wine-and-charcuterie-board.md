# Wine and Charcuterie: The Complete Pairing Guide

> **Quick Answer:** For one bottle, pour **dry sparkling wine** (Champagne, Cava or Crémant). Its bubbles and acid suit almost everything on a board. For a red, choose a **light, low-tannin one** like Pinot Noir or Beaujolais. Save big reds for aged hard cheese.

<h2 id="why">Why wine and charcuterie work: the 4 rules</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">Everything on a board is fat, salt or sweet. Four things in wine decide whether a pairing works.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Acid cuts fat.</strong> A high-acid wine cleans your palate, so the next bite of salami tastes as good as the first. This is why crisp whites and sparkling wine work so well.</li>
<li><strong>Tannin fights soft, salty food.</strong> Tannin is the drying grip in big reds. A little is fine with firm cheese. A lot, from Cabernet or Barolo, turns prosciutto metallic and makes brie taste bitter.</li>
<li><strong>Bubbles scrub.</strong> Carbonation lifts fat off your tongue. That's why sparkling wine is the safest single bottle for a rich board.</li>
<li><strong>Sweet balances salt.</strong> Salty food makes wine taste fruitier, and a sweet wine makes salty cheese taste milder. It's the reason port and blue cheese are a classic.</li>
</ul>
<p class="sk-note">Short version: <strong>high acid, low tannin</strong> works with almost everything. More on the science in <a href="/blog/why-bubbles-cut-fat/">why bubbles cut fat</a> and <a href="/blog/acid-rule/">the acid rule</a>.</p>
:::

<h2 id="how-many">One, two or three bottles: the easy plan</h2>
:::

- **One bottle:** dry sparkling wine. If you'd rather have still wine, a dry rosé.
- **Two bottles:** one sparkling wine or crisp white for prosciutto, fresh cheese and seafood, plus one light red (Pinot Noir or Beaujolais) for salami and aged cheese.
- **Three bottles, for a party:** start with sparkling, then a white or rosé, then a light red. If there's a blue cheese, add a small bottle of [port](/pairings/wine/port/) at the end.
- **Order:** pour light to heavy, and dry to sweet: sparkling, white, rosé, red, then port.

**How much to buy.** A 750 ml bottle pours about five 5-oz glasses. Plan on half a bottle per wine-drinking guest for a two- to three-hour party.

| Guests | Bottles | A good mix |
|---|---|---|
| 4 guests | 2 | 1 sparkling, 1 light red |
| 8 guests | 4 | 1 sparkling, 1 white or rosé, 2 light red |
| 12 guests | 6 | 2 sparkling, 2 white or rosé, 2 red |
| 20 guests | 10 | 3 sparkling, 3 white or rosé, 4 red |

:::html
<h3 id="sparkling">Sparkling wine: the safest single bottle</h3>
:::

Yes, Champagne is excellent with a cheese or charcuterie board, arguably the best match there is. The bubbles scrub fat, the high acid cuts salt, and Champagne's toasty, bready notes echo aged cheese.

| Wine | What it's like | When to pour it |
|---|---|---|
| **Champagne**, especially Blanc de Blancs | Highest acid, fine bubbles, toasty notes | A smaller or special board |
| **Cava** (Spain) | Made like Champagne for much less; a little earthier | Spanish boards and big parties |
| **Crémant** (France) | Made like Champagne in other French regions; great value | Any mixed board |
| **Prosecco** (Italy) | Lighter, fruitier, a touch sweeter | Prosciutto, mild cheese, Italian boards |
| **Lambrusco** (Italy) | Dry sparkling red, berry fruit | Mortadella, salami, Parmigiano |

Full lists: [Champagne and Prosecco pairings](/pairings/wine/champagne-prosecco/). Bottles: [the best Champagne under $30](/blog/best-champagne-under-30/) and [the best Prosecco](/blog/best-prosecco/).

:::html
<h3 id="whites">Crisp white wine: underrated with charcuterie</h3>
:::

White wine's acid does exactly what a board needs, with no tannin to clash.

| Wine | Why it works | Best with |
|---|---|---|
| **[Sauvignon Blanc](/pairings/wine/sauvignon-blanc/)** (Sancerre, New Zealand) | Grassy and very bright | Goat cheese, prosciutto, olives |
| **Albariño** (Spain) | Salty, citrusy, built for cured food | Jamón, Manchego, a Spanish board |
| **[Riesling](/pairings/wine/riesling/)**, dry or off-dry | Acid plus a touch of fruit | Pâté, spicy salami, washed-rind cheese |
| **[Chardonnay](/pairings/wine/chardonnay/)**, unoaked or lightly oaked (Chablis) | Mineral and clean | Brie, Comté, smoked salmon |

**Skip heavily oaked Chardonnay** with cured meat. The oak and salt clash.

:::html
<h3 id="rose">Dry rosé: the goes-with-everything wine</h3>
:::

- **Acid** cuts fat, the way a cornichon or a grape does.
- **Red-fruit flavor** (strawberry, raspberry) echoes the sweetness in prosciutto and aged cheese.
- **Almost no tannin**, so it clashes with almost nothing.
- **Buy** pale, dry, Provence-style rosé. **Skip** off-dry "blush" rosé.

Which cheeses and meats to put out with it: [rosé cheese pairing ideas](/pairings/wine/rose/).

:::html
<h3 id="light-reds">Light reds: the safest red</h3>
:::

| Wine | Why it works | Best with |
|---|---|---|
| **[Pinot Noir](/pairings/wine/pinot-noir/)** | Low tannin, bright acid, red cherry | Prosciutto, salami, Gruyère, brie |
| **[Beaujolais](/pairings/wine/beaujolais/)** (Gamay) | The most food-friendly light red, and great value | Almost anything on a standard board |

**Tip:** serve light reds slightly cool. Twenty minutes in the fridge makes them taste fresher next to salty food. The cheeses that suit the safest red, ranked: [Pinot Noir cheese pairing](/pairings/wine/pinot-noir/).

:::html
<h3 id="medium-reds">Medium reds: the salami wines</h3>
:::

| Wine | Why it works | Best with |
|---|---|---|
| **[Chianti and Sangiovese](/pairings/wine/chianti-sangiovese/)** | High acid, cherry, a little grip | Salami, finocchiona, Parmigiano, an Italian board |
| **Barbera** (Piedmont) | Lots of acid, very little tannin | Coppa, soppressata, mild cheese |
| **[Merlot](/pairings/wine/merlot/)** | Soft and plummy | Coppa, Genoa salami, Gouda |

:::html
<h3 id="big-reds">Big reds: only with the right board</h3>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Yes, with aged hard cheese.</strong> Aged cheddar, Manchego, Parmigiano and aged Gouda soften big tannins. A board built around them can take <a href="/pairings/wine/cabernet-sauvignon/">Cabernet Sauvignon</a>, <a href="/pairings/wine/malbec/">Malbec</a> or Rioja.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Not with prosciutto or delicate meats.</strong> Tannin strips the fat and leaves a harsh, metallic taste.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Not with fresh or soft cheese.</strong> Brie or chèvre with tannin tastes bitter and chalky.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Not with lots of sweet things.</strong> A dry red tastes sour next to honey or fig jam.</li>
</ul>
<p class="sk-note">Bringing a big red? Build the board toward it: heavy on aged hard cheese and bold salami, light on delicate meat, no triple-crème.</p>
:::

:::html
<h3 id="sweet">Sherry, port and sweet wine: the finishers</h3>
:::

- **Fino or manzanilla sherry** (dry, served cold): the classic with jamón, Marcona almonds and olives.
- **[Port](/pairings/wine/port/):** Stilton, Gorgonzola and aged cheddar, with walnuts and dried figs. Pour small, 2 to 3 oz.
- **Sauternes or late-harvest Riesling:** Roquefort, foie gras-style pâté, honeycomb.

<h2 id="by-board">The best wine for each kind of board</h2>
<p class="sk-lede">Easiest approach: match the wine to the board's theme or its strongest item.</p>
:::

| Board | Best wine | Also good |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic mixed** (cheese and cured meat) | Champagne or Crémant | Pinot Noir |
| **[Italian antipasto](/boards/italian-antipasto-board/)** | Chianti | Prosecco, Lambrusco |
| **[Spanish tapas](/boards/spanish-tapas-board/)** | Cava or fino sherry | Albariño, Rioja |
| **[French](/boards/french-brasserie-board/)** | Beaujolais | Champagne, Sancerre |
| **Aged cheese** (cheddar, Gouda, Manchego) | Cabernet or Rioja | Tawny port |
| **Fresh and soft cheese** (chèvre, brie, burrata) | Sauvignon Blanc | Champagne |
| **[Seafood](/boards/seafood-charcuterie-board/)** | Champagne | Muscadet, Albariño, unoaked Chardonnay |
| **Spicy** (chorizo, pepperoni, hot honey) | Dry rosé | Off-dry Riesling |
| **[Dessert](/boards/dessert-charcuterie-board/)** (chocolate, fruit, sweets) | Tawny port | Moscato d'Asti, Brachetto |
| **[Brunch](/boards/brunch-charcuterie-board/)** | Prosecco or a mimosa | Dry rosé |
| **[Christmas](/holidays/christmas/) and [Thanksgiving](/holidays/thanksgiving/)** | Sparkling to start, Pinot Noir with the meal | Port with the cheese course |

Already have the wine? Build to it. **Light red:** prosciutto or salami, Gruyère or aged Gouda, no triple-crème. **White:** prosciutto or coppa, Comté, fresh chèvre. **Sparkling:** prosciutto, triple-crème brie, Parmigiano, honeycomb. **Big red:** aged cheddar, Manchego, soppressata; skip prosciutto and soft cheese.

<h2 id="serving">Serving wine with a board</h2>
:::

| Wine | Serve at | Easy way |
|---|---|---|
| Sparkling | 40–45°F | In the fridge 3 hours, or an ice bucket for 30 minutes |
| Crisp white and rosé | 45–50°F | Out of the fridge 15 minutes before pouring |
| Light red (Pinot Noir, Beaujolais) | 55–60°F | 20 minutes in the fridge |
| Medium and big reds, port | 60–65°F | Cool room temperature; open big reds 30 minutes ahead |

- **Glasses:** one all-purpose wine glass works for everything, sparkling included.
- **Cheese:** take it out 30 to 60 minutes ahead. Cold cheese tastes muted and fights the wine.
- **Pour small:** 3 to 4 oz per glass at a tasting, so guests can try more than one pairing.
- **Water and plain crackers** between wines reset the palate.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li class="sk-x"><strong>A big red with fresh cheese.</strong> Cabernet or Barolo with chèvre, burrata or mild brie tastes metallic and bitter.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>A bold red with prosciutto.</strong> A 14.5% Cabernet buries it. Go lighter and higher in acid.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>A dry wine with sweet things.</strong> Next to honey, jam or chocolate, a dry wine tastes sour. Bring port or a sweet wine for the dessert end of the board.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Only one wine for a big, mixed board.</strong> Two bottles, one sparkling or white and one light red, cover far more.</li>
<li class="sk-x"><strong>Everything too cold or too warm.</strong> Ice-cold whites hide their flavor; warm reds taste boozy and flat.</li>
</ul>
:::

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**What wine goes best with a charcuterie board?**

Dry sparkling wine, such as Champagne, Cava or Crémant. Its bubbles and acid cut fat and salt and suit almost every cheese and cured meat. For a still wine, a light red like Pinot Noir or Beaujolais is the next safest choice.

**Is red or white wine better with charcuterie?**

Both work if you pick the right style. Crisp whites suit prosciutto, fresh cheese and seafood; light, low-tannin reds suit salami and firm cheese. Avoid big tannic reds unless the board is built around aged hard cheese.

**Is Champagne good with a cheese board?**

Yes. Champagne is one of the best wines for a cheese or charcuterie board: the bubbles scrub fat, the high acid cuts salt, and its toasty notes echo aged cheese. Cava and Crémant do the same job for less.

**What red wine goes with charcuterie?**

Light, low-tannin reds: Pinot Noir and Beaujolais first, then Chianti and Barbera for salami-heavy boards. Save Cabernet and Malbec for boards built around aged cheddar, Manchego or Parmesan.

**What white wine goes with charcuterie?**

High-acid whites: Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño, dry Riesling and unoaked Chardonnay such as Chablis. Skip heavily oaked Chardonnay with cured meat.

**What wine goes with prosciutto?**

Prosecco or Champagne first, then dry rosé, Pinot Noir or Sauvignon Blanc. Avoid big tannic reds, which make delicate prosciutto taste metallic.

**What wine goes with salami?**

Chianti, Beaujolais or Barbera for mild salami like Genoa; Chianti, Barbera or Malbec for bold salami like soppressata. For spicy salami, a dry rosé or off-dry Riesling cools the heat.

**How much wine do I need for a charcuterie board?**

Plan on half a bottle per wine-drinking guest for a two- to three-hour party. A 750 ml bottle pours about five 5-oz glasses, so 8 guests need about 4 bottles.

**Can you serve red and white wine with a charcuterie board?**

Yes, and it's the best approach for a mixed board: a sparkling wine or crisp white for prosciutto, fresh cheese and seafood, and a light red for salami and aged cheese.

**What wine goes with a Christmas charcuterie board?**

Start with sparkling wine, pour Pinot Noir or Beaujolais with the meats and cheeses, and finish with port alongside Stilton or another blue cheese.
