# DIY Charcuterie Gift Basket: 3 Box Ideas ($25, $50, $100) and How to Pack It

> **Quick Answer:** Build the basket around things that don't need a fridge: a **whole, unopened dry salami**, crackers, a jam or honey, nuts, jarred olives or cornichons, a mustard and dark chocolate. Add **one or two cheeses right before you hand it over**, with a tag that says to refrigerate them. A small box runs **about $25 to $35**, a classic basket **$45 to $60** and a luxury box with a wooden board and knives **$95 to $130**. If you're shipping it, either leave the cheese out or send it overnight with gel packs.

A charcuterie gift basket costs less than most store-bought gift boxes, looks more personal, and gets eaten instead of sitting in a cupboard. The trick is choosing food that survives the trip. This guide gives you three baskets at three prices, the shopping list for each, and the packing and shipping rules that keep everything safe.

## What travels well (and what doesn't)

| Item | Needs the fridge? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Whole, unopened dry salami (Genoa, hard salami) | No | USDA: about 6 weeks in the pantry unopened; refrigerate once opened |
| Summer sausage | Check the label | Many gift brands are shelf-stable; others say keep refrigerated |
| Pre-sliced salami or prosciutto packs | Yes | Follow the package; keep cold until it's handed over |
| Hard and semi-firm cheese | Yes | Add it last, keep it cold and tag it "refrigerate" |
| Soft cheese (brie, burrata) | Yes | Leave it out unless you're handing it over in person |
| Crackers and crisps | No | Pack them on top so they don't crush |
| Jams, honey, mustard, membrillo | No | Unopened jars are shelf-stable |
| Nuts, dried fruit, chocolate | No | Keep chocolate away from heat |
| Jarred olives and cornichons | No | Wrap the jars; they're the heaviest items |
| Fresh fruit | Skip | It bruises and spoils; whole apples or clementines are the exception |

The storage times come from the [USDA's sausage storage guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/es/node/3310): a whole hard or dry sausage keeps about **6 weeks in the pantry** and indefinitely in the fridge unopened, and **3 weeks** in the fridge once opened.

## Shopping tips

- **Buy whole meats, not sliced packs.** A whole salami looks like a gift and keeps for weeks; a sliced pack needs a fridge. Our [best salami brands](/blog/best-salami-brands/) and [best summer sausage](/blog/best-summer-sausage/) rankings point to good ones.
- **Warehouse and discount stores win on price.** Costco, Aldi and Trader Joe's all sell good gift-size cheeses and jars. See the [Costco](/blog/best-costco-charcuterie-items/), [Aldi](/blog/best-aldi-charcuterie-items/) and [Trader Joe's](/blog/best-trader-joes-cheeses/) picks.
- **Pick one fancy thing.** One jar of truffle honey or one wedge of real Parmigiano-Reggiano makes the whole basket feel expensive. The rest can be store brand.

## How to pack it

![Hands packing a charcuterie gift basket, with jars at the back, salamis standing upright and crackers going in on top](/images/charcuterie-gift-basket-packing.jpg)

1. **Line the container** with crinkle paper, tissue or a linen napkin that's part of the gift.
2. **Put the heaviest things in first,** at the back and bottom: jars of olives, jam, mustard and honey. Wrap each jar in tissue so they don't clink.
3. **Stand the whole salamis and the board** (if using) upright at the back so they're the first thing you see.
4. **Fill the middle** with nuts, chocolate and the knife set.
5. **Put crackers on top and in front,** where nothing can crush them.
6. **Add the cheese last,** right before you hand it over, in a small insulated pouch or wrapped in parchment. Leave a gap at the front for it.
7. **Wrap it** in clear cellophane gathered at the top with ribbon, and tie on the tag.

## What to write on the tag

A good tag turns a pile of groceries into a board. Include:

- **"Refrigerate the cheese"** (and the meat once it's opened).
- **Three pairings,** for example: cheddar with fig jam, salami with cornichons, gouda with mustard. Browse [pairings](/pairings/) for more.
- **How to serve it:** take the cheese out 30 to 60 minutes before serving, and slice the salami thin. See the [temperature guide](/blog/temperature-guide/).
- **Allergens,** especially nuts and gluten.

## Shipping a charcuterie gift

- **Shelf-stable items only:** ship any way you like. This is the easiest and safest option.
- **With cheese:** use an insulated mailer with frozen gel packs, ship **overnight**, and ship **Monday to Wednesday** so it doesn't sit in a warehouse over the weekend. Tell the recipient it's coming.
- **The workaround:** ship the shelf-stable box and add a note to pick up their favorite cheese, or tuck in a gift card for a local cheese shop.

## FAQ

**What do you put in a charcuterie gift basket?**
A whole dry salami, one or two cheeses, crackers, a jam or honey, nuts, olives or cornichons and a mustard, with dark chocolate as a finish. A cheese knife or a small wooden board makes a gift that lasts after the food is gone.

**Does a charcuterie gift basket need to be refrigerated?**
Most of it does not. Whole, unopened dry salami, crackers, jams, honey, nuts, jarred olives, mustard and chocolate are shelf-stable. Cheese and sliced or opened meat need to stay cold, so add the cheese right before you hand it over and tie on a tag that says to refrigerate it.

**How long does a whole salami last unrefrigerated?**
USDA guidance says a whole, unopened hard or dry sausage such as Genoa or hard salami keeps about 6 weeks in the pantry and indefinitely in the refrigerator. Once it is opened or sliced, keep it in the fridge and use it within 3 weeks.

**How much does a DIY charcuterie gift basket cost?**
About $25 to $35 for a small box with one salami, one cheese, crackers, jam and nuts, about $45 to $60 for a classic basket with two of each and extras, and about $95 to $130 for a luxury box that includes a wooden board and cheese knives.

**Can you ship a charcuterie gift basket?**
Ship the shelf-stable items any way you like. If it includes cheese, use an insulated mailer with frozen gel packs, ship overnight early in the week so it does not sit in a warehouse over a weekend, and tell the recipient it is coming. Otherwise send the shelf-stable box with a note to add their favorite cheese.
