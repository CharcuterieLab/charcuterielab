<h2 id="what-to-look-for">What to look for</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for:</strong> Tomatoes first, then peppers, onions and cilantro. Refrigerated salsas taste fresher; jarred ones last longer and are thicker.</li><li class="sk-x"><strong>Skip:</strong> Choosing by heat level alone. Medium from one brand can be hotter than hot from another. For a mixed crowd, put out a mild salsa and a hot one.</li></ul>
:::

**How much to buy:** about 2 to 3 tablespoons of salsa per guest, so a 16 oz jar covers 10 to 12 people. Once a jar is open, keep it in the fridge and follow the use-by time on the label. Fresh refrigerated salsa goes off faster than jarred.

<h2 id="faq">Salsa questions</h2>
:::

**What is the best store-bought salsa?**

Siete Mild Salsa did best across tests: #1 of 15 at The Takeout and 8.5 out of 10 at Sporked. For a cheaper chunky jar, On The Border Medium.

**What is the best jarred salsa?**

Siete Mild for flavor and On The Border Medium Chunky for value. Pace Restaurant Style scored 9.5 at Sporked.

**What is the best hot salsa?**

Casa Sanchez Hot Salsa Roja if you can find it. Mateo's Hot is the easier-to-find option.

**How much salsa do I need for a party?**

About 2 to 3 tablespoons per guest, or a 16 oz jar for every 10 to 12 people.
