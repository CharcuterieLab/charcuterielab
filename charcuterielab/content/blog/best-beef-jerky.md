<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to put jerky on a charcuterie board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Build a game-day board</strong> around it: jerky, meat sticks, summer sausage, smoked cheddar, pretzels, beer cheese and pickles.</li><li><strong>Cut thick strips</strong> into 2-inch pieces with kitchen shears and fan them out.</li><li><strong>Thin styles graze best:</strong> biltong and tender steak strips are easier to eat standing up than slab jerky.</li><li><strong>Watch the sugar:</strong> teriyaki and extra-tender jerkies run sweet. The sweetest bag in The Kitchn's test had 5 g of sugar per ounce.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> about 1 oz per person alongside other meats.</li></ul>

## Beef jerky questions

**What is the best beef jerky brand?**

Old Trapper Old Fashioned, Sporked's 10/10 after three tastings. Archer Grass-Fed won The Kitchn's blind test.

**What is the best cheap beef jerky?**

Walmart's Great Value Peppered Beef Jerky, which scored 9/10 at Sporked.

**Can you put beef jerky on a charcuterie board?**

Yes, especially a game-day board. Cut it into bite-size pieces, or use thin biltong.

**What's the difference between jerky and biltong?**

Jerky is usually marinated and dried with heat; biltong is cured with vinegar and spices and air-dried, so it's softer and tastes more like plain beef.

**Is Jack Link's good?**

It's the classic budget bag. The Kitchn called it a reliable budget buy, but it didn't beat the winners.
