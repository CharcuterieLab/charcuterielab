<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to oil a charcuterie board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Start clean and bone dry.</strong> Wash, towel off, and let it dry on its edge for a few hours.</li><li><strong>Rub in a tablespoon of oil</strong> with a soft cloth, with the grain, on every side and edge.</li><li><strong>Let it soak in</strong> for a few hours or overnight, then wipe off what's left.</li><li><strong>Seal with board cream</strong> if you have it, and buff.</li><li><strong>Once a month,</strong> or whenever water soaks in instead of beading.</li><li><strong>Never use olive, vegetable or regular coconut oil.</strong> They turn rancid in the wood.</li></ul>

## Cutting board oil questions

**What is the best oil for a charcuterie board?**

Food-grade mineral oil, or a board cream made from mineral oil and beeswax. Both are food safe and never go rancid.

**Can I use olive oil on a wooden board?**

No. Olive and other cooking oils can turn rancid in the wood, leaving a sticky surface and a sour smell.

**Is John Boos Mystery Oil worth it?**

It works well, but it is mostly mineral oil. The Board Cream is the part that adds something plain oil doesn't: a beeswax seal.

**How often should I oil a charcuterie board?**

About once a month, or whenever water soaks in instead of beading on the surface.

**Do I need both oil and board cream?**

No, oil alone protects the wood. The cream adds a water-resistant layer and a soft sheen.
