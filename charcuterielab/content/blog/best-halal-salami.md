<h2 id="how-to-choose">What to check on the label</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>A certification mark</strong> from a recognized halal body, on the package itself; one product being halal doesn't make a brand's whole line halal.</li><li><strong>Casings:</strong> some beef products use pork casings; certified products won't.</li><li><strong>Wine and alcohol</strong> in the ingredients of some European-style cured meats.</li><li><strong>Cross-contact:</strong> give halal meats their own board and knife.</li><li><strong>Sujuk for cooking vs. slicing:</strong> fresh sujuk needs cooking; dry-cured sujuk salami is ready to slice.</li></ul>

## Halal salami questions

**Is there halal salami?**

Yes. Halal salami is made from certified beef, turkey or chicken. Midamar is one widely sold US brand.

**What is sujuk?**

A spiced, garlicky beef sausage from Turkey and the Levant. Some are fresh and need cooking; dry-cured versions can be sliced and served.

**Is bresaola halal?**

Bresaola is beef, but it is halal only when it comes from a certified producer. Look for the certification mark.

**Where can I buy halal deli meats?**

Halal butchers, Middle Eastern and Turkish grocers and their online stores, and sometimes warehouse clubs.

**Is pork-free the same as halal?**

No. Halal requires certified slaughter and no alcohol or pork-derived ingredients, not just no pork.
