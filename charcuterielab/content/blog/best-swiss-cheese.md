# The Best Swiss Cheese for a Charcuterie Board, Ranked

> **Quick Answer:** Our top pick for Swiss cheese on a charcuterie board is Sargento Aged Swiss (4.6 out of 5). Block: Kerrygold Swiss Cheese. Deli-sliced: Boar's Head Gold Label Imported Switzerland Swiss. We ranked 6 widely sold options on how they work on a board, published taste tests and awards, ingredients, value and how easy they are to find.

American Swiss cheese is a mild, nutty, holey cheese in the style of Swiss Emmentaler. At the grocery store it ranges from waxy sandwich slices to aged blocks with real nuttiness. For a board you want flavor and a texture that isn't rubbery, so these rankings lean on two published taste tests, then aging, price and how easy each one is to find.

### 1. Sargento Aged Swiss

**★★★★½ 4.6 out of 5** · Best overall · Best for: Folded slices on any board · $ budget

Aged at least 60 days. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2168403/store-bought-swiss-cheese-ranked/) ranked it 1st of 9 for a more intense nuttiness, a sweet note and a slight funk, with a firm texture that isn't waxy, and [named it the best store-bought Swiss](https://www.tastingtable.com/2184260/best-store-bought-swiss-cheese-sargento/). [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-swiss-cheese/) gave regular Sargento Sliced Swiss 10 out of 10.

- **Why we like it:** The most flavor in a packaged slice, at a supermarket price.
- **Keep in mind:** It comes sliced, so fold or roll it rather than cubing it.
- **Where to buy:** Nearly every grocery store.

### 2. Kerrygold Swiss Cheese

**★★★★½ 4.3 out of 5** · Best block · Best for: Cubes and wedges · $$ mid-range

An Irish Swiss sold as a block. [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-swiss-cheese/) gave it 9 out of 10 as the best funky Swiss, with a sharp flavor that reminded them of Manchego.

- **Why we like it:** A block you can cut into cubes, with more bite than most Swiss.
- **Keep in mind:** Sharper than classic American Swiss.
- **Where to buy:** Walmart and many grocery cheese cases.

### 3. Boar's Head Gold Label Imported Switzerland Swiss

**★★★★☆ 4.2 out of 5** · Best deli-sliced · Best for: Thin deli slices · $$ mid-range

Made in Switzerland and aged over 120 days. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2168403/store-bought-swiss-cheese-ranked/) ranked it 2nd of 9 for its nuttiness, tang and hint of sweetness, and [Sporked](https://sporked.com/article/best-swiss-cheese/) gave it 8.5 out of 10 for delicate, thin slices.

- **Why we like it:** Real Swiss-made cheese, sliced to order.
- **Keep in mind:** Deli prices, and very thin slices tear if you fold them tightly.
- **Where to buy:** Deli counters that carry Boar's Head.

### 4. Trader Joe's Sliced Swiss

## What to look for on the label

**Label check:** "Aged" on the label, or an age in days. Older Swiss is nuttier and firmer. Imported Emmentaler is the traditional version and costs more.

**Skip:** Reduced-fat Swiss for a board. It's drier and has less flavor. Ultra-thin slices also tear when you fold them.

**Serving it:** Swiss is a semi-firm cheese, so take it out of the fridge 30 to 45 minutes before serving. Fold slices into quarters or roll them, or cut a block into cubes. It's easy to like with ham, mustard, apples and rye crackers.

## Swiss cheese questions

**What is the best Swiss cheese brand?**

Sargento Aged Swiss ranked 1st of 9 in Tasting Table's test. For a block you can cube, Kerrygold Swiss scored 9 out of 10 at Sporked.

**Is Swiss cheese the same as Emmental?**

American Swiss is made in the style of Swiss Emmentaler, but it's usually milder and aged for less time. Emmentaler AOP comes from Switzerland.

**What is baby Swiss?**

A younger Swiss with smaller holes and a milder, creamier flavor.

**How long before serving should Swiss come out of the fridge?**

30 to 45 minutes.
