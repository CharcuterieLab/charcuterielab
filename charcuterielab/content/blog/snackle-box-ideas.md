# Snackle Box Ideas: 12 Themes, What to Put In It + How to Pack One

> **Quick Answer:** A **snackle box** is a divided box with a different snack in each compartment, like a charcuterie board with a lid. Fill it with **cheese, meat, something crunchy, fruit, a veggie, a sweet and a dip**. Use a box sold as **food safe**, and keep it in a cooler with ice packs.

<h2 id="what">What is a snackle box?</h2>
<p>The name is <strong>snack + tackle box</strong>. It's a clear, divided box, the kind that looks like a fishing tackle organizer, with a different snack in every compartment.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Where it started:</strong> a TikTok video from November 2019 showed a dollar-store tackle box filled with road-trip snacks for kids (<a href="https://www.bustle.com/life/snackle-box-tiktok-trend-charcuterie-tackle-boxes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Bustle</a>).</li>
<li><strong>When adults took it over:</strong> summer 2022, when a version made for a bachelorette party went viral and turned it into a grown-up "charcuterie box" (<a href="https://barrypopik.com/blog/snackle_box" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Barry Popik's word archive</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Why it works:</strong> nothing touches, nothing spills, everyone can see what's there, and it closes. A board can't do any of that in a car.</li>
</ul>
:::

How it compares with its cousins:

| | Snackle box | [Charcuterie cup](/blog/charcuterie-cups/) | [Jarcuterie](/blog/jarcuterie/) | [Mini board](/blog/mini-charcuterie-board/) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Best for | Road trips, boats, beach, movie night | Parties, showers | Gifts, picnics | Date night at home |

<h2 id="box">Which box to use (and which to skip)</h2>
<p><strong>The most important rule:</strong> use a box that's sold as <strong>food safe</strong>. Many fishing tackle boxes and bead or craft organizers aren't made to touch food, and you can't tell by looking.</p>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>Best:</strong> a divided box sold for snacks, labeled food safe or BPA-free. Many are now sold as "snackle boxes", with a hinged lid and 6 to 24 compartments.</li>
<li><strong>Also great:</strong> a bento box or a divided meal-prep container. Fewer sections, but they seal better for dips.</li>
<li><strong>For kids:</strong> a box with a lid on each compartment, so they can open one section at a time.</li>
<li><strong>Skip:</strong> any box that has ever held fishing gear, and craft organizers with no food-safe label.</li>
<li><strong>Cleaning:</strong> check whether it's dishwasher safe. Lots of small corners are hard to scrub by hand. A bottle brush helps.</li>
</ul>
<p class="sk-note">Size guide: <strong>6 to 8 sections</strong> for one person or a kid's lunch, <strong>10 to 15</strong> for two to four people sharing, and <strong>18 to 24</strong> for a crowd or a big road trip, where you repeat favorites in several sections.</p>
:::

<h2 id="fill">What to put in a snackle box</h2>
<p>Pick at least one from each row. Mix shapes and colors so the box looks full and bright when you open it.</p>
:::

| Type | Easy picks | Pro tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Cheese** | Cheddar cubes, [string cheese](/blog/best-string-cheese/) cut in thirds, mini Babybel-style rounds, pepper jack, gouda | Firm cheeses travel best. Leave soft brie at home on hot days. |
| **Meat** | Folded salami, pepperoni slices, [meat sticks](/blog/best-meat-sticks/) cut in pieces, rolled turkey, [summer sausage](/blog/best-summer-sausage/) rounds | Shelf-stable dry salami and meat sticks are the safest choice for long, warm days. |
| **Crunch** | [Pretzels](/blog/best-pretzels-for-charcuterie/), [crackers](/blog/best-crackers-for-charcuterie/), pita chips, popcorn, nuts, Goldfish-style crackers | Pack in the morning. Put them in the sections furthest from fruit. |
| **Fruit** | Grapes, blueberries, strawberries, clementine segments, dried apricots | Dry the fruit well. Moisture makes everything around it soggy. |
| **Veggies** | Snap peas, mini sweet peppers, baby carrots, cucumber rounds, cherry tomatoes | Crunchy, cold and good for dipping. |
| **Sweet** | Chocolate candies, gummies, yogurt-covered pretzels, mini cookies, chocolate-covered almonds | Chocolate melts in the sun. On beach days, swap it for gummies. |
| **Dip** | Hummus, ranch, guacamole, peanut butter, [pimento cheese](/blog/best-pimento-cheese/) | Always in a small lidded cup set into a section, never loose. |
| **Wild card** | [Pickles](/blog/best-pickles-for-charcuterie/), olives, hard-boiled eggs, mini muffins, beef jerky | One surprise makes it feel special. |

<h2 id="ideas">12 snackle box ideas</h2>
:::

### 1. The Road Trip Box

Car-friendly snacks with no drips and no crumbs: cheddar cubes, meat stick pieces, pretzels, grapes, baby carrots, gummy bears, trail mix and a cup of ranch. Kids in the back seat get their own small box.

### 2. The Beach Day Box

Things that won't melt: dry salami, white cheddar, pita chips, watermelon cubes in a lidded section, cucumber rounds, gummies instead of chocolate, and a cup of hummus. Keep the box in the cooler and only take it out to eat.

![A snackle box on a beach towel next to a cooler, filled with watermelon cubes, cucumber rounds, salami, cheddar, pita chips, gummies and hummus](/images/snackle-box-beach.jpg)

### 3. The Boat Day Box (the adult classic)

The one that made it famous: pepper jack, Genoa salami, Castelvetrano olives, Marcona almonds, strawberries, mini peppers, dark chocolate and a cup of pimento cheese. It goes well with a cold drink.

### 4. Movie Night Box

The couch version: popcorn, peanut butter cups, mini pretzels, string cheese, pepperoni slices, red grapes, sour gummies and a cup of chocolate dip for the pretzels. Make one per person so nobody fights over the candy. For a full board version: [movie night board](/boards/movie-night-charcuterie-board/).

### 5. The Kids' Lunchbox

Small portions, familiar foods: cheese cubes, rolled turkey, crackers, apple slices tossed in lemon juice, cucumber, a few chocolate chips and a cup of ranch. For under-5s, quarter the grapes and cherry tomatoes and leave out whole nuts. More ideas: [charcuterie board for kids](/blog/charcuterie-board-for-kids/).

### 6. Game Day Box

Tailgate flavors: pepper jack, summer sausage, kettle chips, mini pretzel twists, celery sticks, pickle spears, chocolate footballs and a cup of beer cheese or queso. The full spread: [game day board](/boards/game-day-charcuterie-board/).

### 7. The Hiking Box

Shelf-stable and filling: meat sticks, a hard aged cheese like Parmesan chunks (it handles warmth better than soft cheese), almonds, dried apricots, pretzels, dark chocolate and peanut butter in a sealed cup. Keep it in your pack's coolest pocket, and eat the cheese within a few hours.

### 8. The Italian Box

A mini antipasto platter: mozzarella pearls, salami and pepperoni, marinated artichoke hearts, olives, cherry tomatoes, breadsticks, Italian amaretti cookies and a cup of pesto. Inspired by our [Italian antipasto board](/boards/italian-antipasto-board/).

### 9. The Christmas Box

Red, green and white: white cheddar cubes, salami, green grapes, strawberries, rosemary crackers, peppermint bark, red and green chocolate candies and a cup of cranberry salsa. Wrap it with ribbon and it doubles as a gift. More holiday bites: [Christmas appetizers](/blog/christmas-appetizers/).

![A clear snackle box filled with festive red, green and white snacks: strawberries, green grapes, white cheddar, salami, rosemary crackers, peppermint bark and red and green candies, tied with a red ribbon](/images/snackle-box-christmas.jpg)

### 10. The Halloween Box

Orange and black: cheddar, pepperoni, orange clementine "pumpkins" (peeled, with a tiny piece of celery as the stem), black olives, pretzels, candy corn and chocolate. Perfect for trick-or-treat night.

### 11. The Keto Box

No crackers or fruit: aged cheddar, gouda, salami, prosciutto, olives, pecans, cucumber rounds, celery and a cup of guacamole. Plus 90%-cocoa chocolate as the sweet. Full plans: [keto charcuterie board](/blog/keto-charcuterie-board/).

### 12. The Vegan Box

Plant-based all the way: hummus, a [vegan cheese](/blog/best-vegan-cheese/) cut into cubes, marinated chickpeas, mini peppers, snap peas, grapes, pita chips, dark chocolate and roasted almonds. More: [vegan charcuterie board](/boards/vegan-charcuterie-board/).

<h2 id="how-much">How much to pack</h2>
<p>A snackle box is either a snack or a meal. Use the same amounts as for a board (our <a href="/blog/how-much-charcuterie-per-person/">per-person guide</a>):</p>
:::

| Per person | As a snack | As lunch or a meal |
|---|---|---|
| Cheese | 2 oz | 4 oz |
| Meat | 2 oz | 4 oz |
| Crackers or chips | 6 to 8 | 12 to 15 |
| Fruit | ½ cup | 1 cup |
| Veggies | ½ cup | ½ to 1 cup |
| Dip | 2 tbsp | ¼ cup |
| Sweet | A small handful | A small handful |

**Sharing one box?** Use 2 compartments for each food that everyone loves (usually the cheese and the sweets), so two people can dig in at once.

:::html
<h2 id="pack">How to pack a snackle box (step by step)</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Chill the box.</strong> Put the empty box in the fridge for 15 minutes so it starts cold.</li>
<li><strong>Dips first.</strong> Set the lidded dip cups into their sections.</li>
<li><strong>Wet foods next.</strong> Fruit, veggies, olives and pickles go in sections next to each other, all on one side.</li>
<li><strong>Protein in the middle.</strong> Cheese and meat act as a buffer between the wet side and the dry side.</li>
<li><strong>Crunch last, furthest from the wet side.</strong> Pack crackers and chips on the morning you go.</li>
<li><strong>Fill every section to the top.</strong> Full sections don't rattle, and it looks better when you open the lid.</li>
<li><strong>Lay a sheet of parchment under the lid</strong> before closing it. It keeps small things from jumping between sections when the box tips.</li>
</ol>
:::

![An overhead view of a snackle box being packed in order: lidded dip cups, fruit and veggies on one side, cheese and meat in the middle, crackers on the far side](/images/snackle-box-packing-order.jpg)

**Make ahead:** You can pack everything except the crackers, chips and cut apples the night before. Keep the box in the fridge and add the crunchy things in the morning.

:::html
<h2 id="cold">Keeping it cold and safe</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks">
<li><strong>The rule:</strong> cheese, meat, cut fruit and dips shouldn't be out of the cold for more than <strong>2 hours</strong>, or <strong>1 hour above 90°F</strong> (<a href="https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-the-2-Hour-Rule-with-leaving-food-out" target="_blank" rel="noopener">USDA</a>). On a beach or a boat, that hour goes fast.</li>
<li><strong>Use a cooler or an insulated bag</strong> with ice packs underneath the box. Put it in the shade.</li>
<li><strong>Freeze a few grapes.</strong> They work as tiny ice packs and thaw into a cold snack.</li>
<li><strong>Choose shelf-stable foods for long days:</strong> dry salami, meat sticks, hard aged cheese, nuts, dried fruit, pretzels.</li>
<li><strong>Toss the leftovers</strong> if the box spent the afternoon in the sun. Wash the box in hot soapy water as soon as you get home.</li>
</ul>
<p class="sk-note">Taking a full board instead? See <a href="/blog/how-to-transport-charcuterie-board/">how to transport a charcuterie board</a>.</p>
:::

## Common snackle box mistakes

- **Wet next to crunchy.** Strawberries beside crackers means soggy crackers by lunch. Keep a row of cheese between them.
- **Loose dip.** It ends up everywhere. Always use a lidded cup.
- **Chocolate on a hot day.** It melts into the sections around it. Use gummies or yogurt-covered pretzels instead.
- **Half-full sections.** They look sad and everything rattles. Fill each one to the top, or choose a smaller box.
- **A box that isn't food safe.** Check the label before you buy.

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
:::

**What is a snackle box?**

A snackle box is a divided box, often a clear tackle-style organizer, with a different snack in each compartment, like a portable charcuterie board. The idea spread on TikTok in 2019 as a road-trip snack box for kids, and adults picked it up in 2022 for boat days, beach trips and parties.

**What do you put in a snackle box?**

Fill each compartment with a different type of snack: cheese, a meat, something crunchy like crackers or pretzels, fresh fruit, a vegetable, a sweet, and a dip or spread in a lidded cup. Put wet items in their own section and crunchy ones as far away from them as possible.

**Is it safe to use a fishing tackle box for food?**

Only if it is sold as food safe. Many tackle boxes and craft organizers are not made for food contact. Look for a divided container labeled food safe or BPA-free and sold for snacks, or use a bento box, and never use a box that has held fishing gear.

**How do you keep a snackle box cold?**

Chill the filled box in the fridge for at least an hour, then carry it in a cooler or insulated bag with ice packs underneath. Perishable food should be out of the cooler for no more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.

**How far ahead can you make a snackle box?**

Up to the night before, if you leave out the crackers, chips and cut apples or bananas. Pack the crunchy items in the morning so they stay crisp, and keep the box in the fridge until you leave.
