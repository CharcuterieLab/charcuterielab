<h2 id="what-to-look-for">What to look for</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for:</strong> For Parmigiano-Reggiano, the dotted name stamped into the rind and a DOP mark. An age of 24 months is the classic; 36 months is drier and more crystalline.</li><li class="sk-x"><strong>Skip:</strong> Pre-shredded or grated Parmesan for a board. Buy a wedge and break it with a small knife.</li></ul>

<h2 id="faq">Parmesan questions</h2>
:::

**What is the best Parmesan for a cheese board?**

A wedge of real Parmigiano-Reggiano aged 24 months. Boar's Head won America's Test Kitchen's tasting; Kirkland is the best value.

**Is Parmesan the same as Parmigiano-Reggiano?**

In the US, no. Parmigiano-Reggiano is a protected Italian cheese; Parmesan can be made anywhere.

**How do I serve Parmesan on a board?**

Break it into rough chunks with a short, pointed knife instead of slicing. Take it out 45 to 60 minutes before serving.
