# The Best Pâté for a Charcuterie Board: What to Buy

> **Quick Answer:** The best store-bought pâtés for a charcuterie board come from Les Trois Petits Cochons, a New York maker since 1975 whose Mousse Truffée won a Gold sofi award, and Alexian, whose Pâté de Campagne is sold at many supermarket cheese counters. For a smooth, rich spread, buy a mousse; for a rustic slice, buy a pâté de campagne. No published US taste test has ranked pâté brands, so this guide names what to buy without star scores.

Pâté makes a board feel like a French bistro: one small slab next to cornichons, mustard and a sliced baguette. There are two main kinds. Mousse is smooth and spreadable, usually made with liver. Pâté de campagne (country pâté) is coarse and sliceable, mostly pork. Both are sold ready to serve in the specialty cheese case.

*How we picked these: no published side-by-side taste test of store-bought pâté in the US exists yet, so this guide is not scored with stars like our other buying guides. Instead it names the widely sold options, what each is like and where to find it, using award results where they exist, the few published tests that include them and how this kind of spread behaves on a board. Sources are listed at the end, and our method is on the [buying guides hub](/blog/best-charcuterie-brands/#how-we-rank). Stock last checked October 2026.*

### Les Trois Petits Cochons pâtés and mousses

**Best overall** · Best for: The widest range · $$ mid-range

Three Little Pigs started as a charcuterie shop in Greenwich Village in 1975 and now sells pâtés, mousses and rillettes through thousands of US stores. It has won several Gold sofi awards from the Specialty Food Association, including one for its Mousse Truffée ([Les Trois Petits Cochons](https://3pigs.com/pages/about-us)).

- **Why we like it:** Award-winning and fairly easy to find, with a style for every board.
- **Keep in mind:** The truffle mousse costs more than the plain ones.
- **Where to buy:** Whole Foods, specialty grocers, many supermarket cheese cases and online.

### Alexian Pâté de Campagne

**Best country pâté** · Best for: A rustic, sliceable pâté · $ budget

A classic coarse country pâté, sold in 5 oz pieces in many supermarket specialty cases ([ShopRite](https://shoprite.com/product/alexian-pt-de-campagne-5-oz-id-00042251000484)).

- **Why we like it:** A small piece is just right for a board for 6 to 8.
- **Keep in mind:** Coarse and porky, so guests expecting a smooth spread may be surprised.
- **Where to buy:** ShopRite, Zabar's and many supermarket cheese cases.

### Alexian Duck Liver and Pork Mousse with Cognac

**Best mousse** · Best for: A smooth, rich spread · $$ mid-range

A smooth mousse of duck liver and pork with mushrooms, spices and a little cognac, which won a Good Food Award ([Alexian](https://alexianpate.com/ap_product/duck-with-cognac/)).

- **Why we like it:** Silky, rich and spreadable.
- **Keep in mind:** Contains egg and milk. Keep it refrigerated.
- **Where to buy:** Supermarket specialty cases and online.

### Canned or shelf-stable pâté

**Safest choice for pregnancy** · Best for: Guests who are pregnant · $ budget

US food-safety guidance for pregnancy lists refrigerated pâtés and meat spreads as foods to avoid, but says canned or shelf-stable pâtés are fine ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/health-educators/listeria-food-safety-moms-be)). A tin of shelf-stable pâté means everyone can try some.

- **Why we like it:** Keeps in the pantry until opened.
- **Keep in mind:** Usually milder and softer than fresh pâté.
- **Where to buy:** The canned meat or international aisle, and online.

## How to choose

- **For a classic bistro board:** a pâté de campagne, sliced, with cornichons and grainy mustard.
- **For spreading:** a duck or chicken liver mousse, served in its tub or turned out onto the board.
- **For a holiday splurge:** a truffle mousse.

Pâté goes on crusty baguette, toasted brioche or plain crackers. Add something sharp to cut the richness: cornichons, pickled onions, mustard or a fruit jam like fig or cherry. Our [rillettes guide](/ingredients/rillettes/) covers pâté's spreadable cousin.

## What to look for on the label

**Label check:** Check the date and keep it cold. Fresh pâté is perishable: refrigerate it, and eat it within a few days of opening.

**Skip:** Leaving pâté out for the whole party. Like other perishable foods, it shouldn't sit at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Put out a small piece and refill from the fridge.

**How much to buy:** about 1 oz per guest. A 5 oz piece serves 6 to 8 people as one item on a board.
**How to serve it:** take it out of the fridge about 15 minutes before serving, and slice country pâté with a warm knife.
**Pregnant guests:** refrigerated pâté is on the FDA's avoid list during pregnancy. Canned or shelf-stable pâté is fine.

## Pâté questions

**What is the best store-bought pâté?**

Les Trois Petits Cochons, a New York maker with several Gold sofi awards, and Alexian, whose Pâté de Campagne and duck mousse are sold in many supermarkets.

**What do you eat with pâté on a charcuterie board?**

Sliced baguette or crackers, cornichons, grainy mustard and a sweet jam such as fig. Pickled onions also cut the richness.

**Can you eat pâté when pregnant?**

The FDA lists refrigerated pâtés and meat spreads as foods to avoid during pregnancy, but canned or shelf-stable pâtés are fine. Ask your doctor if you're unsure.

**What is the difference between pâté and mousse?**

Mousse is smooth and spreadable, usually made with liver. Pâté de campagne is coarse and sliceable, mostly pork.
