# Turkey Charcuterie Board: How to Build It for Thanksgiving (Serves 8 to 20)

> **Quick Answer:** Make a small round **body** (a pear half or mini brie), then fan food in **arcs behind it** as tail feathers. Add a cheddar beak and red pepper wattle. Plan **2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest**.

Kids remember it, and every row is one ingredient in a curve, so there's no guessing.

## Board size and how many it serves

| Board | Feather rows | Serves (before dinner) | Serves (as the party food) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 to 16 inch round | 4 to 5 rows | 6 to 8 | 4 to 6 |
| 18 inch round or a round tray | 6 to 7 rows | 10 to 12 | 7 to 8 |
| Large tray plus a side board | 7 rows plus a side board | 18 to 20 | 12 to 14 |

A round board works best because the feathers need a full half circle. A rectangle works too: put the body near one long edge and fan the rows toward the other.

## Shopping list for 8, 12 or 20 guests

These follow the Lab standard for a board before a meal (2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest), with 10% extra from 20 guests up. If the turkey is the main food at a Friendsgiving or open house, multiply the meat and cheese by 1.5.

| Item | 8 guests | 12 guests | 20 guests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cheese (2 to 3 kinds, in slices or cubes) | 1 lb | 1½ lb | 2¾ lb |
| Cured meat (1 to 2 kinds) | 1 lb | 1½ lb | 2¾ lb |
| Crackers (round and rectangular) | 50 to 65 | 75 to 95 | 135 to 175 |
| Red and green grapes | 1 lb | 1½ lb | 2½ lb |
| Dried apricots | 1 cup | 1½ cups | 2½ cups |
| Pecans or candied pecans | ¾ cup | 1 cup | 2 cups |
| Pear (body), red bell pepper (wattle) | 1 each | 1 each | 1 each |

Use the [Party Planner](/party-planner/) if your guest count is different. It uses the same amounts.

## The fall colors that make it look like a turkey

A turkey reads as a turkey because the feathers alternate warm colors. Aim for a light row, a dark row, an orange row, a red row, and repeat.

| Color | Cheese | Meat and savory | Fruit and extras |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orange | [Aged cheddar](/ingredients/aged-cheddar/), [Red Leicester](/ingredients/red-leicester/), [Mimolette](/ingredients/mimolette/) | Cheddar crackers | [Dried apricots](/ingredients/dried-apricots/), clementine segments |
| Red and deep red | [Wensleydale with cranberries](/ingredients/wensleydale-cranberry/) | [Genoa salami](/ingredients/genoa-salami/), [soppressata](/ingredients/soppressata/), [pepperoni](/ingredients/pepperoni/) | Red grapes, [dried cranberries](/ingredients/dried-cranberries/), [pomegranate](/ingredients/pomegranate/) |
| Tan and brown | [Aged gouda](/ingredients/aged-gouda/), [Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/) | Wheat and rye crackers, pretzels | [Pecans](/ingredients/pecans/), [dried figs](/ingredients/dried-figs/) |
| Cream and green | White cheddar, [havarti](/ingredients/havarti/), [brie](/ingredients/brie/) | Green olives | Green grapes, pear slices |

## How to build the turkey, step by step

1. **Place the body.** Set a pear half cut side down, or a 3 to 4 inch bowl, about two thirds of the way down the board and centered. Everything fans out behind it.
2. **Outer row first.** Lay the largest item, usually rectangular crackers, in a wide arc around the top edge of the board. This is the longest row and sets the shape.
3. **Second row: cheese.** Inside the cracker arc, shingle cheese slices or rectangles in an overlapping curve. Orange cheddar here gives the strongest color.
4. **Third row: meat.** Fold salami in half and then in half again, and line the folded pieces up in the next arc. Pepperoni slices overlapped like coins also work.
5. **Fourth row and on: fruit, nuts and a second cheese.** Keep going inward with grapes, dried apricots, a light cheese and pecans, one ingredient per row, until you reach the body.
6. **The face.** Put a round cracker or a round slice of cheese on top of the body as the head. Add two black peppercorns, olive slices or candy eyes, a small triangle of cheddar for the beak and a thin strip of red bell pepper hanging beside it as the wattle.
7. **Feet.** Two pretzel sticks or short carrot sticks under the body finish it.
8. **Extra crackers on the side.** The turkey holds fewer crackers than guests need, so put the rest in a basket or on a plate next to the board.

## Veggie tray turkey (great for kids)

For a lighter or kid-friendly version, use the same layout with vegetables:

- **Body:** a bowl of [ranch dip](/blog/best-ranch-dip/) or hummus
- **Feathers:** rows of baby carrots, yellow and orange pepper strips, cucumber rounds, cherry tomatoes, celery and broccoli
- **Amount:** about 1 cup of vegetables and 2 tablespoons of dip per guest before dinner

Kids eat more vegetables when they look like a turkey, and it costs less than the charcuterie version.

## Make-ahead timing

- **The night before:** slice the cheese, fold the salami, wash and dry the grapes, and cut the vegetables. Store each in its own sealed container.
- **1 hour before:** take out the aged cheese (45 to 60 minutes out of the fridge is best).
- **30 minutes before:** take out the meat and any soft cheese, then build the rows. Cut the pear last so it doesn't brown, or brush the cut side with lemon juice.
- **Once it's out:** serve within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. On Thanksgiving the oven makes the kitchen warm, so set the board in another room.

## FAQ

**How do you make a turkey charcuterie board?**

Put a small round bowl or a pear half low in the center of a round board for the body. Fan rows of crackers, cheese slices, folded salami, grapes and dried apricots out behind it in arcs for the tail feathers. Add a round cracker or cheese slice for the head, two peppercorns or candy eyes, a cheddar triangle for the beak and a strip of red pepper for the wattle.

**How much food do you need for a turkey charcuterie board?**

Before Thanksgiving dinner, plan 2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest: 1 lb of each for 8 guests, 1½ lb for 12 and about 2¾ lb for 20. If the board is the main party food, plan 3 oz each per guest.

**What size board do you need for a turkey board?**

A 14 to 16 inch round board fits a turkey for about 8 guests before a meal. Use an 18 inch round board or a large round tray for 12, and for 20 guests build the turkey on a large tray and put extra crackers and cheese on a side board.

**What can I use for the turkey's body?**

A halved pear placed cut side down, a small round bowl of hummus or cheese dip, a mini cheese ball, or a small wheel of brie all work. Pick something round and brown or tan so it reads as the body.

**Can you make a turkey charcuterie board the night before?**

Cut the cheese, fold the salami and wash the fruit the night before, and store each in its own container. Build the turkey about 30 minutes before guests arrive so the crackers stay crisp, and serve it within 2 hours.
