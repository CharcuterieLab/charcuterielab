## FAQ

**What goes on a dessert charcuterie board?**
A dessert board centers on sweet elements: chocolate (dark, milk, white), cookies or brownies, fresh fruit, dried fruit, caramel or chocolate dipping sauce, and sweet spreads like Nutella or fruit preserves. Balance sweetness with contrast elements: salted nuts, a small portion of sharp cheese (blue cheese with honey is a classic), or dark chocolate with high cacao percentage to offset pure sweetness.

**What cheeses work on a dessert board?**
Not all boards need cheese, but those that include it should choose sweet-leaning options: mascarpone (pairs with berries), brie (with honey and walnuts), Gorgonzola Dolce (with dark chocolate), or aged Manchego (with quince paste and dark chocolate). Avoid sharp, salty cheeses that clash with dessert flavors.

**How do you make a dessert board without it becoming overwhelming?**
Structure is the key. Group similar elements together and create visual anchors (a chocolate dipping bowl, a large fruit cluster). Use color contrast — dark chocolate against pale cheeses, bright berries against neutral crackers or cookies. Limit sweet spreads to 1–2 options to avoid flavor fatigue.

**What's the difference between a dessert board and a candy board?**
A dessert board uses elevated, thoughtfully curated sweet elements — artisan chocolate, fresh and dried fruit, quality cookies, seasonal components. A candy board is less curated, typically featuring convenience candy. The dessert board approach applies the same quality and pairing principles as a savory charcuterie board.

**Can a dessert board work as a full dessert course?**
Yes — a well-built dessert board functions as a complete dessert course for 4–10 people and scales easily. It's interactive, shareable, and lower effort than plated desserts. For a dinner party, a dessert board served with dessert wine is a sophisticated and conversation-driving finale.
