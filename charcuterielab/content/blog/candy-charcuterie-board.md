# Candy Charcuterie Board: How to Build One for Any Holiday (Serves 8 to 20)

> **Quick Answer:** A candy charcuterie board is a board of candy arranged the way you'd arrange cheese and meat: **bowls first, big pieces next, then fill every gap by color**. Mix four types: **chocolate, chewy, crunchy and a salty item** like pretzels. Plan **about 2 oz of candy per guest** when it's one of several desserts and **3 to 4 oz per guest** when it's the main event, so about 1½ to 3 lb for 12. Change the colors and it works for Halloween, Christmas, Valentine's Day, Easter and birthdays.

A candy board takes about 20 minutes, needs no cooking, and looks impressive because candy is already colorful. The trick is the same as a charcuterie board: contrast. Pair soft with crunchy, sweet with salty, and group colors so the board looks planned rather than dumped out of a bag.

## How much candy for 8, 12 or 20 guests

| | 8 guests | 12 guests | 20 guests |
|---|---|---|---|
| One of several desserts (2 oz each) | 1 lb | 1½ lb | 2½ lb |
| The main dessert or party snack (3 to 4 oz each) | 1½ to 2 lb | 2¼ to 3 lb | 3¾ to 5 lb |
| Board size | 12 × 18 inch | 15 × 22 inch | Two boards or a sheet pan plus a board |

Kids eat more candy than adults at a party, so lean to the top of the range for a kids' birthday and the bottom for a dinner party.

## The four types to mix

| Type | Pick 2 to 4 | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate | Mini candy bars, truffles, chocolate-covered pretzels, peanut butter cups, foil-wrapped chocolates, a [good dark chocolate](/blog/best-dark-chocolate/) broken into squares | The richest bite; most guests start here |
| Chewy | Gummy bears, sour belts, licorice twists, taffy, fruit slices | Color and a texture change |
| Crunchy or hard | Candy-coated chocolates, rock candy, lollipops, peppermints, malted milk balls | Height, sparkle and something to suck on slowly |
| Salty | Mini pretzels, pretzel rods, kettle corn, salted nuts, chocolate-covered potato chips | Breaks up the sweetness so guests keep eating |

Add one **"wow" item** for height or size: a jar of lollipops standing upright, a giant chocolate bar, cotton candy piled in a corner or a stack of macarons.

## How to build it, step by step

1. **Choose the board.** A wood board, a marble slab or a rimmed sheet pan all work. Line wood with parchment if the candy is sticky.
2. **Bowls first.** Place 2 or 3 small bowls for tiny or rolling candy, like sprinkles, candy-coated chocolates and nuts.
3. **Big items next.** Set the largest pieces, such as candy bars, pretzel rods and lollipops, spread out across the board.
4. **Color blocks.** Fill the space in sections by color, or run a rainbow from one corner to the other. Same-color candy grouped together looks much better than a mix.
5. **Fill every gap.** Use the smallest candies to cover any board that still shows. A full board looks generous.
6. **Last touches.** Cotton candy, fresh berries or marshmallows go on right before serving, because they soften or get sticky.

## Holiday versions

| Occasion | Colors | Candy ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Halloween | Orange, black, purple, green | Candy corn, mini chocolate bars, gummy worms, black licorice, sour candy, caramel apple pops |
| Christmas | Red, green, white | Candy canes, peppermint bark, red and green candy-coated chocolates, chocolate coins, gumdrops, white chocolate pretzels |

## Candy plus cheese: the grown-up board

A candy board doesn't have to be all candy. For an adult party, add a few cheeses that work with sweets: [aged gouda](/ingredients/aged-gouda/), whose caramel notes match chocolate and toffee, [aged cheddar](/ingredients/aged-cheddar/) with caramels and apple slices, and a creamy [brie](/ingredients/brie/) with chocolate-covered pretzels. The salt in the cheese does the same job as the pretzels. See [chocolate on a charcuterie board](/blog/charcuterie-board-chocolate/) for more pairings.

## Kids, allergies and keeping it fresh

- **Little kids:** hard candy, gumballs, nuts and popcorn are choking hazards for children under 4. Put them on a separate tray out of reach, or leave them off a toddler party.
- **Allergies:** many candies contain peanuts, tree nuts, milk, soy or gluten, or are made in shared facilities. Keep nut candy in its own bowl with a label, and keep the original wrappers so parents can check ingredients.
- **Chocolate:** keep the board out of the sun and away from the stove. Chocolate softens in a warm room.
- **Make ahead:** build it several hours ahead, or the night before covered tightly with plastic wrap. Add anything sticky right before serving.

## What it costs

Bulk and fun-size bags keep a candy board cheap. Expect roughly **$1.50 to $3 per guest** for a board of store-brand and fun-size candy, more if you add truffles, macarons or specialty chocolate. The day after Halloween, Valentine's Day and Easter is the cheapest time to stock up for the next board.

## FAQ

**What do you put on a candy charcuterie board?**
Mix four types: chocolate (mini bars, truffles, chocolate-covered pretzels), chewy candy (gummies, licorice, taffy), crunchy or hard candy (rock candy, candy-coated chocolates, lollipops) and one or two salty items such as pretzels or popcorn. Add a few big pieces for height, like a chocolate bar broken into squares or a pile of cotton candy.

**How much candy do you need for a candy board?**
Plan about 2 oz of candy per guest when the board is one of several desserts, and 3 to 4 oz per guest when it is the main dessert or party snack. For 12 guests that is about 1½ lb as a side dessert or 2¼ to 3 lb as the main event.

**How do you arrange a candy charcuterie board?**
Put small bowls on the board first for loose candy, then lay the biggest items, then fill in by color in blocks or a rainbow so each color sits in its own section. Fill every gap with small candy so no board shows, and keep chocolate away from any heat.

**How far ahead can you make a candy board?**
A candy board can be built several hours ahead, or the night before if you cover it tightly with plastic wrap. Leave out anything that softens or gets sticky, like cotton candy, fresh fruit and marshmallows, until right before serving.

**Is a candy board safe for toddlers?**
Hard candy, gumballs, nuts and popcorn are choking hazards for children under 4, so put those on a separate tray out of reach or leave them off. Use soft, meltable treats for little kids and always supervise them while they eat.
