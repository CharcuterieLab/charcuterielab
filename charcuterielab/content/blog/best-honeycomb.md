A slab of honeycomb is the fastest way to make a cheese board look special. It's just honey still in the beeswax cells the bees built, and the whole thing is edible: the honey floods out and the wax chews like soft gum. It's especially good with salty blue cheese, aged cheddar and creamy brie.

<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to serve honeycomb on a board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Keep it contained:</strong> leave it in its tray or set it on a small plate so the honey doesn't run into the crackers.</li><li><strong>Give it a knife:</strong> a small spreader or honeycomb knife so guests can cut a piece with honey.</li><li><strong>Put it next to blue cheese,</strong> aged cheddar, Manchego or brie; sweet and salty is the point.</li><li><strong>Eat the wax:</strong> it's edible; chew it and swallow it or discreetly leave it.</li><li><strong>Leftovers:</strong> cover and keep at room temperature; honey doesn't spoil.</li><li><strong>No honey for babies</strong> under 1 year old.</li></ul>

## Honeycomb questions

**Can you eat honeycomb wax?**

Yes. Beeswax is edible. Most people chew the comb and swallow it, or leave the wax after the honey is gone.

**What cheese goes with honeycomb?**

Salty and sharp cheeses: blue cheese, aged cheddar, Manchego and Parmesan, plus creamy brie.

**How do you store leftover honeycomb?**

Covered, at room temperature. Honey doesn't spoil, though it may crystallize.

**Where can I buy honeycomb?**

Specialty grocers, kitchen stores, some warehouse clubs, and directly from honey makers online.

**Is honeycomb better than honey for a cheese board?**

It looks more impressive and adds a chewy texture; liquid honey is easier to drizzle. Many boards use both.
