# Jarcuterie: How to Make Charcuterie Jars (12, 24 or 50)

> **Quick Answer:** Charcuterie in a **12–16 oz mason jar**: nuts in the bottom, a **skewer** of salami, cheese and a grape, and **breadsticks** standing up. Plan **1 jar per guest** with other food, **2** if it's the appetizer. Add crackers last.

Sturdier than a cup, and with a lid it's a take-home favor. Prefer cups? See [charcuterie cups](/blog/charcuterie-cups/).

## What goes in a charcuterie jar

| Layer | What to use | Per jar |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Bottom | Nuts, dried apricots, a few olives in a small paper liner, or chocolate-covered almonds | About 2 tablespoons |
| 2. The skewer | Folded [Genoa salami](/ingredients/genoa-salami/) or [prosciutto](/ingredients/prosciutto-di-parma/), 2 or 3 cheese cubes, a grape, olive or tomato | About 1 oz meat and 1 oz cheese |
| 3. Standing up | 2 breadsticks ([grissini](/ingredients/grissini/)), [pretzel rods](/blog/best-pretzels-for-charcuterie/) or 3 or 4 crackers | Added last |
| 4. Top | A sprig of rosemary, a few berries, a small piece of chocolate | For looks |

**Pick firm cheeses** that hold a cube: [aged cheddar](/ingredients/aged-cheddar/), [Manchego](/ingredients/manchego/), [Gouda](/ingredients/aged-gouda/), [pepper Jack](/ingredients/pepper-jack/) and mozzarella pearls. Soft brie squashes and smears the glass.

## Jar size

| Jar | Best for |
|---|---|
| 8 oz jelly or wide-mouth jar | Kids, a lighter snack, a dessert jar |
| 12 oz wide-mouth jar | The standard adult jar, with a 6-inch skewer |
| 16 oz wide-mouth jar | A heartier jar, or when the jars are dinner |

Wide-mouth jars are easier to fill and easier to eat from. Use 6-inch bamboo skewers for 12 oz jars and 8-inch skewers for 16 oz jars.

## Shopping list for 12, 24 or 50 jars

These follow the Lab standard (2 oz each of meat and cheese per guest before a meal), with one jar counted as half a serving, and 10% extra from 20 jars up.

| Item | 12 jars | 24 jars | 50 jars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salami or other cured meat | 12 oz | 1¾ lb | 3½ lb |
| Firm cheese (2 kinds) | 12 oz | 1¾ lb | 3½ lb |
| Grapes, cherry tomatoes or olives (for skewers) | 1 lb | 2 lb | 4 lb |
| Breadsticks or pretzel rods | 24 | 48 | 100 |
| Nuts or dried fruit (bottom layer) | 1½ cups | 3 cups | 6 cups |
| Wide-mouth jars (12 oz) | 12 | 24 | 50 |
| Skewers (6 inch) | 12 | 24 | 50 |

Mason jars are usually sold by the case. If you're making 50, ask friends to save theirs, or look for bulk packs in canning season.

## How to build them, step by step

1. **Set up an assembly line.** Line the jars up on a sheet pan and work one layer at a time across all of them.
2. **Fill the bottoms.** Add the nuts or dried fruit. If you use olives, put them in a small paper cupcake liner so the brine doesn't reach the crackers.
3. **Make the skewers.** Thread the folded meat, the cheese cubes and a grape or tomato onto each skewer. Fold salami in quarters so it holds its shape.
4. **Add the skewers.** Stand one in each jar, leaning against the side.
5. **Chill.** Cover the jars with lids or plastic wrap and refrigerate until about 30 minutes before serving.
6. **Add crackers last.** Stand the breadsticks or crackers up along the glass, away from anything wet, just before serving.
7. **Finish.** Tuck in a rosemary sprig. For favors, add a lid, a ribbon and a tag.

## 6 jar ideas

- **Bridal shower:** white cheddar, prosciutto, green grapes, white chocolate almonds, a sprig of baby's breath on the outside of the jar (not in the food).
- **Wedding cocktail hour:** Manchego, chorizo, Marcona almonds and a fig on the skewer, with a tag for each table.
- **Holiday:** white cheddar stars, salami, cranberries and rosemary for red, green and white.
- **Kids:** an 8 oz jar with mild cheddar, turkey pepperoni, grapes and pretzel sticks; no hard nuts for children under 4.
- **Game day:** pepper Jack, pepperoni, a cherry tomato and pretzel rods.
- **Dessert jar:** strawberries, brownie bites, marshmallows and a chocolate-dipped pretzel rod.

## Make-ahead timing and food safety

- **The day before:** cube the cheese, fold the meat and wash and fully dry the fruit. Store them separately.
- **The morning of:** fill the bottoms, make the skewers and put them in the jars. Lid and refrigerate.
- **Just before serving:** add crackers and breadsticks.
- **Once out:** serve within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. For a long event, put out half the jars and refill from the fridge.

For **pregnant guests**, make a few jars with pasteurized cheeses and crisped (cooked) prosciutto or pepperoni; see the [pregnancy-safe board guide](/blog/pregnancy-safe-charcuterie-board/).

## FAQ

**What is jarcuterie?**

Jarcuterie is charcuterie served in individual jars: a skewer of cured meat, cheese and fruit, a breadstick or crackers standing up, and nuts, olives or berries in the bottom. Each guest gets their own, so it's tidy and easy to carry around a party.

**What size jar is best for jarcuterie?**

A 12 to 16 oz wide-mouth mason jar fits a 6-inch skewer and breadsticks standing up. Use 8 oz jars for kids or for a lighter snack.

**How much meat and cheese goes in each jar?**

About 1 oz of meat and 1 oz of cheese per jar. Plan one jar per guest alongside other food, or two per guest if the jars are the whole appetizer.

**How far ahead can you make jarcuterie?**

Fill the jars the morning of the party, lid or cover them and refrigerate. Add crackers and breadsticks just before serving so they stay crisp, and serve within 2 hours.

**How do you keep crackers crisp in jarcuterie?**

Put dry items like nuts in the bottom, keep wet items like olives and berries on the skewer or in a small cup, and stand crackers and breadsticks up along the side so they don't touch anything wet. Add them last.
