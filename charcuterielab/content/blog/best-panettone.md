<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to serve panettone on a board</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Cut it:</strong> slice the loaf into wedges, then into finger-size strips. Toast them lightly if the loaf is a few days old.</li><li><strong>Pair it with</strong> mascarpone, Gorgonzola dolce, Parmigiano-Reggiano and thin prosciutto.</li><li><strong>Add</strong> honey, orange marmalade, dark chocolate and toasted hazelnuts.</li><li><strong>Drink:</strong> Moscato d'Asti, prosecco or espresso.</li><li><strong>How much:</strong> a 2 lb loaf gives about 12 slices; cut into strips, it covers a board for 12 to 16 guests.</li></ul>

## Panettone questions

**What's the best panettone brand?**

Olivieri 1882 won Wirecutter's, Food & Wine's and The Kitchn's tests. Under $30, try Ferrara Bakery or Madi.

**What's the difference between panettone and pandoro?**

Panettone has raisins and candied citrus; pandoro is a plain, star-shaped golden bread dusted with powdered sugar.

**What cheese goes with panettone?**

Mascarpone, Gorgonzola dolce and Parmigiano-Reggiano. Creamy or salty cheese balances the sweet bread.

**How long does panettone last?**

Artisan loaves taste best within a few weeks; supermarket loaves carry best-by dates months out. Once opened, keep it wrapped airtight.

**Why is good panettone so expensive?**

It takes days of natural sourdough rising, plus lots of butter and egg yolk. Cheaper loaves use faster methods.
