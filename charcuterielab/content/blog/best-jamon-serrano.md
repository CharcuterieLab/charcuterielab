<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to read a Spanish ham label</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Serrano</strong> comes from white pigs. The Consorcio Serrano seal guarantees Spanish origin and at least 12 months of curing.</li><li><strong>Ibérico</strong> comes from black Iberian pigs and is graded by seal color: black (100% Ibérico, acorn-fed), red (acorn-fed, mixed breed), green (free-range, grain-fed) and white (grain-fed).</li><li><strong>"Pata negra"</strong> may only be used on the black-seal grade.</li><li><strong>On the board:</strong> take packs out 30 minutes early, serve at room temperature and lay slices flat or in loose folds. About 1 oz per guest when it's one of several meats.</li></ul>

## Spanish ham questions

**What's the difference between serrano and Ibérico?**

Serrano is made from white pigs. Ibérico comes from black Iberian pigs, at least 50% Iberian breed, and is graded by seal color from white (grain-fed) to black (100% Ibérico, acorn-fed bellota).

**Serrano vs prosciutto: what's the difference?**

Both are dry-cured hams from white pigs. Serrano is usually cured longer, so it's drier, deeper in color and stronger in flavor. Prosciutto di Parma is softer and sweeter.

**How do I serve jamón on a charcuterie board?**

At room temperature: take the pack out about 30 minutes early. Lay the slices flat or in loose folds rather than stacked, and pair them with Manchego, Marcona almonds and olives.

**How much jamón per person?**

About 1 oz per guest when it's one of two or three meats. A 14 lb bone-in leg serves about 20 people as a main appetizer.

**Does jamón need to be refrigerated?**

Sliced packs, yes: keep them cold until 30 minutes before serving. A whole leg on a stand is kept at room temperature, covered with its own fat or a cloth, and used up within a few weeks.
