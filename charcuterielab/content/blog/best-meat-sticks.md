:::html
<h2 id="what-to-look-for">What to look for</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Look for:</strong> The first ingredient should be beef (or pork). Skip sticks made with mechanically separated chicken: Sporked disqualified them, and The Takeout ranked Slim Jim last for it.</li><li class="sk-x"><strong>Skip:</strong> Using meat sticks as your only meat. Pair them with one sliced cured meat, like salami or summer sausage, so the board doesn&#x27;t read as a snack bag.</li></ul>
:::

**Serving it:** cut sticks in half and stand them upright in a small cup or jar, or fan them across the board next to cheese cubes. One or two sticks per guest is plenty alongside other meats.

<h2 id="faq">Meat stick questions</h2>
:::

**What is the best meat stick brand?**

Chomps. It won both Sporked's and The Takeout's tests for its beefy flavor, firm texture and short ingredient list.

**Are meat sticks good on a charcuterie board?**

Yes, for casual boards like game day, kids' boards and hiking. Pair them with at least one sliced cured meat.

**Do meat sticks need to be refrigerated?**

Most are shelf-stable until opened. Check the package.

**What should I avoid in a meat stick?**

Mechanically separated chicken and long lists of fillers. Beef should be the first ingredient.
