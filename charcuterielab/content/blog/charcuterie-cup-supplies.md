<h2 id="how-to-choose">How much to buy, and how to keep cups safe</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Cups:</strong> one per guest, plus 10 percent for spares.</li><li><strong>Picks:</strong> 1 to 3 per cup. Foxes Love Lemons uses 8 to 24 skewers for 8 cups.</li><li><strong>What goes in each 9 oz cup:</strong> a skewer or two of folded salami and cheese, 2 or 3 crackers or a breadstick, a few grapes or berries, and a sprig of rosemary.</li><li><strong>Build ahead:</strong> up to a day early, chilled and covered; add crackers and breadsticks just before serving so they stay crisp.</li><li><strong>Food safety:</strong> meat and cheese shouldn't sit out more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90&deg;F). ## Charcuterie cup supply questions

**What size cups are best for charcuterie cups?**

9 oz clear plastic cups are the most common; 9 to 12 oz gives skewers room to stand.

**What size skewers for charcuterie cups?**

About 4 to 6 inches, so they stand roughly 2 inches above the rim.

**How far ahead can I make charcuterie cups?**

Up to a day ahead, covered and chilled. Add crackers just before serving.

**What paper do you use for a grazing table?**

Food-grade, uncoated butcher paper, white or kraft brown, 18 to 24 inches wide.

**What do I need to sell charcuterie cups?**

Clear dome cups with lids, labels with ingredients and allergens, and a cooler. Check your local cottage food rules first.
