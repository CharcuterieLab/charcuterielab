<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to choose</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Serving straight from the jar?</strong> Buy marinated. Water-packed hearts taste flat until you dress them.</li><li><strong>Read the oil.</strong> Most US jars use sunflower or canola oil, not olive oil. Country of origin (often Peru or Spain) is in small type.</li><li><strong>Quartered beats whole</strong> for a board: one bite, no knife.</li><li><strong>Drain them well</strong> and pat dry so the oil doesn't run into the crackers. Serve in a small bowl with picks.</li></ul><p class="sk-note">How much: about 2 oz per guest when they're one of several antipasti. ## Artichoke heart questions

**Marinated or water-packed artichoke hearts for a charcuterie board?**

Marinated, if you're serving them straight from the jar: the top two in Sporked's test were both marinated. Water-packed hearts are milder and work if you add your own olive oil, lemon and herbs.

**Should you drain jarred artichoke hearts?**

Yes. Drain marinated hearts well and pat them dry so oil doesn't run into the crackers and cheese. Rinse water-packed hearts to cut the canned taste.

**How long do artichoke hearts last after opening?**

Refrigerate them after opening, covered in their liquid, and use a clean fork. Mezzetta says its opened products keep anywhere from 5 days to 3 months depending on the product, so follow your jar's label, and throw it out if it smells off or shows mold.

**How many artichoke hearts per person?**

About 2 oz per guest when they're one of several antipasti. A 12 oz jar covers about 6 people, and a 24 oz jar about 12.

**Are jarred artichoke hearts healthy?**

They keep much of the fiber and vitamins of fresh artichokes, but they're higher in sodium, and the marinade adds fat. Rinsing water-packed hearts lowers the salt.
