<h2 id="how-to-choose">Romano vs Pecorino Romano, and how to serve it</h2>
<ul class="sk-checks"><li><strong>Read the label.</strong> &ldquo;Pecorino Romano&rdquo; is Italian sheep's milk. Plain &ldquo;Romano&rdquo; made in the US is cow's milk, younger and much milder; ATK's tasters preferred every import.</li><li><strong>On a board</strong> break it into thin shards, under 1 oz per person, and put honey, pears or fig jam right next to it.</li><li><strong>For nibbling</strong> buy Pecorino Toscano; for grating over pasta or a board salad, Romano.</li><li><strong>Drink:</strong> Chianti, Montepulciano d'Abruzzo or a dry Lambrusco.</li><li><strong>Storage:</strong> wrap in cheese paper. Hard cheeses like this keep for weeks in the fridge.</li></ul>

## Pecorino Romano questions

**What is the best Pecorino Romano brand?**

Boar's Head won America's Test Kitchen's blind tasting, with Locatelli, Zerto and Fulvi close behind. Kirkland Signature at Costco is the best value.

**Is Locatelli real Pecorino Romano?**

Yes. Locatelli is a brand of imported sheep's-milk Pecorino Romano DOP from Sardinia, not a different cheese.

**What's the difference between Romano and Pecorino Romano?**

Pecorino Romano is a protected Italian sheep's-milk cheese, usually aged 8 months or more. American Romano is usually cow's milk, younger and milder.

**Is Pecorino Romano good on a charcuterie board?**

In small amounts, yes: thin shards with honey or pears. For a milder slicing cheese, use Pecorino Toscano.

**How much Pecorino Romano per person?**

Less than 1 oz per person. It's very salty.
