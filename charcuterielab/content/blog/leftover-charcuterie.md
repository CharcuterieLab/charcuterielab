# What to Do With Leftover Charcuterie: 12 Ideas (and How Long It Keeps)

> **Quick Answer:** Safe if it went back in the fridge **within 2 hours**. Then crisp the meat for **pasta, eggs or salad**, melt the cheese into **grilled cheese or mac and cheese**, or top a **flatbread**. Sliced meat keeps 3–5 days.

## First: what's safe to keep

Before you plan the leftovers, think back to how long the board sat out.

- **Under 2 hours** (or under 1 hour in a hot room above 90°F): wrap everything and refrigerate. ([USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety))
- **Over 2 hours:** throw away the soft cheese, sliced meat, cut fruit and dips. Whole hard cheese and whole dry salami are sturdier, but if you're unsure, toss it.

Then split the board up. Don't refrigerate it assembled: crackers go soft and fruit juice soaks into the cheese. Our guide to [storing a charcuterie board](/blog/how-to-store-charcuterie-board/) shows how.

## How long leftovers keep in the fridge

| Leftover | How long | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dry salami, pepperoni (opened) | About 3 weeks | ([University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/preserving-and-preparing/storage-times-food-refrigerator-and-freezer)) |
| Sliced deli meat, sliced prosciutto | 3 to 5 days | Treat like opened lunch meat ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts)) |
| Hard cheese (cheddar, Parmesan, Gouda) | Up to about 4 weeks | Rewrap in fresh paper ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/can-you-freeze-cheese)) |
| Soft and fresh cheese (brie, mozzarella, goat cheese) | About a week | Burrata only a day or two |
| Cut fruit | 2 to 3 days | Berries and grapes go first |
| Crackers | 1 to 2 weeks in an airtight bag | Re-crisp in a 300°F oven for 5 minutes |
| Dips and spreads | Per the jar, or 3 to 4 days if homemade | |

For the right way to wrap each cheese, see [how to store cheese](/blog/cheese-storage/).

## 12 ideas for leftover charcuterie

### For the cured meat

**1. Crispy salami or prosciutto topping.** Lay slices on a sheet pan and bake at 375°F for 8 to 12 minutes, or crisp them in a dry skillet. Crumble over pasta, salads, soup or eggs. It's the fastest way to use up a few slices.

**2. Charcuterie pasta.** Sauté chopped salami or soppressata with garlic and chili flakes, toss with pasta, a splash of pasta water and grated Parmesan or pecorino. Finish with crispy prosciutto on top.

**3. Antipasto pasta salad.** Cubed salami and hard cheese, olives, marinated artichokes, cherry tomatoes and rotini in an Italian vinaigrette. It uses up half the board in one bowl.

**4. Flatbread or pizza night.** Store-bought naan or pizza dough, a smear of fig jam or tomato sauce, torn mozzarella or brie, salami and a handful of arugula after baking.

### For the cheese

**5. Fromage fort.** The classic French answer to leftover cheese: blend about 1 lb of mixed cheese ends with a clove of garlic, ¼ cup of dry white wine and a pinch of pepper until creamy. Spread it on toast and broil until bubbling.

**6. Grilled cheese or panini.** Brie with fig jam and prosciutto, cheddar with apple slices, or Gouda with salami. Leftover jam and mustard from the board go right in.

**7. Mac and cheese.** Grate every hard and semi-hard cheese you have into the sauce. A mix of cheddar, Gruyère and Gouda is better than any single cheese.

**8. Frittata or quiche.** Eggs, a little milk, crumbled cheese, chopped salami and any leftover vegetables or roasted peppers. Bake until just set.

**9. Baked brie, round two.** A half wheel of brie still bakes beautifully: top with leftover jam and nuts and bake at 350°F for about 10 minutes. See our [baked brie recipe](/blog/baked-brie-recipe/).

### For everything else

**10. Olive tapenade.** Blend leftover olives with a little olive oil, garlic, lemon and capers for a spread.

**11. Fruit compote or smoothies.** Simmer soft grapes, berries and figs with a spoonful of honey for a compote over yogurt or pancakes, or freeze them for smoothies.

**12. Cracker crumbs.** Crush stale crackers for a crunchy topping on mac and cheese or casseroles. Leftover nuts go into granola or salads.

## Can you freeze leftover charcuterie?

Some of it. Dry salami freezes for 1 to 2 months, and hard cheese for up to about 6 months, though it turns crumbly and is best for cooking. Soft cheese, cut fruit and crackers don't freeze well. Our guide to [freezing cheese and salami](/blog/can-you-freeze-cheese/) has the details.

## FAQ

**Is leftover charcuterie safe to eat?**

Yes, if it went back in the fridge within 2 hours of being set out (1 hour above 90°F). Anything perishable left out longer should be thrown away.

**How long does leftover charcuterie last in the fridge?**

Opened dry salami keeps about 3 weeks. Sliced deli meat and prosciutto keep 3 to 5 days. Hard cheese keeps up to about 4 weeks once opened, and soft fresh cheese about a week.

**What can I make with leftover cheese?**

Fromage fort (blended cheese spread with wine and garlic), mac and cheese, grilled cheese, a frittata or quiche, or a cheese sauce. Mixing several cheeses works well in all of them.

**What can I make with leftover salami and prosciutto?**

Crisp them in a pan or oven and crumble over pasta, salads or eggs, or use them on pizza, flatbread, panini and in an antipasto pasta salad.

**Can I freeze leftover charcuterie?**

Dry salami and hard cheese freeze reasonably well for 1 to 2 months and up to about 6 months respectively, though cheese turns crumbly and is best for cooking. Soft cheese and crackers don't freeze well.
