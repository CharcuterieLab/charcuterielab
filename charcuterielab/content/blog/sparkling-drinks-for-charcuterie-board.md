# Sparkling Drinks for a Charcuterie Board: Wine, Cider and Sparkling Water

> **Quick Answer:** The best all-round sparkling drink for a charcuterie board is **dry sparkling wine**: Champagne, Cava or Crémant. For salami and mortadella, try dry **Lambrusco**. For blue cheese and dessert, **Moscato d'Asti**. Without alcohol, **sparkling cider** or a **mineral sparkling water** does the same job.

Bubbles are the most useful thing you can pour with a board. Carbonation and acid scrub fat and salt off your tongue, so every bite of cheese or salami tastes fresh. That's why one bottle of bubbly suits a board full of very different foods.

## Which sparkling drink, for which board

| Sparkling drink | Best with | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Champagne, Cava, Crémant** (dry, *Brut*) | Almost everything: [brie](/pairings/food/brie/), aged cheese, [prosciutto](/pairings/food/prosciutto-di-parma/), fried and salty snacks | High acid and fine bubbles; toasty flavors suit aged cheese. |
| **Prosecco** | [Prosciutto](/pairings/food/prosciutto-di-parma/), mild cheese, fruit, [burrata](/pairings/food/burrata/) | Softer and fruitier; less good with strong, aged cheese. |
| **Dry Lambrusco** (red, *secco*) | [Salami](/pairings/food/genoa-salami/), [mortadella](/pairings/food/mortadella/), [Parmigiano](/pairings/food/parmigiano-reggiano/) | Fizzy red with a little tannin: the local wine with Emilia's meats and cheese. |
| **Sparkling rosé** | Mixed boards, [goat cheese](/pairings/food/fresh-chevre/), salami | Red-fruit flavor with white-wine freshness. |
| **[Moscato d'Asti](/pairings/wine/moscato/)** | [Blue cheese](/pairings/food/blue-cheese/), fruit, dessert boards | Sweet, lightly fizzy, about 5.5% alcohol. |
| **Dry [hard cider](/pairings/beer/hard-cider/)** | [Brie](/pairings/food/brie/), [Camembert](/pairings/food/camembert/), cheddar, ham | Apple acidity works like a slice of apple on the board. |
| **Sparkling cider** (no alcohol) | Brie, cheddar, ham, kids' boards | Apple and bubbles; the safest alcohol-free pick. |
| **Non-alcoholic sparkling wine** | Brie, prosciutto, smoked salmon | The closest thing to Champagne without alcohol. |
| **Sparkling water** | Everything, between bites | Bubbles without flavor: the palate cleanser. |

## Champagne, Cava, Crémant or Prosecco?

- **Champagne** is the toastiest and most complex. Lovely with aged cheese and fried snacks, but it costs the most. Good bottles start under $30: see [real Champagne under $30](/blog/best-champagne-under-30/).
- **Cava** (Spain) and **Crémant** (France, outside Champagne) are made the same way, bottle-fermented, for about half the price. They're the best value for a party.
- **Prosecco** (Italy) is usually made in big tanks, so it's fruitier, softer and lighter. Great with prosciutto and fruit; less so with strong cheese. See the [best Prosecco](/blog/best-prosecco/).

### How dry? Read the label

| Label | How it tastes | On a board |
|---|---|---|
| Brut Nature, Extra Brut | Bone dry | Oysters, salty cheese, fried snacks |
| **Brut** | Dry | The safe choice for any board |
| Extra Dry | Slightly sweet (sweeter than Brut) | Fruit, prosciutto, mild cheese |
| Demi-Sec, Dolce | Sweet | Blue cheese and dessert |

## Sparkling drinks without alcohol

- **Sparkling cider** is the easiest swap; see the [best sparkling cider](/blog/best-sparkling-cider/).
- **Non-alcoholic sparkling wine** has come a long way; see the [best non-alcoholic wine](/blog/best-non-alcoholic-wine/).
- **Sparkling water** works with everything. A strongly carbonated, mineral water (Topo Chico, Gerolsteiner) stands up to rich cheese and salami. Softer bubbles (San Pellegrino) suit mild cheese and fruit. Add a slice of lemon, orange or a few cranberries.
- **Ginger beer and kombucha** are tart and a little sweet, which suits goat cheese, spicy salami and fruit.

More ideas: [zero-proof pairings](/pairings/zero-proof/) and [non-alcoholic charcuterie pairings](/blog/non-alcoholic-charcuterie-pairings/).

## How to serve it

- **Chill it well:** about 40–45°F. Warm bubbles foam over and taste flat. An hour in a bucket of ice and water does it.
- **Glasses:** flutes keep the bubbles longest; a white-wine glass shows more flavor. Either is fine.
- **How much:** a 750 ml bottle pours about six flutes. For a party where sparkling is the main drink, plan about half a bottle per person.
- **Open it safely:** hold the cork, turn the bottle, and point it away from people.

**Please drink responsibly.** Put out water, and make sure there's a good alcohol-free option.

## FAQ

**What sparkling drink goes best with a charcuterie board?**

Dry sparkling wine: Champagne, Cava or Crémant. Its bubbles and acid cut through fat and salt, so it suits almost every cheese and cured meat on the board.

**Is Prosecco good with charcuterie?**

Yes, especially with prosciutto, mild cheese and fruit. It's softer and fruitier than Champagne, so it's less good with strong aged cheese.

**What sparkling water goes with a cheese board?**

A strongly carbonated, mineral water like Topo Chico or Gerolsteiner for rich cheese and salami; softer bubbles like San Pellegrino for mild cheese. Add a slice of lemon or orange.

**What does Brut mean on sparkling wine?**

Dry. Brut is the most common dry style. Confusingly, Extra Dry is a little sweeter than Brut, and Demi-Sec is noticeably sweet.

**Is there a red sparkling wine for charcuterie?**

Yes: dry Lambrusco from Emilia-Romagna. It's fizzy, fruity and a little tannic, and it's the local wine with mortadella, prosciutto and Parmigiano.
