# Christmas Wine and Cheese Pairings: What to Pour With Every Holiday Board

> **Quick Answer:** If you buy one bottle, make it **dry sparkling wine** (Champagne, Cava or Crémant). Then add **port for Stilton** and a fruity, soft red like **Zinfandel or Pinot Noir** for ham and aged cheddar. Pour **Moscato** with the dessert board.

Christmas boards are richer than the rest of the year: blue cheese, baked brie, glazed ham, chocolate, cranberries. The wines that work are the ones with bubbles, sweetness or ripe fruit. Big, tannic reds are the ones that go wrong.

## What to pour, from Christmas Eve to dessert

**Christmas Eve seafood.** For a [Feast of the Seven Fishes board](/blog/feast-of-the-seven-fishes-board/), pour Champagne, Cava or a crisp [Pinot Grigio](/pairings/wine/pinot-grigio/). Skip reds; tannin and fish taste metallic together.

**Christmas morning.** Prosecco, on its own or with orange juice, suits a [Christmas morning board](/blog/christmas-morning-charcuterie-board/) of pastries, fruit and ham. Sparkling cider for the kids.

**The big board before dinner.** Open sparkling wine first; it goes with everything. Then put out one red and one sweet bottle: a fruity [Zinfandel](/pairings/wine/zinfandel/) or [Pinot Noir](/pairings/wine/pinot-noir/), and [port](/pairings/wine/port/) for the blue cheese.

**Dessert board.** [Moscato](/pairings/wine/moscato/) with cookies, fruit and biscotti; port with dark chocolate and Stilton. See the [Christmas cookie board](/blog/christmas-cookie-charcuterie-board/).

**Cold night, warm drink.** [Mulled wine](/blog/best-mulled-wine/) suits spiced nuts, gingerbread and aged cheddar. It's too sweet and hot for delicate cheese.

## A three-bottle Christmas plan

If you want one plan for a mixed crowd of 8 to 10:

1. **One sparkling:** Champagne for a splurge, Cava or Crémant for value. See [real Champagne under $30](/blog/best-champagne-under-30/) and the [best Prosecco](/blog/best-prosecco/).
2. **One red:** Zinfandel for ham and cheddar, or Pinot Noir if the board has more soft cheese.
3. **One sweet:** port for Stilton and chocolate. See the [best port wine](/blog/best-port-wine/).

Plus one alcohol-free sparkling option, like [sparkling cider](/blog/best-sparkling-cider/) or a [non-alcoholic sparkling wine](/blog/best-non-alcoholic-wine/).

## Wines that are hard to pair at Christmas

- **Very oaky, tannic reds** (a big young Cabernet or Malbec): they make brie, goat cheese and fish taste bitter. Save them for aged cheddar and Parmigiano.
- **Light, dry whites with strong cheese:** a delicate white tastes like water next to Stilton.
- **Sweet wine with sweeter food:** if the dessert is sweeter than the wine, the wine tastes sour. Keep port and Moscato for cheese, fruit and dark chocolate, not frosted cookies.

## How much to buy

Plan about **half a bottle per drinking guest** for a two- to three-hour party. A 750 ml bottle pours about five glasses of still wine, a little less sparkling. For port, one bottle covers 15 or more small pours. Buy one extra sparkling bottle; it's the first to run out, and an unopened one keeps for next time.

**Please drink responsibly.** Put water out with the wine, and plan a ride home for anyone who needs one. More ideas for the whole season: [Christmas boards](/holidays/christmas/) and the [charcuterie board wine pairing guide](/blog/wine-and-charcuterie-board/).

## FAQ

**What wine goes with a Christmas cheese board?**

Dry sparkling wine if you only buy one bottle: Champagne, Cava or Crémant suits almost every cheese and meat. Add port for Stilton and a fruity red like Zinfandel or Pinot Noir for ham and aged cheddar.

**What is the classic Christmas cheese and wine pairing?**

Stilton with port. It's the traditional British Christmas pairing: sweet, strong wine against salty, creamy blue.

**What wine goes with Christmas ham on a board?**

Zinfandel or Pinot Noir for red, off-dry Riesling for white. Fruity, soft wines suit sweet, salty glazed ham.

**How much wine do I need for a Christmas party?**

Plan about half a bottle per drinking guest for a two- to three-hour party. A 750 ml bottle pours about five glasses.

**What can non-drinkers have with a Christmas cheese board?**

Sparkling cider, a dry non-alcoholic sparkling wine, or sparkling water with cranberry and orange. They do the same job as Champagne: bubbles and acid cut the fat.
