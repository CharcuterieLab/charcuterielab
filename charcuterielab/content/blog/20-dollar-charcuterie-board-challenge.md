# The $20 Charcuterie Board Challenge: A Full Board for Four on a Budget

> **Quick Answer:** A $20 board for four works as an appetizer: 8 oz of cheese (a block of sharp cheddar plus a small soft cheese), 8 oz of salami, one box of crackers, seasonal fruit, a jar of pickles or olives, and a handful of nuts. Buy blocks, not pre-cut, and shop the store brand. Prices vary, so treat our totals as an estimate.

Here is the challenge: build a full-looking board for four for about $20, take a photo and share it with **#CharcuterieLab20**. Below is how we did it, the rules that keep the cost down, and how to join in.

## The rules of the challenge

1. **$20 total**, before tax, from one grocery run.
2. **Feeds four as an appetizer**: 2 oz each of meat and cheese per person.
3. **Pantry items count as free**: salt, honey, herbs from the garden, anything you already have.
4. **Post a photo** with the total and tag it #CharcuterieLab20.

## Our $20 shopping list

Prices below are **rough estimates** for a regular US supermarket in fall 2026. They are not from a real receipt, and your store will differ. Use them as a guide.

| Item | Amount | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|
| Store-brand sharp cheddar, block | 8 oz | $3 to $4 |
| Small brie or soft cheese wedge | 4 to 8 oz | $4 to $5 |
| Salami, sliced or a small stick | 6 to 8 oz | $4 to $5 |
| Store-brand crackers | 1 box | $2 to $3 |
| Seasonal fruit (grapes, apples or clementines) | About 1 lb | $2 to $3 |
| Pickles or olives | 1 small jar | $2 to $3 |
| **Estimated total** | | **About $17 to $23** |

If your total runs high, drop the soft cheese and buy a second block cheese, or swap the olives for a pickle you already have.

## Five rules that keep the cost down

**1. Buy blocks and cut them yourself.** Pre-cut cubes and slices cost more per ounce, and they dry out faster. The [budget board guide](/blog/budget-charcuterie-board/) has more cheap cheese picks.

**2. One salami goes a long way.** Fold slices into quarters and they cover twice the space of flat slices. A salami river makes one package look like plenty. The [best meats guide](/blog/best-meats-charcuterie-board/) shows which ones stretch furthest.

![Salami folded into an S-shaped river between cheddar wedges and brie on a small round board](/images/20-dollar-charcuterie-board-challenge-salami-river.webp)

**3. Buy fruit that is in season.** Apples, pears, grapes and clementines are usually cheapest in fall and winter. Berries are usually cheapest in summer.

**4. Let the pantry do the work.** A drizzle of honey, a few sprigs of rosemary or a sprinkle of flaky salt costs nothing if you already have it and makes the board look finished.

**5. Fill space with cheap crunch.** Pretzels, popcorn and sliced cucumbers fill gaps for very little. Crackers and fruit should take up more of the board than meat and cheese. A sliced day-old baguette works too; see [what bread to use](/blog/what-bread-for-charcuterie-board/).

## How to make $20 look like $50

- Use a smaller board. A full small board looks more generous than a half-empty large one.
- Cut cheese in two or three shapes: wedges, cubes and crumbles.
- Fan crackers in curved lines instead of stacking them.
- Add height with a small bowl of olives or a jar of pickles.
- Finish with something green, like herbs or cucumber slices.
