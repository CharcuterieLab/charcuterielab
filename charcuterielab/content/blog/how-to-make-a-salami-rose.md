# How to Make a Salami Rose (Step-by-Step with Photos)

> **Quick Answer:** Fold thin salami slices over the rim of a wine glass, half inside and half outside, with each slice overlapping the last by about half. Build **three layers, about 10 to 14 slices**, each layer a little lower and turned a little so the gaps don't line up. Press the slices down into the glass, flip it upside down onto the board and lift. Use **thin Genoa or hard salami about 3 to 4 inches across**, and let it sit out 10 minutes first so it bends without cracking.

A salami rose is the fastest way to make a grocery-store board look like it came from a caterer. It takes about two minutes, it uses salami you were going to put on the board anyway, and it gives the board a focal point that pulls the eye in. The method below is the wine-glass method most people mean when they say "salami rose." There's a no-glass method further down for small boards and charcuterie cups.

![A finished salami rose made from thin Genoa salami, sitting on a wooden charcuterie board beside cheese and basil leaves](/images/salami-rose-finished.jpg)

## What you need

![A wine glass, a fanned stack of thinly sliced Genoa salami and a small wooden board laid out on a marble counter](/images/salami-rose-step-0-what-you-need.jpg)

| Glass | Salami | Slices | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wine glass (rim about 3 in) | Genoa or hard salami, 3 to 4 in across | 10 to 14 | A full, open rose about 3½ in wide |
| Champagne flute | Genoa or large pepperoni | 6 to 8 | A tall, tight rosebud |
| Shot glass | Pepperoni or small salami rounds | 6 to 8 | A mini rose for cups and small boards |

A wine glass with a thin, round rim works best. Avoid glasses with a thick lip or a flared rim, because the slices slide off before you can press them in. A clean, dry glass matters too. Any water on the glass makes the slices slip.

### The best salami for roses

- **Genoa salami** is the easiest. It's mild, wide and naturally pliable when sliced thin. See [Genoa salami](/ingredients/genoa-salami/) and [Genoa vs hard salami](/blog/genoa-salami-vs-hard-salami/).
- **Hard salami** works too. It's a little drier and holds a sharper petal.
- **Pepperoni** makes small roses on a flute or shot glass. Large deli-sliced pepperoni works on a wine glass.
- **Avoid thick-cut salami.** [Soppressata](/ingredients/soppressata/) and other coarse, thick slices split when you bend them over the rim.

Ask the deli counter to slice it **thin, about as thin as a coin**, or buy pre-sliced thin rounds. If you're choosing a brand, our [best salami brands](/blog/best-salami-brands/) ranking notes which pre-sliced packs are thin enough.

## How to make a salami rose with a wine glass

### Step 1: Let the salami warm up for 10 minutes

Take the salami out of the fridge about **10 minutes** before you start. Straight-from-the-fridge slices are stiff and crack at the fold. Slightly warmer slices bend over the rim cleanly. Separate the slices into a loose stack so you can grab them one at a time.

### Step 2: Fold the first slice over the rim

![One slice of salami folded over the rim of a wine glass, half hanging inside the glass and half outside](/images/salami-rose-step-1-first-slice.jpg)

Lay one slice across the rim so that **half of it hangs inside the glass and half hangs outside**. Press it gently so it hugs the rim. This is your first petal.

### Step 3: Finish the first layer

![Five salami slices folded around the full rim of a wine glass, each overlapping the one before by about half](/images/salami-rose-step-2-first-layer.jpg)

Add the next slice so it **overlaps the first by about half**, and keep going around the rim. It takes **4 to 5 slices** to go all the way around. Tuck the last slice under the first one so the circle closes without a gap.

### Step 4: Add the second layer, a little lower

![A second layer of salami slices added to the wine glass, sitting slightly lower than the first and turned so the slices cover the gaps](/images/salami-rose-step-3-second-layer.jpg)

Start the second layer **about half an inch lower** than the first, so the top edge of each slice sits just below the rim. Turn it so each new slice covers a seam from the first layer. Use **4 to 5 slices** again, overlapping by half.

### Step 5: Add the third layer to make the center

![Looking down into the wine glass: three layers of salami folded around the rim, spiraling tightly into the center](/images/salami-rose-step-4-third-layer.jpg)

Add **2 to 4 more slices** even lower, folding them more tightly into the middle of the glass. This layer becomes the tight center of the rose. Look straight down into the glass: you should see a spiral with no hole in the middle. If there's a gap, tuck in one more slice.

### Step 6: Press it down

![Fingers pressing the folded salami slices firmly down into the wine glass](/images/salami-rose-step-5-press.jpg)

Press the slices **firmly down into the glass** with two fingers, working all the way around. This packs the layers together so the rose holds its shape once it's out of the glass. Don't skip this step. It's the difference between a rose and a pile of salami.

### Step 7: Flip and lift

![The wine glass turned upside down on a wooden board, with salami petals visible through the glass](/images/salami-rose-step-6-flip.jpg)

Turn the glass **upside down onto the board** exactly where you want the rose. Hold it there for a few seconds, then lift the glass straight up with a slight twist.

![The wine glass being lifted away to reveal the salami rose standing on the board](/images/salami-rose-step-7-lift.jpg)

### Step 8: Open the petals

![Fingertips gently pulling the outer salami petals open so the rose looks fuller](/images/salami-rose-step-8-fluff.jpg)

Gently pull the **outer petals** open and outward with your fingertips, one at a time. Leave the center tight. A few fresh [basil](/ingredients/fresh-basil/) leaves or a sprig of [rosemary](/ingredients/rosemary-sprigs/) tucked under the base look like leaves and help hold it upright.

## How to make a salami rose without a glass

No wine glass, or making roses for [charcuterie cups](/blog/charcuterie-cups/)? The fold-and-roll method makes a smaller, tighter rose with nothing but your hands.

![Six salami slices folded into half-moons and laid in an overlapping row on a cutting board](/images/salami-rose-noglass-1-row.jpg)

1. **Fold** 6 to 8 thin slices in half to make half-moons.
2. **Line them up** in a row with the folded edges at the bottom and each half-moon overlapping the last by about half.

![Hands rolling the overlapping row of salami half-moons from one end into a tight coil](/images/salami-rose-noglass-2-roll.jpg)

3. **Roll** the row up tightly from one end, keeping the folded edge at the bottom.

![A small salami rosebud standing on its base, its petals opened slightly](/images/salami-rose-noglass-3-finished.jpg)

4. **Stand** it on its folded base and open the outer petals a little. A toothpick through the base holds it if it's going in a cup or on a skewer.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Slices crack at the fold | Too cold or too thick | Let them sit out 10 minutes; ask for thinner slices |
| Rose collapses when you lift the glass | Not pressed down, or too little overlap | Overlap each slice by half and press firmly before flipping |
| Hole in the middle | Third layer too loose | Add 1 or 2 slices folded tightly into the center |
| Slices slide off the rim | Wet glass or thick rim | Dry the glass; use a thin-rimmed wine glass |
| Rose looks flat | Too few slices | Use 12 to 14 slices on a wine glass, in three layers |
| Rose leans over | Uneven layers | Prop it against a cheese wedge, or tuck herbs under the base |

## How many roses to make

A salami rose is part of the meat on the board, not extra. One full rose is about **2 to 3 oz of salami**, so count it toward your total from [how much charcuterie per person](/blog/how-much-charcuterie-per-person/).

| Board | Roses | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| For 2 to 4 | 1 | Put it off-center, near the soft cheese |
| For 6 to 8 | 2 | Place them on opposite corners |
| For 10 to 12 | 3 | Group them like a bouquet, or spread them along a [salami river](/blog/salami-river/) |
| Grazing table | 1 per 4 to 6 guests | Mix salami roses with [prosciutto roses](/blog/prosciutto-rose/) for two colors |

Odd numbers look better on a board than even ones. For more layout rules, see [charcuterie board presentation](/blog/charcuterie-board-presentation/) and [the build order](/blog/build-sequence/).

## Making salami roses ahead

Build the roses in their glasses, cover each glass with plastic wrap and **refrigerate for up to 24 hours**. Flip them onto the board when you build it. Once the board is out, the usual rule applies: **no more than 2 hours at room temperature, or 1 hour above 90°F**. Leftover roses can be wrapped and refrigerated, and they'll flatten a little but taste the same.

## FAQ

**How many slices of salami do you need for a salami rose?**
A full rose on a standard wine glass takes 10 to 14 thin slices in three layers, about 2 to 3 oz of salami. A champagne flute takes 6 to 8 slices for a tighter bud, and a shot glass takes 6 to 8 slices of pepperoni for a mini rose.

**What is the best salami for a salami rose?**
Thinly sliced Genoa or hard salami about 3 to 4 inches across, sliced about as thin as a coin. Thin slices bend over the rim without cracking. Thick-cut salami and soppressata split when folded, so ask the deli to slice it thin or buy pre-sliced thin rounds.

**Can you make a salami rose without a glass?**
Yes. Fold 6 to 8 slices in half, line the half-moons up in a row with each one overlapping the last by about half, then roll the row up tightly from one end and stand it on its base. It makes a smaller, tighter rose and works well for cups and small boards.

**Why does my salami rose fall apart?**
Usually the slices are too thick, too cold or not overlapping enough. Use thin slices, let them sit out about 10 minutes so they bend, overlap each slice by about half, and press the layers down firmly inside the glass before you flip it.

**Can you make salami roses ahead of time?**
Yes. Build the roses in their glasses, cover the tops with plastic wrap and refrigerate them for up to 24 hours. Flip them onto the board just before serving and keep the board out no more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.
