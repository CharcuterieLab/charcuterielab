# Smorgasbord vs. Charcuterie Board: What's the Difference?

> **Quick Answer:** A **smorgasbord** is a Swedish buffet: a whole table of cold and hot dishes, eaten in courses and started with herring. A **charcuterie board** is one board of cured meat, cheese and snacks that people graze in any order. In English, "smorgasbord" also just means a big variety.

## Smorgasbord vs. charcuterie board at a glance

| | Smorgasbord | Charcuterie board |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Sweden | France (the word); the board is a modern American party format |
| What the word means | "Bread-and-butter table" | Prepared, cured meats (from *chair cuite*, cooked flesh) |
| Format | A whole table, served buffet-style | One board or platter |
| Hot food | Yes, usually the last savory course | No, it's all cold |
| Order | Courses: cold fish, other cold dishes, then hot dishes | Graze in any order |
| Signature foods | Pickled herring, gravlax, cold cuts, meatballs, potatoes, bread, butter, cheese | Salami, prosciutto, cheese, crackers, fruit, spreads |
| A meal or a snack? | A full meal | Usually a starter or party food |
| Classic drink | Snaps (aquavit) and beer | Wine, sparkling wine or beer |

## What a smorgasbord is

The word comes from Swedish *smörgås*, an open sandwich (bread and butter), and *bord*, a table. Bread, butter and cheese are always on it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smorgasbord)).

It's not a free-for-all. Swedes traditionally eat a smorgasbord in rounds, with a fresh plate each time:

1. **Cold fish:** pickled herring, salmon, eel.
2. **Other cold dishes:** cold meats, salads, eggs.
3. **Hot dishes:** meatballs, sausages, potatoes.
4. **Dessert,** if there's room.

It grew out of the *brännvinsbord*, a small standing table of bread, butter, cheese, herring and snaps that wealthy Swedish households served before dinner in the 1500s. By the mid-1600s it had moved onto the main table and added warm dishes.

### The Christmas version: julbord

At Christmas, the Swedish smorgasbord becomes the **julbord**: five to seven courses of herring, cold fish like [gravlax](/ingredients/gravlax/), hot fish, Christmas ham with mustard, meatballs and Jansson's temptation (a potato casserole), then cheese and rice pudding.

### How Americans met it

Most Americans first saw a smorgasbord at the **1939 New York World's Fair**, where the Swedish Pavilion's restaurant served one. Since then, English speakers have used "smorgasbord" for any big buffet, and for any large mix of things ("a smorgasbord of options").

## What a charcuterie board is

**Charcuterie** is French for prepared meats: salami, ham, pâté and the like. A **charcuterie board** puts a few of those meats on a board with cheese, crackers, fruit, nuts and spreads, for guests to graze from. It's cold, it's built ahead and nobody follows an order. See [what charcuterie actually means](/blog/what-is-charcuterie/) for more.

## Where they overlap

- **Both are cold-forward spreads to share.** A smorgasbord's cold courses look a lot like a big board: cured fish, sliced meat, cheese, bread and pickles.
- **A grazing table sits in between.** It covers a whole table like a smorgasbord, but it's still cold finger food like a board. See [grazing board vs. charcuterie board](/blog/grazing-board-vs-charcuterie-board/).
- **"Smorgasbord" is the casual word for variety.** If someone calls your huge board "a real smorgasbord," that's a compliment, not a correction.

## Make a smorgasbord-style board

Want the Swedish flavors without cooking a buffet? Build a board with a Nordic accent:

- **Cured fish:** gravlax or [smoked salmon](/ingredients/smoked-salmon/), and a small jar of pickled herring.
- **Meat:** thin-sliced ham, with a sweet-hot mustard.
- **Cheese:** [Jarlsberg](/ingredients/jarlsberg/) and an aged, crumbly hard cheese.
- **Bread:** rye crispbread, thin rye and butter (the *smörgås* part).
- **Sharp and fresh:** cucumber, dill, pickled red onion and boiled eggs.
- **Sweet:** lingonberry jam.

Serve it with cold beer, or aquavit for the traditional pairing. For a complete plan, see our [Scandinavian charcuterie board](/boards/scandinavian-charcuterie-board/).

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a smorgasbord and a charcuterie board?**

A smorgasbord is a Swedish buffet: a whole table of cold and hot dishes, traditionally eaten in courses starting with herring and ending with warm dishes. A charcuterie board is a single board of cured meats, cheese and snacks that guests graze from in any order.

**What does smorgasbord mean?**

It's Swedish: smörgås means an open sandwich, or bread and butter, and bord means table. So it's roughly a bread-and-butter table. In English it also means any large, varied selection.

**Is a smorgasbord the same as a buffet?**

A smorgasbord is a kind of buffet, the Swedish one, with a set order: cold fish first, then other cold dishes, then hot dishes. In English the word is often used for any big buffet.

**Is a grazing table a smorgasbord?**

Not quite, but it's the closest modern relative. Both cover a whole table. A grazing table is still cold, finger-food grazing, while a smorgasbord adds hot dishes and courses.

**What is a julbord?**

The Swedish Christmas smorgasbord. It's eaten in five to seven courses: pickled herring, cold fish like gravlax, hot fish, cold Christmas ham with mustard, warm dishes like meatballs, then cheese and rice pudding.
