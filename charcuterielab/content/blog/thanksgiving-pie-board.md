# Thanksgiving Pie Board: How to Build a Dessert Board of Pies

> **Quick Answer:** A Thanksgiving pie board is dessert served like a charcuterie board: three or four pies cut into small wedges, plus toppings and a few pairings guests can mix and match. Cut each 9-inch pie into 12 to 16 slim wedges instead of 8, so people can try two or three kinds. Plan on 3 pies for 12 guests, keep pumpkin and cream pies cold until the last minute, and don't leave them out for more than 2 hours.

## Why a pie board works

Everyone at Thanksgiving wants a little of every pie and nobody wants a full slice after dinner. Small wedges on a board solve both problems, and the board looks like a centerpiece instead of three pie tins on the counter.

## Choose 3 or 4 pies

Pick pies with different flavors and textures:

| Role | Classic pick | Swap |
|---|---|---|
| Spiced and creamy | Pumpkin | Sweet potato |
| Nutty and rich | Pecan | Chocolate pecan or bourbon pecan |
| Fruity | Apple | Cranberry-apple, pear or cherry |
| Wild card | Chocolate cream or French silk | Key lime, buttermilk chess |

Store-bought pies are fine. Bakery or warehouse-club pies cut cleanly when chilled, and nobody minds when they're arranged on a board.

## The toppings and extras

Put these in small bowls between the pies:

- **Whipped cream,** in a chilled bowl set over ice
- **Caramel sauce** for the apple and pecan
- **Candied pecans** or toasted walnuts
- **A wedge of aged cheddar** next to the apple pie. Apple pie with cheddar is an old New England pairing, and sharp cheese cuts the sweetness.
- **Dark chocolate squares.** See the [best dark chocolate for a board](/blog/best-dark-chocolate/).
- **Fresh fruit:** grapes, pear slices and pomegranate seeds for color
- **Small cookies or shortbread** to fill the gaps

## How much to buy

A pie board comes after a big meal, and guests take several small pieces rather than one big one. Plan on about 1½ standard slices per guest in total.

| Guests | Pies | Cut each pie into | Toppings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 | 12 wedges | 1 cup whipped cream, 1 small bowl each extra |
| 12 | 3 | 12 wedges | 2 cups whipped cream |
| 20 | 4 | 14 to 16 wedges | 3 cups whipped cream |
| 30 | 6 | 14 to 16 wedges | 4 to 5 cups whipped cream |

## How to build the board

1. **Chill the pies well.** Cold pie cuts into neat wedges. Use a long, thin knife and wipe it between cuts.
2. **Cut each pie into slim wedges.** For 12 cuts, slice it into quarters, then each quarter into three.
3. **Arrange each pie in its own section,** wedges overlapping in a fan or a row, with the crust edges facing out.
4. **Place the toppings** in small bowls between the pie sections.
5. **Fill the gaps** with fruit, cookies and nuts.
6. **Label the pies** with small cards, especially anything with nuts.

Build it on a board or a large platter lined with parchment. A wooden board works if you lay parchment under the pies so the filling doesn't soak in.

## Food safety

Pumpkin, sweet potato, cream and custard pies need to stay cold. Keep them in the fridge until you build the board, and put leftovers back within 2 hours. Fruit pies and pecan pie are more forgiving, but they still taste best served within a day.

## FAQ

**What is a pie board?**

Dessert served like a charcuterie board: several pies cut into small wedges, arranged with toppings like whipped cream, caramel, nuts and fruit so guests can try a little of each.

**How many pies do I need for 12 people?**

About 3 pies, each cut into 12 slim wedges. Guests take two or three small pieces after dinner.

**Can you put pumpkin pie on a board?**

Yes, but keep it in the fridge until you build the board and don't leave it out for more than 2 hours.

**What goes on a Thanksgiving dessert board besides pie?**

Whipped cream, caramel sauce, candied pecans, aged cheddar for the apple pie, dark chocolate, grapes, pear slices and small cookies.

**Can I use store-bought pies?**

Yes. Chill them well so they cut cleanly into small wedges.
