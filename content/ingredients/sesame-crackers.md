## The short version

A cracker crusted or studded with toasted sesame. It's nuttier than a plain
cracker and milder than a full multiseed one, which makes it the most flexible
"upgrade" cracker on a board. It's also the one most likely to be a problem for
a guest — sesame is a major allergen.

## What it is

Sesame crackers exist in two traditions that ended up in the same aisle. The
Middle Eastern and Armenian style is a thin whole-wheat flatbread crusted with
seeds. The Italian style is a small olive-oil cracker with sesame worked into
the dough.

**Taste:** nutty, toasty, faintly sweet
**Texture:** crisp with a fine seed crunch
**Look:** pale to golden, evenly freckled with seeds

Roasting is the mechanism. Raw sesame is nearly flavorless; toasted sesame
develops the same browned, nutty compounds that make coffee and bread crust
taste the way they do. A sesame cracker is essentially a delivery system for
that one note, which is why it flatters both hummus and aged gouda.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Hummus and tahini-based dips** | Sesame on sesame, deliberately. The cracker is made of the dip's main ingredient. |
| **Feta and halloumi** | Salty brined cheese against toasted nuttiness. The core Mediterranean board bite. |
| **Labneh with olive oil** | Sour, thick and plain — the cracker supplies everything else. |
| **Aged gouda** | Butterscotch and toasted sesame are close cousins on the flavor wheel. |
| **Medjool dates** | Sweet, sticky and dense needs something dry and nutty to break it up. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Borrowed from the sesame bagel, and it holds up. |
| **Honey and halva** | The dessert corner of a Middle Eastern board. |

**Skip it with:** not much — this is a genuinely versatile cracker. The real
caution isn't a flavor clash. **Sesame is one of the most common major
allergens**, and unlike nuts, people don't expect it in a cracker. If you don't
know your guests, label it or keep it in its own dish.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Label it or separate it.** Sesame allergies are common and rising. A small
   card, or its own dish at the edge, is worth the ten seconds.
2. **Break the large flatbread sheets by hand** into rough shards. The
   Armenian-style ones are meant to be broken, not served whole.
3. **Serve 3–4 per person.**
4. **Place them beside the hummus and the feta**, where the sesame echo does
   the most work.
5. **Buy small boxes.** Sesame oil oxidizes, and a stale sesame cracker tastes
   faintly bitter rather than just soft.
6. **Fix:** 3 minutes at 300°F re-toasts the seeds and sharpens the flavor
   noticeably. Worth doing even with a fresh box.

**Label check:** look for "toasted" sesame and visible seeds on the surface.
Seeds mixed into the dough give you color without much flavor.

**Skip:** sweet sesame snacks — sesame sticks and honey-sesame candies are a
different food and read as dessert on a savory board.

## Quick FAQ

**Are sesame crackers a common allergen?**
Yes. Sesame is now recognized as a major allergen in the US and must be
labeled. Set them apart or tell your guests.

**What cheese goes with sesame crackers?**
Feta, halloumi and aged gouda. Anything salty or caramel-noted.

**Are they gluten-free?**
Usually not — most are wheat-based. Rice-based sesame crackers exist and are
labeled as such.

**How many per person?**
3–4, and they work as a general-purpose base if you only buy one specialty
cracker.
