## The short version

Fresh goat cheese rolled in dried cranberries, sometimes with pistachios. It's
in every supermarket from October through January, it photographs well, and
everyone likes it. It's also the easiest cheese on this list to make yourself,
better and for half the price.

## What it is

The base is a standard fresh chèvre log — tangy, chalky, unaged. The coating is
sweetened dried cranberries, often chopped, sometimes with pistachios or
rosemary. Nothing about it is traditional; it's a modern retail product built
for the holiday cheese case.

**Taste:** tangy goat cheese with sweet-tart fruit and a chewy edge
**Texture:** soft and chalky inside, chewy and studded outside
**Look:** white log with a deep red and green crust

Acid against sugar is the mechanism, and it's the same one behind every good
goat-cheese pairing. Chèvre's brightness sharpens sweetness; sweetness rounds
the acid. What's usually assembled on the board — cheese, then a spoon of jam —
is here built into a single item, which is the entire commercial logic.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Water crackers** | Quiet and dry under a cheese that already brings sweet, tart and chewy. |
| **Honey** | More sweetness works because the goat cheese has so much acid to spare. |
| **Pistachios and walnuts** | Crunch and tannin. Often already in the coating. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and cure against sweet-tart fruit. Underrated. |
| **Fresh rosemary or thyme** | Herbal against sweet — and it's what makes the homemade version better. |
| **Sparkling wine, dry rosé, Sauvignon Blanc** | High acid. The goat cheese rule. |
| **Port and mulled wine** | Holiday drinks with a holiday cheese. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese, which flattens it, and everything crackers,
where garlic fights the fruit. Also skip putting two fruited cheeses on one
board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice with floss or wire**, not a knife — a blade drags the coating into
   the paste and greys the slice.
2. **Lay the discs flat so the red-and-white edge shows.** That cross-section
   is what you're serving.
3. **Temper 20 minutes.** Cold, the coating is hard and the cheese is mute.
4. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Make it yourself if you have ten minutes:** roll a plain chèvre log in
   chopped dried cranberries, pistachios and rosemary, pressing them on with a
   plate. It costs less and tastes fresher than any packaged version.
6. **Put it in the middle of the board as a color anchor** — red and white is
   the strongest color combination available in the cheese case.
7. **Fix:** if the coating is dry and falling off, press it back on with a
   little honey brushed on the log.

**Label check:** look at the base cheese quality, not the coating. A good
chèvre with plain cranberries beats a mediocre one with an elaborate crust.

**Skip:** the cinnamon-and-sugar versions on a savory board. Those belong on a
dessert board.

## Quick FAQ

**Should I just make it myself?**
Yes, if you have ten minutes. Roll a plain chèvre log in chopped dried
cranberries, pistachios and rosemary. It's fresher, cheaper and adjustable.

**How do I slice it without wrecking it?**
Floss or thin wire. A knife drags the coating through the white paste.

**Is it a savory or a dessert cheese?**
Savory, on the sweet end. The cinnamon versions tip into dessert.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.

**What's the best pairing?**
Water crackers and honey, or prosciutto if you want the salt contrast.
