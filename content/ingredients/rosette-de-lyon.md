## The short version

A large French dry sausage from Lyon, cured in a wide casing for three to six
months — much longer and fatter than ordinary saucisson. The slices come out
the size of a coaster, which makes it the most impressive-looking salami you can
put on a board.

## What it is

Rosette takes its name from the *rosette* — the wide end section of pig
intestine used as its casing. That casing is broad and thick, so the sausage
dries slowly and evenly over months rather than weeks. Coarse-cut pork shoulder
and back fat, salt, pepper, sometimes a little wine and sugar.

**Taste:** deep, nutty, gently tangy, less garlicky than saucisson
**Texture:** firm but tender, drier than salami, melting fat
**Look:** very large rounds, deep red with big white fat pieces, tied with
string

Slow drying in a wide casing is the mechanism. A thin sausage dries in weeks and
stays bright and lactic; a thick one dries over months, during which enzymes
break proteins down further. That's why rosette tastes rounder and nuttier than
fuet or ordinary saucisson at the same seasoning level.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette and butter** | Lyonnais *bouchon* service. Bread, butter, sausage, wine. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Sharp vinegar against slow-cured fat. |
| **Comté or Beaufort** | Nutty alpine cheese against a nutty sausage. |
| **Grainy mustard** | Traditional and useful. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin cuts slow-cured fat and the crunch breaks a uniformly soft bite. |
| **Beaujolais** | The Lyonnais wine, and the classic match. |
| **Cornichons again** | Worth repeating — a plate of rosette without them is unbalanced. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. It's a big, savoury sausage and it
dominates anything subtle.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thin — the rounds are large enough that thickness isn't needed for
   presence.**
2. **Lay slices flat, slightly overlapping, as a single row.** The size is the
   feature. Folding it hides it.
3. **Temper 20 minutes** so the fat softens.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person** — that's only two or three slices given the
   diameter.
5. **Peel the casing** unless it's clearly edible.
6. **Put cornichons directly beside it.**
7. **Fix:** if the slices are too big to eat neatly, halve them into
   half-moons rather than slicing thinner.

| **Jésus de Lyon** | French delis, specialty | The larger, even longer-cured relative. Superb. |
| **Saucisson sec** | Whole Foods, grocery | The smaller everyday version — see its own page. |
| **Salame Milano** | Italian delis | The Italian fine-ground equivalent. |

**Label check:** a genuinely wide sausage — three inches or more across — and a
long cure. Small "rosette" is usually just saucisson under a better name.

**Skip:** slicing it thick because the rounds are big. Large diameter plus
thickness is too much sausage in one bite.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is rosette so much bigger than other salami?**
It's cured in the wide end section of the intestine, which allows a much fatter
sausage and a much longer cure.

**Rosette or saucisson sec?**
Rosette is bigger, cured longer, milder on garlic and nuttier. Saucisson is
smaller, tangier and more everyday.

**What is Jésus de Lyon?**
An even larger, longer-cured version from the same tradition. Excellent and
harder to find.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz — only two or three of those large slices.

**What's the classic pairing?**
Baguette, butter, cornichons and Beaujolais. That's a Lyonnais lunch.
