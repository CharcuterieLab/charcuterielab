## The short version

The dry way to add heat. Unlike hot honey or chilli oil, flakes bring nothing wet
with them, so they can go directly on cheese without softening a cracker or
bleeding across a board. The upgrade is buying **Aleppo** or **Calabrian** flakes
rather than the generic red pepper flakes from a pizza shop.

## What it is

Dried chillies crushed coarse. **Aleppo (pul biber)** is mild, fruity, slightly
oily and burgundy-red; **Calabrian** is fruitier and hotter; **Urfa** is dark,
smoky and raisin-like; generic **crushed red pepper** is hotter, sharper and
mostly seed.

**Taste:** varies — fruity and mild (Aleppo) to sharp and hot (generic)
**Texture:** coarse dry flakes
**Look:** red flecks, dark burgundy for Aleppo, bright red for generic

The mechanism is heat without a carrier. Capsaicin needs fat or liquid to spread,
so dry flakes deliver heat in discrete points rather than across the whole bite.
On a board that's useful — you can add heat to one cheese without the heat
migrating to everything around it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | Oil, salt and Aleppo flakes. The heat sits on top rather than soaking in, so each bite is different. |
| **Whipped feta** | Salt and cream with a fruity dry heat scattered over. |
| **Hummus** | The traditional finish, with olive oil. Aleppo is the correct flake here. |
| **Honey** | Sprinkle flakes into a pot of honey and you've made hot honey. Cheaper than buying it. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang and heat, with the colour doing visual work on a white log. |
| **Olive oil and bread** | Oil, flakes and salt in a shallow dish. Ten seconds. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses. Heat covers subtlety.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Pinch and scatter from a height** so the flakes spread rather than clump.
2. **Use Aleppo as the default.** It's mild enough to use generously and fruity
   enough to add flavour, not just burn.
3. **Add it last**, over pale surfaces where it shows.
4. **Put a small dish out** so guests can add their own.
5. **Make hot honey**: stir a teaspoon of flakes into a small pot of honey and let
   it sit twenty minutes.
6. **Say what's spicy.** A red flake on white cheese is a clear signal, but say it
   anyway.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Aleppo and Urfa are much milder than generic crushed red
   pepper. Switch, don't reduce.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aleppo pepper (pul biber)** | Middle Eastern grocers, spice shops | Mild, fruity, slightly oily. The best all-round finishing flake. |
| **Calabrian chilli flakes** | Italian delis | Hotter and fruitier. Excellent on Italian boards. |
| **Urfa biber** | Spice shops, Middle Eastern grocers | Dark, smoky, raisin-like. Remarkable on chocolate and cheese. |
| **Gochugaru** | Korean markets | Bright red, mild, slightly sweet. Good and very cheap. |
| **Crushed red pepper** | Any grocery | The generic pizza-shop flake. Hot and mostly seed. Usable, not ideal. |

**Label check:** buy from a shop with turnover — chilli flakes fade badly within a
year. Aleppo should look burgundy and feel slightly damp, not bright red and dusty.

**Skip:** anything that's mostly pale seeds. Seeds carry heat but no flavour.

## Quick FAQ

**What is Aleppo pepper?**
A mild, fruity Middle Eastern chilli flake, burgundy in colour and slightly oily.
The best default finishing flake for a cheese board.

**How is it different from crushed red pepper?**
Crushed red pepper is hotter, sharper and mostly seeds. Aleppo is milder with much
more flavour.

**Can I make hot honey with chilli flakes?**
Yes. Stir a teaspoon into a small pot of honey and let it sit for twenty minutes.

**What do I sprinkle them on?**
Burrata, whipped feta, hummus, fresh goat cheese, and olive oil.

**Do chilli flakes go stale?**
They fade within about a year. Buy small quantities from a shop with turnover.
