## The short version

A soft cheese with extra cream added to the milk before it's made — legally at
least 75% butterfat in the dry matter. It tastes like salted whipped butter
with a bloomy rind. Serve less than you think, and always with something sharp
beside it.

## What it is

Double-crème starts at 60% butterfat; triple-crème at 75%. The cream goes in
before culturing, so the fat is part of the paste rather than a coating. Most
wear a white bloomy rind like brie, but the paste beneath is denser, whiter and
far richer. Brillat-Savarin, Saint-André, Delice de Bourgogne and Pierre Robert
are the names to know.

**Taste:** buttery, tangy, faintly salty, almost sweet
**Texture:** dense, silky, spoonable at room temperature
**Look:** bright white paste, thick and even, under a white rind

Saturation is the mechanism. Fat coats the tongue and stays there, which is
what makes triple-crème feel luxurious for two bites and heavy by the fourth.
Every good pairing here is doing the same job — stripping that coating off so
the next bite lands as clearly as the first.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Champagne or dry sparkling wine** | Bubbles and acid physically scrub fat off the palate. The classic pairing and not a coincidence. |
| **Tart berries — raspberry, blackberry** | Sharp fruit acid against saturated fat. The most effective contrast available. |
| **Water crackers** | Lean, dry, no fat of their own. A butter cracker here is fat on fat. |
| **Brioche toast points** | The opposite approach — match the richness instead of cutting it. Reserve for a small, formal board. |
| **Cornichons and pickled things** | Straight vinegar as a palate reset between bites. |
| **Honey and fig jam** | Sweetness balances the salt, though it makes an already-rich bite richer. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Salt and crunch, and something to break the texture. |

**Skip it with:** butter crackers and other rich bases, and cured meats — fat
on fat with salt on top, and by the second bite nobody can taste anything. Also
skip serving two triple-crèmes on one board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.** It should be spoonably soft, not chilled and firm.
2. **Serve 1 oz per person** — half what you'd serve of a firmer cheese. It's
   very rich and people take small amounts.
3. **Cut wedges from the center out**, and expect the paste to slump. That's
   correct.
4. **Put something acidic within arm's reach** — berries, cornichons, a sharp
   jam. This is the pairing that makes the cheese work.
5. **Give it a spreading knife**, not a hard cheese blade. It spreads more than
   it slices.
6. **Place it at the mild end of the board**, away from blue and washed-rind
   cheeses.
7. **Fix:** if it's weeping liquid at the cut face, it's fine — that's whey,
   not spoilage. Blot it and serve.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Delice de Bourgogne** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, Murray's | The crowd-pleaser. Rich, tangy, widely liked. Start here. |
| **Brillat-Savarin** | Cheese counters, specialty shops | The benchmark. Lemony and lighter than it should be for the fat content. |
| **Saint-André** | Grocery cheese case, Whole Foods | The easiest to find. Very rich, very mild. |
| **Pierre Robert** | Cheese counters | Aged longer, more savory. The connoisseur pick. |
| **Fromager d'Affinois** | Grocery cheese case | Technically double-crème, widely stocked, and excellent value. |
| **Trader Joe's triple crème brie** | Trader Joe's | Cheap entry point to the category. |

**Label check:** "triple crème" or "75%" on the label. Note the percentage is
fat *in dry matter*, not total weight — the cheese isn't three-quarters fat by
the scale.

**Skip:** buying a large format. This is a cheese people eat an ounce of.
A small wedge from the counter beats a half-wheel that goes off.

## Quick FAQ

**What makes a cheese triple-crème?**
Extra cream added to the milk before culturing, bringing it to at least 75%
butterfat in the dry matter.

**Is it just very rich brie?**
Related but different. Brie gets its character from rind ripening;
triple-crème gets it from added cream. Triple-crème is denser and less
mushroomy.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz — half a normal cheese portion. It's extremely rich.

**What wine goes with it?**
Champagne or any dry sparkling. Acid and bubbles are what make a second bite as
good as the first.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, on the bloomy-rind versions. It's thinner and milder than brie's.
