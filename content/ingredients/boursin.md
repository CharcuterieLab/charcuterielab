## The short version

A soft, crumbly French cheese blended with garlic and herbs, sold in a foil
puck. It's the one soft cheese that needs nothing done to it — and the reason
it works on a board is the same reason it can't go next to anything subtle.

## What it is

Boursin was created in Normandy in 1963, inspired by a regional tradition of
mixing fresh cheese with herbs at the table. The base is a *fromage frais* —
fresh cow's-milk cheese — blended with garlic, parsley, chives and salt. The
texture is deliberately crumbly rather than smooth.

**Taste:** garlicky, herbal, salty, tangy
**Texture:** soft and crumbly, spreads under pressure
**Look:** white flecked with green, formed into a round puck

Pre-seasoning is the mechanism, and it's a genuine advantage on a board. Almost
every soft cheese needs help — salt, oil, herbs, a spread. Boursin arrives
finished, which makes it the highest-reward-per-effort item in this category
and the reason it's on more boards than any other flavored cheese.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Water crackers and crostini** | It's loud; the base should be quiet. Anything seasoned fights it. |
| **Cucumber rounds and endive** | Cool, wet, crisp against garlic and salt. Also the low-carb answer. |
| **Cherry tomatoes** | Acid and sweetness cutting garlic. A very good two-ingredient bite. |
| **Prosciutto and salami** | Garlic and cured pork is a permanent pairing. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweet, smoky, oily — everything Boursin isn't. |
| **Radishes and raw vegetables** | It's the dip. This is what it's for. |
| **Crisp white wine, dry rosé, cold beer** | Acid to cut the salt and the fat. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses — Boursin's garlic lingers on the
palate and mutes whatever comes next — and sweet jams, which fight the garlic
badly. Put it at one end of the board and keep it there.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 20 minutes.** Straight from the fridge it's crumbly and hard to
   spread; warm it's soft and fragrant.
2. **Put it at the end of the board, not the middle.** Whatever sits next to it
   will taste of garlic.
3. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** A 5.2 oz puck covers five to six.
4. **Break the top open** rather than serving an intact puck — it invites the
   first person to dig in.
5. **Give it its own knife.** Garlic transfers, and a shared blade puts it on
   the brie.
6. **Surround it with raw vegetables** if you want it to work harder — it's a
   better dip than most actual dips.
7. **Fix:** too crumbly to spread? Mash it with a fork and a splash of cream.
   It becomes a smooth dip in thirty seconds.

| **Alouette Garlic & Herbs** | Grocery cheese case | The main competitor. Smoother and milder. |
| **Rondelé** | Grocery cheese case | Softer, more spreadable, slightly less flavor. |
| **Herbed cream cheese, homemade** | Any grocery | Cream cheese, garlic, chives, parsley, lemon zest, salt. Cheaper and adjustable. |

**Label check:** garlic and real herbs in the list rather than "natural
flavors." The good versions taste like the ingredients they name.

**Skip:** the dessert and fruit-flavored versions on a savory board. And skip
buying two flavors — one garlic-forward cheese per board is the limit.

## Quick FAQ

**Is Boursin a real cheese?**
Yes — a French fresh cow's-milk cheese blended with garlic and herbs. It's been
made since 1963.

**Does it need anything done to it?**
No, and that's the appeal. Just let it come to room temperature.

**Why does it dominate the board?**
Garlic lingers on the palate. Keep it at one end, give it its own knife, and
nothing else picks it up.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. A 5.2 oz puck serves five to six.

**Can I make my own?**
Yes — cream cheese, minced garlic, chives, parsley, lemon zest and salt. It's
cheaper and you control the garlic.
