## The short version

Cheddar aged past a year: sharp, savory, crumbly, and studded with tiny crunchy
crystals. It's the most familiar cheese on most boards and the one that most
rewards buying a better version. Serve it in thick pieces, not thin slices.

## What it is

Cheddar is named for a process, not a place — *cheddaring* is the stacking and
turning of curd slabs to press out whey. That process makes a dense, low-moisture
cheese that ages well. Six months in, it's mild and firm. At eighteen months to
two years, the proteins have broken down and the flavor concentrates hard.

**Taste:** sharp, tangy, nutty, deeply savory
**Texture:** firm and crumbly, with visible crunchy crystals
**Look:** pale ivory to deep amber, dry-looking, often cloth-bound

Protein breakdown is the mechanism, and the crystals are the visible evidence.
As cheddar ages, enzymes cut proteins into smaller pieces, some of which
crystallize as tyrosine — the crunch people mistake for salt. More crystals
means more breakdown means more flavor, so they're a quality signal, not a
defect.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Tart apple slices** | Acid and sweetness against salt and fat. The oldest pairing in the category and still the best. |
| **Fig jam or apple butter** | Sweetness balances cheddar's acidity the way chutney does on a Ploughman's. |
| **Whole wheat crackers** | Toasted grain meeting the cheese's own nutty notes. Echo rather than contrast. |
| **Oatcakes** | The Scottish and northern English pairing. Sweet oat against sharp cheese. |
| **Grainy mustard and cornichons** | Sharp and vinegary, cutting straight through the fat. |
| **Walnuts and pecans** | Tannic bitterness with a savory, crystalline cheese. |
| **IPA, dry cider, Cabernet Sauvignon** | Bitterness or tannin, both of which stand up to sharpness. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses on the same corner of the board —
cheddar's a bully. And skip thin slicing, which makes it taste harsher rather
than sharper.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 30 minutes.** Cold cheddar tastes flatly acidic; warm, the nutty
   and savory notes come forward.
2. **Cut thick.** Small cubes or short chunky wedges, roughly ½ inch. Thin
   shavings concentrate the sharpness unpleasantly.
3. **Or break it.** A well-aged cheddar crumbles along its own lines — snap it
   with a short blade and leave the pieces rough.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person** as one of three cheeses.
5. **Put something sweet within reach** — jam, honey, apple. Cheddar alone gets
   monotonous by the fourth bite.
6. **Place it centrally.** It's the cheese most guests reach for first, so it
   shouldn't be in a corner.
7. **Fix:** dry, cracked edges from the fridge? Trim a thin layer. The interior
   is fine.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cabot Clothbound Cheddar** | Whole Foods, cheese counters, Murray's | Aged in Jasper Hill's cellars. The best widely available American cheddar. |
| **Cabot Seriously Sharp** | Most grocery stores | The value pick. Genuinely sharp, genuinely cheap. |
| **Kerrygold Reserve Cheddar** | Grocery cheese case | Irish, two years, very crystalline. |
| **Montgomery's or Keen's Cheddar** | Cheese counters, specialty | Traditional English farmhouse. The benchmark, and priced like it. |
| **Tillamook Extra Sharp** | Grocery, West Coast | Reliable, widely stocked, good value. |
| **Beecher's Flagship** | Whole Foods, Costco, Seattle | American, nutty and less acidic. A good change of pace. |

**Label check:** an age statement in months or years, not just "sharp" — the
words sharp and extra sharp aren't regulated. Cloth-bound or bandage-wrapped
means a drier, more complex cheese.

**Skip:** pre-sliced or pre-shredded cheddar for a board, and orange cheddar if
you have a choice. The color is annatto and adds nothing.

## Quick FAQ

**What are the crunchy bits in aged cheddar?**
Tyrosine crystals — clusters of an amino acid released as proteins break down
during aging. A sign of a well-aged cheese, not salt and not a fault.

**How aged is aged?**
Twelve months is a floor. Eighteen months to two years is where most of the
character shows up.

**Why does thin slicing make it taste sharper?**
More surface area hits the tongue at once. Thick pieces taste rounder and
nuttier.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of three cheeses.

**White or orange?**
Identical apart from annatto coloring. White if you're choosing.
