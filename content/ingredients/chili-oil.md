## The short version

Not the thin red oil in a takeaway container — the crunchy kind, thick with fried
chilli flakes, garlic, shallot and soybean. Spooned over burrata or cream cheese
it delivers heat, crunch, salt and umami at once, and it's the single best fusion
idea you can bring to a European cheese board.

## What it is

Neutral oil infused with dried chilli and aromatics, usually with the fried solids
left in. **Lao Gan Ma** is the reference; the crispy chilli style contains fried
garlic, shallot, soybean, and often Sichuan pepper. Heat levels range from mild to
serious.

**Taste:** savoury, salty, umami-rich, warmly hot
**Texture:** oil plus crisp fried bits — the crunch is the point
**Look:** deep red oil with a dense layer of solids at the bottom

The mechanism is fat carrying heat. Capsaicin is oil-soluble, so it spreads and
lingers rather than hitting once. On a soft cheese that means the heat builds
slowly across the whole bite, while the fried solids add the crunch that soft
cheese has no other way of getting.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | The best use of both. Cream with no seasoning, and an oil that brings salt, heat, umami and crunch in one spoon. |
| **Cream cheese** | Spoon it over a block with crackers — the Asian answer to pepper jelly on cream cheese. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang against heat, with the fried garlic doing the savoury work. |
| **Whipped feta** | Salty and creamy, topped with hot and crunchy. |
| **Water crackers and neutral bases** | The plainer the base, the better this reads. |
| **Cucumber and raw vegetables** | The cooling half. Standard in Sichuan cooking. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses and anything sweet. It dominates both.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Stir the jar before spooning.** All the good bits sink to the bottom, and a
   spoonful of plain oil is a waste.
2. **Spoon it over the cheese**, don't drizzle — you want the solids.
3. **Serve the jar with a small spoon** so guests can add their own.
4. **Say it's spicy**, and say which cheeses it's for.
5. **Add it right before serving.** The oil bleeds outward on a plate.
6. **Use a spoonful per cheese**, not per person.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Spoon it over something creamy rather than eating it neat —
   fat blunts the heat considerably.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lao Gan Ma crispy chilli** | Asian markets, most groceries | The benchmark. Cheap, savoury, moderately hot. |
| **Fly By Jing Sichuan Chili Crisp** | Specialty, online | More complex and much more expensive. Excellent. |
| **Chiu Chow chilli oil (Lee Kum Kee)** | Asian markets | Garlicky and mild. A good starting point. |
| **Calabrian chilli oil** | Italian delis | The Italian equivalent. Fruitier, no fried solids. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Hot neutral oil poured over chilli flakes, garlic, shallot and salt. Ten minutes. |

**Label check:** you want visible solids in the jar. Clear red oil with nothing in
it is doing half the job. Most contain soy — worth checking for allergies.

**Skip:** thin bottled chilli oil from the condiment aisle.

## Quick FAQ

**What is chili crisp?**
Chilli oil with the fried solids left in — garlic, shallot, soybean and chilli
flakes. Crunchy as well as hot.

**Does chili oil work on a cheese board?**
Yes, and it's one of the best fusion moves available. Spoon it over burrata,
cream cheese or whipped feta.

**How spicy is it?**
Lao Gan Ma and most supermarket versions are moderate. Some artisan brands are
much hotter — taste before serving.

**Should I stir the jar?**
Always. The fried solids sink, and they're the best part.

**Does it contain allergens?**
Most contain soy, and some contain peanut or wheat. Check the label.
