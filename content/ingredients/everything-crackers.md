## The short version

A cracker carrying everything-bagel seasoning — sesame, poppy, dried garlic,
dried onion and coarse salt. It arrives already topped, which means it's a
finished bite with nothing but cream cheese on it, and a fight with anything
sweet.

## What it is

Everything seasoning was a bagel topping first, and the crackers are a direct
borrowing. The base underneath is usually a plain or lightly seeded cracker;
the seasoning is pressed onto the surface before baking so it toasts along with
the dough.

**Taste:** garlicky, oniony, salty, toasted
**Texture:** crunchy with loose seeds on top
**Look:** speckled black and white and tan, visibly topped

Two mechanisms, both aggressive. Dried garlic and onion are sulfur compounds —
deeply savory and, importantly, *lingering*. And the salt sits on the surface,
so it lands first. Together they mean the cracker isn't a base at all. It's an
ingredient with an opinion.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Whipped cream cheese** | Cool, fatty and blank. It absorbs the garlic instead of arguing with it. The default. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Bagel logic, unchanged. The seasoning was built for this exact combination. |
| **Whipped feta or labneh** | Tangy and thick. Salt on salt works here because the dairy is sour enough to cut it. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Plain tangy cheese is a clean canvas for a loud cracker. |
| **Chive and scallion spreads** | Allium meeting allium. Same family, more of it. |
| **Egg salad, avocado** | Brunch pairings. Mild and creamy is what this cracker needs. |
| **Sharp cheddar** | Holds its own, which most cheeses can't here. |

**Skip it with:** fig jam, honey, quince and fruit generally. Garlic and sweet
is a genuinely bad bite. Also skip delicate cheeses — burrata and young brie
lose completely — and be aware the garlic lingers, so anything eaten *after*
one of these tastes muted for a few minutes.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Put them at the opposite end from the jam.** Physically separate the
   savory corner from the sweet one, or guests will make the bad bite for you.
2. **Serve 3–4 per person.** They're filling and assertive; people eat fewer.
3. **Give them a small dish or their own edge.** They shed seasoning, and loose
   garlic flakes in soft cheese look like a mistake.
4. **Pair them with something bland on purpose** — cream cheese, goat cheese —
   rather than trying to match their intensity.
5. **Always put out a neutral cracker too.** This one can't carry a board
   alone.
6. **DIY option:** brush plain water crackers or crostini with a little oil and
   press on everything-bagel seasoning. Cheaper and fresher-tasting than any
   box.

| **Store brand everything crackers** | Target, Aldi, Kroger | The category is hard to get wrong. Buy on price. |

**Label check:** you want visible whole seeds and flakes. If the seasoning
reads as powder, it will taste like onion soup mix.

**Skip:** everything crackers on a board that already has an everything-topped
bagel chip or an everything cream cheese. One everything item per board.

## Quick FAQ

**What goes on everything crackers?**
Cream cheese first, then smoked salmon, whipped feta or plain goat cheese.
Anything mild and creamy.

**Why do they clash with jam?**
Dried garlic and sugar is an unpleasant combination, and the garlic lingers
long enough to affect the next thing you eat.

**Are they gluten-free?**
Most are not. A few almond-flour versions exist — check the box.

**How many per person?**
3–4, always alongside a neutral cracker.

**Can I make my own?**
Yes, and it's better. Brush plain crackers with oil, press on everything
seasoning, and warm them for 3 minutes at 300°F.
