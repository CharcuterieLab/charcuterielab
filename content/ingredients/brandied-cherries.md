## The short version

Cherries preserved in brandy or spirit-laced syrup. They're dark, glossy, and
slightly boozy, and they take a board from good to deliberate faster than
anything else at the price. The one rule: they go in a small dish with a spoon.
Loose on the wood, the syrup goes everywhere.

## What it is

Whole cherries — usually sour Morello or Amarena — packed in syrup with brandy,
kirsch or a neutral spirit. **Luxardo** are the famous ones (Marasca cherries in
their own thick syrup, only lightly alcoholic). **Amarena Fabbri** are Italian
sour cherries in dense syrup. Home-brandied cherries are boozier than either.

**Taste:** deep, sour-sweet, almond-edged, warming
**Texture:** soft but intact, syrup-coated
**Look:** near-black or deep garnet, glossy

The mechanism is acid and alcohol together. Sour cherries keep more acid than any
other preserved fruit, and the spirit adds a bitterness and warmth that reads as
"grown-up" against salt and fat. It's the same logic as port with stilton, in
spoonable form.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola and stilton** | The port-and-blue pairing, made portable. Acid, sugar, alcohol against salt and funk. |
| **Mascarpone** | Spoon syrup and all over it. Effectively a dessert, and the best two minutes of work on any board. |
| **Duck prosciutto and game charcuterie** | Cherry with duck is a classical pairing; here it's already in sauce form. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel and butterscotch against sour dark fruit. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Obvious and correct. The syrup does the work. |
| **Manchego** | Nutty sheep milk and almond-edged cherry share aromatic ground. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses and anything dry and crisp that the
syrup will soak into.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve them in a small dish** with a spoon. Never directly on wood — the
   syrup stains and spreads.
2. **Bring a little syrup with them.** The syrup is half the ingredient.
3. **Put the dish at the edge**, near the sweet corner, so drips don't cross the
   board.
4. **Provide cocktail picks** if the cherries are stemmed and whole.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — two or three cherries.
6. **Spoon syrup over soft cheese** for the version that impresses people.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? Amarena and Luxardo are syrup-forward. Home-brandied or
   Morello-in-kirsch versions are sharper.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Luxardo Maraschino cherries** | Liquor stores, specialty grocers | The benchmark. Dark, dense, thick syrup. Expensive and lasts forever. |
| **Amarena Fabbri** | Italian delis, Eataly | Sour Italian cherries in syrup, in the blue-and-white jar. Excellent value beside Luxardo. |
| **Morello cherries in kirsch** | European delis, specialty | Sharper and boozier. Best with blue cheese. |
| **Tillen Farms / Filthy cherries** | Grocery, liquor stores | Cocktail cherries. Firmer, less complex, cheaper. |
| **Home-brandied cherries** | Make them | Sour cherries, sugar, brandy, a month in the jar. Cheapest and boozy. |

**Label check:** avoid neon red maraschino cherries with red dye — a completely
different product built for ice cream sundaes.

**Skip:** anything bright red and firm. The good ones are dark and yielding.

## Quick FAQ

**Are brandied cherries the same as maraschino cherries?**
Not the bright red kind. Luxardo maraschino cherries are dark, syrup-preserved
and genuinely good; the neon red ones are dyed and firm.

**How do I serve them on a board?**
In a small dish with a spoon and a little of their syrup — never loose on the
wood.

**Are they alcoholic?**
Luxardo and Amarena are only lightly so. Home-brandied and kirsch-packed cherries
are noticeably boozy.

**What cheese goes with brandied cherries?**
Blue cheeses, mascarpone, and aged gouda. Spoon the syrup over soft cheese.

**How long do they keep?**
Months in the fridge once opened — the sugar and alcohol preserve them.
