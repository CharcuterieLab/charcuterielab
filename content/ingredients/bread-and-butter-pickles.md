## The short version

Cucumber coins pickled sweet with onion, turmeric and mustard seed. They're the
pickle that works like a jam — sweet enough to sit beside a sharp cheese, sour
enough to cut fat. Underrated on a board precisely because everyone assumes
pickles have to be sour.

## What it is

Thin-sliced cucumbers and onions in a vinegar syrup with sugar, turmeric, mustard
seed and celery seed. Usually crinkle-cut. The name comes from the Depression-era
practice of eating them in bread-and-butter sandwiches.

**Taste:** sweet then sour, with mustard and celery seed behind
**Texture:** crisp coins, slightly softer than a dill
**Look:** golden-green crinkle-cut discs, flecked with seed and onion

The mechanism is sugar and acid arriving together. A dill pickle is pure acid,
which is a blunt instrument beside cheese; bread-and-butters carry sugar as well,
so they do what a chutney does — cut the fat and add sweetness in the same bite.
That makes them the better pickle for a cheese-led board.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sweet-sour against sharp and salty. This is the ploughman's arrangement with the chutney replaced by something crunchy. |
| **Pimento cheese** | The Southern pairing. Rich, sharp, creamy against sweet and crisp. |
| **Black forest ham and cooked ham** | Sweet pickle with salty pork is the sandwich logic, on a board. |
| **Butter crackers** | Salty and soft against sweet and crunchy. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel in the cheese meeting sugar and turmeric in the brine. |
| **Fried chicken and burgers** | Their native habitat, and worth knowing if the board is casual. |

**Skip it with:** pâté and French charcuterie. Those want cornichons — sharp, not
sweet.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain and blot.** The syrup is stickier than dill brine and spreads.
2. **Fan the coins** or pile them in a small bowl.
3. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
4. **Put them beside the cheddar**, not the pâté.
5. **Serve cold.**
6. **Use them as the sweet element** if the board has no jam — they cover both
   jobs.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? Buy a "spicy" or "sandwich stacker" version, which
   dial back the sugar.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Refrigerated bread and butter chips** | Grocery, refrigerated section | Crunchier than shelf-stable. The default. |
| **Wickles or Mt. Olive** | Grocery | Widely available. Wickles are spicier and very good. |
| **Spicy bread and butter pickles** | Grocery, specialty | Chilli added. Excellent with cheddar. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Cucumbers, onion, vinegar, sugar, turmeric, mustard seed. Ready in an hour, refrigerator pickle. |
| **Pickled okra or green beans** | Southern grocers | Different vegetable, similar sweet-sour treatment. |

**Label check:** turmeric and mustard seed should be in there — they're what make
the flavour. Watch for high fructose corn syrup in cheap jars.

**Skip:** anything limp in the jar.

## Quick FAQ

**What are bread and butter pickles?**
Sweet-sour cucumber coins pickled with onion, turmeric and mustard seed. Named for
Depression-era bread-and-butter sandwiches.

**Are they better than dill pickles on a cheese board?**
For a cheese-led board, often yes — the sugar lets them work like a chutney as
well as a pickle.

**What cheese goes with bread and butter pickles?**
Aged cheddar above all, plus aged gouda and pimento cheese.

**Can I make them at home?**
Yes — they're a refrigerator pickle, ready in about an hour and keeping for weeks.

**How much per person?**
About three-quarters of an ounce — four or five coins.
