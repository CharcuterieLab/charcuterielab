## The short version

Four ingredients — flour, water, salt, yeast — and the only base on a board
that gives you crunch and softness in the same bite. Buy it the day you serve
it. A baguette bought yesterday is a different, worse ingredient.

## What it is

A lean French loaf, meaning no fat, no sugar, no milk. In France the *pain
traditionnel* is legally limited to those four ingredients. The long thin shape
maximizes crust, and steam in the oven is what makes that crust blister and
shatter.

**Taste:** clean, wheaty, mildly sweet from the crumb
**Texture:** shattering crust over soft open crumb
**Look:** golden, slashed diagonally, irregular holes inside

The two-textures-in-one thing is the mechanism. The crust gives you crunch; the
crumb soaks up oil, soft cheese and juice without falling apart. No cracker can
do both, which is why a baguette belongs on a board even when crackers are
already there.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie and Camembert** | The canonical pairing. Soft crumb against soft cheese, with the crust supplying the only crunch needed. |
| **Triple-crème cheese** | Lean bread against very rich cheese. No fat competing with fat. |
| **Pâté and rillettes** | Rich spreadable meat needs something plain and structural. This is what it's served on in France. |
| **Cultured butter and radishes** | *Radis au beurre.* Three ingredients, no cheese, and it always disappears first. |
| **Good olive oil** | The open crumb acts like a sponge. A cracker can't do this at all. |
| **Jamón and prosciutto** | Salt and fat against neutral wheat — the crumb absorbs the rendered fat instead of skidding. |
| **Fresh chèvre with herbs** | Bright and tangy on a plain background, no cracker flavor interfering. |

**Skip it with:** anything wet that has to sit — marinated tomatoes, wet
mozzarella, dressed salads. Bread goes soggy from underneath in about fifteen
minutes.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy it the same day.** This is the single rule. A day-old baguette is
   stale, not rustic.
2. **Slice on a sharp diagonal, about ⅓ inch thick.** The angle gives you more
   surface for toppings and looks better than coins.
3. **Slice no more than 30 minutes ahead**, or slice early and cover with a
   clean kitchen towel — not plastic, which traps steam and softens the crust.
4. **Serve 3–4 slices per person** alongside crackers. Half a loaf covers six
   people; a full loaf if bread is the main base.
5. **Tear the heel and leave it on the board.** A torn end next to clean slices
   makes the whole thing look less like a catering tray.
6. **Fix:** day-old loaf? Run it briefly under the tap, then 5–7 minutes at
   350°F. The crust re-crisps and the crumb steams back to soft. Serve within
   the hour — it only works once.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-day loaf from a local bakery** | Neighborhood bakery | Always the best option. Ask what time it came out. |
| **In-store bakery baguette** | Kroger, Publix, Whole Foods, Wegmans | Baked in-house that morning. Perfectly good. |
| **La Brea Bakery Take & Bake Baguette** | Most grocery stores, bread aisle | Par-baked. You finish it at home, so it's genuinely fresh at serving. |
| **Trader Joe's par-baked baguettes** | Trader Joe's | Cheap, freezer-friendly, 10 minutes in the oven. |
| **Costco bakery French baguette (2-pack)** | Costco | Big, cheap, good. Freeze the second one. |

**Label check:** the shorter the ingredient list, the better the loaf. Dough
conditioners, preservatives and added sugar produce a soft loaf that never
gets a real crust.

**Skip:** shelf-stable baguettes sold in a plastic sleeve in the bread aisle.
They're soft-crusted sandwich bread in a baguette shape.

## Quick FAQ

**How much baguette per person?**
3–4 slices, or roughly half a loaf per six people when crackers are also out.

**Should I slice it or tear it?**
Slice for a clean board, tear for a rustic one. Do both — a few torn pieces
among the slices looks intentional.

**Can I slice it the night before?**
No. Slice it the day of and cover with a towel. Cut surfaces stale within
hours.

**Should I toast it?**
Only if the toppings are wet or you're making crostini. Otherwise the contrast
between crisp crust and soft crumb is the point, and toasting removes it.

**Can I freeze a baguette?**
Yes, wrapped tightly, for about a month. Thaw at room temperature, then 5
minutes at 350°F to bring the crust back.
