## The short version

Lemon juice, sugar, butter and egg yolk cooked until thick. It is the only sweet
spread that arrives with real acidity, which is why a dessert board with lemon curd
on it stays interesting past the second bite while one built on jam alone does not.

## What it is

A British preserve that is technically a custard. The eggs set it, the butter makes
it rich, and the lemon juice is present in a quantity that would be unpleasant
without both. Sold in jars, refrigerated after opening because of the egg.

**Taste:** sharply sour, then very sweet, with butter behind it
**Texture:** thick, silky, spoonable, not gelled
**Look:** opaque pale yellow, glossy

The mechanism is fat carrying acid. Butter and yolk coat the tongue and blunt the
edge of the lemon just enough to make a mouthful of concentrated citrus pleasant.
That is also why curd works next to cheese — both sides are fat-and-acid systems,
so they meet rather than fight.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Shortbread** | Butter on butter, with the lemon as the only thing standing between it and being too much. The classic. |
| **Mascarpone** | Sweet cream and sour citrus. Spoon them side by side and let people mix. |
| **Berries** | Raspberries especially. Their acid matches the curd rather than competing. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | The surprise. Lactic tang and citrus tang are the same note, and the curd's sugar softens both. |
| **Digestive biscuits** | Wheaty and barely sweet, which is exactly what a very sweet spread needs. |
| **Pizzelle and thin wafers** | Crisp, neutral, and they let the curd be the whole flavour. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and anything salty. Curd is a dessert-corner item and
does not travel across the board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Take it out of the fridge 20 minutes early.** Cold curd is stiff and mutes
   the lemon.
2. **Stir it smooth** in the jar.
3. **Serve in a small bowl** with its own spoon.
4. **Give it a dedicated corner** with the sweet items rather than mixing it in.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
6. **Add lemon zest over the top** if you want it to look like something.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? A pinch of flaky salt across the surface. It sharpens the
   citrus immediately.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **British jarred lemon curd** | Grocery, British import shelves | The default. Look for butter and egg high in the ingredients. |
| **Farmers market curd** | Markets, small producers | Fresher, sharper, usually better. Short shelf life. |
| **Passion fruit or lime curd** | Specialty | Same idea, more interesting. Passion fruit curd is excellent on a board. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Yolks, sugar, juice, zest and butter over a bain-marie. Twenty minutes, and far better. |
| **Vegan lemon curd** | Health food shops | Coconut oil and starch. Sweeter and less sharp, but it works for the dairy-free board. |

**Label check:** real butter and egg yolk. Palm oil and starch means it is a
lemon-flavoured filling, not a curd.

**Skip:** anything bright yellow enough to look artificial. Real curd is pale.

## Quick FAQ

**Is lemon curd the same as lemon jam?**
No. Curd is thickened with egg yolk and butter, which makes it rich and opaque.
Lemon jam or marmalade is fruit and sugar, and is clear.

**Does lemon curd go on a cheese board?**
On a dessert board, yes — with mascarpone, shortbread and berries. On a savoury
charcuterie board it is out of place.

**What cheese goes with lemon curd?**
Mascarpone above all, then fresh goat cheese, where the acid in both lines up.

**How long does an open jar last?**
About two weeks refrigerated. It contains egg, so treat it like a fresh product
rather than a preserve.

**How much per person?**
Half an ounce. It is very sweet.
