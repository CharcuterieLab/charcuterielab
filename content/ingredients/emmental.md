## The short version

The original holey Swiss cheese — mild, sweet, faintly nutty, and much gentler
than Gruyère. It's the alpine cheese for a crowd that doesn't want a challenge,
and the holes have a genuinely interesting explanation.

## What it is

Emmentaler AOP is made in the Emme valley in enormous wheels of up to 120kg —
the largest cheese in common production. The paste is pale, elastic and dotted
with holes ranging from cherry to walnut size.

**Taste:** mild, sweet, nutty, gently savory
**Texture:** firm but elastic, smooth, not crumbly
**Look:** pale yellow with large round holes, thin natural rind

Bacterial gas is the mechanism. A third culture, *Propionibacterium freudenreichii*,
consumes lactic acid during aging and releases carbon dioxide. In a dense
cheese that gas can't escape, so it forms bubbles — the eyes. Those same
bacteria produce propionic acid, which is where the sweet, nutty flavor comes
from. The holes and the taste have the same cause.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cured ham and speck** | Salt and smoke against a mild sweet cheese. The alpine standard. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Sharp vinegar against gentle sweetness. |
| **Apple and grapes** | Fresh acid, no competition. Emmental is mild enough that fruit leads. |
| **Cocktail rye** | Soft dark bread and mild sweet cheese — the northern European bite. |
| **Whole grain mustard** | The cheese needs an edge from somewhere. |
| **Walnuts** | Emmental's own nutty note, reinforced. |
| **Dry Riesling, Chasselas, light lager** | Nothing heavy required. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and other strong cheeses on the same corner —
Emmental is the mildest alpine cheese and it loses easily. Also skip it if the
board already has Gruyère and Comté; three alpine cheeses is one too many.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut into thick batons or triangles**, showing the holes in cross-section.
   The holes are the visual identity — don't hide them.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Use it as the safe cheese.** Every board benefits from one item that no
   guest will hesitate over, and this is a good candidate.
5. **Put mustard or pickles beside it.** On its own it's pleasant but a little
   flat.
6. **Trim the rind.**
7. **Fix:** if it's the most-ignored cheese on the board, it's probably fine —
   just too mild next to whatever else you served. Move it beside the fruit.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Emmentaler AOP (Switzerland)** | Whole Foods, cheese counters, grocery | The real thing. Sweeter and nuttier than the imitations. |
| **Emmi Emmentaler** | Grocery cheese case | Widely distributed Swiss brand. Reliable. |
| **Jarlsberg** | Most grocery stores | Norwegian, sweeter and softer, holes included. Widely liked. |
| **French Emmental** | Grocery | Milder still. Fine, less character. |
| **Baby Swiss** | Grocery deli | American, softer and much milder. Smaller holes. |
| **Gruyère** | Cheese counters | The step up in flavor — see its own page. |

**Label check:** "Emmentaler AOP" and Swiss origin for the original. Generic
"Swiss cheese" at a US deli counter is usually a domestic imitation — perfectly
edible, much blander.

**Skip:** pre-sliced deli Swiss for a board. It dries at the edges and reads as
a sandwich ingredient.

## Quick FAQ

**Why does Swiss cheese have holes?**
A bacterium called *Propionibacterium freudenreichii* produces carbon dioxide
during aging. The gas can't escape the dense paste, so it forms bubbles.

**Emmental or Gruyère?**
Emmental is mild and sweet; Gruyère is salty and savory. Emmental for a cautious
crowd, Gruyère for a cheese board.

**Is Jarlsberg the same thing?**
No — it's Norwegian, made with a similar process but sweeter and softer. Many
people prefer it.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Trim it. It's thin but not pleasant.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
