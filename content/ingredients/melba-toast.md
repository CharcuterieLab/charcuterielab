## The short version

Thin bread, toasted, sliced thinner, and toasted again until it's completely
dry. It tastes of almost nothing and snaps like a cracker, which makes it the
traditional base for pâté and mousse. Cheap, retro, and better than its
reputation.

## What it is

Named for the opera singer Nellie Melba and generally credited to Escoffier,
who was making a very thin dry toast for her while she was ill. The method:
toast a slice, split it horizontally through the middle while it's hot, then
bake the two halves until they curl and dry through.

**Taste:** faintly bready, otherwise neutral
**Texture:** dry, brittle, snaps into shards
**Look:** thin pale rectangles or rounds, often slightly curled

Total dehydration is the mechanism. Melba toast is the driest bread product on
a board — drier than crostini, drier than crispbread. That means it stays crisp
under a wet topping longer than a bread base has any right to, while still
tasting like bread rather than cracker dough.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Chicken liver pâté** | The classic pairing and still the best one. Rich and soft needs dry and plain. |
| **Salmon or trout mousse** | Delicate, expensive and easily overwhelmed. This adds nothing, which is the point. |
| **Soft ripened cheese** | Brie and Camembert get a neutral base with a hint of toast. |
| **Tapenade** | Intense, salty and oily. The toast keeps out of its way. |
| **Egg and tuna salad** | Retro on purpose. It's what these were served with for fifty years. |
| **Caviar and crème fraîche** | A legitimate blini substitute when you don't want to make blini. |
| **Whipped butter and radish** | Three ingredients, no cheese, always finished first. |

**Skip it with:** thick dips — melba toast is thin and rigid and snaps in half
immediately. And skip it as a showpiece: it's a supporting player, and a board
made entirely of it looks like a hospital tray.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Lean into the retro.** Fan them beside a pâté with cornichons and grainy
   mustard and the whole corner reads as a deliberate 1970s dinner-party
   reference rather than a cheap cracker.
2. **Serve 3–4 per person.**
3. **Handle them carefully.** They're brittle and half the box arrives broken.
4. **Put them next to the softest, richest thing on the board** — that's the
   job.
5. **Keep a second, sturdier base out.** Anyone going for the dip will snap
   these.
6. **Make your own:** thin-sliced sandwich bread, crusts off, 300°F for about
   20 minutes, flipping once, until dry and pale gold. Costs pennies and tastes
   better.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Old London Melba Toast, Classic** | Grocery, cracker aisle | The one most people picture. Plain and reliable. |
| **Old London Melba Toast, Sesame** | Same aisle | Adds a nutty note. Good with tapenade and hummus. |
| **Old London Melba Snacks (rounds)** | Same aisle | Small rounds instead of rectangles. Better for one-bite hors d'oeuvres. |
| **Devonsheer Melba Rounds** | Grocery, Amazon | The other long-running brand. Slightly thicker. |
| **Store brand melba toast** | Kroger, Walmart | Essentially identical. Buy on price. |
| **Thin-sliced white or wheat bread** | Any grocery | Make your own. Fifteen minutes and much better texture. |

**Label check:** flour, water, oil, salt and yeast. Anything else is a flavored
cracker pretending to be melba toast.

**Skip:** flavored varieties on a board with pâté or mousse. The whole
selling point of this product is that it doesn't interfere.

## Quick FAQ

**What is melba toast actually for?**
Pâté, mousse and soft rich spreads. It's a neutral, very dry base that lets an
expensive topping be the thing you taste.

**Melba toast or crostini?**
Crostini taste better on their own; melba toast disappears more completely.
For pâté, melba. For anything with tomato, crostini.

**Can I make it at home?**
Yes. Thin-sliced bread, crusts removed, 300°F for about 20 minutes until dry
through.

**How many per person?**
3–4. Put a sturdier base out too, for the dips.

**Is it healthier than crackers?**
Slightly lower in fat, since there's no shortening — but it's still bread. Not
a meaningful difference on a cheese board.
