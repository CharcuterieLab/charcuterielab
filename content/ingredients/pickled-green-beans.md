## The short version

Also called dilly beans. Whole green beans pickled with dill, garlic and often
chilli — long enough to stand upright in a glass, which is unusual on a board
where everything else lies flat. They snap harder than a cucumber pickle and
they're a genuinely better vehicle for cheese.

## What it is

Whole green beans blanched and packed in a hot vinegar brine with dill, garlic,
mustard seed, peppercorns and frequently a dried chilli. Southern American in
origin, now widely made by small producers.

**Taste:** sour, garlicky, dill-forward, sometimes hot
**Texture:** very snappy, firmer than a cucumber pickle
**Look:** long bright green beans, often bundled upright

The mechanism is shape. A board is a horizontal composition and almost everything
on it is flat; a bundle of beans standing in a small jar or glass adds height,
which is the single easiest way to make a board look composed rather than laid
out. The acid function is the same as any pickle — the presentation is what's
different.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp cheese and sour crunch, with the bean sturdy enough to hold a slice of cheese on it. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Wrap a bean in a smear of goat cheese and prosciutto — a complete bite that stands up. |
| **Pastrami and smoked meat** | Deli logic. Fat and smoke cut by dill and vinegar. |
| **Smoked gouda** | Smoke and sour, both assertive. |
| **Pimento cheese** | Southern on Southern. Use the bean as the scoop. |
| **Bloody Marys** | Its most famous use, and worth knowing if the board is a brunch board. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses and Italian cured meats. Dill and garlic are
loud.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Stand them upright** in a small glass or narrow jar. That's the whole
   presentation argument.
2. **Or lay them in a fan** beside the cheddar.
3. **Drain and blot.** The brine spreads.
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person** — two or three beans.
5. **Trim the stem ends** if they're ragged.
6. **Serve cold.**
7. **Fix:** limp? An old jar, or over-blanched before pickling. They should snap
   audibly.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dilly beans** | Farmers markets, Southern grocers, specialty | The traditional name. Usually the best. |
| **Tillen Farms or Talk O' Texas** | Grocery, cocktail aisle | Widely available. Crisp and reliable. |
| **Spicy pickled green beans** | Grocery, specialty | Chilli-spiked. Excellent with cheddar. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Blanch beans 2 minutes, pack with dill, garlic and chilli, cover with hot vinegar brine. Refrigerator pickle, ready in 2 days. |
| **Pickled asparagus** | Specialty, spring | Same idea, more elegant, more expensive. |

**Label check:** the beans should look bright green and firm through the glass.
Olive-drab beans are over-processed.

**Skip:** anything sold in a can.

## Quick FAQ

**What are dilly beans?**
Green beans pickled with dill, garlic and often chilli. The Southern American name
for pickled green beans.

**How do I serve them on a board?**
Stand them upright in a small glass or narrow jar. It adds height to a board where
everything else is flat.

**Are they spicy?**
Some versions are — most jars include a dried chilli. Mild versions are widely
available.

**Can I make them at home?**
Yes. Blanch the beans briefly, pack with dill and garlic, cover with hot vinegar
brine, and refrigerate for two days.

**How much per person?**
Two or three beans, about three-quarters of an ounce.
