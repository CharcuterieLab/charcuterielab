## The short version

Cold poached shrimp and a bowl of red cocktail sauce. It's the most American
party appetizer there is, and next to a cheese board it reads instantly as
special occasion. The rule is simple: keep it on ice, and keep it at one end
of the board, away from strong cheese.

## What it is

Cocktail shrimp are large shrimp, peeled except for the tail, cooked in
seasoned water and chilled. They're served with cocktail sauce: ketchup or
chili sauce sharpened with horseradish and lemon. Grocery stores sell them
cooked and ready, often as a ring with sauce in the middle.

**Taste:** sweet, briny and clean, with horseradish heat from the sauce
**Texture:** firm, snappy and juicy when cooked right
**Look:** curled pink-and-white shrimp with red tails, around a red sauce

Shrimp turn pink because heat breaks apart a protein that holds the red
pigment astaxanthin in a bluish-gray complex. Once freed, the red shows.
Overcooking makes them tough because their muscle proteins tighten fast;
2 to 3 minutes in near-boiling water is enough. Horseradish works because
its sharp compounds hit the nose and cut through sweet, rich shellfish.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cocktail sauce** | Tomato acid and horseradish heat lift sweet shrimp. |
| **Lemon wedges** | Bright acid without the heat. |
| **Smoked salmon and cream cheese** | Make a seafood corner. Same brine, different textures. |
| **Water crackers** | A plain, crisp base that doesn't compete. |
| **Capers and dill pickles** | Briny, sour bites that echo the sauce. |
| **Champagne, Sauvignon Blanc or a crisp lager** | Bubbles and acid love shellfish. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and smoked cheese next to it. Their strong
aromas clash with delicate shellfish. Put the shrimp at the opposite end.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Thaw frozen cooked shrimp** overnight in the fridge, or in a colander
   under cold running water for 10 minutes.
2. **Pat them very dry.** Water dilutes the sauce and makes the board soggy.
3. **Fill a shallow bowl with crushed ice,** cover it with a layer of plastic
   wrap or a smaller plate, and arrange shrimp on top.
4. **Serve 3 or 4 large shrimp per person.**
5. **Set the sauce in the center** and lemon wedges around it.
6. **Fix:** if shrimp taste bland, toss them with a squeeze of lemon and a
   pinch of salt 10 minutes before serving.

**Label check:** the numbers (16/20, 21/25) are shrimp per pound. Lower
means bigger. "Wild-caught" and "farm-raised" both work; "previously frozen"
is normal.

**Skip:** salad-size shrimp (51/60 and up). They're too small to dip and
look skimpy on a board.

## Quick FAQ

**Can you put shrimp on a charcuterie board?**
Yes, as long as it stays cold. Serve it in a bowl or tray of ice at one end
of the board, or on a separate seafood platter beside it.

**How long can shrimp cocktail sit out?**
No more than 2 hours, and less in a warm room. Keep it on ice and refill
from the fridge.

**How many shrimp per person?**
3 or 4 large shrimp per person as part of a board. Plan 5 or 6 if it's the
main appetizer.

**Should I buy cooked or raw shrimp?**
Cooked, peeled shrimp with tails on is the easiest. If you cook your own,
poach large shrimp for 2 to 3 minutes, then chill them in ice water.

**What size shrimp is best for shrimp cocktail?**
16 to 20 or 21 to 25 count per pound. Smaller shrimp are hard to dip; bigger
ones look generous.
