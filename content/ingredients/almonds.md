## The short version

The nut that goes with everything and offends nobody. Almonds are mild enough not
to compete with cheese, crunchy enough to break up a board of soft things, and
cheap enough to use generously. The only real decision is roasted and salted
(yes) versus raw (no).

## What it is

The seed of a stone fruit, related to peaches and apricots. Sold raw, roasted,
salted, smoked, blanched, sliced and slivered. Californian almonds account for
most of the world's supply; Spanish marconas are a distinct, softer variety with
their own page.

**Taste:** mild, sweet, faintly bitter, marzipan on the finish
**Texture:** hard and crisp, with a firm snap
**Look:** brown-skinned ovals, or pale ivory if blanched

The mechanism is neutrality plus structure. Most board ingredients are strongly
flavoured; almonds provide crunch and salt without an opinion. That makes them
the connective tissue of a board — the thing that fills gaps and separates
strong flavours from each other without adding a third argument.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego** | Almond and sheep milk are the Spanish pairing. Salt bridges them. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Crunch against crumble; the almond's mildness lets the cheddar lead. |
| **Dried apricots** | Botanically related fruit and nut. The aromas are almost the same compound. |
| **Grapes** | Wet and sweet against dry and salty. |
| **Prosciutto** | Fat and salt need something to break them; almonds do it without adding flavour. |
| **Honey and blue cheese** | Almonds are the crunch in the classic three-part bite. |

**Skip it with:** nothing — but don't put out three kinds of nut. One or two is a
board; four is a bar snack.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy roasted and salted.** Raw almonds are chalky and taste of nothing on a
   board.
2. **Scatter into gaps** or serve in a small bowl. Almonds are dry, so unlike
   marconas they won't grease anything.
3. **Toast them yourself** if you bought raw: 350°F for 10 minutes, salt while
   warm. It's a real improvement.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
5. **Use sliced or slivered almonds** for rolling a goat cheese log rather than
   whole ones.
6. **Keep them sealed.** Almonds go soft in humid kitchens.
7. **Fix:** stale and soft? 300°F for ten minutes restores the crunch.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Roasted salted almonds** | Any grocery, Costco | The default. Buy the big bag; they keep. |
| **Blanched almonds** | Grocery, baking aisle | Skinless, pale, cleaner-looking on a white board. |
| **Rosemary or garlic almonds** | Trader Joe's, Whole Foods | Flavoured versions. Good, but they compete with the cheese. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Whole Foods, Spanish delis | The Spanish upgrade — softer, sweeter, oil-fried. Worth it. |
| **Smoked almonds** | Any grocery | A different ingredient. Strong, and better beside cheddar than beside delicate cheese. |
| **Sliced / slivered almonds** | Baking aisle | For coating cheese logs, not for eating loose. |

**Label check:** "dry roasted" means no added oil; "roasted" usually means oil.
Both are fine. Check the salt level — some brands are very heavy.

**Skip:** raw almonds for board use, and anything flavoured with sweet coatings
unless it's going in the dessert corner.

## Quick FAQ

**Raw or roasted almonds for a charcuterie board?**
Roasted and salted. Raw almonds are chalky and add nothing.

**Are marcona almonds better?**
Softer, sweeter and richer — a real upgrade, at roughly twice the price. Regular
almonds are still the reliable default.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce, which is roughly 20 almonds.

**Should I toast almonds myself?**
Only if you bought raw. 350°F for ten minutes, salted while warm.

**What cheese goes with almonds?**
Manchego above all, plus aged cheddar, blue cheese, and aged gouda.
