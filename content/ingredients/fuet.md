## The short version

A thin, pale Catalan salami, about as thick as a thumb, dusted white with an
edible surface mould. It's mild, faintly nutty and made for snapping into
pieces by hand. On a board it's the item people eat without thinking.

## What it is

*Fuet* means whip in Catalan — a description of the long thin shape. Lean pork
is ground fine, seasoned lightly with black pepper and garlic, cased in a
narrow casing and dried for two to four weeks. The white bloom on the outside is
*Penicillium* mould, deliberately encouraged, exactly as on a French saucisson.

**Taste:** mild, nutty, gently peppery, faintly tangy
**Texture:** firm but tender, drier at the edges, snaps cleanly
**Look:** pale pink-beige stick, chalky white mould coating

The narrow diameter is the mechanism. A thin sausage dries in weeks rather than
months, so fuet never develops the deep fermented tang of a fat salami — it
stays mild and lightly acidic. That's why it eats like a snack rather than a
statement, and why it's cheap.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Garrotxa or Manchego** | Catalan or Spanish cheese with a Catalan sausage. |
| **Pan con tomate** | The Catalan default. Bread, tomato, oil, fuet. |
| **Olives and pickled guindillas** | Brine and acid against a plain, lightly fermented stick. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Salt and fat with a lean, mild sausage. |
| **Membrillo or fig jam** | Sweetness against a plain salty stick. |
| **Cava** | Catalan sparkling wine, and the bubbles suit a snacking meat. |
| **Cold lager or vermut** | Spanish aperitivo drinking. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses if the fuet is garlic-heavy. Otherwise
it's about as easy-going as cured meat gets.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Snap it, don't slice it.** Fuet is traditionally broken into rough
   two-inch lengths by hand. It looks better and it's what people expect.
2. **Or slice it thin on a steep diagonal** for longer ovals.
3. **Leave the white mould on.** It's edible, it's part of the flavour, and
   scrubbing it off makes the sausage look worse and taste blander.
4. **Temper 15 minutes.**
5. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
6. **Put it somewhere people can graze** — a small glass or a corner. It's a
   snacking meat, not a composed one.
7. **Fix:** hard and over-dry at the ends? Trim an inch. Thin sausages dry from
   the tips inward.

| **Snack-stick salami** | Most grocery | Not the same thing, but the same role on a casual board. |

**Label check:** an even chalky white bloom, not grey or green patches. Pork,
salt, pepper and garlic should be most of the list.

**Skip:** peeling the mould off. It's the single most common mistake with fuet
and saucisson alike.

## Quick FAQ

**Can you eat the white coating?**
Yes. It's an edible *Penicillium* mould, deliberately cultivated, and it adds
flavour. Leave it on.

**Do I slice fuet or snap it?**
Traditionally snapped into rough pieces by hand. Thin diagonal slices also work.

**Fuet or saucisson sec?**
Fuet is thinner, milder and Catalan; saucisson is thicker, tangier and French.
Fuet is the more casual of the two.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz. People graze on it, so err generous.

**Why is it so cheap?**
It's thin, so it dries in weeks rather than months. Less time means less cost.
