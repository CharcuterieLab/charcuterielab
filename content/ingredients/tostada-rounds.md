## The short version

Corn tortillas fried or baked flat and crisp. Unlike every other gluten-free
base, a tostada gives you a wide, flat, unbroken surface — so you can actually
build a topped bite on it rather than just dip. Cheap, corn-sweet, and almost
nobody puts them on a board.

## What it is

A tostada is a corn tortilla cooked until rigid. The masa is made from corn
that's been nixtamalized — simmered in an alkaline solution — which is what
gives it that distinctive sweet-toasted corn flavor and makes the nutrients
available. Small "tostada rounds" are sold specifically for appetizers.

**Taste:** toasted corn, mildly sweet, lightly salted
**Texture:** rigid, snappy, breaks into shards
**Look:** pale gold discs, sometimes blistered, 3–5 inches across

Surface area is the mechanism. Every other gluten-free base is either small
(nut thins), curved (plantain chips) or crumbly (almond flour). A tostada round
is a flat plate you can stack three things on, which is why it's the only one
here that supports a composed, photographable bite.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Queso fresco, cotija, crema** | Salty crumbly cheese on sweet toasted corn. The traditional and still the best. |
| **Refried beans and black beans** | Dense spread on a rigid base — it holds where a chip would snap. |
| **Chorizo and cured Spanish meats** | Fat and paprika against sweet corn is a very old pairing. |
| **Avocado with lime and salt** | Simple, green, acidic. The corn does the seasoning. |
| **Pickled red onion and jalapeño** | Acid and heat cut the starch and wake the whole bite up. |
| **Aged cheddar and Manchego** | Corn's sweetness works with sharp, nutty cheese better than you'd expect. |
| **Beer, margaritas, dry rosé** | Anything acidic or bubbly. |

**Skip it with:** brie, burrata and other delicate European cheeses — corn is
sweet and assertive and it wins. And skip them if the board is strictly
Mediterranean; the flavor is from a different tradition and it shows.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Break them into quarters or sixths.** A whole tostada is a plate, not a
   bite. Broken shards look better and portion properly.
2. **Serve 2–3 rounds per person, broken** — so 8 to 12 pieces.
3. **Warm them 5 minutes at 350°F.** Corn flavor is dramatically better warm,
   and this is the single biggest improvement available on this page.
4. **Build three or four topped ones and leave the rest plain.** A tostada with
   beans, cheese and pickled onion shows guests what to do.
5. **Top at the last minute.** Anything wet softens the center within fifteen
   minutes, and a soggy tostada is unpleasant.
6. **DIY:** cut corn tortillas into quarters, brush with oil, 400°F for 8–10
   minutes. Cheaper and better than the bag.

| **Charras Tostadas** | Latin markets, grocery international aisle | Better corn flavor. Worth seeking out. |
| **Guerrero Tostadas** | Grocery, Latin aisle | Widely stocked, consistent. |
| **Tostada rounds / mini tostadas** | Latin markets, restaurant supply | Pre-sized for appetizers. The convenient version. |
| **Corn tortillas** | Any grocery | The DIY route — cut, oil, bake. Best flavor by a distance. |
| **Fresh masa tortillas** | Tortilleria, Latin market | If you have a tortilleria nearby, this is worth the trip. |

**Label check:** corn, lime (or "calcium hydroxide"), water, salt. That's real
nixtamalized masa. Long ingredient lists mean added flour and preservatives.

**Skip:** flour tostadas — they exist, they're not gluten-free, and they defeat
the purpose.

## Quick FAQ

**Are tostadas gluten-free?**
Corn tostadas are. Flour tostadas are not — check the package.

**Tostadas or tortilla chips?**
Tostadas are flat and rigid with a big surface, so you can build on them.
Tortilla chips are for scooping. Different jobs.

**How many per person?**
2–3 rounds, broken into quarters — about 8 to 12 pieces.

**Should I warm them?**
Yes. Five minutes at 350°F makes a bigger difference here than on any other
base in this tier.

**Can I make them from tortillas?**
Yes, and you should. Quarter them, brush with oil, bake at 400°F for 8–10
minutes.
