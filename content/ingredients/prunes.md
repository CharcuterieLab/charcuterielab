## The short version

The reputation is the only problem. A good Agen prune is dense, dark, winey and
faintly smoky — closer to a date with acid than to anything medicinal. It's the
best dried fruit you can put beside blue cheese or fatty pork, and it costs
almost nothing.

## What it is

Dried plums, specifically from prune varieties like d'Agen that have enough sugar
to dry with the pit in. **Pruneaux d'Agen** from southwest France are the
benchmark; California prunes are the everyday version and perfectly good.

**Taste:** deep, winey, caramel, faintly smoky
**Texture:** dense, moist, sticky
**Look:** near-black, glossy, wrinkled

The mechanism is dark sugar plus retained acid — the same combination that makes
port work with stilton. Plums keep more acid than grapes when dried, so a prune
has the sweetness to blunt salt and enough sharpness to keep it from turning
cloying. That's why it partners the strongest cheeses better than a date does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Roquefort and gorgonzola** | The best dried-fruit-and-blue pairing there is. Winey depth against salt, with acid keeping it clean. |
| **Aged gouda** | Both taste of caramel; the prune brings the fruit the cheese lacks. |
| **Coppa, bacon-wrapped, or pork rillettes** | Prune and pork is a French fixture. Fat and dark sugar. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin against sticky sweetness — a classic pairing in French bread. |
| **Armagnac-soaked, with anything** | Soak them in Armagnac for an hour and they become a luxury ingredient at no real cost. |
| **Aged sheep cheeses** | Salty, dry, nutty against moist and dark. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. The flavour is too dark and heavy for
burrata or ricotta.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy pitted.** Life is short.
2. **No other prep needed** — straight from the bag.
3. **Halve them** if they're large, cut-side up. It stops them looking like
   dark lumps.
4. **Soak in Armagnac, brandy or strong tea** for an hour for the upgraded
   version. Drain and pat dry.
5. **Serve 0.5–1 oz per person** — two or three.
6. **Stuff them** with blue cheese, the same way you'd stuff a date.
7. **Fix:** dry and hard? Ten minutes in hot water or tea. They plump right back.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pruneaux d'Agen** | French delis, Whole Foods, online | The benchmark. Large, moist, complex. Worth the premium. |
| **Sunsweet pitted prunes** | Any grocery | Reliable, moist, cheap. The everyday choice. |
| **Prunes in Armagnac** | French specialty, online | Sold in jars. A luxury board ingredient straight out of the jar. |
| **Stuffed prunes (mi-cuit)** | French delis | Half-dried, very moist, sometimes filled with prune paste. |

**Label check:** buy moist-pack. Prunes labelled "bite size" or feeling hard
through the bag are old.

**Skip:** anything with added sugar or corn syrup. Prunes are sweet enough.

## Quick FAQ

**Are prunes just dried plums?**
Yes — specifically plum varieties with enough sugar to dry whole. "Dried plums"
is the marketing name for the same thing.

**What cheese goes with prunes?**
Roquefort, gorgonzola and other blues, plus aged gouda and aged sheep cheeses.

**How do I make prunes look better on a board?**
Halve them and set them cut-side up, or soak them in Armagnac and serve them
glossy.

**Are Agen prunes worth the extra money?**
Yes, if the board is the point. They're markedly moister and more complex than
supermarket prunes.

**How many per person?**
Two or three. They're rich and dense.
