## The short version

Small, soft, yeast-raised pancakes, traditionally made with buckwheat flour.
They're the classic base for caviar and smoked salmon, and on a board full of
cold crisp things they're the only item that's warm and yielding. That contrast
is what makes them worth the effort.

## What it is

Russian blini are a leavened pancake — yeast, not baking powder — usually made
with a mix of buckwheat and wheat flour. The buckwheat gives an earthy, almost
nutty flavor that plain pancakes lack. Cocktail blini are the small version,
about two inches across.

**Taste:** earthy, faintly sour, buttery
**Texture:** soft, spongy, tender
**Look:** small pale discs, slightly domed, speckled from the buckwheat

Warmth and give are the mechanism, and they're rare on a board for a practical
reason: almost everything else is served cold and crisp because that's what
survives sitting out. A warm soft blini is a deliberate exception, and the
contrast it creates against cold crème fraîche and cold fish is the entire
reason the pairing became canonical.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Caviar and crème fraîche** | The pairing, unchanged for two centuries. Warm, soft, cold, salty, sour — four things at once. |
| **Smoked salmon and dill** | The accessible version, and nearly as good. Buckwheat's earthiness suits smoke. |
| **Sour cream and chives** | The plain Russian way. Simple and very good. |
| **Trout roe or salmon roe** | A tenth of the price of caviar and it works the same way. |
| **Cultured butter and flaky salt** | Warm blini with good butter needs nothing else. |
| **Herring and pickled onion** | The northern European board direction. |
| **Champagne, vodka, dry riesling** | The traditional drinks, and the acid and cold are doing real work. |

**Skip it with:** aged hard cheeses and cured meats. Blini are soft, warm and
delicate; a shard of parmesan or a slice of salami on one is a texture
mismatch. Keep them in the fish-and-dairy corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve them warm.** Cold blini are a different, sadder food. This is the
   only rule that really matters.
2. **Warm frozen or refrigerated blini** at 300°F for 5 minutes, covered with
   foil so they don't dry out.
3. **Put out a bowl of crème fraîche and let guests build.** Pre-topped blini
   go soggy and look tired within fifteen minutes.
4. **Serve 3–4 per person.** They're small.
5. **Keep them in a napkin-lined bowl** rather than flat on the board — it
   holds the heat and it looks deliberate.
6. **Replenish in small batches** so there's always a warm one.
7. **Fix:** dried-out blini can't be revived. Warm fewer, more often.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Frozen or refrigerated cocktail blini** | Grocery freezer, specialty and gourmet sections, Whole Foods | Availability varies by store and season — check the freezer near the smoked salmon. |
| **Eastern European groceries** | Local Russian, Polish or Ukrainian market | The best source by far, and much cheaper. |
| **Buckwheat flour** | Bob's Red Mill, most grocery baking aisles | The DIY route. Buckwheat and wheat flour, yeast, milk, egg, butter. |
| **Brioche toast points** | See that page | The substitute if you can't find blini. Rich rather than warm-soft, but the same role. |
| **Melba toast rounds** | Grocery cracker aisle | The lean substitute. Crisp instead of soft, but it's what caviar service uses when blini aren't available. |

**Label check:** buckwheat should appear in the flour list. All-wheat "blini"
are just small pancakes and they lose the earthy note the pairing depends on.

**Skip:** sweet pancakes or crêpes as a substitute. They're sugared, and sugar
with caviar is wrong.

## Quick FAQ

**What are blini?**
Small yeast-raised Russian pancakes, traditionally made with buckwheat flour.
The classic base for caviar.

**Do I have to serve them warm?**
Effectively yes. Cold blini lose the contrast that makes the pairing work.

**How many per person?**
3–4. They're about two inches across.

**Where do I buy them?**
Check the freezer section near the smoked salmon, or an Eastern European
grocery. Availability is inconsistent in mainstream supermarkets.

**What if I can't find them?**
Brioche toast points or melba toast rounds. Both are used in caviar service and
both are easier to buy.
