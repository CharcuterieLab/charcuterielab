## The short version

A coarse-ground, pressed salami from southern Italy, usually spiked with chili.
Chunkier and more rustic than Genoa salami, with visible fat and a flattened
oval shape. Buy *piccante* — the heat is what makes it distinctive.

## What it is

The name comes from *soppressare*, to press. Coarsely chopped pork is seasoned
— chili and paprika in the south, black pepper and wine further north — cased,
then weighted during drying, which flattens the salami and drives out moisture.
Soppressata di Calabria DOP is the protected version.

**Taste:** rich, porky, peppery, often genuinely hot
**Texture:** coarse and chunky, firm, chewier than fine-ground salami
**Look:** flattened oval slices, deep red with large irregular white fat pieces

Coarse grinding is the mechanism, and it changes the eating experience more than
the seasoning does. Fine-ground salami reads as uniform and smooth; soppressata's
large fat and meat pieces give every slice a different texture. That rusticity
is the point — it's the salami that tastes handmade.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged provolone or pecorino** | Sharp salty cheese against fat and chili. The southern Italian plate. |
| **Fresh mozzarella or burrata** | Cool milk quenching the heat. The best contrast for the hot version. |
| **Honey or fig jam** | Sweetness against chili — the pairing most people don't expect and always like. |
| **Olive oil crackers and taralli** | Plain Mediterranean bases, nothing competing. |
| **Marinated olives and roasted peppers** | Oily, briny, sweet neighbors. |
| **Pickled cherry peppers** | Acid and more heat, for people who want it. |
| **Aglianico, Primitivo, Nero d'Avola** | Southern Italian reds with enough body for chili. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses other than mozzarella — the chili and
the fat bury anything subtle. And don't put it beside the brie.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice it thin — about ⅛ inch.** Thicker and the chew becomes work.
2. **Fan the slices in an overlapping arc** so the coarse fat pattern shows.
   That marbling is the visual identity.
3. **Temper 20 minutes** so the fat softens.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Put something cool and mild beside it** — fresh mozzarella, honey, or
   both — if you bought the hot version.
6. **Peel the casing** if it's plastic; leave it if it's natural and edible
   (it'll be papery and dusted white).
7. **Fix:** too hot for the room? Serve it with more honey and more cheese
   rather than moving it.

| **Fra' Mani Soppressata** | Specialty grocers, online | Berkeley-made, exceptional. |
| **Genoa salami** | Any grocery | The milder, finer-ground alternative — see its own page. |

**Label check:** *piccante* or "hot" for the chili version, *dolce* for mild.
Look for large irregular fat pieces — that's the coarse grind you're paying for.

**Skip:** anything uniformly pink with tiny even fat flecks. That's a fine
grind wearing the name.

## Quick FAQ

**Soppressata or Genoa salami?**
Soppressata is coarse-ground, pressed flat and usually spicy. Genoa is
fine-ground, round and mild. Soppressata is the more interesting board meat.

**Is it always spicy?**
No — *dolce* versions exist and are mild. But the chili is what distinguishes
the southern style.

**Can I eat the casing?**
Natural casing is edible but papery, and most people peel it. Plastic casing
must come off.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of several meats.

**What cools the heat?**
Fresh mozzarella, honey, or both. Milk fat and sugar both blunt capsaicin.
