## The short version

A soft, buttery Danish cheese shot through with tiny irregular holes. Mild,
creamy and completely inoffensive. It's the cheese to include when you don't
know your guests — and the one to skip when you do.

## What it is

Havarti is a washed-curd cheese: some whey is drained and replaced with water
during making, which removes lactic acid and leaves the cheese sweeter and
milder. It's aged only about three months. The small irregular eyes throughout
the paste come from the culture rather than from gas-producing bacteria.

**Taste:** buttery, milky, faintly sweet, gently tangy
**Texture:** soft, supple, sliceable, almost springy
**Look:** pale cream with many small irregular holes, no rind

Curd washing is the mechanism, and it explains the whole personality. Washing
out lactic acid removes the sharp edge a cheese would otherwise develop, so
Havarti stays mild and buttery no matter how long you leave it. That's a
deliberate design choice — this cheese was built not to challenge anyone.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Butter crackers** | Mild on mild, but the salt in the cracker gives the bite an edge. |
| **Apple and grapes** | Fresh acid against a cheese that has almost none of its own. |
| **Honey and fig jam** | Sweetness gives it somewhere to go. It needs the help. |
| **Ham and turkey** | The sandwich pairing, and it works on a board. |
| **Cocktail rye and pumpernickel** | Danish cheese, Danish bread. |
| **Dill Havarti with smoked salmon** | The flavored version has a real use. |
| **Light lager, dry Riesling, cider** | Nothing assertive required. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese, pecorino and anything sharp — Havarti has no
defense. It also disappears entirely next to aged cheddar, so give it distance.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut into thin slices or small cubes.** It's soft enough to slice cleanly
   and it holds a shape.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.** It gets noticeably creamier.
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Use it as the safe cheese** — the one a cautious guest reaches for. Every
   mixed board benefits from having one.
5. **Put it beside the fruit and the honey**, not beside the aged cheeses.
6. **Consider the flavored versions.** Dill, jalapeño and horseradish Havarti
   are among the few flavored cheeses that genuinely improve on the original.
7. **Fix:** if it's being ignored, it's too plain for the company it's in.
   Drizzle honey over it or move it next to the jam.

| **Arla Havarti** | Grocery cheese case | Another Danish standard, widely stocked. |
| **Dill Havarti** | Grocery cheese case | Genuinely good with smoked fish and cucumber. |
| **Creamy Havarti** | Grocery | A softer, higher-fat version. Better on a board than the standard. |
| **Boar's Head Havarti** | Deli counter | Cut to order, which beats pre-packaged. |
| **Fontina or Monterey Jack** | Grocery cheese case | Similar mild semi-soft alternatives — see their own pages. |

**Label check:** "creamy Havarti" or a higher fat percentage if you have the
choice. The standard version is drier and duller.

**Skip:** pre-sliced Havarti in sandwich squares. It dries at the edges and
reads as deli meat's companion rather than a board cheese.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is Havarti so mild?**
It's a washed-curd cheese — lactic acid is rinsed out during making, so it
never develops sharpness.

**Is it worth putting on a cheese board?**
Yes, as the safe option. Every mixed board should have one item nobody
hesitates over.

**Havarti or Monterey Jack?**
Very similar role. Havarti is slightly richer and more buttery; Jack is
slightly firmer.

**Are the flavored versions any good?**
Unusually, yes. Dill and jalapeño Havarti are among the better flavored cheeses
on the market.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
