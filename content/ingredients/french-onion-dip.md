## The short version

Sour cream, onion and salt, served cold next to a bowl of chips. French
onion dip is the original American party dip, and it still outsells almost
everything in the dip aisle. On a board it's the savory, tangy anchor of the
snack corner, and nothing makes a kettle chip taste better.

## What it is

The story goes that a California home cook stirred Lipton's dry onion soup
mix into sour cream in 1954, and the recipe spread through newspapers and
onto the soup box. It's called French onion after the soup it was based on,
not because it's French. Today you can buy it in tubs or make it with fresh
caramelized onions.

**Taste:** tangy, savory and salty, with sweet roasted onion
**Texture:** thick, smooth and cool, with tiny bits of onion
**Look:** pale beige with brown onion flecks

The magic is browned onion. Dried or caramelized onions have gone through
the Maillard reaction and caramelization, which turn plain sugars and amino
acids into deep, savory, slightly sweet flavor compounds. Cold sour cream's
fat and lactic tang carry that flavor and coat the salt on a chip, which is
why the pairing tastes bigger than either part.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Kettle or ridged potato chips** | Thick, crunchy chips scoop without snapping. The classic. |
| **Mini pretzels and bagel chips** | Salty, toasty crunch. |
| **Carrots, celery and cucumber** | Cool, crisp vegetables for the lighter option. |
| **Beef jerky** | Smoky, savory meat and onion. A game-day combo. |
| **Pepperoni** | Spicy fat next to cool cream. |
| **Lager, a dry cider or a Martini** | Crisp, cold drinks for a rich dip. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses on the same plate. The onion
overwhelms them. Keep it in the chip corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make it a day ahead** if you're mixing from a packet. The onion needs
   time to soften.
2. **Stir it well** and spoon it into a small bowl.
3. **Top it with chopped chives** or a few crispy fried onions for crunch.
4. **Serve about 2 tablespoons per person.**
5. **Put the chips right beside it,** ridged or kettle cooked.
6. **Fix:** if it's too thick, stir in a spoonful of milk. If it's bland,
   add a pinch of salt and a few drops of Worcestershire.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is it called French onion dip?**
It's named after French onion soup, because the original was made by
stirring dry onion soup mix into sour cream. It isn't actually French.

**How do you make French onion dip fast?**
Stir a packet of dry onion soup mix into 16 oz of sour cream and chill it
for at least an hour so the onion softens.

**What do you serve with French onion dip?**
Ridged potato chips and kettle chips first, then pretzels, carrots, celery
and bagel chips.

**Can you make it ahead?**
Yes. It's better made a day ahead, because the dried onion needs time to
rehydrate and flavor the sour cream.

**How long can French onion dip sit out?**
Up to 2 hours at room temperature. Nest the bowl in ice for a long party.
