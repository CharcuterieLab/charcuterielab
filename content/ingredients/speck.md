## The short version

Boned pork leg from the Italian Alps, salt-cured with juniper and bay, then
cold-smoked over beech and aged for months. It sits exactly between prosciutto
and German ham — Italian in structure, alpine in flavor — and it's the most
useful smoked meat on a board.

## What it is

Speck Alto Adige IGP comes from South Tyrol, a German-speaking province of
northern Italy. The rule the producers quote is *a little salt, a little smoke,
a lot of fresh air*: hams are boned and flattened, rubbed with salt and a
juniper-heavy spice mix, cold-smoked at under 20°C, then aged 22 weeks.

**Taste:** smoky, juniper-scented, savory, moderately salty
**Texture:** firmer than prosciutto, still yielding
**Look:** deep red muscle, thick white fat edge, dark smoked exterior

Cold smoke is the mechanism, and the temperature is the whole point. Hot
smoking cooks meat and drives smoke hard into the surface; cold smoking below
20°C only perfumes it. That's why speck tastes of smoke as one note among
juniper, salt and pork rather than as a coating.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gruyère, Comté or Fontina** | Alpine ham, alpine cheese. The regional pairing on both sides of the border. |
| **Rye crispbread and dark bread** | Sour earthy rye against juniper and smoke. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Sharp vinegar cutting smoked fat. Essential. |
| **Horseradish or grainy mustard** | Heat and acid, and traditional in South Tyrol. |
| **Apple and pear** | Fresh acid resetting the palate. |
| **Rye bread with butter** | The mountain-hut lunch, and it's very good. |
| **Lagrein, Pinot Bianco, dark beer** | Alto Adige wines, or malt against smoke. |

**Skip it with:** smoked gouda or any other smoked item — one smoked thing per
board. And skip delicate fresh cheeses, which the smoke overwhelms.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice it thin but not paper-thin.** Speck has more structure than
   prosciutto and a slightly thicker slice eats better.
2. **Temper 20 minutes.**
3. **Fold slices into loose quarters** rather than draping. It's firmer and
   holds a fold.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Put the acid next to it** — cornichons, pickled onion, mustard. Smoked fat
   without acid gets heavy by the third bite.
6. **Leave the fat edge on.**
7. **Fix:** if it tastes harshly smoky, it's likely a cheaper hot-smoked
   imitation. Real speck is gentle.

| **Tiroler Speck** | German and Austrian markets | The Austrian equivalent, often smokier. |

**Label check:** the IGP mark and Alto Adige or Südtirol origin. Generic "speck"
in the US sometimes means a fatty cooking cure rather than this ham.

**Skip:** speck sold as a lardon or cooking cube for a board. That's a
different cut and it needs cooking.

## Quick FAQ

**Is speck the same as prosciutto?**
Same idea, different treatment. Speck is boned, spiced with juniper and cold-
smoked; prosciutto is left on the bone and never smoked.

**How smoky is it?**
Gently. Cold smoke below 20°C perfumes the ham rather than coating it.

**Is it Italian or German?**
Both, in a sense. Alto Adige is an Italian province where German is the majority
language, and speck belongs to that culture.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of several meats.

**Do I trim the fat?**
No. The fat carries the smoke and the juniper.
