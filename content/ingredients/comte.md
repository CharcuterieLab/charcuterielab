## The short version

A firm alpine cheese from the Jura mountains, aged six months to three years.
Nutty, brothy, and unusually complex for its price. If you buy one French
cheese for a board and want it to be interesting rather than safe, buy this.

## What it is

Comté is made in enormous 40kg wheels from raw milk of Montbéliarde cows, in
village dairies called *fruitières*. Every wheel is scored out of 20 by a jury
before sale — wheels above 14 get a green band, 12–14 a brown one, and anything
lower can't be sold as Comté at all.

**Taste:** nutty, brothy, faintly fruity, sometimes caramel or hazelnut
**Texture:** firm and dense, supple rather than crumbly, small crystals with
age
**Look:** pale gold to deep yellow, natural brown rind

Seasonality is the mechanism, and it's unusual to find it this visible in a
cheese. Summer wheels are made from milk of cows on mountain pasture and come
out golden and floral; winter wheels come from hay-fed cows and are paler and
nuttier. Two wedges of the same cheese can taste noticeably different, and good
cheesemongers will tell you which you're getting.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette and cornichons** | The French cheese plate. Plain bread, sharp vinegar, complex cheese. |
| **Walnuts and hazelnuts** | Comté's own nutty notes, amplified. |
| **Cured ham — jambon de Bayonne** | Salt and cure against a brothy, savory cheese. |
| **Apple and pear** | Acid and sweetness cutting dense fat. |
| **Whole grain mustard** | Sharp, vinegary, and traditional in the Jura. |
| **Vin Jaune, Chardonnay, dry Riesling** | Jura wines are the regional pairing; Vin Jaune with Comté is a classic. |
| **Dark beer, dry cider** | Both work well against alpine cheeses. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses on the same corner, and very sweet
jams — Comté's complexity gets buried under too much sugar.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut it into thick batons or short wedges**, not thin slices. Its texture
   is supple and a thick piece eats better.
2. **Temper 30 minutes** — this is a cheese with a lot to say and cold mutes
   most of it.
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Ask the counter for the age and season** when you buy, then say it when
   you serve. It's a genuinely interesting thing to know about a cheese.
5. **Put it next to Gruyère if you have both** — the comparison is one of the
   best on any board.
6. **Trim the rind.** It's natural and edible in theory but tough and not
   pleasant.
7. **Fix:** wrap in parchment or wax paper. Comté suffocates in plastic and
   develops off flavors within a week.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Comté AOP, 12 months** | Whole Foods, cheese counters, Costco | The everyday version and a genuine bargain. |
| **Comté AOP, 18–24 months** | Cheese counters, specialty | More crystalline and more complex. The one to buy for a cheese board. |
| **Comté Marcel Petite Fort St Antoine** | Specialty shops, Murray's | Aged in a mountain fort. The famous one, and it earns it. |
| **Costco Comté** | Costco | Real AOP at an unusually good price. |
| **Trader Joe's Comté** | Trader Joe's | Cheap entry point, usually younger. |
| **Beaufort or Gruyère** | Cheese counters | The alpine neighbors — see their own pages. |

**Label check:** the AOP mark and, if you can see it, the green band, which
means the wheel scored above 14 out of 20.

**Skip:** pre-packaged Comté that's been cut for weeks. Alpine cheeses dry at
the face fast and lose the supple texture.

## Quick FAQ

**Comté or Gruyère?**
Comté is fruitier and more varied; Gruyère is saltier and more direct. Comté is
usually the better value.

**Why do two pieces taste different?**
Summer milk from mountain pasture versus winter hay-fed milk. The seasonal
difference is real and visible in the color.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Technically yes, but it's tough and unpleasant. Trim it.

**What does the green band mean?**
The wheel scored above 14 out of 20 in the mandatory jury tasting.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of three cheeses.
