## The short version

Beef brisket or navel, wet-brined, coated in cracked pepper and coriander,
smoked, then steamed until tender. It's a Jewish-American deli institution, and
on a board it wants to be sliced thick, served warm, and put on rye with
mustard.

## What it is

Pastrami arrived in New York with Romanian Jewish immigrants in the late 1800s —
the technique traces to *pastramă*, a Romanian preserved meat. Beef is cured in
brine with garlic, coriander and pepper, rubbed with a heavy pepper-coriander
crust, cold-smoked, then steamed for hours until the connective tissue gives.

**Taste:** peppery, coriander-forward, smoky, salty, deeply beefy
**Texture:** tender and moist, pulling apart along the grain
**Look:** deep red-pink meat with a black pepper crust and a smoke ring

Steaming after smoking is the mechanism, and it's what separates pastrami from
every other item in this section. Everything else here is preserved by drying;
pastrami is deliberately re-moistened. Hours of steam break down collagen and
put water back, which is why it's tender rather than firm — and why it must be
served warm.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Rye bread and yellow mustard** | The deli plate. Sour bread, sharp mustard, warm fat. Non-negotiable. |
| **Cocktail rye or pumpernickel** | Board-sized versions of the same idea. |
| **Half-sour pickles** | Acid and crunch against soft fatty meat. |
| **Sauerkraut** | Sour and cold against warm and rich. |
| **Russian dressing** | If you're building Reuben bites, which is a legitimate board move. |
| **Aged cheddar or Swiss** | Sharp or mild cheese, both work. |
| **Cold lager, cream soda, dry cider** | The deli drinks, and all three cut warm fat and pepper. |

**Skip it with:** smoked gouda — pastrami is already smoked — and delicate
fresh cheeses.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thick — about ¼ inch — against the grain.** Thin-sliced pastrami is
   a supermarket product; hand-cut thick slices are the real thing.
2. **Serve it warm.** Steam it for a few minutes or wrap in foil and warm at
   300°F. Cold pastrami is firm and much less good.
3. **Serve 1.5–2 oz per person** — more than the dry cures, because it's
   moister and less salty per ounce.
4. **Build a few bites** — cocktail rye, mustard, folded pastrami, a pickle
   slice — and leave the rest for people to assemble.
5. **Put mustard and pickles directly beside it.**
6. **Keep it in one warm corner** rather than spread across the board.
7. **Fix:** dry and tough? It needed steam, not more time. Ten minutes over
   simmering water rescues it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Jewish deli pastrami, hand-cut** | Delis, Katz's-style shops, online | The real thing. Ask for it thick-cut and warm. |
| **Boar's Head Pastrami** | Deli counters | The everyday version. Ask for a thick cut. |
| **Whole pastrami navel or brisket** | Butchers, online, specialty | Steam it yourself. Far better and cheaper by the pound. |
| **Montreal smoked meat** | Montreal delis, online | The Canadian cousin — less sweet, more peppery. Excellent. |
| **Corned beef** | Any deli | Same brine, no smoke, no pepper crust. A different product. |
| **Summer sausage** | Any grocery | If you want an American cured meat that needs no warming. |

**Label check:** a visible black pepper and coriander crust, and a smoke ring
under it. Uniformly pink pastrami with no crust has been pressed and formed.

**Skip:** thin-sliced pre-packaged pastrami for a board. It dries out and it
misses the point of the cut.

## Quick FAQ

**Is pastrami cured or cooked?**
Both — brined and cured, then smoked, then steamed for hours. It's a cooked
product.

**Pastrami or corned beef?**
Same brine to start. Corned beef is boiled; pastrami gets a pepper-coriander
crust and is smoked, then steamed.

**Does it have to be warm?**
It's much better warm. Cold pastrami firms up and loses its tenderness.

**How much per person?**
1.5–2 oz — more than a dry-cured meat, since it's moister.

**What's Montreal smoked meat?**
A close cousin — less sugar in the cure, more pepper, and often a longer smoke.
Worth trying if you can get it.
