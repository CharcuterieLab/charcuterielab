## The short version

A cracker loaded with sesame, flax, sunflower, poppy or chia. It brings crunch,
toasted flavor and a little oil of its own, which makes it the best all-purpose
"nice" cracker on a board. It also sheds seeds everywhere, which nobody
mentions.

## What it is

Seeded crackers are a modern category rather than a traditional one — most are
a grain base with seeds pressed into the surface or mixed into the dough.
Toasting the seeds during the bake is what gives them their flavor.

**Taste:** toasty, nutty, faintly bitter at the edges
**Texture:** craggy, hard crunch, substantial
**Look:** speckled and bumpy, tan to dark brown

Two mechanisms are working at once. Toasted seeds bring Maillard flavor — the
same browning that makes bread crust taste good — and seed oils add fat and a
touch of bitterness. Together they can stand up to a strong cheese and still
flatter a mild one.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Soft goat cheese with honey** | Bitter seed oil holds the honey back from being cloying. The most reliable trio on any board. |
| **Blue cheese** | Bitterness and funk are neighbors. The seeds meet blue halfway instead of hiding from it. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel notes in the cheese, toasted notes in the seeds. Straight echo pairing. |
| **Hummus and whipped feta** | Enough structure to scoop, enough flavor to matter. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Sesame and salmon is a borrowed bagel logic, and it works. |
| **Fig jam and quince paste** | Dense sweet pastes need a rough, savory surface or they slide. |
| **Sharp cheddar** | The seed crunch resets your mouth between bites of a fatty cheese. |

**Skip it with:** caviar and delicate cured fish (the seeds compete), and mild
bloomy brie, which vanishes underneath. Also worth knowing: seeds get stuck in
teeth. If your guests are dressed up and mingling, put out a second, smoother
option too.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Give them their own spot at the edge.** They shed seeds onto everything,
   and loose seeds in soft cheese look like an accident.
2. **Fan them, don't stack.** The bumpy surface means stacks slide apart.
3. **Serve 3–4 per person.** They're big, dense and filling.
4. **Pair them next to the goat cheese and the honey**, which is where most
   people will use them anyway.
5. **Buy small boxes.** Seed oils oxidize — a half-used box goes flat-tasting
   in weeks, not months.
6. **Fix:** if they've softened, 4 minutes at 300°F re-toasts the seeds and
   brings the flavor back sharper than it was.

**Label check:** you want to *see* whole seeds, not just read "flaxseed" ninth
on the list. Visible seeds mean crunch; ground seeds only mean color.

**Skip:** "veggie" crackers made with vegetable powders. They read as seeded on
the box and taste faintly sweet on the board.

## Quick FAQ

**Are seeded crackers gluten-free?**
Some are — Mary's Gone Crackers, Crunchmaster and Simple Mills are. Most others
are wheat-based. Check the box.

**How many do I need?**
3–4 per person. They're larger and heavier than a water cracker.

**Do they go stale faster?**
They go *rancid* faster, which tastes different from stale — flat and slightly
sharp. Buy small boxes and store them airtight.

**Which seeded cracker is best for a beginner board?**
Milton's Original Multi-Grain. It's mild enough to work with almost anything
and easy to find.
