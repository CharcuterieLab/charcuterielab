## The short version

A Provençal paste of olives, capers, anchovy and olive oil. It's the most savoury
thing you can put on a board — no sugar anywhere — which makes it the counterweight
to a table full of jam and honey. Use it sparingly; it's very salty.

## What it is

Black or green olives pounded with capers, anchovy, garlic and olive oil, often
with lemon and thyme. The name comes from *tapeno*, Provençal for caper — the
capers, not the olives, give it its name. Black olive tapenade is the standard;
green is milder and more herbal.

**Taste:** intensely salty, briny, faintly bitter, umami-rich
**Texture:** coarse paste, oily, with visible olive pieces
**Look:** near-black or olive-green, glossy

The mechanism is salt and glutamate together. Olives, capers and anchovy are all
high in free glutamate, so tapenade delivers savoury depth rather than just
saltiness. Against a fresh, mild cheese that depth does what a jam's sugar does —
it makes the cheese taste of more — but from the opposite direction.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | The classic. Chalky lactic tang against oily salt, with neither having any sugar to muddle it. |
| **Baguette and crostini** | The traditional service. Spread thin on toasted bread, nothing else needed. |
| **Burrata** | Cream as a foil for concentrated salt. A restaurant plate in two ingredients. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweetness against brine — the Provençal combination, and it fixes tapenade's one weakness. |
| **Manchego and hard sheep cheese** | Salt on salt, which works because the textures are so different. |
| **Hard-boiled eggs and raw vegetables** | The wider Provençal table. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and anything already very salty. Two salt bombs
cancel each other out.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with a small spoon.
2. **Loosen it with olive oil** if it's stiff — most jarred tapenade is.
3. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.** It's very salty and people take a little.
4. **Give it a plain base** — baguette or a neutral cracker, never a salted one.
5. **Put it next to the fresh cheese**, away from the jam.
6. **Say it contains anchovy.** Many people assume tapenade is vegetarian.
7. **Fix:** too salty? Stir in more olive oil and a squeeze of lemon.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **French black olive tapenade** | Specialty, cheese shops | The standard. Look for coarse texture, not purée. |
| **Green olive tapenade** | Specialty, Italian delis | Milder, more herbal. Better with burrata. |
| **Trader Joe's olive tapenade** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and good. |
| **Anchovy-free tapenade** | Specialty | For vegetarian guests. Milder, and it loses some depth. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Pulse olives, capers, anchovy, garlic and oil in a processor. Five minutes, coarse not smooth. |

**Label check:** olives, capers, anchovy and olive oil should be the ingredients.
Watch for sunflower oil, which is a cost-cutting sign.

**Skip:** anything blended completely smooth — texture is part of the point.

## Quick FAQ

**What is tapenade?**
A Provençal paste of olives, capers, anchovy and olive oil, served on bread or
with fresh cheese.

**Is tapenade vegetarian?**
Usually not — traditional tapenade contains anchovy. Anchovy-free versions exist
and are labelled as such.

**What cheese goes with tapenade?**
Fresh goat cheese above all, plus burrata and hard sheep cheeses like manchego.

**Why is it called tapenade if it's mostly olives?**
The name comes from *tapeno*, the Provençal word for caper.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce. It's very salty and a little goes far.
