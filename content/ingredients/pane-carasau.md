## The short version

Paper-thin Sardinian flatbread, baked in sheets so fine you can almost see
through them. Italians call it *carta da musica* — music paper — because it's
as thin as a sheet of sheet music. It shatters spectacularly and it is the best
looking thing you can put on a board.

## What it is

Pane carasau is shepherds' bread from Sardinia, made to last for months in the
hills. A round of dough is baked until it puffs into a balloon, split into two
paper-thin discs while hot, then baked again until rigid. The technique is
thousands of years old — versions have been found in Sardinian sites dating
back to the Bronze Age.

**Taste:** plain toasted wheat, lightly salted
**Texture:** brittle to the point of fragility, shatters rather than snaps
**Look:** large pale translucent sheets, blistered, irregular

Thinness is the mechanism, and it does two things at once. It makes the bread
almost weightless in the mouth, so a topping tastes like the topping and not
like bread — and it gives a board a dramatic vertical, jagged element that no
round cracker can provide.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pecorino Sardo and aged pecorino** | Sardinian bread with Sardinian cheese. Salt and sheep's milk against a weightless base. |
| **Prosciutto and salumi** | Drape a slice over a shard. The bread disappears and the meat is all you taste. |
| **Burrata and ricotta** | Soft, wet and mild against dry, thin and crisp. The best texture contrast in this section. |
| **Olive oil and rosemary** | Brush a sheet, scatter rosemary and salt, warm it 3 minutes. That's *pane guttiau*, the traditional treatment. |
| **Honey and aged cheese** | The thinness means the honey leads and the bread just carries it. |
| **Bottarga, if you can find it** | Sardinia's other famous product. Shaved over oiled carasau, it's a genuine delicacy. |
| **Vermentino, Cannonau, prosecco** | Sardinian wines with Sardinian bread. |

**Skip it with:** thick dips and anything wet. This is the most fragile base
here — it snaps under a spoonful of hummus and dissolves under a tomato.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Break the sheets by hand into large irregular shards.** Never cut them —
   torn edges are the whole aesthetic.
2. **Stand some shards upright**, leaning against a cheese or a bowl. Vertical
   pane carasau is the most striking thing you can do on a board, and it's
   free.
3. **Serve 2–3 shards per person.**
4. **Try *pane guttiau*:** brush a sheet with olive oil, scatter coarse salt
   and rosemary, and warm at 350°F for 3 minutes. It takes five minutes and
   transforms the bread.
5. **Handle it as little as possible.** Every touch costs you a shard.
6. **Fix:** it's already bone dry, so it doesn't go stale. Humidity softens it,
   and 2 minutes at 300°F brings it straight back.

**Label check:** durum wheat semolina, water, salt, yeast. It's an ancient
bread with an ancient ingredient list.

**Skip:** buying it if the package looks crushed. It's fragile enough in
transit that a damaged box means crumbs.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is it called music paper bread?**
Because the sheets are as thin as sheet music. *Carta da musica* is the Italian
name; *pane carasau* is the Sardinian one.

**How do I serve it?**
Break it into large shards by hand and stand a few upright on the board. Don't
cut it.

**What is pane guttiau?**
The same bread brushed with olive oil, salted and warmed. Five minutes of work
for a large improvement.

**How many per person?**
2–3 shards.

**Can I dip with it?**
No. It's the most fragile base in this whole section and it will snap.
