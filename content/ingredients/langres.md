## The short version

A small orange washed-rind cheese from the Champagne region with a concave
dimple — the *fontaine* — in its top. You pour champagne or marc into the well
and let it soak in. This is the traditional service, not a party trick.

## What it is

Langres AOP is made in Champagne-Ardenne from cow's milk, washed with brine
and annatto during a five-week aging. Unlike most cheeses it's never turned
during ripening, which is why the top sinks into a well as the paste settles.
The rind is washed to a deep orange; the paste stays firm at the center and
softens toward the edge.

**Taste:** rich, savory, tangy, moderately funky, less intense than Époisses
**Texture:** soft and creamy near the rind, firmer in the middle
**Look:** small orange cylinder with a distinct hollow in the top

Not turning the cheese is the mechanism, and it's the rare case where a
production shortcut became the defining feature. Wheels are normally flipped so
they settle evenly. Leaving Langres alone lets gravity pull the paste down and
the surface sink — creating a well that some enterprising cheesemaker
eventually decided to fill.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Champagne, poured into the well** | The traditional service. Let it soak twenty minutes before serving. |
| **Marc de Champagne** | The stronger regional version of the same idea. |
| **Baguette** | Plain bread and a spoon. Nothing should compete. |
| **Cornichons** | Sharp acid against a rich washed-rind paste. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin against a very high-fat washed-rind paste. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness against funk, as with any washed-rind. |
| **Champagne, alongside as well as inside** | Acid and bubbles against saturated fat. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses nearby and aged hard cheeses. Like all
washed-rinds it takes over its corner of the board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Pour a splash of champagne or marc into the well twenty minutes before
   serving.** It soaks in and perfumes the paste. This is the point of the
   cheese.
2. **Temper a full hour.**
3. **Serve it whole on its own small plate** with a spoon, and let people take
   from the top down.
4. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** One small cheese serves four to six.
5. **Give it its own plate and knife.** The aroma travels.
6. **The rind is edible** but many trim it.
7. **Fix:** if it's firm all the way through it's young. Give it a few more
   days wrapped in the fridge.

**Label check:** the AOP mark and a visible well in the top. A flat-topped
orange cheese isn't Langres.

**Skip:** the large format for a board. The small cheeses are the right size
and the well is proportionally deeper.

## Quick FAQ

**Do you really pour champagne into it?**
Yes. It's the traditional service — a splash in the well twenty minutes before
serving, left to soak in.

**Why does it have a hollow in the top?**
It's never turned during ripening, so the paste settles and the surface sinks.
Most cheeses are flipped; this one isn't.

**How strong is it?**
Moderately — noticeably milder than Époisses, stronger than Taleggio.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, though many people trim it.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. One small cheese serves four to six.
