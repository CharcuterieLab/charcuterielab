## The short version

A cultured cashew base with a genuine *Penicillium candidum* bloomy rind — the
same mold used on dairy brie. It's the most technically ambitious plant-based
cheese being made, it's expensive, and on a board it's the one that reads as a
real cheese from across the room.

## What it is

The base is cashews, soaked and blended, cultured with lactic acid bacteria,
formed into a wheel, then sprayed with the same white surface mold used on
brie and Camembert. The mold grows a real rind and ripens the cheese from the
outside in, exactly as it does on dairy. Making it work took the category
years.

**Taste:** rich, tangy, faintly mushroomy, nutty underneath
**Texture:** dense and creamy, softening under the rind with age
**Look:** small white wheel with a genuine bloomy rind

Real rind ripening is the mechanism, and it's why this works where flavored nut
paste doesn't. *P. candidum* produces the same enzymes on a nut substrate as on
milk, breaking proteins and fats down into the same mushroomy, savory compounds
that make brie taste like brie. The base is different; the process is
identical.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Water crackers** | Lean and dry under a rich cheese. Nothing should compete. |
| **Fig jam and fruit preserve** | Sweet against tangy and rich. The brie playbook, unchanged. |
| **Baguette** | Plain bread, as with dairy brie. |
| **Grapes and pear** | Fresh acid cutting a dense, fatty paste. |
| **Walnuts and Marcona almonds** | Tannin and salt against a rich cultured cashew paste. |
| **Agave or maple instead of honey** | Same job, and vegan if that matters at your table. |
| **Sparkling wine, dry rosé** | Acid and bubbles against fat. |

**Skip it with:** a dairy brie on the same board, which invites a comparison it
will lose. Serve it as the cheese it is.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.** These wheels are dense cold and much better warm —
   more so than dairy brie.
2. **Cut wedges from the center out**, keeping rind on each piece.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.** It's expensive and rich.
4. **Make it the plant-based centerpiece.** On a mixed board, give it its own
   corner with its own crackers and knife.
5. **Say what it is.** People assume plant-based cheese is bad; naming it after
   they've tried it is the more persuasive order.
6. **The rind is edible**, as with dairy brie.
7. **Fix:** if it's firm and chalky throughout, it's young. A few days in the
   fridge widens the softened layer under the rind.

**Label check:** a real bloomy rind and "cultured" in the ingredients. Products
with a white coating but no live culture are a different thing.

**Skip:** coconut-oil-and-starch "brie." It melts convincingly and tastes of
almost nothing.

## Quick FAQ

**Is vegan brie actually made with the same mold?**
Yes — the better ones use *Penicillium candidum*, the same surface mold as
dairy brie, and it forms a real rind.

**Does it taste like brie?**
Close in character — rich, tangy, faintly mushroomy — but nuttier underneath.
It's good on its own terms rather than indistinguishable.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, exactly as with dairy brie.

**Why is it so expensive?**
Cashews are costly, the cultures and aging take weeks, and production is small.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.
