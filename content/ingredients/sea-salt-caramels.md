## The short version

Soft caramel with flaky salt on top. It's the least surprising thing in this
category and the most reliably eaten — everybody likes salted caramel, and on a
board it bridges the savoury half and the sweet half better than plain chocolate
does. Unwrap them; a bowl of wrappers looks like a checkout counter.

## What it is

Sugar cooked to caramel with cream and butter, set soft, and finished with flaky
sea salt. The French *caramel au beurre salé* from Brittany is the origin —
made with salted butter rather than salted on top. Soft caramels are the board
version; hard toffee is a different product.

**Taste:** deep caramelised sugar, butter, cream, salt
**Texture:** soft, chewy, yielding
**Look:** amber-brown squares topped with visible salt flakes

The mechanism is salt on sugar. Sodium suppresses bitterness and amplifies
sweetness perception, so a salted caramel tastes both sweeter and less cloying
than an unsalted one — and the flake on top gives a distinct crunch and salt burst
separate from the caramel itself. It's the same trick as flaky salt on chocolate,
built into the confection.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Dark chocolate** | Bitter against sweet, hard against soft. The two halves of the dessert corner. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Genuinely good and rarely tried — sharp crystalline cheese against soft salted sugar. |
| **Pecans and toasted nuts** | Caramel and pecan is a fixed pairing. The nut supplies the crunch. |
| **Medjool dates** | Both caramel-flavoured and both soft. Doubling down works. |
| **Flaky sea salt** | Add more if the caramels are under-salted. Most are. |
| **Coffee and dessert wine** | Coffee's bitterness cuts the butterfat; a sweet wine matches the caramel rather than fighting it. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and savoury cheese, other than the cheddar
exception above.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Unwrap them.** A bowl of twisted wrappers undoes the whole board.
2. **Cut large ones in half** — a full square is a lot of caramel.
3. **Add extra flaky salt** on the cut face if they taste flat.
4. **Serve in a small bowl or on a small plate** — they stick to wood.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — one or two.
6. **Keep them cool but not cold.** Fridge-cold caramel is hard and unpleasant.
7. **Fix:** sticking together in a heap? Chill them for ten minutes, then separate
   and serve at room temperature.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **French caramels au beurre salé** | French delis, specialty | Made with salted Breton butter. The best version. |
| **Trader Joe's fleur de sel caramels** | Trader Joe's | Excellent value and genuinely good. |
| **Local chocolatier caramels** | Chocolatiers, farmers markets | Usually soft, fresh, and well-salted. |
| **Chocolate-covered salted caramels** | Chocolatiers, grocery | Two things at once. Neater to serve, no wrapper. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Sugar, cream, butter, salt. Straightforward but needs a thermometer. |

**Label check:** cream and butter should be in the ingredients. Cheap caramels use
corn syrup and vegetable fat and taste waxy.

**Skip:** hard toffee and anything individually wrapped that you don't intend to
unwrap.

## Quick FAQ

**Do salted caramels go with cheese?**
With aged cheddar, surprisingly well — sharp crystalline cheese against soft
salted sugar. Otherwise keep them in the dessert corner.

**Should I unwrap them?**
Yes. Wrappers on a board look like a checkout counter, and guests are less likely
to take one.

**What's caramel au beurre salé?**
The Breton original, made with salted butter rather than salted on top. Deeper and
less sweet.

**Why does salt make caramel taste better?**
Salt suppresses bitterness and heightens sweetness perception, so the caramel
tastes richer and less cloying.

**How many per person?**
One or two, about half an ounce. They're very rich.
