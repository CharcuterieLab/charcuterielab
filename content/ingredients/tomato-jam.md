## The short version

A jam that sits on the savoury side of the line. Tomatoes cooked with sugar,
vinegar and usually ginger or chilli, producing something between a jam and a
chutney. It's the right spread for a board where everything else is sweet and
you need one thing that isn't.

## What it is

Tomatoes reduced with sugar, vinegar and spice — commonly ginger, chilli, cumin
or coriander. Related to chutney but smoother and sweeter. The Southern American
version is spiced and bacon-adjacent; Italian *confettura di pomodoro* is more
purely sweet.

**Taste:** sweet, tangy, savoury, gently spiced
**Texture:** thick and slightly stringy, jam-like
**Look:** deep brick red, glossy

The mechanism is umami inside a sweet preserve. Tomatoes carry glutamate, so
tomato jam brings a savoury depth no fruit jam has. On a board that means it can
partner meat and sharp cheese directly, doing the job mustard does but with
sweetness instead of heat.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp acid and salt meeting sweet umami. The pairing that makes tomato jam worth buying. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang against sweet-savoury, with the jam's spice giving the bite a finish. |
| **Pimento cheese** | Southern logic. Two savoury spreads, one sweet, one sharp. |
| **Manchego** | Nutty sheep cheese and sweet tomato — a Spanish *pan con tomate* idea, concentrated. |
| **Coppa and salami** | Fatty spiced pork with something sweet and acidic to cut it. |
| **Fried cheese and croquettes** | Its natural home. Anything hot and crisp. |

**Skip it with:** delicate bloomy cheeses. The spice and acid overwhelm them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with a spreader.
2. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
3. **Put it on the savoury side of the board**, near the meat and the cheddar.
4. **Use it as the anti-sweet element** if the rest of the board runs to jam,
   honey and dried fruit.
5. **Stir it well** — it separates more than fruit jams.
6. **Buy the spiced version** rather than the plain sweet one for board use.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? A splash of sherry vinegar sharpens it immediately.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Southern-style tomato jam** | Specialty, farmers markets | Spiced with ginger and chilli. The best board version. |
| **Confettura di pomodoro** | Italian delis | Sweeter and purer. Good with hard cheese. |
| **Trader Joe's tomato spread** | Trader Joe's, seasonal | Cheap entry point when they carry it. |
| **Green tomato jam** | Farmers markets, late summer | Tarter and more unusual. Excellent with goat cheese. |
| **Tomato chutney** | Indian and British grocers | Chunkier and more acidic. A close substitute. |

**Label check:** look for vinegar in the ingredients — it's what keeps tomato jam
from being cloying.

**Skip:** anything that reads like a sweet ketchup on the label.

## Quick FAQ

**What is tomato jam?**
Tomatoes cooked down with sugar, vinegar and spice — sweeter than chutney,
savoury enough to work as a condiment.

**What cheese goes with tomato jam?**
Aged cheddar above all, plus fresh goat cheese, manchego, and pimento cheese.

**Is it sweet or savoury?**
Both. That's the point — it's the one preserve that can sit next to meat without
reading as dessert.

**How is it different from chutney?**
Chutney is chunkier and more acidic, with more spice and often dried fruit.
Tomato jam is smoother and sweeter.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce.
