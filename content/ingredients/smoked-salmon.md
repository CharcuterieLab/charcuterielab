## The short version

Salmon cured in salt then smoked. **Cold-smoked** stays silky and translucent
and slices into sheets; **hot-smoked** is cooked through and flakes. They aren't
interchangeable, and most board recipes mean the cold-smoked kind.

## What it is

Cold-smoked salmon is salt-cured for hours to days, then smoked below about
30°C — warm enough to flavour, too cool to cook. Hot-smoked salmon is smoked at
70–80°C and comes out fully cooked. Regional styles differ: Scottish is dry-
cured and firm, Norwegian is milder and fattier, Nova is lightly brined.

**Cold-smoked:** silky, translucent, sliceable in sheets, delicate and salty
**Hot-smoked:** opaque, flaky, firmer, more assertively smoky
**Look:** deep orange-pink, glossy; hot-smoked is paler and matte

Temperature is the mechanism and the only real distinction. Below about 30°C the
proteins never denature, so the flesh keeps its raw translucent texture and
takes smoke as perfume. Above 60°C it cooks, sets and flakes. Everything else —
brine, wood, region — is a variation on top.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese and dark bread** | The default, and it works because cool fat and sour rye both cut salt and smoke. |
| **Blini and crème fraîche** | The elegant version. Warm, soft, cold, sour. |
| **Capers, red onion, dill** | Acid, allium and herb. The three classic accompaniments, all doing the same job. |
| **Lemon** | Squeeze at the last moment. Acid is what lifts smoked fish. |
| **Pumpernickel or rye crispbread** | Sour dark bread against oily smoked fish. |
| **Cucumber** | Cool and clean between bites. |
| **Champagne, Chablis, dry Riesling, aquavit** | High-acid or clean spirits. |

**Skip it with:** strong cured meats and blue cheese, both of which erase it.
Smoked salmon is expensive and delicate — give it its own corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Know which type you have.** Silky sheets are cold-smoked; flaky chunks are
   hot-smoked. Serve them differently.
2. **Cold-smoked: drape slices in loose ribbons**, never flat-stacked. Lift from
   the centre and let them fall.
3. **Hot-smoked: break into rough flakes** with a fork and pile them.
4. **Temper only 15 minutes.** It should be cool, not cold — and never warm.
5. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
6. **Put lemon, capers and dill directly beside it**, and add the lemon at the
   table.
7. **Fix:** slices stuck together? Warm the pack in your hands for a minute
   rather than tearing them apart.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Scottish cold-smoked salmon** | Whole Foods, specialty, deli counters | Firmer and drier-cured. The classic board choice. |
| **Norwegian cold-smoked** | Most grocery stores | Milder, fattier, widely available. |
| **Nova / Nova lox** | Delis, grocery | Lightly brined and cold-smoked. The New York deli standard. |
| **Hot-smoked salmon fillet** | Grocery, Whole Foods | Flaky and assertive. A different board item. |
| **Gravlax** | Whole Foods, Scandinavian markets | Cured, not smoked — see its own page. |
| **Smoked trout** | Whole Foods, specialty | Cheaper, milder, underrated — see its own page. |

**Label check:** "cold-smoked" or "smoked" with a silky appearance versus
"hot-smoked" or "kiln-roasted" for the flaky kind. Check the date — smoked
salmon has a short window once opened.

**Skip:** buying more than you'll serve. It's expensive and it doesn't keep
well once the pack is open.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between lox and smoked salmon?**
True lox is salt-cured and not smoked at all. "Nova lox" is lightly brined and
cold-smoked. In everyday American usage the words have blurred.

**Cold-smoked or hot-smoked?**
Cold-smoked for silky slices on a board. Hot-smoked when you want flakes for a
dip or a salad.

**Is cold-smoked salmon raw?**
Technically uncooked, but cured with salt and smoked. It's safe to eat as sold.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz. It's rich and expensive.

**What are the three things to serve with it?**
Lemon, capers and dill. Then dark bread and something creamy.
