## The short version

The sharp one. Dijon is smooth, hotter than whole grain, and made with white wine
or verjuice instead of plain vinegar, which gives it a cleaner acidity. Where
whole grain adds texture, Dijon adds heat — a thin smear under a slice of ham
does more than a spoonful of anything else.

## What it is

Brown mustard seeds ground fine and mixed with white wine, verjuice or wine
vinegar. The name is a style, not a protected origin — most "Dijon" mustard is not
made in Dijon. Heat comes from compounds that form when the seed meets liquid and
fade steadily after opening.

**Taste:** sharp, hot, clean, with a wine edge
**Texture:** smooth and creamy, spreads thin
**Look:** pale yellow-beige, matte

The mechanism is volatile heat. Dijon's pungency comes from allyl isothiocyanate,
the same compound as horseradish and wasabi — it hits the nose rather than the
tongue and clears immediately. That short, sharp burst is what makes it work as an
undercoat: it cuts fat and then gets out of the way.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pâté and terrines** | The classical service. Rich liver fat needs sharp acid and heat, and Dijon supplies both without texture getting in the way. |
| **Cornichons** | Sold together, served together. Two acids at different intensities. |
| **Saucisson sec and dry salami** | Fat and salt cut by a clean burst of heat. |
| **Gruyère and alpine cheese** | Nutty, sweet, dense cheese with a sharp mustard is the ham-and-cheese logic without the ham. |
| **Black forest ham** | Smoke and salt against wine-sharp heat. |
| **Baguette** | Spread thin. The base for everything above. |

**Skip it with:** fresh delicate cheeses and anything sweet you want to taste.
Dijon dominates.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with a small spoon or spreader.
2. **Serve 0.25 oz per person.** A little goes a long way.
3. **Spread it thin**, not spooned in a lump — that's the difference between it
   working and it burning.
4. **Buy a fresh jar** for a board. An open jar six months old has lost most of
   its heat.
5. **Put it beside the pâté and the meat.**
6. **Put out whole grain too** if the board is meat-heavy. They do different jobs.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Stir in a little crème fraîche or honey.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Maille Dijon originale** | Any grocery | The standard. Sharp, consistent, cheap. |
| **Edmond Fallot Dijon** | Specialty, cheese shops | Stone-ground in Burgundy. Noticeably better. |
| **Amora Dijon** | French delis | The everyday French supermarket brand. Very sharp. |
| **Tarragon or green peppercorn Dijon** | Specialty | Flavoured versions. Good with pâté. |
| **Honey Dijon** | Any grocery | Sweetened. Softer, and better with ham than with pâté. |

**Label check:** the ingredients should include white wine or wine vinegar rather
than distilled vinegar alone. Buy the smallest jar you'll use.

**Skip:** anything that's been open for months. Mustard doesn't spoil, but flat
mustard is pointless.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between Dijon and whole grain mustard?**
Dijon is smooth, sharper and hotter, made with wine. Whole grain has intact seeds
that add texture and is milder.

**Is Dijon mustard actually from Dijon?**
Usually not. It's a style, not a protected origin — most jars are made elsewhere.

**Why does my mustard taste weak?**
It's old. Mustard's heat is volatile and fades within months of opening.

**What goes with Dijon on a board?**
Pâté and terrines above all, plus cured salami, cornichons, gruyère, and ham.

**How much per person?**
About a quarter-ounce, spread thin rather than spooned.
