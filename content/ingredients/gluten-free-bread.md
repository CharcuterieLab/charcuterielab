## The short version

Gluten-free bread served fresh on a board is a mistake — it's dense, slightly
gummy and it tells your guest they got the sad version. Toasted, it's genuinely
good. That's the whole page: **always toast it.** Make gluten-free crostini and
nobody feels like an afterthought.

## What it is

Without gluten there's no elastic network to trap gas, so gluten-free loaves
rely on starches (rice, tapioca, potato) plus gums to hold structure. The
result is denser, moister and more fragile than wheat bread, with a crumb that
compresses instead of springing back.

**Taste:** mild, slightly sweet, sometimes faintly beany
**Texture:** dense and soft fresh; crisp and clean toasted
**Look:** tight even crumb, pale, usually smaller slices

Moisture is the mechanism, and it's why toasting transforms these loaves rather
than just warming them. Gluten-free breads hold more water than wheat bread;
driving that water off in a hot oven is what turns a gummy slice into a crisp
one. It's the same principle as crostini, and it matters twice as much here.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese and honey** | Toasted GF bread is mild and slightly sweet; this leans into it. |
| **Brie and Camembert** | Soft cheese needs a crisp base, and toasting is what supplies it. |
| **Pâté and chicken liver mousse** | Rich and soft against dry and crisp. The bread contributes nothing, correctly. |
| **Smoked salmon and crème fraîche** | Works as well as rye here, provided the bread is toasted hard. |
| **Whipped ricotta and olive oil** | Wet toppings need the moisture driven out first. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat on a plain warm base. |
| **Sparkling wine, dry white** | The pairing is really with the topping — the bread stays out of it. |

**Skip it with:** nothing specific. The only real failure is serving it
untoasted, and that's a preparation problem rather than a pairing one.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Toast it. Always.** This is the single most useful thing on this page.
   Untoasted gluten-free bread is the reason people think it's bad.
2. **Make gluten-free crostini:** slice ⅓ inch thick, brush both sides with
   olive oil, 375°F for 12–15 minutes flipping once. A little longer than wheat
   crostini, because there's more water to drive off.
3. **Cool on a rack, never in the pan.** Trapped steam undoes everything.
4. **Serve 2 pieces per person.**
5. **Use a separate board, knife and toaster tray.** For a guest with celiac
   disease, crumbs from wheat bread are the actual risk — not the loaf.
6. **Don't announce it as the gluten-free option.** Put it out as crostini
   among the other bases. It reads as a choice rather than an accommodation.
7. **Fix:** these loaves stale fast and freeze well. Most brands are sold
   frozen for that reason — keep it frozen and toast slices straight from the
   freezer.

| **Base Culture** | Whole Foods, Amazon, freezer | Almond-based and grain-free. Denser — note that it contains nuts. |
| **Store bakery GF loaf** | Whole Foods, Wegmans | Quality varies. Ask whether it's baked in a separate facility if celiac is a concern. |
| **A dedicated GF bakery** | Local | If you have one, this is a different food entirely. Worth the trip. |

**Label check:** for celiac disease, look for a certified gluten-free mark
rather than just "gluten-free" on the front. And check for almond or coconut if
a guest also avoids nuts.

**Skip:** serving any of these as plain fresh bread on a board. Even the good
ones need the oven.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is gluten-free bread better toasted?**
It holds more water than wheat bread. Toasting drives that moisture off, which
is what turns a dense, gummy slice into a crisp one.

**Which gluten-free bread is best for a charcuterie board?**
Canyon Bakehouse for availability, Schär for the baguette-style loaves. Both
make good crostini.

**How do I avoid cross-contamination?**
Separate knife, separate board, separate toaster tray. Crumbs are the real
risk, not the loaf.

**How much per person?**
2 pieces, cut small. Toasted crostini go further than fresh slices.

**Can I freeze it?**
Yes, and you should — most gluten-free bread is sold frozen because it stales
within a day or two. Toast straight from frozen.
