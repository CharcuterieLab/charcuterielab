## The short version

The whole roe sac of grey mullet or tuna, salted, pressed and air-dried into a
firm amber block. Shaved paper-thin it delivers more savoury intensity per gram
than anything else in this section. A little goes a very long way.

## What it is

Bottarga di muggine (grey mullet) is the Sardinian and Sicilian standard, and
the finer of the two; bottarga di tonno (tuna) is darker, coarser and stronger.
The intact roe sac is salted, pressed under weights for weeks, then air-dried
for months. Some blocks are sealed in wax for keeping.

**Taste:** intensely savoury, briny, nutty, faintly bitter at the finish
**Texture:** firm and waxy, shaving into translucent flakes
**Look:** amber to deep orange-brown block, sometimes wax-coated

Concentration is the mechanism, taken further than anywhere else here. Pressing
and drying remove most of the water from an already protein-dense food, leaving
free amino acids and salt at extraordinary density. That's why it's used in
shavings measured in grams rather than ounces.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Olive oil and lemon on plain crackers** | Fat and acid are the only things that carry it. The Sardinian service. |
| **Pane carasau** | Sardinian roe on Sardinian bread. Paper-thin against paper-thin. |
| **Burrata or fresh ricotta** | Cool bland milk fat against extreme savouriness. Excellent. |
| **Raw fennel or celery** | Crisp, cold and slightly sweet. |
| **Cherry tomatoes** | Acid and sweetness against salt. |
| **Butter on bread** | Same logic as anchovies — fat blunts salt and carries umami. |
| **Vermentino, Champagne, Fino sherry** | Saline, high-acid, cold. |

**Skip it with:** every cheese except very mild fresh ones, and any other
strong savoury item. Bottarga is the single most concentrated thing you can put
on a board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Shave it, don't slice it.** Use a microplane or a vegetable peeler for
   translucent flakes. Thick pieces are unpleasantly salty and waxy.
2. **Serve about a quarter ounce per person** — genuinely a few grams each.
3. **Shave it over something** rather than serving it alone: crackers with
   olive oil, burrata, sliced fennel.
4. **Put olive oil and lemon beside it.** They're not optional; bottarga
   without fat and acid is just salt.
5. **Remove the wax coating** if there is one, and peel the thin membrane.
6. **Shave it at the table.** It's a nice moment and it stops the flakes drying
   out.
7. **Fix:** too salty in a bite? More olive oil, not less bottarga. Fat carries
   it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bottarga di muggine (grey mullet)** | Italian delis, specialty grocers, online | The finer type. Amber, delicate, expensive. Start here. |
| **Bottarga di tonno (tuna)** | Italian delis, specialty | Darker, coarser, stronger and cheaper. |
| **Grated bottarga in a jar** | Italian markets, online | Convenient for pasta. Loses aroma fast — a whole block is better. |
| **Karasumi** | Japanese markets | The Japanese equivalent, made from mullet roe. |
| **Anchovies** | Whole Foods, specialty | The other Mediterranean umami bomb — see its own page. |
| **Any Italian specialty importer** | Online | Best selection; availability in general grocery is poor. |

**Label check:** *muggine* means grey mullet, *tonno* means tuna. A whole block
keeps far better than pre-grated.

**Skip:** treating it as a portion of fish. It's a seasoning with a place on a
board, not a serving of seafood.

## Quick FAQ

**What is bottarga?**
Salt-cured, pressed and dried fish roe — usually grey mullet or tuna — sold as
a firm block and shaved thin.

**Mullet or tuna?**
Mullet is finer, more delicate and more expensive. Tuna is stronger, coarser and
cheaper. Mullet first.

**How much do I serve?**
About a quarter ounce per person. It's the most concentrated item here.

**Do I need olive oil with it?**
Yes. Fat and acid are what carry the flavour — plain bottarga on a dry cracker
just reads as salt.

**Is it like caviar?**
No. Caviar is loose fresh eggs; bottarga is the whole sac, pressed and dried
into a firm block. Completely different texture and use.
