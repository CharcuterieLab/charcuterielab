## The short version

The practical answer to the olive question. Buy three or four varieties from an
olive bar, dress them with olive oil, citrus zest, garlic and herbs, and serve
them in one bowl. It looks considered, it costs the same as buying one jar, and
it gives guests a choice.

## What it is

A mix of olive varieties — typically Castelvetrano, Kalamata, Cerignola and a
dry-cured black — marinated together in olive oil with lemon or orange zest,
garlic, rosemary, thyme, chilli flakes and sometimes fennel seed.

**Taste:** varied by olive, unified by citrus, herb and garlic
**Texture:** mixed — crisp, meaty, chewy, all in one bowl
**Look:** green, purple and black together, glossy with oil and flecked with zest

The mechanism is variety within a single container. A board benefits from having
one thing that offers a range rather than a single note — it lets guests find the
olive they like without you committing the whole bowl to a Kalamata. The
marinade is what makes four different olives read as one dish.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego and hard sheep cheeses** | The mix always contains something salty and something mild, so it works with a cheese of any intensity. |
| **Prosciutto and cured meat** | Brine and acid cutting fat, in whatever intensity the guest picks. |
| **Feta** | Same table, same flavours; the marinade's herbs tie them together. |
| **Marcona almonds** | The full Mediterranean bar snack. |
| **Baguette and crusty bread** | The oil at the bottom of the bowl is the reason to have bread. |
| **Vermouth, sherry, dry white wine** | The drinks these belong beside. |

**Skip it with:** nothing — a mixed bowl always contains something that fits.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy three or four varieties** from an olive bar: one bright green
   (Castelvetrano), one dark and winey (Kalamata), one large (Cerignola), one
   wrinkled and intense (oil-cured).
2. **Drain them well.**
3. **Dress:** olive oil, strips of lemon or orange zest, a smashed garlic clove,
   rosemary or thyme sprigs, chilli flakes.
4. **Let them sit at room temperature for an hour**, or overnight in the fridge.
5. **Serve at room temperature** with a pit bowl and a small spoon.
6. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
7. **Fix:** dull? Warm the dressed olives in a pan for five minutes. Heat releases
   the citrus oils and it makes an obvious difference.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Olive bar selection** | Whole Foods, Wegmans, Italian delis | The best way to buy. Pick three or four and dress them yourself. |
| **Divina Mediterranean mix** | Grocery, specialty | Pre-mixed jarred. Convenient and decent. |
| **Trader Joe's olive medley** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and reliable. Dress it anyway. |
| **Citrus-marinated olives** | Olive bars | Already dressed. Fine, and usually over-oiled. |
| **Olives with feta or garlic** | Olive bars | Add-ins that make the bowl look fuller. |

**Label check:** if buying pre-mixed, check that the olives are in oil or brine
rather than water, and that they're not all one variety dyed different colours.

**Skip:** any mix where every olive is the same size and shape — that's one olive
sold as four.

## Quick FAQ

**What olives should I mix for a board?**
Castelvetrano for mild and green, Kalamata for winey and dark, Cerignola for size,
and an oil-cured black for intensity.

**How do I marinate olives?**
Drain them, then toss with olive oil, citrus zest, smashed garlic, herbs and
chilli flakes. Leave an hour at room temperature.

**Should olives be served cold?**
No — room temperature. Cold olives taste of much less.

**Can I make them ahead?**
Yes, up to a week in the fridge. Bring them back to room temperature before
serving.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce, or four to five olives.
