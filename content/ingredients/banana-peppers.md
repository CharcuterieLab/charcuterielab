## The short version

The bright yellow pickled pepper rings from every sub shop. They're tangy,
a little sweet and barely hot, and on a board they do the job of a pickle
for Italian meats: cut the fat, wake up the cheese. They cost a couple of
dollars and keep for months.

## What it is

Banana peppers are a mild variety of Capsicum annuum, long, curved and
pale yellow, which is where the name comes from. Most are sold pickled in
vinegar brine with salt and sometimes a little sugar or turmeric, sliced
into rings or left whole. Hot banana peppers (Hungarian wax) look similar
but bite harder.

**Taste:** tangy, mildly sweet and gently peppery
**Texture:** tender but still crisp, with a soft snap
**Look:** pale yellow-green rings with a few seeds

The brightness is vinegar acid, and that acid is the point. Acid makes your
mouth water and cuts through fat, so a ring of pepper between bites of
salami and provolone resets your palate. Banana peppers have very little
capsaicin, so they give tang without heat, which is why they're the pepper
kids will eat.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Provolone** | Tangy rings cut the butterfat. The Italian sub pairing. |
| **Hard salami and pepperoni** | Acid against fatty, salty meat. |
| **Cream cheese** | Stuff whole peppers for a creamy, tangy bite. |
| **Black olives** | A classic relish-tray duo. |
| **Pepperoncini** | Serve both and compare mild and slightly hotter. |
| **Lager, a crisp white or a light red** | Easy drinks that don't fight vinegar. |

**Skip it with:** dessert items. Vinegar brine and chocolate or caramel
don't mix.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain them** and pat dry on a paper towel.
2. **Serve rings in a small bowl** so the brine doesn't run.
3. **Serve about an ounce per person.**
4. **For a party bite,** stuff whole banana peppers with cream cheese, or
   wrap a ring with a slice of salami.
5. **Place them between the meats and the cheese** so guests use them.
6. **Fix:** if they're too sour, rinse them briefly and toss with a few
   drops of olive oil.

**Label check:** "mild" means banana peppers. "Hot banana" or "hot wax"
means Hungarian wax peppers with real heat.

**Skip:** yellow-dyed versions if you want a natural look. Check the label
for yellow 5 and choose one colored with turmeric or nothing at all.

## Quick FAQ

**What are banana peppers?**
A mild, long yellow pepper, usually sold pickled and sliced into rings.
They're named for their color and curved shape.

**Are banana peppers spicy?**
Barely. Most are 0 to 500 Scoville units, far milder than a jalapeño. Hot
banana peppers exist but are labeled hot.

**What's the difference between banana peppers and pepperoncini?**
They look alike, but pepperoncini are a different variety, slightly hotter
and more bitter, and usually sold whole. Banana peppers are milder, sweeter
and usually sliced.

**What goes with banana peppers on a board?**
Provolone, hard salami, pepperoni and cream cheese. Stuff whole ones with
cream cheese for an easy bite.

**How long do opened banana peppers last?**
About 2 to 3 months in the fridge, as long as they stay covered in brine.
