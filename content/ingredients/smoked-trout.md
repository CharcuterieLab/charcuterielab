## The short version

Hot-smoked trout fillet — firm, flaky and mild, at roughly half the price of
smoked salmon. It's the fish most people overlook, and mashed into cream cheese
with lemon it makes the best cheap dip on any board.

## What it is

Trout is usually hot-smoked: brined briefly, then smoked at 70–80°C until
cooked through. That leaves it opaque and flaky rather than silky. Cold-smoked
trout exists and slices like salmon, but the hot-smoked fillet is what most
shops carry.

**Taste:** mild, sweet, gently smoky, less oily than salmon
**Texture:** firm and flaky, breaking into large moist pieces
**Look:** pale pink-bronze fillet with golden smoked skin

Lower fat is the mechanism, and it's why trout tastes cleaner than salmon.
Trout carries noticeably less oil, so the smoke sits lighter and the flavour is
gentler — which makes it the better choice when there's already a lot going on
elsewhere on the board.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese, mashed together** | Flake the trout into softened cream cheese with lemon, horseradish and chives. The best-value dip on any board. |
| **Rye crispbread and dark bread** | Sour and dry against mild smoke. |
| **Horseradish and crème fraîche** | Heat and cool fat. The classic British service. |
| **Lemon and capers** | Acid and brine, as with any smoked fish. |
| **Cucumber and dill** | Cool, clean and herbal. |
| **Pickled onion** | Sharp and colourful. |
| **Dry Riesling, Sancerre, cold cider** | High-acid and cold, which is what lifts gentle smoke. |

**Skip it with:** strong cured meats and blue cheese. Trout is the mildest fish
here and almost anything assertive erases it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Flake it with a fork into large pieces**, skin and pin bones removed. Don't
   shred it to mush.
2. **Or make the dip** — that's the highest-value move on this page. Flake one
   fillet into 8 oz of softened cream cheese with lemon juice, a spoon of
   horseradish and chopped chives.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
4. **Put lemon and horseradish beside it.**
5. **Pile the flakes rather than arranging them.** Smoked fish looks better
   loose than styled.
6. **Keep it cool.** It's a cooked, moist product with a shorter window than
   dry-cured meat.
7. **Fix:** dry at the edges? Fold it into the dip rather than serving it
   plain.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot-smoked trout fillet** | Whole Foods, most grocery, specialty | The standard. Peppered and plain both work. |
| **Ducktrap or Spence & Co** | Whole Foods, grocery | Widely distributed American smokehouses. Reliable. |
| **Smoked trout pâté / dip** | Whole Foods, specialty | Convenient, though making your own is better and cheaper. |
| **Cold-smoked trout** | Specialty grocers | Slices like salmon. Rarer, excellent. |
| **Smoked mackerel** | Specialty, British and Polish markets | Oilier and bolder. Cheaper still, and very good. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Grocery, Whole Foods | The richer alternative — see its own page. |

**Label check:** hot-smoked fillets are opaque and flaky; cold-smoked is
translucent and sliceable. Peppered versions are good but narrow the pairings.

**Skip:** buying it far ahead. It's a moist cooked product, not a dry cure, and
it doesn't keep like salami.

## Quick FAQ

**Smoked trout or smoked salmon?**
Trout is milder, flakier, less oily and about half the price. Salmon is richer
and silkier. Trout is the better value and the better dip.

**Is it cooked?**
Hot-smoked trout is fully cooked. Cold-smoked trout is not, and slices like
salmon.

**What's the best thing to do with it?**
Flake it into cream cheese with lemon, horseradish and chives. Ten minutes, and
it's the item people finish first.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz, or one fillet for every three or four people if you're making dip.

**Do I remove the skin?**
Yes — peel it off and check for pin bones before flaking.
