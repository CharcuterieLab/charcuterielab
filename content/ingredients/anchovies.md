## The short version

Two very different products. **Brown salt-cured anchovies** — the Cantabrian
fillets in oil — are dense, savoury and expensive, and nothing like the harsh
strips on a cheap pizza. **White *boquerones*** are vinegar-cured, mild and
bright. Both belong on boards; they do opposite jobs.

## What it is

Salt-cured anchovies are packed in salt for six months to two years, then
filleted and packed in oil. The long cure ferments the flesh, breaking proteins
into free amino acids and producing enormous savoury depth. *Boquerones en
vinagre* skip all of that — fresh anchovies marinated in vinegar and oil for a
day or two, served white and firm.

**Salt-cured:** deep reddish-brown, dense, intensely savoury and salty
**Boquerones:** pale white-grey, firm, bright, sharply acidic
**Look:** brown fillets glossy with oil; or white fillets in oil and parsley

Fermentation is the mechanism behind the brown ones. Months in salt converts
muscle protein into free glutamates — the same compounds that make aged
parmesan and soy sauce taste savoury. That's why a good anchovy tastes of depth
rather than just fish, and why it functions as a seasoning.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Butter on bread** | The definitive pairing. Cold butter, dark bread, one anchovy. Fat blunts salt and carries umami. |
| **Pane carasau or olive oil crackers** | Plain, thin and Mediterranean. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweet and smoky against salt. A Catalan standard. |
| **Boiled egg** | Rich, mild and starchy — classic with both types. |
| **Green olives and pickled guindillas** | The *gilda* pinxto: olive, guindilla and anchovy on a stick. |
| **Tomato, rubbed on toast** | Acid and sweetness laid down before the salt hits. |
| **Fino sherry, Txakoli, cold beer** | Dry, saline and cold — the only register that survives this much salt. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses and blue cheese. Anchovies are the most
concentrated savoury item in this section and they flatten everything mild.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve them in a small dish with their oil**, not loose on the board.
2. **Put butter and plain bread directly beside them.** Anchovy on buttered
   bread converts people who think they hate anchovies.
3. **Serve two to three fillets per person.** They're intense and expensive.
4. **Make gildas if you want one assembled bite** — a green olive, a pickled
   guindilla and a folded anchovy on a cocktail stick. Two minutes, and it's a
   proper Basque pintxo.
5. **Keep them at one end.** The aroma and the salt travel.
6. **Say which type they are.** People who dislike brown anchovies often love
   boquerones, and vice versa.
7. **Fix:** too salty? Soak brown fillets in milk or water for ten minutes, pat
   dry, and re-oil.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cantabrian anchovies (Ortiz, Nardin, Codesa)** | Whole Foods, specialty grocers, online | The benchmark. Hand-filleted, expensive, worth it. |
| **Ortiz anchovy fillets in olive oil** | Whole Foods, most grocery | The most available good brand. |
| **Boquerones en vinagre** | Spanish grocers, Whole Foods, tapas suppliers | White, vinegar-cured, mild. A completely different board item. |
| **Salt-packed whole anchovies** | Italian delis, specialty | Cheapest per ounce. You rinse and fillet them yourself. |
| **Sardines** | Grocery, specialty | Milder tinned fish — see its own page. |
| **Bottarga** | Italian delis, specialty | Cured fish roe, the other umami bomb — see its own page. |

**Label check:** Cantabrian Sea origin and packed in olive oil for the brown
ones. *Boquerones* or "white anchovies in vinegar" for the mild ones.

**Skip:** cheap flat tins for a board. They're a cooking product — fine melted
into a sauce, harsh eaten straight.

## Quick FAQ

**Why are Cantabrian anchovies so expensive?**
Larger fish, six months to two years in salt, and hand filleting. They're a
different product from mass-market tins.

**What are boquerones?**
Fresh anchovies cured in vinegar rather than salt. White, firm, bright and mild.

**I hate anchovies. Should I try again?**
Probably. Most people's experience is cheap tinned fillets on pizza. A good
Cantabrian fillet on buttered bread is a different food.

**How much per person?**
Two to three fillets. They're intense.

**What's a gilda?**
A Basque pintxo — green olive, pickled guindilla pepper and an anchovy on a
stick. The best two-minute thing on this page.
