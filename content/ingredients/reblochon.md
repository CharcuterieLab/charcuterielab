## The short version

A soft washed-rind cheese from the Savoie Alps, nutty and supple with a gentle
funk. Much milder than Époisses, richer than Taleggio. Its name comes from a
medieval scheme to underpay the landlord, which is a better origin story than
most cheeses get.

## What it is

Reblochon AOP is made in the Haute-Savoie. Tenant farmers were taxed on milk
yield, so they under-milked their cows while the landlord's agent watched, then
milked again — *re-blocher* — afterwards. That second milking was richer in fat
and became this cheese. Raw-milk Reblochon can't be legally imported to the US,
so what's available here is a pasteurized version or a Reblochon-style cheese.

**Taste:** nutty, buttery, faintly barnyard, savory, mildly sweet
**Texture:** soft and supple, yielding but not runny
**Look:** flat disc, pale ivory paste, thin washed orange-pink rind with a
white bloom

Second-milking fat is the mechanism and it's still true today. The higher fat
content gives Reblochon its supple, buttery texture and blunts the sharpness a
washed rind would otherwise contribute — which is why it's one of the gentlest
cheeses in this category despite the orange rind.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette** | Plain bread under a soft, mildly funky cheese. |
| **Boiled potatoes** | *Tartiflette* — Reblochon melted over potatoes, bacon and onion — is the regional dish. |
| **Cured ham and bacon** | Salt and smoke with a buttery alpine paste. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onions** | Sharp vinegar cutting fat. |
| **Apple and pear** | Fresh acid against richness. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin cuts the fat and the crunch breaks a uniformly supple bite. |
| **Savoie whites, dry cider, light reds** | Regional and acidic. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses in the same corner. Reblochon is gentle
for a washed-rind but still more aromatic than a brie.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.** It should be soft and yielding.
2. **Cut wedges from the center out**, keeping rind on each piece.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.** One disc serves four to six.
4. **Put it in the middle of the board's intensity range**, between the mild
   cheeses and the strong ones.
5. **Eat the rind or trim it** — it's edible and part of the flavor, though
   some find it too funky.
6. **Consider tartiflette** if you're feeding people properly: sliced potatoes,
   bacon, onion, a halved Reblochon on top, baked until molten.
7. **Fix:** an ammonia smell means it's over-ripe. A gentle barnyard smell is
   correct.

## Quick FAQ

**Why can't I find real Reblochon in America?**
It's a raw-milk cheese aged under 60 days, which US regulations prohibit for
import. Pasteurized versions and Reblochon-style cheeses are what's available.

**What does the name mean?**
*Re-blocher* — to milk again. Tenant farmers under-milked in front of the
landlord's agent and milked again afterwards, using that richer milk for this
cheese.

**How strong is it?**
Mild for a washed-rind. Nutty and buttery with a light barnyard note.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, though it's the funkiest part. Some trim it.

**What's tartiflette?**
The regional dish — potatoes, bacon, onion and a halved Reblochon baked until
molten. Worth making.
