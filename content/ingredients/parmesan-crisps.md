## The short version

Shredded parmesan baked until it melts, spreads and hardens into a lacy disc.
No flour, no carbs, nothing but cheese. It's the best gluten-free base on a
board — with the catch that it adds cheese rather than giving cheese somewhere
to sit.

## What it is

The Italian original is *frico*, from Friuli, where it was a way to use up
cheese ends. Grated hard cheese hits a hot surface, the fat renders out, the
proteins brown and fuse, and the moisture leaves. What's left is a rigid,
translucent, deeply savory wafer.

**Taste:** intensely salty, nutty, concentrated umami
**Texture:** brittle, lacy, dissolves rather than crumbles
**Look:** golden, irregular, full of small holes

Concentration is the mechanism. Removing the water from an already savory
cheese leaves everything else behind at double strength. It also means the
crisp has no neutral background at all — every pairing has to survive a very
loud partner.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Prosciutto** | Salt, fat and umami stacked deliberately. Small bite, enormous flavor. |
| **Cherry tomatoes and basil** | Acid and freshness are the only things that cut this much salt. The most balanced use. |
| **Aged balsamic** | Sweet-sour syrup against concentrated salt. Two drops is enough. |
| **Fresh mozzarella or burrata** | Loud and quiet, hard and soft. The contrast is the point. |
| **Figs and honey** | Sweet against savory, and the crisp's brittleness carries a soft fig well. |
| **Soups and salads as a garnish** | Worth knowing — a broken crisp on a soup is a restaurant move. |
| **Prosecco, dry white, light red** | Acidic wine resets your palate after all that salt. |

**Skip it with:** other aged hard cheeses — parmesan next to pecorino next to
aged gouda is the same note three times. Skip blue cheese too; both are salt
bombs and neither wins. And never make this your only base: a board of cheese
crisps and cheese is a board with nothing to stand on.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Give them their own small dish.** They release oil, and greasy rings on a
   wooden board look bad in photos and worse in person.
2. **Serve 3–4 per person** as an accent, not a foundation.
3. **Always put out a real cracker alongside.** Guests need something neutral,
   and a gluten-free guest needs a *second* gluten-free option that isn't
   dairy.
4. **Make them fresh if you can.** One tablespoon mounds of finely grated
   parmesan on parchment, 375°F for 5–6 minutes until golden at the edges.
   Cool flat for two minutes. Homemade is dramatically better than boxed.
5. **Drape them over a rolling pin while warm** for curved tuiles if you want a
   showpiece.
6. **Fix:** they go soft in humid rooms. 2 minutes at 325°F and a full cool
   restores the snap.

**Label check:** the ingredient list should read *cheese*, and maybe salt. Some
brands add rice flour or starch to cut costs, which makes them chewy rather
than brittle.

**Skip:** flavored versions on a cheese board — pizza, ranch and jalapeño
crisps are a snack food and read as one next to real cheese.

## Quick FAQ

**Are parmesan crisps gluten-free?**
Yes, if the only ingredient is cheese. Check the label — some brands add flour
or starch.

**Are they keto?**
Yes. Essentially zero carbs, all fat and protein. It's why the category grew.

**How do I make parmesan crisps?**
Tablespoon mounds of finely grated parmesan on parchment, 375°F for 5–6
minutes, cool flat. That's the whole recipe.

**How many per person?**
3–4 as an accent. Don't build a board on them.

**Do they count as the cracker or the cheese?**
The cheese. Plan your board accordingly and put out a real base too.
