## The short version

Soft, white, barely salted cow's or buffalo milk cheese, sold swimming in
liquid. On its own it tastes of almost nothing, which is the point and the
problem — put it on a board unseasoned and it's the item nobody touches.

## What it is

Mozzarella is a *pasta filata* — stretched-curd — cheese. Curd is heated in hot
water and pulled until it becomes elastic, then formed into balls and dropped
into brine. It's eaten within days rather than aged, so there's no rind, no
mold, and very little salt penetration.

**Taste:** milky, sweet, lactic, very mildly salted
**Texture:** soft and springy with a slight squeak, layered when torn
**Look:** bright white spheres in cloudy liquid

Mildness is the mechanism, and it cuts both ways. Fresh mozzarella has almost
no flavor of its own to clash with anything, so it takes seasoning and
accompaniment better than any other cheese on a board. Left plain, that same
neutrality reads as blandness.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Ripe tomato, basil, olive oil** | Caprese. Acid, herb and fat supplying everything the cheese doesn't. |
| **Flaky salt and cracked pepper** | Not a pairing so much as a requirement. Season it before it goes out. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and cure against sweet milk — the reason this pairing is on every Italian menu. |
| **Aged balsamic** | Sharp and syrupy against soft and plain. Two drops per piece. |
| **Marinated olives and artichokes** | Briny, oily neighbors that lend the cheese their seasoning. |
| **Peaches, melon, figs** | Summer fruit sugar with unsalted milk fat. |
| **Pesto** | Herb, garlic, oil and salt in one spoonful. The efficient answer. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese, aged cheddar and strong cured meats like
soppressata — anything assertive erases it. And skip serving it straight from
the tub with no seasoning at all.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain it and pat it dry.** Wet mozzarella makes a puddle and dilutes
   everything it touches.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.** Cold mozzarella is rubbery; at room temperature it's
   tender.
3. **Tear it, don't slice it.** Torn edges are rough and hold oil and salt;
   sliced faces are smooth and shed them.
4. **Season it before serving** — olive oil, flaky salt, pepper. This is the
   step people skip and it's the one that matters.
5. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** An 8 oz ball serves four to six.
6. **Put it in a shallow dish** at the fresh end of the board, away from blue
   and washed-rind cheeses.
7. **Fix:** bland and watery? More salt and more oil, not more cheese. It
   almost always needs both.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **BelGioioso Fresh Mozzarella** | Most grocery stores | The reliable American default. |
| **Buffalo mozzarella (mozzarella di bufala)** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, Italian delis | Made from water buffalo milk. Tangier, richer, noticeably better. Worth the premium. |
| **Ovoline or bocconcini** | Grocery cheese case | Small balls, easier to portion for a board. |
| **Ciliegine** | Grocery, deli | Cherry-sized. Best for skewers with tomato and basil. |
| **Local Italian deli mozzarella** | Italian markets | Often made that morning. A different food from the tub version. |
| **Trader Joe's Fresh Mozzarella** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and fine, especially for seasoning heavily. |

**Label check:** buy it packed in liquid, not shrink-wrapped. The dry
shrink-wrapped blocks are low-moisture mozzarella — a pizza cheese, not a board
cheese.

**Skip:** low-moisture mozzarella, pre-shredded mozzarella, and anything sold
in a solid block at room temperature. Different product entirely.

## Quick FAQ

**Fresh mozzarella vs. buffalo mozzarella?**
Buffalo mozzarella uses water buffalo milk — tangier, richer, softer. Fresh
mozzarella usually means the cow's milk version.

**Why does mine taste like nothing?**
It's underseasoned. Olive oil, flaky salt and pepper, and it becomes a
different cheese.

**How long does it keep?**
A few days in its liquid, refrigerated. It's a fresh cheese, not an aged one.

**Can I use the low-moisture kind?**
Not for a board. That's pizza cheese — firm, rubbery and made for melting.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz. An 8 oz ball covers four to six on a mixed board.
