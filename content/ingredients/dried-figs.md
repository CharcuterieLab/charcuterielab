## The short version

Fresh figs have a six-week season; dried figs are always there and cost a
fraction as much. The one rule is to halve them — sliced lengthwise, the pale
seeded interior against the dark skin is genuinely handsome. Whole, they look
like brown pebbles.

## What it is

Figs dried to chewy density. Two types dominate: **Turkish (Smyrna/Calimyrna)**,
which are pale golden, large, and nutty; and **Black Mission**, which are smaller,
darker, sweeter and jammier. Both are sold whole and pressed flat.

**Taste:** honeyed, jammy, faintly nutty, with a seed crunch
**Texture:** dense and chewy, seeds throughout
**Look:** dark or golden halves showing a pale seeded centre

The mechanism is sugar plus texture. A date is pure caramel with no structure; a
dried fig delivers similar sweetness with thousands of tiny crunchy seeds
running through it. On a board of soft cheese and soft cured meat, that texture
is doing as much work as the flavour.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | Honeyed sugar against salt and funk. The seeds give the bite something to push against. |
| **Manchego** | The Spanish pairing: nutty sheep cheese and nutty golden fig. |
| **Parmigiano-Reggiano** | Crystalline salt and crunchy seeds — two textures, one bite. |
| **Prosciutto** | Wrap a fig half. Salt and honey, in the format that works with everything. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannic bitterness against jammy sugar. Fig and walnut is a fixed combination in bread for a reason. |
| **Aged goat cheese** | Sharp and dry against sticky and sweet. |

**Skip it with:** honey, jam and dates all on the same board. Pick one sweet
anchor and let it work.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice them in half lengthwise.** Non-negotiable — the interior is what
   makes them look like food.
2. **Snip off the hard stem tip** with scissors.
3. **Fan the halves cut-side up.**
4. **Stuff them** if you want: goat cheese or blue pressed into the cut side,
   the same treatment as a date.
5. **Serve 1 oz per person** — two or three figs.
6. **Soak in warm water, wine or brandy for ten minutes** if they've gone hard,
   then pat dry.
7. **Fix:** white powder on the outside? That's sugar bloom, not mould. Wipe or
   ignore it.

| Product | Where | Notes |

| **Black Mission figs** | Grocery, Whole Foods | Smaller, darker, sweeter, jammier. Best colour contrast on a board. |
| **Fig cake / pan de higo** | Spanish delis, Whole Foods | Pressed figs with almonds, sold as a disc. Slice thin — outstanding with manchego. |
| **Adriatic figs** | Specialty, online | Pale green-gold, delicate. A step up in price and flavour. |
| **Fig jam** | Grocery | The spreadable version. Different ingredient, same partners. |

**Label check:** pliable and soft through the bag. Hard bricks of fig need
soaking and are usually old stock.

**Skip:** figs with added sugar or glucose syrup — they're already sweet enough.

## Quick FAQ

**Should I cut dried figs for a board?**
Yes — halve them lengthwise so the seeded interior shows. Whole figs look like
brown lumps.

**Turkish or Black Mission figs?**
Turkish are larger, golden, and nuttier. Black Mission are sweeter and darker,
with better colour contrast.

**What's the white powder on my dried figs?**
Sugar bloom — natural sugars crystallising on the surface. It's harmless.

**How do I soften hard dried figs?**
Soak in warm water, wine or brandy for ten minutes and pat dry.

**What cheese goes with dried figs?**
Blue cheese, manchego, Parmigiano-Reggiano, and aged goat cheese.
