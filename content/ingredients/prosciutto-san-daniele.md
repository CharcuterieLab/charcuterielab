## The short version

The other great Italian ham, from a small town in Friuli near the Slovenian
border. Sweeter and softer than Parma, pressed into a distinctive flat shape,
and always sold with the trotter still attached. If you've only had Parma, this
is worth the comparison.

## What it is

Prosciutto di San Daniele DOP is made in one town, from Italian-born pigs, with
sea salt and nothing else. Two things set it apart: the hams are pressed during
curing, which gives them their flat guitar shape and drives salt deeper, and the
trotter is left on, which producers say helps regulate moisture as the ham
dries. Minimum thirteen months.

**Taste:** sweeter than Parma, delicate, faintly nutty
**Texture:** softer and more yielding, almost buttery
**Look:** darker rose-red, flatter profile, trotter attached on the whole leg

Microclimate is the mechanism producers point to. San Daniele sits where humid
Adriatic air meets dry Alpine air coming down off the Carnic Alps, and the
constant exchange dries the hams slowly and evenly. Whether or not you credit
the romance, the result is measurably softer and sweeter than Parma.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh figs and melon** | Sweet fruit against a ham that's already sweet — it amplifies rather than contrasts, and it works. |
| **Burrata** | Milky and unsalted under silky and salted. |
| **Grissini, wrapped** | Dry crunch inside soft silk. |
| **Comté or aged Montasio** | Friuli borders the Alps; nutty mountain cheese is the regional match. |
| **Baguette and butter** | Plain and rich, letting the ham lead. |
| **Ripe pear** | Cold sweet water cutting salt. |
| **Friulano, Prosecco, Ribolla Gialla** | Friulian whites, high-acid and local. |

**Skip it with:** loud crackers, smoked cheese and anything spicy. This is the
most delicate meat in the section — treat it accordingly.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice paper-thin, to order if possible.**
2. **Temper 20 minutes.** Softer than Parma to begin with, and cold blunts it
   completely.
3. **Drape in loose folds**, never flat-stacked.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Put Parma beside it if you have both.** Side by side, the sweetness
   difference is obvious and it makes a genuinely interesting board.
6. **Leave the fat on.**
7. **Fix:** if slices tear, they're too cold or cut too thin for the machine.
   Ask for a fractionally thicker cut.

**Label check:** the DOP mark and "San Daniele." Pork and salt should be the
only ingredients.

**Skip:** buying it and Parma in the same week unless you'll serve them
together. Both are expensive and both are best fresh-sliced.

## Quick FAQ

**San Daniele or Parma?**
San Daniele is sweeter and softer; Parma is more savory and slightly firmer.
Neither is better — serve both if you can.

**Why is the trotter left on?**
Producers hold that it helps regulate moisture as the leg dries. It's also the
visual signature of the DOP.

**Why is it flat?**
The hams are pressed during curing, which shapes them and helps the salt
penetrate evenly.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of several meats.

**Does it need to be sliced to order?**
It's noticeably better, but good pre-sliced packs are widely sold.
