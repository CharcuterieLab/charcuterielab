## The short version

If you buy one jar for a cheese board, buy this one. Fig jam is thick, seedy,
and sweet without being bright, which means it flatters almost every cheese
instead of arguing with a few. It's also stiff enough to sit on a knife and on a
cracker without running, which is more than most jams manage.

## What it is

Figs cooked down with sugar, usually with lemon and sometimes with balsamic or
port. Sold as jam, preserve, confit or *confettura di fichi*. The texture ranges
from smooth spread to chunky preserve with visible fruit and seeds.

**Taste:** deep, honeyed, mildly tannic, faintly caramel
**Texture:** thick and seedy, holds a shape
**Look:** dark purple-brown, glossy, flecked with seeds

The mechanism is low acid and high body. Bright jams — raspberry, apricot,
marmalade — bring acid that competes with the cheese's own. Fig jam is
comparatively low in acid and high in pectin and seed, so it reads as sweetness
plus texture rather than as a second flavour fighting for attention. That's why
it's the safe default across a whole board rather than the perfect match for one
cheese.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Sweet against soft fat, with the seeds giving the bite something to push against. The most-made bite on any board. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | Sugar blunts salt and funk directly, and fig's faint tannin keeps it from turning cloying. |
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk nuttiness and fig honey are a fixed Spanish pairing, usually with membrillo alongside. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat against dark sugar. Spread it thin on the cracker, then lay the meat over. |
| **Water crackers and neutral bases** | The jam is the flavour; the cracker just has to hold it without adding anything. |
| **Goat cheese** | Tang cutting sugar. The two-ingredient board that always works. |

**Skip it with:** other sweet things. Fig jam plus honey plus dates is one flat
note across the whole board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant it into a small bowl.** A jar on a board looks like you ran out of
   time.
2. **Give it its own spreader.** Jam on a cheese knife ends up on every cheese.
3. **Stir it first.** Jam separates in the jar and the top inch is often stiffer.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — a small bowl covers eight people.
5. **Put it next to the blue or the brie**, not in the middle. It should be
   obvious what it's for.
6. **Loosen it with a teaspoon of water** if it's too stiff to spread.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? Stir in a few drops of balsamic or lemon juice. It sharpens
   the whole jar.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dalmatia fig spread** | Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, cheese shops | The category standard. Thick, seedy, not too sweet. |
| **Trader Joe's fig butter** | Trader Joe's | Smoother and cheaper. Excellent value. |
| **Italian confettura di fichi** | Italian delis, Eataly | Chunkier and less sugary. Worth the premium. |
| **Fig and balsamic jam** | Specialty grocers | Sharper. Better for cured meat than for soft cheese. |
| **Fig jam with walnuts** | Cheese shops | Texture already built in. Good with blue cheese. |

**Label check:** figs should be the first ingredient, not sugar or glucose syrup.
A jar where sugar leads will be sweet and thin.

**Skip:** fig-flavoured jelly with no visible seeds — the seeds are half the point.

## Quick FAQ

**What cheese goes with fig jam?**
Almost all of them. Brie, blue cheese, manchego and goat cheese are the standouts.

**Is fig jam the same as fig butter or fig spread?**
Roughly. Fig butter is smoother and usually less sweet; fig spread and fig jam are
used interchangeably.

**How much fig jam do I need for a board?**
About half an ounce per person — a small bowl serves eight.

**Should I take it out of the jar?**
Yes. Decant into a small bowl with its own spreader so it doesn't end up on every
cheese knife.

**How do I make it less sweet?**
Stir in a few drops of balsamic vinegar or lemon juice.
