## The short version

A soft, spreadable cured sausage from Mallorca, coloured deep orange by
pimentón. It's 'nduja's calmer cousin — paprika-sweet rather than chili-hot —
and the traditional way to eat it is warmed onto bread with honey drizzled over.

## What it is

Sobrasada is finely ground pork and pork fat mixed with generous quantities of
sweet or hot pimentón, salt and pepper, cased and cured for weeks to months in
the mild Balearic climate. The high fat content and short cure mean it never
firms up — it stays spreadable throughout. Sobrasada de Mallorca is a protected
IGP.

**Taste:** sweet-smoky paprika, rich, mildly spiced, deeply porky
**Texture:** soft and spreadable, like a coarse pâté
**Look:** vivid orange-red paste, glossy with fat

Paprika volume is the mechanism. Sobrasada carries so much pimentón that the
spice functions as both preservative and bulk — it's what colours the fat,
carries the smoke, and keeps the paste stable. Where 'nduja gets its heat from
Calabrian chili, sobrasada gets sweetness and smoke from Spanish paprika.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Warm bread with honey** | The traditional Mallorcan service — *pa amb sobrassada i mel*. Sweet against smoky fat. |
| **Crostini, warm** | Heat loosens it into a glaze. |
| **Manchego or Mahón** | Spanish cheese against Spanish paprika fat. |
| **Membrillo** | Sweet quince, same logic as the honey. |
| **Piquillo peppers** | Sweet and smoky, doubling the pimentón note. |
| **Fried or scrambled eggs** | If the board runs into brunch. Exceptional. |
| **Cava, Rioja, Mallorcan reds** | Bubbles or structure against fat. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses — the paprika and fat bury them. And
don't serve sobrasada and 'nduja together; they occupy the same slot and the
'nduja wins.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve it in a small bowl with its own knife.** It stains everything.
2. **Warm it.** Twenty seconds in a pan or thirty in a low oven turns it from
   a firm paste into a glossy spread. This is the difference between good and
   very good.
3. **Put honey right next to it.** Not optional — it's the pairing the sausage
   was built around.
4. **Serve three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce per person.**
5. **Warm bread alongside**, not cold crackers.
6. **Peel the casing** and scoop the paste out.
7. **Fix:** too firm to spread? It's cold. Warm it rather than working it.

**Label check:** pimentón high in the ingredients and a deep even orange
throughout. Brown or dull paste means it's old.

**Skip:** serving it cold on a cracker. It reads as greasy that way and
excellent warm.

## Quick FAQ

**Is sobrasada the same as 'nduja?**
Same idea, different country and heat level. Sobrasada is Mallorcan and
paprika-sweet; 'nduja is Calabrian and chili-hot.

**Do I have to warm it?**
It's much better warm. Cold sobrasada is firm and reads as greasy.

**What's the traditional pairing?**
Warm bread with honey drizzled over the sobrasada. It sounds odd and it's
excellent.

**How much per person?**
Three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce. It's very rich.

**Is it spicy?**
Usually mild — most sobrasada uses sweet pimentón. Hot versions exist and say
so.
