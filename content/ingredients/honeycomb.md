## The short version

Raw honey still in the beeswax comb the bees built. You eat the wax along with
the honey — it's edible, mildly chewy, and it disappears as you chew. On a board
it does two jobs at once: it's the honey, and it's the thing everybody looks at
and asks about.

## What it is

A section of honeycomb cut straight from the frame, sold in a plastic box or a
jar of honey. The wax is a soft, pale-gold hexagonal structure filled with liquid
honey. Nothing has been processed, heated, or filtered.

**Taste:** honey, plus a faint clean waxiness
**Texture:** liquid honey inside a soft chewable wax
**Look:** golden hexagons, dripping — the most striking thing on any board

The mechanism is presentation over flavour. The honey inside tastes much like any
raw honey. What the comb adds is structure and spectacle: it gives the board a
visual centre, a reason for guests to ask a question, and something that reads as
expensive without being complicated.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | The pairing everyone photographs, and it works — sugar suppressing salt, with the wax adding chew against creamy cheese. |
| **Manchego** | Firm sheep cheese and a square of comb, eaten together. The textures suit each other. |
| **Brie** | Soft against soft, sweet against fat, with the comb's structure the only firm thing in the bite. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp and crystalline against sweet and waxy. |
| **Walnuts and toasted nuts** | Honey, nut, cheese — with the comb replacing the drizzle. |
| **Warm bread** | The wax softens on warm bread, which is the easiest way to eat it. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, but don't also put out a pot of honey. One or the
other.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut a square with a sharp knife**, two to three inches. Don't put out the
   whole box.
2. **Set it on a small plate or slate**, not on bare wood — it leaks steadily.
3. **Put it near the centre.** It's the centrepiece; treat it as one.
4. **Give people a knife.** They cut off a chunk and eat it, wax and all.
5. **Tell people the wax is edible.** Most guests will hesitate otherwise.
6. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
7. **Fix:** too firm to cut cleanly? Let it sit at room temperature for twenty
   minutes first.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Comb honey in a box** | Whole Foods, farmers markets, cheese shops | A single cut square. The standard buy. |
| **Chunk honey (comb in a jar)** | Farmers markets, specialty | A piece of comb suspended in liquid honey. Best value and less messy. |
| **Local apiary comb** | Farmers markets | Freshest and often cheapest. Ask about the floral source. |
| **Savannah Bee or Bee Raw comb** | Whole Foods, online | Reliable retail brands. |

**Label check:** it should be raw comb honey with nothing added. Buy from
somewhere with turnover; comb crystallises over time and gets grainy.

**Skip:** honeycomb candy (cinder toffee). Different product entirely, despite
the name.

## Quick FAQ

**Can you eat the honeycomb wax?**
Yes. Beeswax is edible — soft, mildly chewy, and it breaks down as you chew. Some
people chew and discard it like gum; both are fine.

**What cheese goes with honeycomb?**
Blue cheese above all, plus manchego, brie and aged cheddar.

**How do I serve honeycomb on a board?**
Cut a two- or three-inch square, set it on a small plate so the drips are
contained, and put a knife beside it.

**Is comb honey better than regular honey?**
It doesn't taste dramatically different. What it adds is texture and
presentation.

**How do I stop it making a mess?**
Serve it on a small plate or slate rather than directly on the board, and let it
come to room temperature so it cuts cleanly.
