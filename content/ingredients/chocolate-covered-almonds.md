## The short version

Chocolate and toasted almond, pre-assembled and requiring nothing from you. They
fill the dessert corner, they look like small dark pebbles that sit well in a
bowl, and they're the thing people eat last while still standing at the board.
Buy sea-salt dark chocolate versions.

## What it is

Roasted almonds enrobed in chocolate, usually with a thin cocoa or sugar shell to
stop them sticking. Dark, milk and white versions exist; some are salted, some are
dusted in cocoa powder, some are turbinado-crusted.

**Taste:** bitter chocolate, sweet coating, toasted nut, often salt
**Texture:** hard shell, snap of chocolate, crunch of almond
**Look:** dark round pebbles, matte if cocoa-dusted, glossy if enrobed

The mechanism is a complete bite in one piece. A dessert corner usually asks the
guest to assemble something — chocolate plus a nut plus a piece of dried fruit.
This is all three moves in one pick-up, which is why the bowl empties before the
plain chocolate does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Dark chocolate shards** | Same flavour, different texture and format. They belong in the same corner. |
| **Dried cherries** | Tart fruit, bitter chocolate, toasted nut — the three-part dessert bite. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | Bitter chocolate handles the salt, the almond adds crunch. Better than plain chocolate for this. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel and butterscotch against cocoa and toasted almond. |
| **Medjool dates** | Both very sweet and dense. The Middle Eastern dessert corner. |
| **Espresso** | If the board runs into after-dinner territory. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and savoury cheese. Keep it in the sweet corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the bag into a small bowl.
2. **Serve in a bowl**, not scattered — the cocoa dust transfers to everything.
3. **Put them in the dessert corner** with the chocolate, dried fruit and nuts.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Keep them cool but not cold.** Chocolate blooms in a warm room and tastes
   flat cold.
6. **Flag the allergens.** Tree nuts and dairy, both common.
7. **Fix:** whitish bloom? Cocoa butter, harmless. They still taste fine.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dark chocolate sea salt almonds** | Trader Joe's, grocery | The best board version. Salt is what makes them work with cheese. |
| **Cocoa-dusted almonds** | Trader Joe's, specialty | Matte, bitter, less sweet. Very good and less obviously candy. |
| **Chocolate-covered marcona almonds** | Cheese shops, specialty | The upgrade. Softer nut, better chocolate. |
| **Chocolate-covered hazelnuts** | Italian delis, specialty | Gianduja logic. Arguably better than almonds. |
| **Chocolate-covered espresso beans** | Grocery, specialty | Same corner, more bitter, more caffeine. |

**Label check:** dark chocolate with cocoa listed first. Avoid "chocolatey
coating" or compound chocolate, which uses vegetable fat instead of cocoa butter.

**Skip:** milk chocolate versions if the board has cheese on it — too sweet to
pair.

## Quick FAQ

**What kind should I buy for a cheese board?**
Dark chocolate with sea salt, or cocoa-dusted. Milk chocolate is too sweet to pair
with cheese.

**Do they go with cheese?**
Yes — particularly blue cheese and aged gouda. The bitterness handles salt and the
almond adds crunch.

**What's the white film on them?**
Cocoa butter bloom from temperature change. Harmless.

**What allergens do they contain?**
Tree nuts, dairy, and usually soy lecithin. Worth flagging on a board.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce — four or five almonds.
