## The short version

Brioche with the crusts cut off, toasted in butter, and sliced into triangles.
It's the richest base you can put on a board — enriched with eggs, butter and a
little sugar — which makes it right for pâté, foie gras and smoked salmon, and
wrong for anything already fatty.

## What it is

Brioche is a French enriched dough: flour, yeast, eggs, a large amount of
butter, and enough sugar to brown quickly. Toast points are the classic service
form — crusts removed, the slice toasted or oven-baked, then cut corner to
corner into two or four triangles.

**Taste:** buttery, faintly sweet, eggy
**Texture:** crisp shell over a tender, almost custardy interior
**Look:** deep gold triangles, fine even crumb, no crust

Richness is the mechanism, and it's a deliberate departure from how most of
this category works. Nearly every other base on a board exists to contrast with
a rich topping. Brioche does the opposite — it *matches* richness, which is
why it's the traditional partner for the richest foods on any table.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Chicken liver pâté and mousse** | Rich on rich, on purpose. The bread's sugar balances the liver's minerality. |
| **Foie gras** | The classic service, unchanged for a century. Nothing else is used. |
| **Smoked salmon and crème fraîche** | Butter and salmon fat with a sweet edge underneath. |
| **Triple-crème cheese** | Both are enriched with cream. It reads as one luxurious thing rather than two. |
| **Fig jam and honey** | Brioche is halfway to a pastry, and this leans into it. |
| **Caviar with crème fraîche** | An accepted alternative to blini, and easier to make. |
| **Champagne, Sauternes, dessert wine** | The whole corner of the board this bread belongs to. |

**Skip it with:** salami, prosciutto and cured meats — fat on fat with salt on
top, and the sweetness fights the cure. Skip aged hard cheeses too; parmesan
and brioche is a strange bite. And be careful about volume: two rich things on
a board is elegant, five is heavy.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut the crusts off.** It sounds fussy and it's the entire difference
   between toast points and toast.
2. **Slice about ½ inch thick**, butter both sides lightly, and bake at 375°F
   for 8–10 minutes, flipping once, until golden.
3. **Cut corner to corner while warm** into triangles — two per slice for a
   board, four for passed hors d'oeuvres.
4. **Serve 2–3 points per person.** They're very rich.
5. **Make them a few hours ahead at most.** Brioche stales faster than lean
   bread because of the sugar, and refrigerating makes it worse rather than
   better.
6. **Fix:** re-crisp at 350°F for 3 minutes. Softened toast points recover
   well, unlike softened crackers.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **St Pierre Brioche Loaf** | Most grocery stores, bread aisle | Widely available, sliced, and consistently good. The easy default. |
| **Store bakery brioche loaf** | Whole Foods, Wegmans, grocery bakeries | Better crumb if it's baked in-house. Ask for it unsliced and cut it thick. |
| **Trader Joe's brioche** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and reliable. Comes and goes. |
| **Local French bakery brioche** | Neighborhood bakery | Best version by a distance, and worth it if the board is small and fancy. |
| **Costco brioche loaves** | Costco | Large format, good price. Freeze what you don't use. |
| **Challah** | Grocery bakery, Jewish bakery | Close substitute — enriched with egg but less butter. Slightly less rich, works the same way. |

**Label check:** butter and eggs should be high in the ingredient list. Brioche
made with oil and "egg product" is sweet sandwich bread.

**Skip:** brioche buns. The shape is wrong and they're usually softer and
sweeter than a loaf.

## Quick FAQ

**What are toast points, exactly?**
Bread with the crusts removed, toasted, and cut diagonally into triangles.
Brioche is the rich version; melba toast is the lean one.

**Can I make them ahead?**
A few hours, kept airtight at room temperature. Don't refrigerate.

**How many per person?**
2–3. They're rich enough that people take fewer than you'd expect.

**Brioche or melba toast for pâté?**
Brioche if you want richness and the pâté is the centerpiece. Melba toast if
you want the pâté to taste lighter and cleaner.

**Can I use challah instead?**
Yes. Slightly less buttery, otherwise the same job.
