## The short version

Feta, yoghurt or cream cheese, olive oil and lemon, blitzed until light. It takes
five minutes, costs almost nothing, and it's reliably the first thing to
disappear from a board. Top it with hot honey or chopped pistachios and it looks
like restaurant food.

## What it is

Block feta broken up and blended with Greek yoghurt, cream cheese or ricotta,
plus olive oil, lemon juice and often garlic, until it's smooth and aerated. The
result is much lighter and less aggressively salty than feta alone.

**Taste:** salty, tangy, lemony, creamy
**Texture:** light and fluffy, thicker than yoghurt
**Look:** bright white, swirled, with oil and a topping

The mechanism is dilution plus aeration. Feta on its own is too salty and too
crumbly to eat in quantity. Blending it with something mild and whipping air in
drops the salt concentration and changes the texture entirely — you get feta's
flavour in a form people can eat by the spoonful.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pita chips and lavash** | Sturdy warm bread is what it's built for. Anything that snaps is the wrong base. |
| **Olives and briny things** | Same table, same flavours, and the brine cuts the dairy fat. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweet and smoky against salt and tang, with the red on white doing the visual work. |
| **Hot honey** | Drizzled on top. Salt, fat, sweet, heat — the reason this dish is on every restaurant menu. |
| **Cucumber and raw vegetables** | The lighter counterweight. Also the Greek precedent. |
| **Pomegranate seeds** | Scattered over. Acid, crunch, and the best colour contrast available. |

**Skip it with:** other salty spreads. Whipped feta plus tapenade plus olives is
one flat salty note.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make it:** 8 oz block feta, 1/2 cup Greek yoghurt or cream cheese, 2 tbsp
   olive oil, juice of half a lemon. Blend 2 minutes until light.
2. **Use block feta in brine**, never pre-crumbled — crumbled feta is dry and
   won't whip smooth.
3. **Spread in a shallow bowl** and swirl a hollow into it.
4. **Top it:** hot honey, olive oil, za'atar, chopped pistachios, pomegranate
   seeds, or all of them.
5. **Serve at room temperature.**
6. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
7. **Fix:** grainy? Blend longer, and add a spoonful more yoghurt. Two full
   minutes in a food processor is the difference.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulgarian or Greek block feta in brine** | Grocery, Middle Eastern markets | The best base. Creamier and less crumbly than French. |
| **Greek yoghurt (full fat)** | Any grocery | The lightener. Cream cheese gives a denser result. |
| **Prepared whipped feta** | Grocery, deli counters | Exists and is fine, but homemade is five minutes and much better. |
| **Sheep's milk feta** | Cheese shops | Richer and tangier. Worth the upgrade. |

**Label check:** buy feta packed in brine, in a block. Pre-crumbled feta is
coated in anti-caking agents and won't whip properly.

**Skip:** cow's milk "feta-style" cheese, which is milder and blander.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I make whipped feta?**
Blend 8 oz block feta with 1/2 cup Greek yoghurt, 2 tbsp olive oil and lemon juice
for two full minutes until light and fluffy.

**Why is my whipped feta grainy?**
Either you used pre-crumbled feta, or you didn't blend long enough. Use block feta
in brine and give it two minutes.

**What do you serve with whipped feta?**
Pita chips, warm flatbread, cucumber and raw vegetables, with hot honey or
pistachios on top.

**Can I make it ahead?**
Yes — three days in the fridge. Bring it back to room temperature and re-swirl
before serving.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce. People eat more of this than any other spread on the board.
