## The short version

The easiest nut to eat and the least interesting to pair. Cashews are soft,
sweet, mild and completely without bitterness, which makes them a crowd-pleaser
and a poor contrast ingredient. Where they earn a place is on fusion and tropical
boards, and beside anything spicy.

## What it is

The seed of the cashew apple, always sold roasted — raw cashews are never truly
raw, since the shell contains a caustic oil related to poison ivy that requires
heat processing. Sold whole, halves or pieces, salted or unsalted.

**Taste:** sweet, milky, buttery, barely bitter
**Texture:** softer and denser than other nuts; crumbles rather than snaps
**Look:** pale ivory crescents

The mechanism is softness. Cashews are the only common nut with real give, which
means they don't provide the crunch contrast a board usually wants from nuts.
What they do provide is a creamy, sweet buffer against heat and spice — which is
why they appear in curries, in Sichuan cooking, and beside chilli-cured meats.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Spicy coppa and soppressata** | Fat and sweetness absorbing chilli heat. The most useful thing cashews do on a board. |
| **Dried mango** | Both sweet, both soft, both tropical. A coherent corner of a fusion board. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel and butterscotch in the cheese; sweet milky nut alongside. |
| **Brie** | Soft against soft — unusual, and it works if the rest of the board has crunch. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | The tang supplies the edge the cashew doesn't have. |
| **Curry-spiced or honey-roasted** | Cashews take flavour coatings better than any other nut. |

**Skip it with:** boards built on contrast. If everything else is soft, cashews
add nothing.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Buy roasted and salted.
2. **Serve in a bowl.** Cashews shed a fine oily dust that gets on everything.
3. **Use whole cashews.** Pieces look like an ingredient.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person** — people eat a lot of these.
5. **Pair them with something crunchy** elsewhere on the board, since they can't
   supply crunch themselves.
6. **Look for spiced versions** — curry, chilli-lime, honey-roasted all work
   better here than they would on a harder nut.
7. **Fix:** soft and stale? 300°F for eight minutes restores some crispness,
   though cashews never get properly hard.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Roasted salted whole cashews** | Any grocery, Costco | The default. Whole, not pieces. |
| **Jumbo cashews** | Costco, specialty | Larger and better-looking on a board. |
| **Curry or chilli-lime cashews** | Trader Joe's, Whole Foods | Where cashews get interesting. Excellent on fusion boards. |
| **Honey-roasted cashews** | Grocery | Sweet version, dessert corner. |
| **Raw cashews** | Health stores | Soft and bland. For cooking, not boards. |

**Label check:** whole cashews cost more than pieces for a reason — pieces are
broken and stale faster. Check for oil separation in the bag.

**Skip:** cashew pieces and anything heavily coated in maltodextrin.

## Quick FAQ

**Are cashews good on a charcuterie board?**
They're a crowd-pleaser but a weak contrast — soft, sweet, and low in bitterness.
Best on fusion boards and beside spicy meats.

**Why are cashews always sold roasted?**
The shell contains a caustic oil related to poison ivy, so they're always heat-
processed. Even "raw" cashews have been steamed.

**What goes with cashews?**
Spicy cured meats, dried mango, aged gouda, and fresh goat cheese.

**Whole or pieces?**
Whole. Pieces are broken, stale faster, and look like a baking ingredient.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce. People eat more cashews than any other nut.
