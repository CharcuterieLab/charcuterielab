## The short version

A thin, hard cracker made from flour and water with almost no fat. It has
barely any flavor, and that is the point — it is a plate, not a food. Use it
whenever the cheese, meat, or spread is the thing you want people to taste.

## What it is

Water crackers descend from the ship's biscuit: flour, water, salt, a touch of
oil, rolled paper-thin and baked hot and fast until it blisters and snaps.
Carr's has been making the "table water" version in England since the 1800s,
which is why the two names mean the same thing.

**Taste:** near-neutral, faintly toasty, lightly salted.
**Texture:** brittle, clean snap, no greasy film on the tongue.
**Look:** pale, dimpled, usually round or rounded-square.

The near-zero fat is what makes it useful. Butter crackers coat your palate,
which flattens a delicate cheese; a water cracker gets out of the way.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie, Camembert, triple-crème** | Dry snap against wet cream. Avoids fat-on-fat, which is what makes a buttery cracker feel heavy here. |
| **Fresh goat cheese, burrata, chèvre** | Nothing competes with the tang, so the cheese reads bright instead of muddled. |
| **Blue cheese, Gorgonzola, Stilton** | A loud cheese needs a silent base. The cracker carries the punch without adding to it. |
| **Smoked salmon, pâté, caviar** | The classic use. Delicate, expensive toppings get ruined by a flavored cracker. |
| **Prosciutto and other thin-sliced meats** | Salt and silk against dry crunch. Drape, don't pile — the cracker is thin. |
| **Fig jam, honey, quince paste** | Sweet spreads need a plain canvas or they taste like dessert. |
| **Champagne, dry sparkling, dry white** | Bubbles and acid reset the palate; the cracker resets the texture. |

**Skip it with:** thick dips and hummus (it snaps), wet marinated toppings
(soggy in minutes), and anything already bland — two neutral things on top of
each other is just crunch.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Keep the sleeve sealed until the last minute.** Humidity is the only
   thing that ruins them.
2. **Fan them, don't stack them.** A shingled curve or two short rows of five
   or six. A tall column reads as clutter and gets knocked over.
3. **Serve 4–6 per person** on a mixed board — roughly one sleeve per six
   people. Double it if crackers are the only carb.
4. **Put them at the edge of the board, or in a small dish beside it.** Away
   from cut fruit and soft cheese, which weep moisture.
5. **Hold half the box back and refill mid-party.** Fresher on hour two, and
   the board doesn't look picked-over.
6. **Don't pre-top more than ten minutes ahead.** Anything wet will soften
   them from the underside.
7. **Gone soft?** Three to four minutes at 300°F, then cool completely. They
   crisp right back.

**Label check:** the ingredients should start with flour, water, and salt. If
butter or palm oil is near the top, it's a butter cracker with a water-cracker
name, and it will mute whatever you put on it.

**Skip:** Carr's Roasted Garlic & Herb and Toasted Sesame for a general board.
They're good crackers, but they stop being neutral, which was the whole reason
to buy a water cracker.

## Quick FAQ

**Are "table water crackers" different from water crackers?**
No. "Table water" is Carr's name for it, and it stuck.

**How many do I need?**
4–6 per person on a board with other carbs. About one sleeve per six people.

**How long do they last once opened?**
Three to five days sealed in a bag or airtight container. Unopened, months.

**Are they gluten-free?**
No — they're wheat flour. For a GF board, use rice or seed crackers instead.

**Are they healthy?**
They're mostly flour and water, so they're low in fat and low in calories per
cracker. What goes on top is where the calories live.
