## The short version

The French answer to prosciutto and serrano, from the Basque country in the
southwest. Salted with local salt from the Adour basin, often finished with a
rub of Espelette pepper. Softer than serrano, more savoury than Parma, and much
less known outside France.

## What it is

Jambon de Bayonne IGP is made in the Adour basin from pigs raised across
southwest France. The hams are salted with salt from the Salies-de-Béarn
springs, rested through winter, then dried through spring and summer for a
minimum of seven months — usually nine to twelve. Many are rubbed with Espelette
pepper before the final drying.

**Taste:** savoury, gently sweet, faintly peppery, moderately salty
**Texture:** soft and supple, more yielding than serrano
**Look:** deep rose-pink, wide white fat cap, sometimes a red pepper-dusted rind

The Adour climate is the mechanism producers point to — dry southerly *foehn*
winds through spring and summer, moderated by Atlantic humidity, which dries the
hams steadily rather than fast. The result sits between the Spanish and Italian
styles: drier than Parma, softer than serrano.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Ossau-Iraty** | Basque ham with Basque sheep cheese. The regional pairing and a very good one. |
| **Black cherry jam** | The Basque condiment, and it works with the ham as well as the cheese. |
| **Baguette and butter** | Plain French service. |
| **Espelette pepper** | Already on the rind of many hams; a pinch alongside makes the link. |
| **Melon and figs** | Sweet fruit against salt, as with any dry-cured ham. |
| **Comté** | Nutty and firm against soft and savoury. |
| **Irouléguy, Jurançon, dry cider** | Southwest French wines. |

**Skip it with:** everything crackers and smoked cheese. It's more robust than
Parma but still a delicate dry-cured ham.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thin and drape in loose folds.**
2. **Temper 20 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Leave the fat cap on.** Bayonne hams have a generous one and it's where
   much of the flavour sits.
5. **Put black cherry jam beside it** if you have it — it's the regional
   pairing and almost nobody outside France does it.
6. **Serve it alongside serrano or Parma** for a three-country comparison. The
   differences are clear and it makes a better board than any one ham alone.
7. **Fix:** slices sticking? Temper longer rather than pulling them apart cold.

**Label check:** the IGP mark and the Basque cross branded on the rind. Pork
and salt should be the ingredients.

**Skip:** buying all three of Bayonne, Parma and serrano for a small board —
one good ham beats three thin scatterings.

## Quick FAQ

**How is Bayonne different from prosciutto?**
Bayonne is drier and more savoury, with a wider fat cap and often a rub of
Espelette pepper. Parma is sweeter and silkier.

**Is it smoked?**
No. Some hams from the region are, but Jambon de Bayonne IGP is air-dried only.

**What's the red dust on the rind?**
Espelette pepper — a mild Basque chili — rubbed on before the final drying.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of several meats.

**What's the regional pairing?**
Ossau-Iraty cheese and black cherry jam from Itxassou. All three come from the
same corner of France.
