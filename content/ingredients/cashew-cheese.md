## The short version

Soaked cashews blended smooth, often cultured with the same bacteria used in
dairy cheese. It's the plant-based category that actually works — rich, tangy
and spreadable — and it belongs on a board on its own terms rather than as a
substitute.

## What it is

Raw cashews are soaked, blended with water, salt and usually lemon or
nutritional yeast, then either served fresh or cultured with live cultures and
aged. The cultured versions develop genuine tang and, in some cases, rinds.
Cashews are used because they're low in fiber and high in fat, so they blend to
a genuinely smooth paste where almonds stay grainy.

**Taste:** rich, nutty, tangy when cultured, faintly sweet
**Texture:** smooth and spreadable, firmer when aged
**Look:** pale ivory, matte, sometimes with a bloomy or ash rind

Fermentation is the mechanism, and it's what separates the good products from
the bad. Uncultured cashew cheese is a nut purée with salt — pleasant, not
cheese-like. Cultured versions use the same lactic acid bacteria as dairy
cheesemaking, which produce the same acids and aromatic compounds, and they
taste genuinely tangy as a result. Read the label for "cultured" or "live
cultures."

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Seeded crackers** | Crunch and bitterness against a smooth rich paste. |
| **Fig jam and hot honey** | Sweetness against tang. Note: honey isn't vegan — use agave or fruit preserve if that matters. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Acid cutting nut fat. |
| **Fresh herbs — chives, dill, thyme** | Cashew cheese takes herbs extremely well. |
| **Roasted red peppers and olives** | Oily, briny neighbors on a Mediterranean board. |
| **Cracked pepper and olive oil** | Simple seasoning; the paste is rich enough to need little else. |
| **Crisp white wine, dry rosé, sparkling** | Acid against fat, same as with dairy. |

**Skip it with:** direct comparison to dairy cheese on the same board. It's a
good thing on its own and a poor imitation of something else — serving it
beside a brie invites the wrong judgement.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Treat it as a spread, not a wedge.** Even the firm ones are softer than
   they look.
2. **Temper 20 minutes** and spread it in a shallow bowl or on a small plate.
3. **Season the surface** — olive oil, cracked pepper, chopped herbs, flaky
   salt. Almost every plant-based cheese benefits.
4. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Label it clearly**, both so vegan guests find it and so nobody puts a
   dairy knife in it.
6. **Give it its own knife and its own crackers**, for the same reason.
7. **Fix:** if it tastes flat, it's uncultured. Add lemon juice, salt and
   nutritional yeast and stir — it improves considerably.

| **Rebel Cheese, Blode Kuh and other artisan makers** | Specialty shops, online | Small-batch cultured vegan cheese. Expensive and impressive. |
| **Homemade** | Any grocery | Soak raw cashews four hours, blend with lemon, salt and nutritional yeast. Cheap and easily good. |
| **Almond ricotta** | Whole Foods, natural grocers | The lighter plant-based option — see its own page. |

**Label check:** "cultured" or "live cultures" in the ingredients. Products
without them are nut purée and taste like it.

**Skip:** anything built on coconut oil and starch. Those melt convincingly and
taste of very little — a cooking product rather than a board one.

## Quick FAQ

**Is cashew cheese actually cheese?**
Not by any dairy definition, but the cultured versions use the same bacteria
and develop real tang. It's a fermented food in its own right.

**Which brand should I buy first?**
Miyoko's. It's cultured, widely available and the most consistently good.

**Does it contain nuts?**
Yes — cashews, a tree nut. It's dairy-free, not allergen-free.

**How do I make it?**
Soak raw cashews four hours, blend with lemon juice, salt, nutritional yeast
and a little water. Culture it with a probiotic capsule if you want tang.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz. It's rich.
