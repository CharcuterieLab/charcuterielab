## The short version

Tapioca starch blended with ground shrimp, dried into hard chips, then fried
until they puff into light, savory wafers. Gluten-free, intensely umami, and
almost weightless. They anchor an Asian fusion board the way water crackers
anchor a European one.

## What it is

Krupuk (Indonesia) or *prawn crackers* (British Chinese takeaway) start as a
dough of tapioca starch and pounded shrimp, steamed into a log, sliced thin and
sun-dried. The dried chips are hard and translucent. Dropped into hot oil, the
water inside flashes to steam and they expand several times over in a second.

**Taste:** savory, briny, deeply umami, salty
**Texture:** extraordinarily light, airy, dissolves on the tongue
**Look:** pale pink or white, irregular, honeycombed with tiny holes

Glutamates are the mechanism. Dried shrimp is one of the most glutamate-dense
ingredients in any cuisine, so a prawn cracker carries more savory intensity
than anything else in this section — including cheese crisps. That's an asset
next to mild fresh things and a problem next to anything that was going to be
the savory element itself.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese and cucumber** | Cool, mild and wet against light and intensely savory. The easiest fusion bite. |
| **Smoked salmon and avocado** | Marine flavors together, with the cracker's lightness keeping it from getting heavy. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tangy dairy is one of the few cheese types that holds its own against this much umami. |
| **Sweet chili sauce** | The traditional accompaniment. Sugar and acid balancing salt and shrimp. |
| **Pickled vegetables and ginger** | Acid cuts the salt and resets the palate. |
| **Peanut sauce or satay** | Indonesian and correct — this is how krupuk is eaten at home. |
| **Cold lager, riesling, sparkling wine** | Cold, crisp and slightly sweet against salt. |

**Skip it with:** aged European cheeses — parmesan, aged gouda, cheddar. Both
are umami-heavy and they collide rather than combine. Fruit jams also clash
badly with the shrimp.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy them ready-fried unless you want the show.** The bagged puffed ones
   are perfectly good and take no work.
2. **To fry them yourself:** 350°F oil, a few at a time, 3–5 seconds. They
   expand instantly. Drain on paper. It's genuinely fun to watch.
3. **Serve them in a bowl, not on the board.** They're light enough to blow
   around and they crush under anything.
4. **Serve 4–5 per person.**
5. **Label the shellfish.** This is not optional — shellfish allergy is common
   and severe, and nobody expects it in a cracker.
6. **Keep them away from the cheese.** Build a distinct fusion corner rather
   than mixing them into a European spread.
7. **Fix:** they go soft in humid air within an hour. 3 minutes at 300°F brings
   them back.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ready-fried prawn crackers** | Asian markets, grocery international aisle | The convenient version. Look for bags in the snack section. |
| **Dried krupuk chips (unfried)** | Asian markets, Amazon | Cheap, shelf-stable, and you fry them to order. Far better texture. |
| **Indonesian *krupuk udang*** | Indonesian and Southeast Asian markets | The traditional product. Best flavor. |
| **Vegetarian krupuk (*emping*, melinjo nut)** | Asian markets | Shellfish-free alternative. Bitter and nutty rather than briny. |
| **Any Asian grocery** | H Mart, 99 Ranch, local Asian market | Best selection and a fraction of supermarket prices. |
| **Papadum** | Indian groceries | The other puffed wafer, without shellfish. See that page. |

**Label check:** tapioca starch and shrimp. Some cheaper versions are mostly
starch with shrimp flavoring, and they taste noticeably flatter.

**Skip:** mixing them into a cheese-forward European board. They belong to a
fusion board or their own corner.

## Quick FAQ

**Are prawn crackers gluten-free?**
Usually — tapioca starch and shrimp. Check the label, as some blends add wheat
starch.

**Do they contain shellfish?**
Yes, and that's the important warning on this page. Label them clearly.

**Can I fry them myself?**
Yes, and it's worth doing once. 350°F oil, three to five seconds, and they
expand instantly.

**How many per person?**
4–5. They're light and people take handfuls.

**Is there a shellfish-free version?**
Yes — *emping*, made from melinjo nuts. Bitter and nutty rather than briny, and
it puffs the same way.
