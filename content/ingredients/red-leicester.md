## The short version

A firm English cheese, deep orange, milder and sweeter than cheddar with a
flakier texture and a distinctly nutty finish. It's cheap, widely available and
consistently underrated — the English cheese most likely to surprise someone.

## What it is

Red Leicester has been made in Leicestershire since the 17th century. The color
comes from annatto, added originally because a rich orange paste signaled a
cheese made from good summer milk — over time the coloring became the standard
regardless. It's made much like cheddar but pressed less firmly and aged
shorter, typically three to nine months.

**Taste:** mellow, sweet, nutty, gently savory, faintly caramel
**Texture:** firm but flaky, crumblier and moister than cheddar
**Look:** deep orange-russet paste, dry natural rind on farmhouse versions

Lower pressing is the mechanism. Cheddar is stacked and pressed hard to expel
whey, producing a dense paste that ages sharp. Red Leicester keeps more
moisture, so it stays mellow and develops nutty and caramel notes rather than
acidic ones — a different destination from a similar starting point.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream crackers and oatcakes** | Plain English bases. The Ploughman's setup. |
| **Chutney and pickle** | Sweet and sharp against a mellow nutty cheese. The traditional service. |
| **Apple and pear** | Fresh acid against sweetness. |
| **Walnuts and pecans** | Tannin reinforcing the cheese's own nuttiness. |
| **Grainy mustard** | Sharpness the cheese doesn't supply itself. |
| **Cured ham** | Salt against mellow sweetness. |
| **Ale, bitter, dry cider** | English pub drinking. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses in the same corner. And don't serve it
beside aged cheddar unless the comparison is deliberate — Red Leicester reads
as the mild one.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut into thick chunks or short batons.** It's flaky and a thin slice
   crumbles.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** It's mild and people take plenty.
4. **Use it for color** alongside pale cheeses — the russet orange is deeper
   and more natural-looking than Cotswold's.
5. **Put chutney beside it.** This is a cheese that wants a sweet-sharp
   partner.
6. **Buy a farmhouse version if you can find one** — Sparkenhoe is
   cloth-bound, raw milk and a different class of cheese.
7. **Fix:** crumbling as you cut is normal. Break it rather than fighting it.

**Label check:** cloth-bound and farmhouse versions are a significant step up.

**Skip:** assuming the color means flavored or aged. It's annatto, and it tells
you nothing about the cheese.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is Red Leicester orange?**
Annatto. It was originally added because a rich orange paste signaled cheese
made from good summer milk; it became standard regardless of the milk.

**Is it just orange cheddar?**
No. It's pressed less firmly, keeps more moisture, and ages mellow and nutty
rather than sharp and acidic.

**What's the best version?**
Sparkenhoe — a raw-milk, cloth-bound farmhouse Red Leicester. A different class
from the supermarket block.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.

**What goes with it?**
Chutney, oatcakes and ale. The Ploughman's plate.
