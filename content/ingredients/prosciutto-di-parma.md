## The short version

Dry-cured pork leg from Parma, aged at least twelve months. By law it contains
pork, sea salt and nothing else — no nitrates, no sugar, no smoke. Slice it
paper-thin, drape it rather than fold it flat, and leave the fat on.

## What it is

Prosciutto di Parma DOP comes from pigs raised in a defined region of northern
Italy, salted by a *maestro salatore*, then hung to air-dry for a minimum of
twelve months — often twenty-four. The ducal crown branded on the skin is
applied only after inspection. The whole process is salt, air and time.

**Taste:** delicate, sweet, nutty, gently salty
**Texture:** silky and yielding, melts at body temperature
**Look:** deep rose-pink muscle with a clean white fat cap

Slow proteolysis is the mechanism. Over a year, enzymes break muscle protein
into free amino acids — the same process that makes aged cheese taste savory.
That's where the sweetness and the nutty depth come from, and it's why a
twelve-month ham tastes fundamentally different from a fast-cured one, not just
saltier.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Melon or fresh figs** | Sweet cold fruit against salt and fat. *Prosciutto e melone* is the summer standard for a reason. |
| **Burrata or fresh mozzarella** | Unsalted milk fat with salted pork fat — each one seasons the other. |
| **Parmigiano-Reggiano** | Same province, same savory register. Salt on salt works because both are complex. |
| **Grissini, wrapped** | The aperitivo bite. Dry crunch inside silk. |
| **Baguette and good butter** | France's approach and it's excellent — plain bread lets the ham lead. |
| **Aged balsamic** | A few drops. Sweet-sour syrup cutting fat. |
| **Prosecco, Lambrusco, dry Franciacorta** | Bubbles scrub fat off the palate between bites. |

**Skip it with:** everything crackers and other loud bases, smoked cheese, and
anything spicy. Parma ham is delicate — the whole point is subtlety, and a
strong partner erases what you paid for.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Ask for it sliced paper-thin, to order.** Pre-packaged is acceptable;
   freshly sliced is a different food. If you can see through it, it's right.
2. **Take it out of the fridge 20 minutes early.** Cold prosciutto is waxy;
   at room temperature the fat softens and the aroma opens.
3. **Drape, don't stack.** Lift each slice from the center and let it fall into
   loose folds — ribbons, not a flat pile. Flat slices stick together and read
   as deli meat.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person** as one of two or three meats.
5. **Leave the fat on.** It's where the flavor lives, and trimming it is the
   most common mistake with any dry-cured ham.
6. **Separate the slices with the paper** if it came interleaved — pulling
   stuck slices apart tears them.
7. **Fix:** slices welded together? Ten minutes at room temperature and they
   release. Don't force them cold.

| **Costco Prosciutto di Parma** | Costco | Real DOP, best price per ounce. |

**Label check:** the five-pointed ducal crown of Parma branded on the skin, and
a DOP mark. The ingredient list should read pork and salt.

**Skip:** anything labeled just "prosciutto" without an origin. It's usually a
fast-cured domestic ham — fine in cooking, flat on a board.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between prosciutto and prosciutto di Parma?**
*Prosciutto* just means ham in Italian. Prosciutto di Parma is the protected
one — a defined region, a minimum twelve months, and only pork and salt.

**Should I trim the fat?**
No. The fat carries most of the flavor and melts on the tongue.

**How thin should it be sliced?**
Thin enough to see light through. Ask the counter to slice it to order.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of two or three meats.

**Is it raw?**
It's uncooked but cured — salt and twelve-plus months of drying make it safe to
eat as is.
