## The short version

The dried fruit that stays sour. Buy **tart Montmorency** cherries — the
sweetened Bing ones are basically candy and add nothing a raisin wouldn't. Scatter
them into gaps rather than arranging them; they're small enough that a neat pile
looks fussy and a scatter looks abundant.

## What it is

Sour cherries dried to chewy raisin density, usually with a little sugar or
apple juice added to offset the acid. Montmorency is the standard tart variety.
Bing and Rainier dried cherries are sweet varieties and taste much milder.

**Taste:** sharply tart, deep, faintly almond-like
**Texture:** dense and chewy, slightly leathery
**Look:** small dark red wrinkled pieces

The mechanism is acid at high concentration. Dried cherries carry more acid per
gram than any other dried fruit, so a small scatter changes a rich board more
than an equal weight of anything else. They also serve a purely visual role:
dark red flecks against pale cheese make a board look composed.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Stilton and blue cheese** | Sharp acid rather than flat sugar against the salt — a cleaner pairing than dates make. |
| **Aged cheddar** | The classic. Cheddar with dried cherries is sold as one product for a reason. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a goat cheese log in chopped dried cherries and pistachios; it's a standard for good reason. |
| **Walnuts and pecans** | Tannin and tart, the flavour base of every good trail mix. |
| **Duck prosciutto and game meats** | Cherry with duck is a classical pairing; dried is the board-friendly version. |
| **Dark chocolate** | The dessert corner. Tart cuts sweet. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. The acid is too concentrated for
burrata or mozzarella.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the bag.
2. **Scatter, don't arrange.** Sprinkle them into gaps between cheeses and
   crackers. That's their job.
3. **Or fill a tiny bowl** if the board has no gaps to fill.
4. **Chop them** if you're rolling a goat cheese log or mixing them into nuts.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — they're intense.
6. **Buy tart, not sweetened.** Read the ingredients.
7. **Fix:** dried out and hard? Soak in warm water or port for ten minutes, then
   pat very dry.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tart Montmorency cherries** | Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, bulk bins | The one to buy. Sharp, dark, only lightly sweetened. |
| **Traverse Bay / Cherry Republic** | Michigan producers, online | Excellent quality if you want to go deeper. |
| **Dried Bing cherries** | Grocery | Sweet variety, much milder. Fine, but closer to a raisin. |
| **Unsweetened dried cherries** | Health stores, online | Genuinely sour. Great with blue cheese, hard going alone. |
| **Cherries in port or brandy** | Specialty | The wet version. Different ingredient, similar partners. |

**Label check:** check for added sugar and oil. A little sugar is standard; a lot
means candy. Sunflower oil is used to stop clumping and is fine.

**Skip:** anything labelled "cherry flavoured fruit pieces" — usually apple.

## Quick FAQ

**Tart or sweet dried cherries?**
Tart Montmorency. The sweet Bing kind adds little a raisin wouldn't.

**How do I serve dried cherries on a board?**
Scatter them into gaps between cheeses and crackers rather than arranging them
in a pile.

**What cheese goes with dried cherries?**
Aged cheddar, stilton and blue cheeses, and fresh goat cheese.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce. They're concentrated and sharp.

**Can I soften hard dried cherries?**
Soak in warm water or port for ten minutes and pat dry thoroughly before they go
on the board.
