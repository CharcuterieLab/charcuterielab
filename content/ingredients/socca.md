## The short version

A thin chickpea-flour pancake from Nice, baked screaming hot until the edges
char. Four ingredients, naturally gluten-free, and about a dollar a batch. It's
the only warm gluten-free base on a board that anyone will fight over, and you
can't buy it — you have to make it.

## What it is

Socca is street food from Nice; the Genoese version is *farinata*, and the two
are essentially the same thing. Chickpea flour, water, olive oil and salt,
rested for an hour so the flour hydrates, then poured thin into a
blazing-hot pan and baked until blistered.

**Taste:** nutty, savory, olive-oil rich, faintly bitter at the char
**Texture:** creamy-soft center, crisp charred edge
**Look:** deep gold with dark blistered spots, torn into rough wedges

Heat is the mechanism, and it's what makes socca different from every packaged
gluten-free base. Nothing from a box arrives warm. A warm, freshly torn
flatbread on a board full of cold cheese and cured meat creates a contrast that
no cracker can, and it's the reason people notice it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Warm, nutty flatbread under cool tangy cheese. The best socca bite. |
| **Tapenade** | Salty, briny, intense — and socca is from the same stretch of coast. |
| **Ratatouille or roasted peppers** | Soft vegetables on a soft-centered base, with the char supplying the edge. |
| **Rosemary and flaky salt** | Traditional. Scatter on before it goes in the oven. |
| **Anchovies and olive oil** | The Niçoise version, and better than it sounds if your crowd is game. |
| **Whipped feta or labneh** | Cool creamy dairy against warm chickpea. |
| **Ripe tomato with basil** | The socca is doing the job bread does in panzanella. |

**Skip it with:** nothing much — socca is broadly agreeable. The real
constraint is timing: it's only excellent for about twenty minutes after it
comes out of the oven.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Whisk 1 cup chickpea flour with 1¼ cups water, 2 tbsp olive oil and 1 tsp
   salt.** Let it rest at least 30 minutes — 2 hours is better. This step is
   not optional; unrested batter tastes raw and chalky.
2. **Put a cast-iron skillet in the oven at 450–500°F** while it heats. The pan
   must be genuinely hot before the batter hits it.
3. **Add a tablespoon of oil to the hot pan**, swirl, pour in a thin layer of
   batter, and bake 10–12 minutes until set with dark blistered edges.
4. **Finish under the broiler for 1–2 minutes** if you want more char. This is
   where the flavor is.
5. **Tear it, don't cut it**, into rough wedges. Serve immediately.
6. **Serve 2–3 wedges per person.** Make it last, right before guests arrive.
7. **Fix:** it doesn't reheat well. Make a second batch rather than warming a
   cold one — the batter keeps in the fridge for two days.

## What to buy

You can't buy socca; you buy the flour. That's the whole shopping list.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bob's Red Mill Garbanzo Bean Flour** | Most grocery stores, baking aisle | The easiest to find. Works perfectly. |
| **Anthony's Chickpea Flour** | Amazon | Cheaper in bulk if you'll make it often. |
| **Besan** | Indian groceries | The same flour under its Indian name, at a fraction of the price. Buy it here. |
| **A cast-iron skillet** | Anywhere | The one piece of equipment that matters. A hot pan is the whole technique. |
| **Good olive oil** | Grocery | It's one of four ingredients, so it's a quarter of the flavor. |

**Label check:** chickpea flour, garbanzo bean flour and besan are the same
thing. *Gram flour* usually is too. Don't buy "chana dal flour" expecting
identical results — it's a slightly different legume.

**Skip:** boxed socca or farinata mixes. It's four ingredients; a mix saves you
nothing and costs four times as much.

## Quick FAQ

**Is socca gluten-free?**
Yes, completely. Chickpea flour, water, olive oil, salt.

**Socca or farinata — what's the difference?**
Socca is from Nice, farinata from Liguria. Same batter, same method, different
name.

**Why does my batter need to rest?**
Chickpea flour hydrates slowly. Unrested batter tastes raw and gritty; rested
batter tastes nutty and smooth. Thirty minutes minimum.

**Can I make it ahead?**
Make the *batter* ahead — it keeps two days refrigerated. The socca itself is
only good for about twenty minutes.

**How many per person?**
2–3 torn wedges. One skillet feeds four to six alongside other bases.
