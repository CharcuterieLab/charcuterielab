## The short version

Thin wafers of ground lentil that puff from flat discs into blistered,
lacy sheets in about thirty seconds. Naturally gluten-free, a few cents each,
and unlike anything else on a board. The most spectacular thing in this entire
section for the least money.

## What it is

Papadums are made from ground lentil, chickpea or rice flour with salt and
usually black pepper or cumin, rolled paper-thin and dried. They arrive as
hard, flat, unappetizing discs. Cooked — fried, dry-roasted or microwaved —
the trapped moisture flashes to steam and the wafer puffs and blisters
instantly.

**Taste:** savory, peppery, faintly earthy from the lentil
**Texture:** impossibly light, airy, shatters at a touch
**Look:** large blistered discs, irregular and translucent in places

Steam is the mechanism, and it's the reason this belongs on a board rather than
in a bag. A papadum cooked to order arrives at the table warm, puffed and
audibly crisp. Nothing else in this section is *made* in front of your guests
in half a minute, and that theatre is a large part of the appeal.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Mango chutney** | The traditional pairing. Sweet, acidic and spiced against a plain savory wafer. |
| **Raita and yogurt dips** | Cool, thick and mild — the counterweight to pepper and lentil. |
| **Whipped feta or labneh** | Salty dairy on an earthy base. The pairing that bridges this to a European board. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tangy and mild, letting the papadum's pepper come through. |
| **Lime pickle, in tiny amounts** | Intense, salty, sour. A quarter-teaspoon is plenty. |
| **Coriander chutney** | Bright and herbal against a warm crisp wafer. |
| **Beer, riesling, gin and tonic** | Anything cold with bubbles or sweetness against the pepper. |

**Skip it with:** delicate European cheeses — brie and burrata are lost under
the pepper and lentil. And skip stacking anything heavy on them; they're the
most fragile thing here after pane carasau.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cook them to order, right before serving.** This is the whole point.
2. **Microwave method (easiest):** 30–45 seconds on a plate, one at a time. No
   oil, no mess, and it works remarkably well.
3. **Dry-roast method:** hold over a gas flame or press into a dry hot skillet
   for 10–15 seconds a side, turning constantly. Better flavor.
4. **Fry method (traditional):** 3–5 seconds in hot oil. Best texture, most
   effort, and they puff dramatically.
5. **Break into large shards** and pile them. Never serve whole — they're
   bigger than a bread plate.
6. **Serve 1–2 per person**, broken.
7. **Fix:** they go soft within about twenty minutes in a humid room. Cook in
   two batches rather than all at once.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep Papadum** | Indian groceries, Amazon, some supermarkets | Widely available in the US. Plain and black pepper are the useful ones. |
| **Lijjat Papad** | Indian groceries, Amazon | The famous Indian brand, made by a women's cooperative. Excellent. |
| **Swad Papad** | Indian groceries, Amazon | Reliable and cheap. |
| **Patak's Papadums** | Grocery international aisle, Whole Foods | The easiest to find in a mainstream supermarket. |
| **Any Indian grocery** | Local | Best selection and roughly a third of the price. Ask for *urad dal* papad. |
| **Ready-cooked papadums** | Grocery international aisle | Convenient, but you lose the warmth and the theatre. |

**Label check:** urad dal (black gram) or chickpea flour first. Most are
naturally gluten-free, but some rice-flour blends add wheat — check if it
matters.

**Skip:** the pre-cooked bagged version if you can cook them yourself. Thirty
seconds in a microwave is a genuinely better product.

## Quick FAQ

**Are papadums gluten-free?**
Usually — most are lentil or chickpea flour. Check the label, as a few blends
contain wheat.

**Can I cook them in the microwave?**
Yes, and it's the easiest method. 30–45 seconds on a plate, one at a time, no
oil.

**How many per person?**
1–2, broken into shards.

**How far ahead can I cook them?**
Twenty minutes at most. They soften. Cook in batches during the party instead.

**What do I serve with them on a cheese board?**
Mango chutney and whipped feta or goat cheese. That's the bridge between an
Indian ingredient and a European board.
