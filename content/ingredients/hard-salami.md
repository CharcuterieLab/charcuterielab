## The short version

The deli salami Americans grew up on: firm, round slices, mild garlic and
pepper, often a little smoke. It's drier and less funky than Genoa, which
makes it the salami kids eat and the one that folds into the neatest roses.

## What it is

Hard salami is an American style descended from Italian dry sausages.
Ground pork, usually with beef, is seasoned with salt, garlic and pepper,
stuffed into a casing, fermented and dried until firm. Many brands are also
smoked. "Hard" describes the texture, not the flavor.

**Taste:** mild, salty and garlicky, with a gentle tang and sometimes smoke
**Texture:** firm, dense and dry; it slices into clean, sturdy rounds
**Look:** deep red with small, even white flecks of fat

The firmness comes from water loss. As the sausage dries, it can lose about
a third of its weight, which concentrates the salt and protein and tightens
the bind. Fermentation adds lactic acid, which gives the tang and helps
proteins set firm. Beef adds a darker color and a leaner, chewier bite than
an all-pork salami.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Provolone** | Mild stretched cheese and salty salami. The Italian sub on a board. |
| **Colby Jack** | Buttery cheese softens the salt. A kid-friendly pair. |
| **Banana peppers** | Tangy, mild heat cuts the fat. |
| **Spicy brown mustard** | Sharp heat and salt, like a deli sandwich. |
| **Butter crackers** | A soft, buttery base for a firm slice. |
| **Lager, Chianti or a fruity red** | Acidic or crisp drinks rinse the salt. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses like burrata. Firm, salty salami
overwhelms them. Pair it with sturdy, mild cheeses instead.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Ask the deli for slices** a little thicker than sandwich-thin, about
   1/16 inch.
2. **Fold slices in half, then in half again,** and tuck them in rows.
3. **Or make a salami rose:** layer 8 to 10 overlapping slices over the rim
   of a glass, flip it over and pull the glass out.
4. **Serve about 1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Let it sit out 15 minutes.** Cold fat tastes waxy.
6. **Fix:** if slices crack when folded, they're too cold. Let them warm for
   10 minutes, then fold.

## Quick FAQ

**What is hard salami?**
An American deli salami that's dried until firm. It's usually pork and
beef, often smoked, and milder than Genoa.

**What's the difference between hard salami and Genoa salami?**
Genoa is softer, usually all pork, with garlic and wine. Hard salami is
drier and firmer, often pork and beef, and milder.

**Is hard salami good for a salami rose?**
Yes. Its firm, round slices hold a fold well. Ask the deli for slices a
little thicker than sandwich-thin.

**Do you need to cook hard salami?**
No. It's cured and ready to eat, either fermented and dried or fully cooked
depending on the brand.

**How much hard salami per person?**
About 1.5 oz as one of two meats, or 2 oz if it's the only meat.
