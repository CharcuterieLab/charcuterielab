## The short version

A semi-firm alpine cheese from Switzerland and Savoie, made to be melted and
scraped. *Racler* means to scrape. Cold it's a decent savory board cheese; hot,
poured over potatoes and cornichons, it stops being a board and becomes dinner.

## What it is

Raclette is a washed-rind cooked cheese from the Valais in Switzerland, with
French versions from Savoie. Traditionally a half-wheel was set beside a fire
and the melted face scraped onto plates as it softened. It's made specifically
to melt smoothly without splitting.

**Taste:** savory, nutty, faintly funky, milky
**Texture:** semi-firm and supple cold; smooth and stretchy melted
**Look:** pale gold paste, tan-orange washed rind

Melting behavior is the mechanism, and it's engineered. Raclette has enough
moisture and the right fat-to-protein ratio to flow smoothly at moderate heat
instead of separating into oil and rubber. Very few cheeses do this well —
which is why raclette, Gruyère and fontina turn up in every melted dish and
cheddar mostly doesn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Boiled baby potatoes** | The traditional dish. Starch and salt under molten cheese. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onions** | Sharp vinegar is essential against this much melted fat. Not optional. |
| **Air-dried beef or cured ham** | Salt and cure with a rich alpine cheese. |
| **Cocktail rye and dark bread** | Something dense to hold melted cheese. |
| **Cracked black pepper and paprika** | The Swiss table seasoning for raclette. |
| **Dry Riesling, Chasselas, Fendant** | High-acid alpine whites, which is what the fat needs. |
| **Kirsch or dry cider** | The traditional digestif, for the same reason. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses on the same board, and heavy reds —
tannin and molten cheese is an unpleasant combination.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decide cold or hot.** Cold, it's a savory semi-firm cheese and you serve
   1–2 oz per person. Hot, it's the main event and you need 4–6 oz per person.
2. **For a board, slice it thin.** It's supple and slices cleanly.
3. **For melting without a raclette grill:** lay slices in a small cast-iron
   pan or on a sheet, broil 2–3 minutes until bubbling, and scrape it over
   potatoes.
4. **Have the acid ready before the cheese comes out.** Cornichons and pickled
   onions on the table first — melted raclette without them is heavy.
5. **Temper 30 minutes** if serving cold.
6. **Trim the rind** for cold service; leave it on for melting, where it holds
   the slice together.
7. **Fix:** split and oily when melted? Too hot. Lower heat, shorter time.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raclette de Savoie IGP** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods | The French version. Widely available and reliable. |
| **Swiss Raclette du Valais AOP** | Cheese counters, specialty | The original. Nuttier and more savory. |
| **Emmi Raclette** | Grocery cheese case | Widely distributed Swiss brand, often pre-sliced for melting. |
| **Pre-sliced raclette** | Grocery cheese case, Trader Joe's | Convenient for melting. Fine for that, less good on a board. |
| **Smoked raclette** | Cheese counters | A real variant. Good, though it narrows the pairings. |
| **Gruyère or fontina** | Grocery, cheese counters | Both melt well if raclette isn't stocked — see their own pages. |

**Label check:** for melting, pre-sliced is genuinely convenient. For a board,
buy a piece and slice it yourself — the pre-sliced dries at the edges.

**Skip:** buying it as a cold board cheese if you were hoping for something
dramatic. Cold raclette is good but unremarkable; the drama is in the melting.

## Quick FAQ

**What does raclette mean?**
It's from *racler*, to scrape — the traditional service, where the melted face
of a half-wheel is scraped onto plates.

**Do I need a raclette grill?**
No. A cast-iron pan under the broiler works, or slices melted on a sheet and
scraped off.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz on a board. 4–6 oz if you're melting it as a meal.

**Why are pickles always served with it?**
Acid cuts a large quantity of melted fat. Without them the dish is heavy.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Trim it for cold service. For melting, leave it on — it holds the slice
together.
