## The short version

Blanched almonds blended with lemon, salt and water into something with the
loose, grainy texture of fresh ricotta. It's lighter than cashew cheese, it's
genuinely easy to make at home, and unlike most plant-based cheese it isn't
trying to be anything it can't be.

## What it is

Blanched almonds — skins off, which matters for color and bitterness — are
soaked, then blended with water, lemon juice, salt and often a little olive
oil. Almonds have more fiber than cashews, so the result stays slightly grainy
rather than going completely smooth. That graininess is what makes it read as
ricotta.

**Taste:** mild, nutty, lemony, clean, lightly salted
**Texture:** soft and slightly grainy, spoonable, loose
**Look:** white to pale ivory, matte, visibly textured

Particle size doing the work is the mechanism, and it's an honest one. Where
cashew cheese aims for smoothness and cultured tang, almond ricotta leans into
the fact that almonds don't blend perfectly — the texture lands close enough to
dairy ricotta that the comparison flatters it instead of exposing it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Crostini** | It's a spread and it needs a rigid base. Same as dairy ricotta. |
| **Lemon zest and olive oil** | Acid and fat, and it's already lemony. Lean in. |
| **Fresh figs and peaches** | Ripe fruit against a clean, mild base. |
| **Hot honey or agave** | Sweet and hot against mild and nutty. |
| **Roasted tomatoes** | Sweet, acidic and warm on a cool spread. |
| **Pistachios and toasted almonds** | Crunch and more nut. |
| **Basil, mint, cracked pepper** | It takes herbs well and needs seasoning. |

**Skip it with:** side-by-side comparison to dairy ricotta, which it loses. And
skip serving it plain — like dairy ricotta, it needs salt, oil and something on
top.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Season it before it goes out.** Olive oil, flaky salt, cracked pepper,
   lemon zest. This is non-negotiable for any plain white spread.
2. **Spread it in a shallow bowl** and swirl the top, pooling oil in the dent.
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Put crostini directly beside it.**
5. **Label it and give it its own spoon**, so vegan guests can find it and
   nobody cross-contaminates it.
6. **Make it yourself:** soak a cup of blanched almonds four hours, blend with
   ¼ cup water, the juice of a lemon, a teaspoon of salt and a splash of olive
   oil. Twenty minutes, and better than most packaged versions.
7. **Fix:** too thick? More water, a tablespoon at a time. Too bland? More salt
   and lemon, almost always both.

| **Dairy ricotta** | Any grocery | If dairy isn't the issue, whipped ricotta is better and cheaper. |

**Label check:** blanched or "almond milk" rather than whole almonds with
skins. And check for added gums, which make the texture gluey rather than
grainy.

**Skip:** buying it if nobody at the table needs it. Whipped dairy ricotta is
better, cheaper and easier.

## Quick FAQ

**Is almond ricotta like real ricotta?**
Closer than most plant-based substitutes, because almonds don't blend
completely smooth and the graininess reads as ricotta-like.

**Can I make it at home?**
Yes, easily. Soak blanched almonds, blend with lemon, salt, water and olive
oil. Twenty minutes.

**Why blanched almonds?**
The skins make it grey and slightly bitter. Blanched almonds give a white,
clean-tasting result.

**Does it contain nuts?**
Yes — almonds. It's dairy-free, not allergen-free.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz, seasoned and served with crostini.
