## The short version

A Japanese paste of yuzu citrus peel, fresh chilies and salt, aged briefly
so the flavors meld. It's floral, sharp, salty and hot all at once. You use a
tiny dab, and it makes rich foods like burrata, mortadella and salmon taste
lighter and brighter. It's the most useful condiment most people have never
heard of.

## What it is

Yuzu kosho comes from Kyushu, in southern Japan. Yuzu is a fragrant citrus,
like a cross between a lemon, a mandarin and a grapefruit. Its zest is
ground with fresh chilies and salt, then left to ferment for a short time.
Green yuzu kosho uses unripe yuzu and green chilies; red uses ripe yuzu and
red chilies. Kosho means chili in the local dialect, not black pepper.

**Taste:** floral citrus, sharp chili heat, very salty
**Texture:** a coarse, moist paste
**Look:** bright green or orange-red flecked paste in a tiny jar

Zest is the mechanism. Most of a citrus fruit's aroma lives in the oil
glands of the peel, not the juice, so a paste made from zest delivers
fragrance without much acidity. Salt draws out and preserves those oils, and
chili adds heat that cuts through fat. The result seasons a rich bite in one
move: aroma, salt and heat together.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | A tiny dab of salty heat on mild cream. Restaurants love this one. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Citrus and chili cut through oily fish. |
| **Mortadella** | Bright heat against soft, fatty pork. |
| **Cream cheese** | Stir a little in for a two-minute yuzu dip. |
| **Rice crackers** | A crisp, neutral base for a Japanese-leaning board. |
| **Sake, Champagne or a crisp lager** | Clean and bright, they match the citrus. |

**Skip it with:** sweet spreads and dessert items. Its salt and heat clash
with sugar.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Use very little.** About a quarter teaspoon per person is plenty.
2. **Serve it in the smallest dish you own** with a tiny spoon or a
   toothpick.
3. **Pre-dab a few bites** on burrata or salmon so guests see how to use it.
4. **Mix a teaspoon into 4 oz of cream cheese** for a milder spread.
5. **Place it beside the richest items** on the board.
6. **Fix:** if it has dried out in the jar, stir in a few drops of lemon
   juice or water.

**Label check:** yuzu, chili and salt should be the main ingredients. Some
cheaper versions use other citrus or add sugar.

**Skip:** spreading it like jam. It's a seasoning. Too much tastes only of
salt.

## Quick FAQ

**What is yuzu kosho?**
A fermented Japanese paste made from yuzu citrus zest, fresh chili peppers
and salt. Despite the name, it contains no black pepper; kosho means chili in
the Kyushu dialect.

**Is yuzu kosho spicy?**
Yes, but it's used in tiny amounts. A quarter teaspoon gives a bright, clean
heat rather than a burn.

**Green or red yuzu kosho?**
Green uses unripe yuzu and green chilies, so it's sharper and more herbal.
Red uses ripe yuzu and red chilies and is a bit rounder.

**How long does it keep?**
Months in the fridge once opened. It's very salty, which preserves it.

**Is it vegan and gluten-free?**
Traditional yuzu kosho is just yuzu, chili and salt, so yes. Check the label
for added ingredients.
