## The short version

Pork belly cured with salt and spices but never smoked — the Italian relative
of bacon. It comes rolled (*arrotolata*) or flat (*tesa*), and only the
properly aged rolled kind should go on a board uncooked. The flat slab is a
cooking ingredient.

## What it is

Pancetta is the same cut as bacon, cured differently: salt, black pepper,
juniper, nutmeg and sometimes garlic, then air-dried rather than smoked.
*Arrotolata* is rolled into a cylinder and aged for months, producing a
sliceable spiral. *Tesa* is left flat and is generally sold for cooking.

**Taste:** rich, porky, peppery, gently spiced
**Texture:** soft, unctuous, fat melting at room temperature
**Look:** rolled spiral of pink meat and white fat, or a flat streaky slab

Fat ratio is the mechanism, and it sets the serving size. Pork belly is
roughly half fat, far more than a ham or a salami, so pancetta delivers a much
bigger hit of richness per slice. That's why an ounce is plenty and why acid
beside it isn't optional.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pecorino Romano or parmesan** | Salt and umami against pure pork fat. The carbonara logic, uncooked. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Sharp vinegar cutting a very fatty slice. Necessary rather than optional. |
| **Ciabatta or grilled bread** | Something absorbent under melting fat. |
| **Cracked black pepper** | Pancetta is already peppered; more is welcome. |
| **Ripe melon or figs** | Sweet cold fruit against warm fat, as with prosciutto. |
| **Marinated artichokes and olives** | Briny and oily neighbors. |
| **Lambrusco, Barbera, dry Prosecco** | High-acid Italian wines. Fat needs acid. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. And skip serving pancetta, guanciale
and lardo on the same board — three cured fats is a lot of the same idea.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy *arrotolata* — rolled and aged — if you're serving it raw.** Flat
   *tesa* pancetta is a cooking cure and can be undercured for direct eating.
   Ask the counter if you're unsure.
2. **Slice thin.** The spiral pattern is the visual appeal and thin slices are
   the only comfortable way to eat this much fat.
3. **Lay slices flat so the spiral shows.**
4. **Temper 20 minutes** so the fat softens and stops eating waxy.
5. **Serve three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce per person.** Half the
   portion of a leaner meat.
6. **Put acid right beside it.**
7. **Fix:** if the fat is firm and unpleasant, it's cold. Give it another
   fifteen minutes rather than more salt.

**Label check:** *arrotolata* (rolled) for board use, *tesa* (flat) for
cooking. Pre-diced pancetta is always a cooking product.

**Skip:** serving flat pancetta raw. It's cured for cooking and it isn't
pleasant uncooked.

## Quick FAQ

**Is pancetta just Italian bacon?**
Same cut, different cure. Bacon is smoked; pancetta is salt-cured and air-dried
with spices.

**Can I eat pancetta raw?**
Properly aged *arrotolata* pancetta, yes — it's a dry cure. Flat *tesa*
pancetta is meant to be cooked.

**Rolled or flat?**
Rolled for a board, flat for the pan.

**How much per person?**
Three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce. It's about half fat.

**What cuts the richness?**
Vinegar — cornichons, pickled onions — and high-acid wine.
