## The short version

Cured beef from Armenia and Turkey, pressed flat, air-dried, and coated in a
thick red paste of fenugreek, garlic and paprika. It's spicy, garlicky and
intensely aromatic. Sliced thin and served with labneh and warm bread, it's
the boldest meat you can put on a mezze board.

## What it is

Pastirma (also basturma or bastirma) is made from a whole beef muscle, often
the eye of round or tenderloin. It's salted, pressed to squeeze out moisture,
and air-dried for weeks. Then it's coated in çemen, a paste of ground
fenugreek, garlic, paprika and hot pepper, and dried again. The name comes
from the Turkish word for "pressed," and it's the ancestor of the word
pastrami.

**Taste:** beefy, garlicky, earthy and warm, with a maple-like fenugreek note and mild heat
**Texture:** firm and lean, tender when sliced thin
**Look:** deep red meat with a thick brick-red spice crust

Pressing is the mechanism. Squeezing the meat flattens it into an even
thickness and forces out water, so it dries fast and evenly with less
spoilage. The çemen paste then seals the surface. Fenugreek contains
sotolon, the same compound that gives maple syrup its smell, which is why
pastirma has a sweet-savory aroma unlike any European cured meat.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Labneh** | Cool, tangy dairy calms the garlic and spice. The essential partner. |
| **Feta** | Salty, creamy and tangy, the classic Turkish pairing. |
| **Warm pita or pita chips** | A plain starch to carry the spice. |
| **Kalamata olives** | Briny fruit and fat against lean, spiced meat. |
| **Dried apricots** | Sweetness tames the heat. |
| **Rakı, a dry rosé or a crisp lager** | Anise or bright acid cuts through the garlic. |

**Skip it with:** delicate meats like prosciutto. Fenugreek and garlic
linger, and anything subtle tastes of pastirma afterward.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice it paper-thin** if it isn't pre-sliced. Thick slices are chewy.
2. **Leave the spice crust on.** It's the point. Scrape a little off if
   it's very thick.
3. **Serve about 0.75 oz per person.** The flavor is intense.
4. **Fold the slices loosely** and place them right next to a bowl of labneh.
5. **Keep it at one end of the board** with the mezze items, away from the
   delicate cheeses.
6. **Fix:** if guests find it too strong, crisp a few slices in a dry pan for
   30 seconds. Heat mellows the garlic.

## Quick FAQ

**What is pastirma?**
Air-dried, pressed beef coated in a thick paste called çemen, made from
fenugreek, garlic and red pepper. It's eaten across Armenia, Turkey and the
Middle East.

**Is pastirma the same as pastrami?**
They share a name origin, but pastrami is brined, smoked and cooked.
Pastirma is cured and air-dried, not cooked.

**Do you eat the spice coating?**
Yes. The çemen is the flavor. Some people scrape off a little if it's very
thick.

**Why does it smell so strong?**
Fenugreek and garlic. Fenugreek has a maple-like, curry-like aroma that
lingers, so serve it with something cooling like labneh.

**Where can I buy it?**
Armenian, Turkish and Middle Eastern markets. Ohanyan's basturma from Fresno
is sold in many of them and online.
