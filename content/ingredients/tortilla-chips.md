## The short version

Corn tortillas cut into wedges and fried or baked. They are naturally gluten-free,
cost almost nothing, and are structurally the strongest scoop you can put on a
board — a thick restaurant-style chip will carry a load of dip that would snap a
cracker. Every fusion or Tex-Mex board starts here, and they earn a place on a
plain one whenever somebody at the table avoids wheat.

## What it is

Nixtamalised corn — cooked in an alkaline solution, hulled and ground — pressed
into tortillas, cut and fried. The nixtamal step is what gives real corn chips
their flavour and is the difference between a tortilla chip and a corn snack.

**Taste:** toasted corn, salt, a faint lime note from the nixtamal
**Texture:** hard, brittle, snapping cleanly
**Look:** pale gold triangles or rounds, sometimes blue or with visible corn flecks

The mechanism is nixtamalisation. Soaking corn in lime water breaks down the hull
and frees niacin and aroma compounds that are otherwise locked up, which is why
masa smells of toasted corn and plain cornmeal does not. It also strengthens the
dough, which is why a tortilla chip is stiff enough to scoop where a cornmeal
cracker crumbles.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pepper jack** | Mild cheese with chili on a corn base. The bite everyone builds first. |
| **Cotija** | Salty, dry and crumbly. Grated over the chips, it turns a bowl into a dish. |
| **Pickled jalapeños** | Acid and heat on a neutral crunch. The three-part bite. |
| **Kimchi** | The Korean-Mexican combination that built a thousand food trucks. Drain the kimchi first. |
| **Queso fresco** | Fresh, mild and milky against toasted corn. Traditional and correct. |
| **Hummus** | Not traditional at all, and it works — a thick dip needs a strong scoop more than it needs a matching cuisine. |

**Skip it with:** delicate bloomy cheese and fine dry-cured ham. The corn flavour
is stronger than either.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy thick restaurant-style chips.** Thin ones break in every dip on the board.
2. **Fan them in a shallow bowl or along an edge**, not piled loose.
3. **Warm them for five minutes** in a low oven if the bag has been open. It makes
   a real difference.
4. **Keep them away from wet items** — they go soft fast.
5. **Serve 6 to 8 chips per person.**
6. **Put them beside the dips**, not scattered across the whole board.
7. **Fix:** stale and bendy? Five minutes at 300F and a pinch of salt while warm
   brings them back completely.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Restaurant-style thick tortilla chips** | Any grocery | The default and the right choice. Sturdy enough for anything. |
| **Blue corn chips** | Larger supermarkets, health food shops | Nuttier, and a genuinely different colour on the board. |
| **Local tortilleria chips** | Mexican grocers | Fried that morning. Not comparable to a bag. |
| **Lightly salted or unsalted chips** | Any grocery | Better beside salty cheese and cured meat. |
| **Scoop-shaped chips** | Any grocery | Ugly, and unbeatable for thick dips on a family board. |

**Label check:** corn, oil, salt, and lime or calcium hydroxide. That last one means
real nixtamal.

**Skip:** flavoured chips. Ranch and chili-lime dust fights everything on a board.

## Quick FAQ

**Are tortilla chips gluten-free?**
Corn tortilla chips are, and it is one of the few genuinely gluten-free bases that
is also cheap and available everywhere. Check for shared-equipment warnings if the
allergy is severe.

**What is the difference between tortilla chips and tostada rounds?**
Tostadas are whole flat tortillas, fried crisp — a plate you build on. Chips are
wedges you scoop with. Different jobs.

**Which chips are best for a board?**
Thick restaurant-style rounds or triangles. Thin chips snap in anything
substantial.

**Do they go with cheese?**
With fresh and semi-soft cheeses, yes — pepper jack, cotija, queso fresco. Less so
with aged European cheese.

**How much per person?**
Six to eight chips.
