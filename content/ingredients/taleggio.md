## The short version

A square Italian washed-rind cheese that smells considerably stronger than it
tastes. The aroma is barnyard; the flavor is buttery, fruity and mild. It's the
cheese to use when you want to introduce someone to stinky cheese without
losing them.

## What it is

Taleggio DOP comes from Lombardy and has been made since at least the 10th
century. The rind is washed with brine during aging, which encourages
*Brevibacterium linens* — the same bacterium responsible for the smell of
Époisses, Limburger and, not coincidentally, human skin.

**Taste:** buttery, fruity, tangy, mildly savory, much milder than the smell
**Texture:** soft and yielding, becoming runny near the rind
**Look:** square block, pale paste, thin pinkish-orange rind

The aroma-flavor gap is the mechanism, and it's why Taleggio works where
stronger washed-rinds don't. *B. linens* produces powerful sulphur compounds
that dominate the smell but contribute relatively little to the taste, because
the rind is thin and the aging short. You get the drama without the
commitment.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Crostini and baguette** | Plain bread under a soft, aromatic cheese. Nothing should compete. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness against funk. The single most effective pairing for any washed-rind. |
| **Ripe pear** | Sweet, wet and clean against a rich, aromatic paste. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin cutting butterfat and rounding off the barnyard note. |
| **Speck or prosciutto** | Salt and smoke with something soft and pungent. |
| **Fig jam and mostarda** | Sweet and sharp preserves both work. |
| **Nebbiolo, Barbera, off-dry Riesling** | Northern Italian reds, or a touch of sweetness in the wine. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses next to it — Taleggio's aroma travels
across a board and lands on whatever's beside it. Give it space.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.** Cold Taleggio is firm and closed; warm, it becomes
   soft and fruity. Under-tempered is the most common mistake here.
2. **Cut into rectangular slabs** from the square block, keeping some rind on
   each piece.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.** It's rich and aromatic.
4. **Put it at one end of the board with its own knife**, away from the mild
   cheeses. The smell transfers.
5. **Say it smells stronger than it tastes** before anyone tries it. That one
   sentence is the difference between people trying it and avoiding it.
6. **Eat the rind or don't — it's edible**, but many people trim it. Either is
   fine.
7. **Fix:** an ammonia smell that doesn't fade after ten minutes means it's
   over-ripe. A strong barnyard smell that softens is exactly right.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Taleggio DOP** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, Italian delis | The only version worth buying. Look for the DOP stamp. |
| **Taleggio, cut to order** | Cheese counter | Better than pre-packaged — you can see and smell the paste. |
| **Robiola** | Cheese counters, specialty | The softer, milder Lombard cousin. Even gentler. |
| **Reblochon or Reblochon-style** | Cheese counters | The French washed-rind equivalent — see its own page. |
| **Fontina Val d'Aosta** | Cheese counters | Milder washed-rind, if Taleggio is a step too far. |
| **Époisses** | Specialty shops | The next level up in intensity, when you're ready. |

**Label check:** the DOP stamp, and a paste that's soft and even rather than
chalky in the middle. A chalky core means it's underripe.

**Skip:** buying it more than two or three days ahead. Taleggio ripens fast and
tips into ammonia quickly at the end.

## Quick FAQ

**Does Taleggio taste as strong as it smells?**
No, and that gap is the point. It smells barnyard and tastes buttery and
fruity.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, it's edible. Many people trim it anyway. Both are acceptable.

**How do I know if it's gone off?**
A sharp ammonia smell that doesn't fade after ten minutes out of the fridge.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz. It's rich.

**What if my guests are nervous about stinky cheese?**
This is the right cheese for that. Tell them it tastes far milder than it
smells, and serve it with honey.
