## The short version

The classic Italian cheese-plate jam, and specifically **sour cherry** — *amarena*
or Morello. Sweet cherry preserve tastes like red sugar; sour cherry keeps enough
acid and tannin to work against hard salty sheep cheese the way a glass of red
wine does.

## What it is

Cherries cooked with sugar, sometimes with a little lemon or wine. The important
distinction is variety: **Amarena, Morello and Montmorency** are sour cherries and
make a dark, complex preserve; Bing and other sweet cherries make a much milder
one.

**Taste:** tart, deep, faintly almond and wine-like
**Texture:** loose, with whole or halved fruit
**Look:** dark garnet, glossy, with visible cherries

The mechanism is acid plus a faint bitterness from the fruit's stone-adjacent
compounds. That combination is unusual in a jam — most are sugar and acid only —
and it's what lets cherry preserve stand next to pecorino, a cheese salty enough
to flatten almost any other sweet element.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pecorino romano and hard sheep cheese** | The Italian cheese-plate standard. Very salty, very mineral cheese needs a jam with acid, not just sugar. |
| **Manchego** | Same logic in Spanish. Sheep milk and sour cherry are a fixed combination. |
| **Duck prosciutto and game charcuterie** | Cherry with duck is classical; the preserve is the board-friendly form. |
| **Brie** | Tart fruit slicing soft fat, with the dark colour making the plate. |
| **Gorgonzola** | Sweetness for the salt, acid to stop it cloying. |
| **Chocolate, in a dessert corner** | Cherry and dark chocolate is obvious and correct. |

**Skip it with:** mild fresh cheeses like mozzarella. The preserve is too dark
and dominant.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with its own spoon — this one is loose enough to
   spoon rather than spread.
2. **Buy sour cherry.** Check the label for amarena, Morello or Montmorency.
3. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
4. **Put it next to the hard sheep cheese**, which is where it does its best work.
5. **Keep it off the crackers** — it's wet, and it will soften them.
6. **Add a spoonful of its syrup** over mascarpone if the board runs to dessert.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? Stir in a teaspoon of balsamic or red wine vinegar.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amarena cherry preserve** | Italian delis, Eataly | The best version. Dark, sour, complex. |
| **Sour cherry preserve (Morello)** | Whole Foods, European delis | Widely available. Look for Polish and German brands. |
| **Bonne Maman cherry preserve** | Any grocery | Sweet cherry. Fine, but much milder. |
| **Cherry mostarda** | Italian delis | Mustard-spiked cherry. Excellent with hard cheese and boiled meats. |
| **Amarena Fabbri cherries** | Italian delis | Whole cherries in syrup rather than preserve — see brandied cherries. |

**Label check:** the variety matters more than the brand. If the label doesn't say
sour, amarena, Morello or Montmorency, assume it's sweet cherry.

**Skip:** anything with artificial colour or "cherry flavour".

## Quick FAQ

**What cheese goes with cherry preserves?**
Hard sheep cheeses above all — pecorino romano and manchego — plus brie and blue
cheese.

**Sour or sweet cherry preserve?**
Sour. Amarena, Morello or Montmorency. Sweet cherry preserve is much flatter on a
cheese board.

**What is amarena?**
An Italian sour cherry, used for the best cherry preserves and for the syrup-
packed cherries sold in jars.

**Can I use it with meat?**
Yes — especially duck prosciutto and game charcuterie. Cherry and duck is a
classical pairing.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce, spooned rather than spread.
