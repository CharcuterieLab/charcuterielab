## The short version

Thin-sliced red onion in hot vinegar turns electric pink in about twenty minutes.
It costs a dollar, takes fifteen minutes of actual work, and it's the best-looking
thing you can add to a board. It's also sharp enough to do the acid job that
pickles are there for.

## What it is

Red onion sliced thin and steeped in vinegar with sugar, salt and often
peppercorns, bay or oregano. A quick refrigerator pickle, not a preserved one —
it keeps two or three weeks and is at its best in the first few days.

**Taste:** sharply sour, faintly sweet, with the onion's bite softened
**Texture:** tender-crisp ribbons
**Look:** vivid magenta-pink strands

The mechanism is acid changing both flavour and colour. The vinegar tames the
onion's harsh sulphur compounds — raw onion is aggressive, pickled onion is
bright — and it also reacts with the anthocyanins in the skin, which is why the
colour shifts from purple to hot pink. You get a functional acid and a visual
centrepiece from one jar.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Pink against white, sharp against tangy. The prettiest and easiest pairing on this list. |
| **Burrata** | Cream with no acid meeting acid with no fat. Scatter them over the top. |
| **Prosciutto and cured meat** | Salt and fat cut cleanly, without the sweetness a chutney would bring. |
| **Hummus and dips** | Scattered on top for colour and sharpness — the standard mezze finish. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp on sharp, with the onion's residual sweetness bridging. |
| **Smoked salmon and cream cheese** | The bagel board. Onion is already part of it. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, though a board with pickles, cornichons and pickled
onions has more acid than it needs.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make them:** slice one red onion very thin (a mandoline helps). Pack into a
   jar. Pour over 1/2 cup vinegar, 1/2 cup hot water, 1 tbsp sugar, 1.5 tsp salt.
2. **Wait 20 minutes.** They're usable then, better after an hour, best after a day.
3. **Drain before serving.** They carry a lot of liquid.
4. **Serve in a small bowl** or scattered over cheese and dips.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
6. **Slice them thin.** Thick slices stay harsh and don't turn pink properly.
7. **Fix:** still harsh? They need longer. A day in the fridge mellows them
   completely.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Red onions + vinegar** | Any grocery | Make them. It's fifteen minutes and costs a dollar. |
| **Apple cider or white wine vinegar** | Grocery | Cider is rounder, white wine sharper. Both work. |
| **Jarred pickled red onions** | Specialty, Mexican grocers | They exist and are fine. Homemade is better and cheaper. |
| **Cebollas encurtidas** | Mexican grocers | The Yucatán version, often with orange and habanero. Excellent. |
| **Pickled shallots** | French delis, specialty | Milder and more refined. Good on a French board. |

**Label check:** if buying, look for a short ingredient list. Some jarred versions
are heavily sweetened.

**Skip:** buying them at all, honestly. This is the easiest thing on the whole
site to make.

## Quick FAQ

**How do you make pickled red onions?**
Slice one red onion thin, cover with 1/2 cup vinegar, 1/2 cup hot water, 1 tbsp
sugar and 1.5 tsp salt. Ready in 20 minutes.

**Why do they turn pink?**
The vinegar's acid reacts with anthocyanin pigments in the onion skin, shifting
the colour from purple to bright magenta.

**How long do they keep?**
Two to three weeks in the fridge, best in the first few days.

**What do they go with?**
Fresh goat cheese, burrata, cured meat, hummus and smoked salmon.

**Do I need a mandoline?**
It helps. Thin slices pickle faster, taste milder and turn a better colour.
