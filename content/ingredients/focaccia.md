## The short version

A thick Italian flatbread dimpled with your fingers, drowned in olive oil and
scattered with coarse salt. It's the richest bread you can put on a board, and
the only one that's already seasoned. Cut it into cubes, warm it, and keep it
near the fresh cheese.

## What it is

Focaccia is a high-hydration dough pressed into an oiled pan, dimpled so the
oil pools in the dents, salted, and baked hot. The dimples aren't decorative —
they hold oil against the surface so the top fries slightly while the inside
steams.

**Taste:** salty, olive-oil rich, faintly yeasty
**Texture:** crisp oiled top, soft airy interior, chewy base
**Look:** golden and dimpled, often flecked with rosemary or sea salt

Fat and salt together are the mechanism, and they're already applied. Every
other bread on a board is a blank that you season with what you put on it.
Focaccia arrives seasoned, which makes it excellent with mild things and
redundant with strong ones.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata and stracciatella** | Milky and unsalted meets salted and oily. The single best focaccia pairing. |
| **Fresh mozzarella and tomato** | Caprese without assembly. The bread supplies the olive oil the salad needs. |
| **Prosciutto and mortadella** | Fatty cured pork on soft oiled bread is a Ligurian street snack, not an invention. |
| **Pesto and sun-dried tomato** | Olive-oil sauces on an olive-oil bread. Same register throughout. |
| **Marinated artichokes and olives** | The antipasto corner, held together by the bread's own oil. |
| **Aged provolone and pecorino** | Sharp salty cheese cutting through the richness. |
| **Dry rosé, Vermentino, Chianti** | Acidic wines to slice the oil. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and other very strong cheeses — focaccia is rich
enough that adding intensity makes the bite heavy rather than balanced. And
skip it on a dessert board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut it into 1-inch cubes, not slices.** Slices are sandwich portions;
   cubes are board portions and they stack beautifully.
2. **Warm it 5 minutes at 350°F** before serving. Cold focaccia is dense and
   greasy; warm focaccia is the best bread on the table.
3. **Serve 2–3 cubes per person.** It's very filling.
4. **Split a few cubes horizontally** and lay a sliver of prosciutto inside for
   two-bite sandwiches. It takes three minutes and it's always the first thing
   gone.
5. **Buy it the same day.** Focaccia's whole appeal is the contrast between
   crisp top and soft middle, and that's gone by day two.
6. **Fix:** day-old focaccia is excellent grilled or griddled cut-side down in
   a dry pan. Don't microwave it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Store bakery focaccia** | Whole Foods, Wegmans, Publix, most grocery bakeries | Baked that morning. The default and usually very good. |
| **Local Italian bakery focaccia** | Neighborhood Italian bakery | Best version. Ask what time it came out. |
| **Trader Joe's focaccia** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and reliable. Rosemary and plain both work. |
| **Costco focaccia and flatbreads** | Costco | Large format, good price. Freeze half. |
| **Par-baked focaccia** | Grocery bread aisle, freezer | You finish it at home, so it's genuinely fresh at serving time. |

**Label check:** olive oil should be high in the ingredient list. Focaccia made
with vegetable oil is a soft flatbread with a fancy name.

**Skip:** heavily topped focaccia — the ones covered in vegetables, cheese or
sauce. Those are a meal, and they crowd everything else on a board.

## Quick FAQ

**Focaccia or ciabatta for a charcuterie board?**
Focaccia if the board leans rich and soft (burrata, mozzarella, prosciutto).
Ciabatta if you want something drier that tears well.

**How much per person?**
2–3 one-inch cubes. It's filling.

**Should I serve it warm?**
Yes. Five minutes at 350°F makes a large difference and costs nothing.

**Can I buy it the day before?**
You can, but it won't be the same. If you must, revive it in a hot dry pan
rather than the oven.

**Why is mine greasy rather than rich?**
It's cold. Focaccia's oil firms up at room temperature and reads as greasy;
warming it fixes the impression entirely.
