## The short version

Sweet, floral honey carrying the mushroomy, faintly garlicky perfume of truffle.
It sounds wrong and tastes right, especially over hard salty sheep cheese. It is
also the ingredient most often faked — most jars are honey plus synthetic
2,4-dithiapentane, and the good ones have visible truffle pieces.

## What it is

Honey infused with truffle, usually black summer truffle, sometimes with truffle
aroma added. Italian producers dominate. A quarter-ounce is a serving, both
because of the price and because the aroma is overpowering in quantity.

**Taste:** sweet, then earthy and mushroomy, faintly of garlic
**Texture:** standard honey viscosity, sometimes with visible flecks
**Look:** deep amber, flecked with dark truffle shavings in the good jars

The mechanism is aroma at a very low threshold. Truffle compounds are detectable
at tiny concentrations, so a drizzle changes an entire bite. Honey is the ideal
carrier because it's viscous — it stays where you put it and releases the aroma
slowly, rather than dispersing the way an oil would.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pecorino romano and aged sheep cheese** | The definitive pairing. Hard, extremely salty, and mineral — the truffle's earthiness sits directly on top of it. |
| **Parmigiano-Reggiano** | Crystalline salt and umami; truffle amplifies the savoury notes already there. |
| **Burrata** | Cream as a blank canvas for aroma. Restaurant-plate territory. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel underneath, earth on top. An unexpected pairing that works. |
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Mushroom notes are already in a bloomy rind; truffle extends them. |
| **Warm bread and butter** | Not a cheese pairing, but the best way to taste the honey on its own. |

**Skip it with:** fruit, other strong condiments, and cured meat. Truffle wants
a plain salty surface, not competition.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve in a very small pot** with a tiny spoon. It should look precious,
   because it is.
2. **Drizzle it over the pecorino yourself** so guests see what it's for.
3. **Use a quarter-ounce per person at most.** Too much and everything tastes of
   truffle.
4. **Put it away from the fruit and jam** — the aroma travels.
5. **Tell people what it is.** Truffle is genuinely divisive and some guests will
   want to avoid it.
6. **Buy small.** The aroma fades over months once opened.
7. **Fix:** overwhelming? Cut it with plain honey in the pot, half and half.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Italian truffle honey with visible truffle** | Italian delis, Eataly, cheese shops | Look for shavings in the jar. That's the tell. |
| **Savini or Urbani truffle honey** | Specialty, online | Reliable producers. Expensive and worth it once. |
| **Sabatino truffle honey** | Whole Foods, grocery | Widely available and reasonable. Mostly aroma-driven. |
| **Truffle-infused hot honey** | Specialty | A recent hybrid. Better than it sounds with salami. |

**Label check:** ingredients should list truffle (*Tuber aestivum*) rather than
only "truffle flavour" or "aroma". Visible black flecks are a good sign.

**Skip:** anything cheap. Truffle honey under about ten dollars is honey with
synthetic aroma, and it tastes like it.

## Quick FAQ

**What is truffle honey?**
Honey infused with truffle, usually black summer truffle. Sweet first, then
earthy and mushroomy.

**What cheese goes with truffle honey?**
Pecorino romano and other hard salty sheep cheeses, Parmigiano-Reggiano, burrata,
and aged gouda.

**How can I tell if it has real truffle?**
Look for visible dark flecks and a truffle species named in the ingredients.
Cheap jars use synthetic aroma only.

**How much should I use?**
A quarter-ounce per person at most. The aroma is powerful and dominates a board
in quantity.

**Does it go off?**
The honey keeps almost indefinitely, but the truffle aroma fades within months of
opening. Buy small jars.
