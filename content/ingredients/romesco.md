## The short version

A Catalan sauce of roasted red peppers, tomatoes, almonds, bread and smoked
paprika, blended thick. It's smoky, sweet, nutty and faintly acidic, and it's the
best dip on a Spanish board by a distance — but almost nobody puts it out, which
is the argument for doing it.

## What it is

Traditionally made with *nyora* or ñora peppers, roasted tomato, toasted almonds
or hazelnuts, stale bread, garlic, sherry vinegar and olive oil. The bread and
nuts are what make it thick — it's an emulsion, closer to a pesto than to a purée.

**Taste:** sweet roasted pepper, smoky paprika, nutty, tangy
**Texture:** thick and slightly grainy from the nuts and bread
**Look:** brick red-orange, matte, coarse

The mechanism is nuts doing the work of fat. Most dips get their body from oil or
dairy; romesco gets it from ground almonds and bread, which gives a heavier, more
savoury mouthfeel and a slight grit. On a board that means it behaves like a
condiment for cheese and meat rather than a scoop-and-eat dip.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk salt and almond in the sauce come from the same region and the same flavour family. |
| **Jamón serrano** | Sweet smoky pepper against salty dry-cured ham. The Catalan bar plate. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Almond doubled — in the sauce and beside it. Different textures, one flavour. |
| **Grilled vegetables and asparagus** | Its original use. *Calçots* dipped in romesco is the whole point of the sauce. |
| **Baguette and crusty bread** | The base. Bread is already in the sauce, so it's a natural fit. |
| **Grilled shrimp and white fish** | If the board leans seafood, this is its condiment. |

**Skip it with:** delicate soft cheeses and sweet jams. Romesco is assertive and
already carries its own sweetness.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with a spoon.
2. **Pool olive oil on top** and dust with smoked paprika — it looks better and
   the aroma carries.
3. **Serve at room temperature.** Cold flattens it entirely.
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
5. **Put it on a Spanish or vegetable board**, where it makes the most sense.
6. **Loosen with olive oil** if it's very thick.
7. **Fix:** flat? Sherry vinegar, half a teaspoon at a time. It's almost always
   what's missing.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spanish jarred romesco** | Spanish delis, Whole Foods, online | The convenient buy. Quality varies; look for almonds high in the list. |
| **Mitica or Casa Mas romesco** | Cheese shops, specialty | Reliable brands, proper texture. |
| **Trader Joe's romesco** | Trader Joe's, seasonal | Cheap and decent when they carry it. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Jarred roasted peppers, toasted almonds, stale bread, garlic, smoked paprika, sherry vinegar. Ten minutes in a processor. |
| **Ajvar** | Balkan and Eastern European grocers | A related roasted pepper spread without nuts. Cheaper and also excellent. |

**Label check:** almonds or hazelnuts should be in the ingredients. A "romesco"
with no nuts is a roasted pepper sauce.

**Skip:** anything smooth and glossy — romesco should be coarse.

## Quick FAQ

**What is romesco?**
A Catalan sauce of roasted red peppers, tomato, almonds, bread, garlic and smoked
paprika, blended thick.

**What goes with romesco on a board?**
Manchego, jamón serrano, marcona almonds, grilled vegetables and crusty bread.

**Does romesco contain nuts?**
Yes — almonds, sometimes hazelnuts. It's a tree nut allergen and worth flagging.

**Can I make romesco at home?**
Yes. Blend jarred roasted peppers, toasted almonds, stale bread, garlic, smoked
paprika and sherry vinegar. Keep it coarse.

**Is it spicy?**
Not usually. It's smoky and sweet rather than hot, though some versions add
chilli.
