## The short version

Italy's blue cheese, made in two distinct grades. *Dolce* is soft, sweet and
spoonably creamy — the gentlest blue there is. *Piccante* is firmer, saltier
and much sharper. Nearly everyone who says they hate blue cheese has only ever
met a piccante.

## What it is

Gorgonzola DOP is made in Lombardy and Piedmont from cow's milk inoculated with
*Penicillium roqueforti*. The wheels are pierced with needles so air reaches
the interior and the mold can grow into veins. *Dolce* is aged about two months;
*piccante* three to four or more.

**Taste (dolce):** sweet, creamy, mildly tangy, gentle blue
**Taste (piccante):** salty, sharp, spicy, assertive
**Texture:** soft and almost spreadable dolce; firm and crumbly piccante
**Look:** ivory paste with blue-green veining, thin natural rind

Air, not time alone, is the mechanism. Blue mold needs oxygen, so blue cheeses
are pierced to let it in — the veins follow the needle tracks. More piercing and
longer aging means more mold, more of the sharp compounds it produces, and a
firmer, drier paste. That's the whole difference between the two grades.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Walnut raisin bread** | Sweet raisin, tannic walnut, salty blue. The single best bite in this category. |
| **Honey** | Sweet against salt and funk. The most reliable blue-cheese pairing there is. |
| **Fresh pear** | Cool, sweet and wet against sharp and salty. Classic and correct. |
| **Oatcakes** | Sweet oat, crumbly texture, and enough body to hold a wet cheese. |
| **Fig jam and mostarda** | Dense preserves that stand up to a strong cheese. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin meeting blue-mold bitterness halfway. |
| **Sweet wine — Sauternes, Moscato, port** | Sugar against salt. The classic rule for every blue. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses anywhere nearby, and smoked cheeses —
two assertive aromas colliding. Give it its own end of the board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy *dolce* unless you know the crowd.** It converts skeptics; piccante
   confirms their opinion.
2. **Temper 45 minutes.** Cold blue tastes mostly of salt; warm, the sweetness
   and the creaminess arrive.
3. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** Blue is rich and most people take a small
   amount.
4. **Give it its own knife and its own corner.** Blue mold transfers to
   whatever the blade touches next.
5. **Put honey directly beside it** with a spoon. This is the pairing that
   makes blue work for people who think they don't like it.
6. **Dolce is spreadable** — serve it with a small spoon rather than a cheese
   knife.
7. **Fix:** if the paste is browning at the edges, trim it. Blue cheese
   oxidizes at cut faces faster than most.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola Dolce DOP** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, most grocery | The gentle one. Start here for any mixed crowd. |
| **Gorgonzola Piccante DOP** | Cheese counters, Italian delis | The sharp one. For a board where blue is the point. |
| **Gorgonzola Cremificato** | Cheese counters | An extra-creamy dolce style. Excellent spooned onto crostini. |
| **Cambozola** | Grocery cheese case | Blue-brie hybrid, even gentler — see its own page. |
| **Stilton** | Whole Foods, cheese counters | The English alternative, firmer and nuttier — see its own page. |
| **Trader Joe's Gorgonzola** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and usually dolce-style. |

**Label check:** *dolce* or *piccante* on the label, plus the DOP mark.
Unlabeled "gorgonzola" at a US counter is usually a domestic imitation and
tends toward sharp.

**Skip:** pre-crumbled blue cheese for a board. It's dried, often anti-caked,
and it looks like salad topping.

## Quick FAQ

**Gorgonzola dolce or piccante?**
Dolce for a mixed crowd — soft, sweet and gentle. Piccante if the board is for
people who already like blue cheese.

**Why does blue cheese have veins?**
The wheels are pierced with needles so oxygen reaches the interior, letting the
*Penicillium* mold grow. The veins follow the needle tracks.

**What's the one pairing to get right?**
Honey. Then walnut raisin bread. Both work on the same sweet-against-salt
principle.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. Blue is rich.

**Why does it need its own knife?**
Blue mold transfers to whatever the blade touches next, and it will end up on
the brie.
