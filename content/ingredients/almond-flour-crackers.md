## The short version

A grain-free cracker made from ground almonds. It's the best-tasting
gluten-free option on a board — rich, nutty, faintly sweet — and the one most
guests won't identify as a substitute. Its two limits: it crumbles in dip, and
it's made of nuts.

## What it is

Almond flour crackers are built from blanched almond meal bound with seeds
(usually flax and sunflower) and a little starch. There's no wheat, no rice and
often no grain at all, which is why they land in the paleo aisle as often as
the gluten-free one.

**Taste:** buttery, nutty, gently sweet
**Texture:** short and crumbly rather than snappy — closer to shortbread
**Look:** pale tan, slightly thick, matte

Fat is the mechanism, and it's the reason these work where other gluten-free
crackers don't. Almonds are roughly half oil, so the cracker has richness built
in and none of the dry, chalky quality that rice-flour crackers get. It behaves
much more like a butter cracker than like a health food.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tangy against rich and nutty. The pairing that makes guests forget it's a substitute. |
| **Brie and Camembert** | Both are soft and rich; the cracker's crumble is the only texture in the bite, and it's enough. |
| **Fig jam and honey** | The cracker is already faintly sweet, so this reads as a finished cookie-adjacent bite. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt cuts almond richness cleanly. A very good two-ingredient bite. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp acid against nutty fat, in the same way apples work with cheddar. |
| **Avocado and flaky salt** | The low-carb board's default, and better here than on a seed cracker. |
| **Sparkling wine, dry rosé** | Acidity to cut a fatty cracker. |

**Skip it with:** thick dips and anything requiring a scoop — these crumble
under pressure, and half the cracker ends up in the hummus. And treat them as a
cheese-board cracker, not a party-chip.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Fan them flat and handle them once.** They're brittle in a crumbly way,
   not a snappy way, and they break along invisible lines.
2. **Serve 4–5 per person.**
3. **Never make them the only gluten-free option.** They contain tree nuts, and
   the overlap between "gluten-free guest" and "nut-free guest" is real. Put
   out a cassava or rice-based cracker too.
4. **Label them.** A small card saying "almond" takes ten seconds and prevents
   a genuinely bad evening.
5. **Keep them away from the dip corner** so nobody tries to scoop with one.
6. **Fix:** they soften rather than stale. 3 minutes at 300°F and a full cool
   restores the snap.

## Quick FAQ

**Are almond flour crackers gluten-free?**
Yes, and usually grain-free too. They are **not** nut-free — that's the catch.

**Do they taste gluten-free?**
No, and that's their selling point. They read as rich and buttery rather than
as a substitute.

**Can I dip with them?**
Not really. They crumble. Use cassava crackers or plantain chips for scooping.

**How many per person?**
4–5. They're rich and filling.

**Are they keto?**
Low-carb but not zero — most contain some starch. Check the box if it matters.
