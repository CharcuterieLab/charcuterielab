## The short version

Not really a nut in behaviour — low in fat, high in starch, soft and crumbly like
a cooked potato. That makes them a genuine oddity on a board and a very good one
in December. Vacuum-packed pre-cooked chestnuts are widely available and, warmed
in a pan with butter, close enough to the roasted version.

## What it is

The seed of the sweet chestnut tree, cooked before eating. Sold in shell (roast
or boil), vacuum-packed cooked, jarred, or as purée. Italian, French and Spanish
chestnuts are the ones to look for; the season runs October to January.

**Taste:** sweet, starchy, faintly earthy, like sweet potato
**Texture:** soft, floury, crumbly — no crunch at all
**Look:** irregular pale golden lobes

The mechanism is starch on a board that has none. Everything else is fat, salt,
sugar, acid or crunch. Chestnut is the only ingredient that behaves like a
carbohydrate, which makes it filling and comforting in a way nothing else on the
board is — closer to what bread does than to what a nut does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola** | Starchy sweetness against salt and funk. A classic northern Italian combination. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat against soft starch, the way ham works with potatoes. |
| **Comté and gruyère** | Alpine cheese and chestnut share a nutty sweetness and a winter context. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against earthy sweet. |
| **Pears** | Both autumn, both sweet and soft. |
| **Mulled wine and dark beer** | The seasonal context that makes chestnuts make sense. |

**Skip it with:** delicate summer ingredients. A chestnut on a board with burrata
and berries makes no sense.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Score an X** through the shell on the flat side of each nut with a sharp
   knife. This is not optional — unscored chestnuts explode.
2. **Roast at 425°F for 20–25 minutes**, or in a pan over high heat, shaking.
3. **Peel while hot**, wrapped in a towel. Cold chestnuts are almost impossible
   to peel.
4. **Or buy vacuum-packed**, and warm them in butter and salt for five minutes.
   This is what most people should do.
5. **Serve warm**, in a bowl or wrapped in a cloth napkin.
6. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
7. **Fix:** stubborn inner skin? That's normal and the main reason to buy them
   pre-peeled.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fresh chestnuts in shell** | Grocery, Italian markets, Oct–Jan | The real thing. Buy heavy, unwrinkled, glossy nuts. |
| **Vacuum-packed cooked chestnuts** | Grocery, Trader Joe's, seasonal | The practical choice. Warm in butter — genuinely good. |
| **Jarred chestnuts in water** | Italian delis | Drain well and warm. Softer than vacuum-packed. |
| **Marrons glacés** | French delis, Christmas | Candied chestnuts. A luxury dessert item, and spectacular. |
| **Chestnut purée / crème de marrons** | French delis | Sweet spread. Belongs with the jams, not the nuts. |

**Label check:** fresh chestnuts should be heavy and glossy with taut shells. A
rattling or light chestnut has dried out inside.

**Skip:** horse chestnuts (conkers) — different tree, and not edible.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I have to score chestnuts before roasting?**
Yes. Cut an X through the shell on the flat side, or they burst in the oven.

**Are vacuum-packed chestnuts any good?**
Yes. Warmed in butter with salt they're close to freshly roasted, and they skip
the peeling entirely.

**Why are chestnuts hard to peel?**
The inner skin clings, especially once cool. Peel them while hot, wrapped in a
towel.

**What cheese goes with chestnuts?**
Gorgonzola, comté and gruyère, and aged gouda.

**When are chestnuts in season?**
October through January. They don't keep well, so buy them fresh in that window.
