## The short version

Dried, seasoned beef, the most popular cured meat in America by a mile. On a
board it does what bresaola does: lean, savory, chewy contrast to soft
cheese. Pick a tender, peppery style, snip it short and it disappears
faster than the salami.

## What it is

Jerky is whole-muscle beef, usually lean round, sliced thin, marinated in
salt, sugar and seasonings (often soy and Worcestershire), and dried at low
heat or in a smoker until it loses most of its water. Native peoples of the
Americas dried meat this way long before the word jerky, from the Quechua
"ch'arki," came into English.

**Taste:** savory, salty, smoky and a little sweet, with black pepper
**Texture:** chewy to tender, depending on the cut and the cut direction
**Look:** dark mahogany strips, matte or slightly glossy

Tenderness is about grain. Jerky sliced against the grain has short muscle
fibers that break easily, so it's tender. Sliced with the grain, the fibers
run the whole length and it's very chewy. Drying removes water so bacteria
can't grow, and the salt and sugar in the marinade pull more moisture out.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pepper jack or Colby Jack** | Creamy, mild cheese softens a lean, salty chew. |
| **Dill pickles** | Sour juice cuts the salt and resets your palate. |
| **Trail mix** | Sweet and crunchy against savory and chewy. A hiking board. |
| **Kettle chips** | Crunch and salt for a game-day board. |
| **Biltong** | Serve both and taste two ways of drying beef. |
| **IPA, a brown ale or bourbon** | Bitterness or oak stands up to smoke and pepper. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. The smoke and pepper drown them.
Choose mild semi-soft cheeses instead.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Choose a tender style.** "Tender cut," "steak strips" or biltong-style
   jerky are easiest to eat.
2. **Snip it into 2-inch pieces** with kitchen scissors.
3. **Serve half an ounce to an ounce per person.** It's dense.
4. **Stack it loosely** or stand it in a small cup.
5. **It keeps for hours** on the board, so it can go on first.
6. **Fix:** if it's too tough, wrap it in a damp paper towel for 10 minutes
   to soften the surface.

## Quick FAQ

**Is beef jerky good on a charcuterie board?**
Yes, if you choose a tender style and cut it into short strips. It's the
American cousin of air-dried meats like bresaola and biltong.

**What's the difference between jerky and biltong?**
Jerky is sliced thin, marinated and dried with heat, often with smoke.
Biltong is vinegar-cured and air-dried in thicker pieces, then sliced, so
it's softer.

**How much jerky per person?**
About half an ounce to an ounce. It's dense and salty, so a little goes a
long way.

**Is beef jerky gluten-free?**
Not always. Many marinades use soy sauce made with wheat. Look for a
gluten-free label.

**How do you cut jerky for a board?**
Snip it into 2-inch pieces with kitchen scissors, which is easier than a
knife.
