## The short version

The best effort-to-payoff ratio on a board. Pecans, egg white or butter, brown
sugar, salt, fifteen minutes in the oven — and you have something people
specifically remember. They're also the correct partner for blue cheese, which
otherwise needs both sweetness and crunch from two separate ingredients.

## What it is

Pecan halves coated in a sugar glaze and baked or pan-cooked until the coating
sets hard. The two common methods: an **egg-white glaze** (sets crisp and dry,
best for a board) or a **butter-and-sugar caramel** (stickier, richer, harder to
handle). Cinnamon, cayenne and rosemary are the usual additions.

**Taste:** sweet, buttery, toasty, with salt and often a little heat
**Texture:** hard-glazed shell over a soft nut
**Look:** glossy amber halves, slightly clustered

The mechanism is doing two jobs with one ingredient. A blue cheese wants
sweetness and crunch; put out honey and plain nuts and that's two dishes and two
motions. A candied pecan is both, in one pick-up — which is why it's the standard
on restaurant cheese plates.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola and stilton** | Sugar blunts the salt, the glaze provides the crunch, the pecan provides the fat. A complete pairing in one piece. |
| **Brie** | Sweet crunch against soft fat, doing the job a cracker usually does. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sweet, with the cheddar's crystals meeting the sugar shell. |
| **Pears and apples** | Autumn board logic; the spices in the glaze suit both. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang, sugar, crunch. Chop them and roll a log in them. |
| **Bourbon and dessert wine** | The obvious drinks pairing, and the reason these appear on so many restaurant plates. |

**Skip it with:** other sweet things. Candied pecans plus honey plus jam is
three versions of the same idea.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make them:** whisk one egg white with a splash of water until frothy, toss
   with 2 cups pecan halves, then with 1/2 cup brown sugar, 1 tsp salt, and
   cinnamon or cayenne to taste.
2. **Spread on parchment** and bake at 300°F for 25 minutes, stirring twice.
3. **Cool completely on the sheet.** They crisp as they cool — don't judge them
   warm.
4. **Break apart** any clusters.
5. **Serve in a small bowl.** The glaze is sticky enough to mark cheese.
6. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — they're very sweet.
7. **Fix:** soft the next day? Humidity. Ten minutes at 275°F re-crisps them.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw pecan halves + brown sugar** | Any grocery | Make them. Fifteen minutes of work, and far better than any bought version. |
| **Trader Joe's candied pecans** | Trader Joe's | The best supermarket version. Seasonal. |
| **Praline pecans** | Southern grocers, online | Butter-caramel style. Stickier and richer. |
| **Spiced candied pecans** | Specialty, farmers markets | Cayenne or rosemary versions. Excellent with blue cheese. |
| **Sugared walnuts** | Grocery | Same technique, more bitterness. Also good with blue. |

**Label check:** bought versions vary hugely in sugar. Look for pecans first in
the ingredients, not sugar.

**Skip:** anything glazed in corn syrup — sticky rather than crisp, and it will
smear across the board.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I make candied pecans?**
Toss pecan halves in frothy egg white, then in brown sugar and salt, and bake at
300°F for 25 minutes, stirring twice. Cool completely before serving.

**Why egg white instead of butter?**
Egg white sets into a dry crisp shell, which is what you want on a board. Butter
caramel is stickier and marks the cheese.

**What cheese goes with candied pecans?**
Blue cheeses above all — gorgonzola and stilton — plus brie and aged cheddar.

**How far ahead can I make them?**
A week in an airtight container. Re-crisp at 275°F for ten minutes if they soften.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce. They're rich and very sweet.
