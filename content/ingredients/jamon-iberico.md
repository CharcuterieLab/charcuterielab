## The short version

Ham from black Iberian pigs, the best of them fattened on acorns in oak
pastures and cured for three to four years. It is the most expensive charcuterie
in general circulation, and the fat genuinely melts at hand temperature. Serve
it alone, at room temperature, and don't put a cracker under it.

## What it is

Iberian pigs are a distinct breed that stores fat within the muscle rather than
around it. The top grade, *bellota*, comes from pigs that roam oak *dehesa*
pastures in the autumn eating acorns, which are high in oleic acid — the same
fat that dominates olive oil. Cured two to four years.

**Taste:** nutty, sweet, deeply savory, long finish
**Texture:** silky, the fat liquefying almost on contact
**Look:** dark red, heavily marbled with glossy yellowish fat

Oleic acid is the mechanism, and it's why the fat behaves the way it does.
Acorn feeding raises the proportion of unsaturated fat, which lowers its melting
point below body temperature. Rest a slice on your palm and it turns glossy in
seconds — that's the single best test of whether you have the real thing.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Nothing** | Genuinely the correct answer. Room temperature, on its own, first. |
| **Manchego** | If you add one thing. Nutty sheep's milk beside nutty pork. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Both taste of oleic-rich fat. Direct echo. |
| **Pan con tomate** | Bread rubbed with tomato and oil. The Spanish service. |
| **Fino or Manzanilla sherry** | The classic pairing — dry, saline, and it cuts the fat. |
| **Cava** | Bubbles against melting fat. |
| **Olive oil crackers** | If you need a base, keep it plain and Mediterranean. |

**Skip it with:** everything crackers, smoked cheese, blue cheese, mustard,
pickles. This is the most expensive thing on the board and almost any partner
diminishes it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 30 minutes** — longer than any other ham. The fat has to soften or
   you lose the entire point.
2. **Serve it on a plate at room temperature, not on cold marble or slate.**
   Cold stone re-firms the fat within a minute.
3. **Slice thin and slightly irregular**, in small pieces about two inches
   long. Hand-carved is traditional and looks it.
4. **Lay slices flat and slightly separated**, not stacked. Stacked slices
   fuse.
5. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** It's extraordinarily rich.
6. **Serve it first**, before anything strong.
7. **Fix:** if it looks dull and firm, it's cold. Warmth is the whole
   preparation.

|---|---|---|
| **Jamón Ibérico de Bellota (black label)** | Spanish specialty grocers, online, high-end delis | 100% Iberian, acorn-fed, 3–4 years. The top grade. |
| **Jamón Ibérico de Bellota (red label)** | Same | 50–75% Iberian, acorn-fed. Excellent and cheaper. |
| **Jamón Ibérico de Cebo (white/green label)** | Whole Foods, Spanish grocers | Grain-fed. Good, much cheaper, noticeably less complex. |
| **Fermín or Cinco Jotas** | Whole Foods, specialty, online | The best-known importers into the US. |
| **Pre-sliced vacuum packs** | Whole Foods, Spanish grocers | The practical way to buy it. A 2 oz pack serves two. |
| **Jamón serrano** | Most grocery | The everyday Spanish ham at a fraction of the price. |

**Label check:** the label color grades it — black is *bellota* 100% Ibérico,
red is *bellota* crossbred, green is *cebo de campo*, white is *cebo*. All are
Ibérico; only the black and red are acorn-fed.

**Skip:** buying it for a large crowd. A little of the good grade beats a lot
of the cheap one, and at a party most of it goes unnoticed.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is jamón ibérico so expensive?**
A distinct slow-growing breed, free-range acorn feeding, and three to four
years of curing. The best hams take longer to make than most wine.

**What does *bellota* mean?**
Acorn. It means the pig was finished on acorns during the autumn *montanera*,
which is what produces the melting fat.

**How do I know it's real?**
Rest a slice on your palm. Genuine bellota fat goes glossy and starts to melt
within seconds.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. It's the richest and most expensive item you'll serve.

**What should I serve with it?**
Ideally nothing. If you must, Manchego, Marcona almonds and a dry sherry.
