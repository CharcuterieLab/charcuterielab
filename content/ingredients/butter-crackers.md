## The short version

A flaky, salty, slightly sweet cracker with a lot of fat baked into it. Unlike
a water cracker, it is an ingredient — it brings flavor, and that flavor either
helps or fights whatever you put on it. Great with sharp, salty, funky things.
Wrong under anything delicate.

## What it is

The American snack cracker: soft wheat flour, oil, a little sugar, salt, and a
salted top. It's laminated so it bakes into thin flaky layers, which is why it
crumbles instead of snapping. Most of the famous ones contain no actual butter
— "butter cracker" describes the flavor, not the ingredient list.

**Taste:** buttery, salty, faintly sweet
**Texture:** flaky, crumbly, melts fast
**Look:** golden, round, scalloped edge, docked with tiny holes

The fat is the whole story. It coats your palate, which rounds off sharp edges
— useful when the cheese is aggressive, ruinous when the cheese is subtle.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Sharp cheddar** | The fat blunts cheddar's acidic bite. This is the pairing the cracker was made for. |
| **Pimento cheese** | Salt, fat and tang need a sweet-ish carrier or they read as one flat note. |
| **Beer cheese and other sharp spreads** | Same logic — the sugar in the cracker lifts a savory spread off the tongue. |
| **Summer sausage, hard salami** | Salt on salt would be too much, except the cracker's sweetness breaks it up. |
| **Smoked gouda** | Sweetness and smoke are a classic axis; the cracker adds the sweet half. |
| **Hot pepper jelly** | Fat is what carries capsaicin. The heat spreads and softens instead of spiking. |
| **Apple butter, chutney** | Sweet-on-sweet works here because the salt line keeps it from being dessert. |

**Skip it with:** brie, Camembert and triple-crèmes (fat on fat, and the
cracker wins), smoked salmon or caviar (it bulldozes them), and any cheese you
paid real money for — you won't taste it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Put them out last.** They go soft faster than any other cracker on this
   list once they're near cut fruit or soft cheese.
2. **Stack, don't fan.** Two short leaning stacks look intentional. Fanned, the
   scalloped edges read as messy.
3. **Serve 4–5 per person** on a mixed board. They're filling — people eat
   fewer of these than water crackers.
4. **Give them their own corner** next to the sharp cheese and the sausage, not
   in the middle where everything migrates.
5. **Don't build them ahead.** Anything wet turns them to paste in ten minutes.
6. **Fix:** gone soft? 3 minutes at 300°F, then cool completely. They come
   back, but only once.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ritz Original** (13.7 oz) | Every grocery store | The benchmark. Sweeter and softer than the rest. |
| **Ritz Original, Family Size** (20.5 oz) | Grocery, Walmart, club stores | Same cracker, better price if you host often. |
| **Club Original** (Kellanova) | Grocery, cracker aisle | Longer, flatter, less sweet. Better under sliced meat. |
| **Town House Original** | Grocery, cracker aisle | Flakier and lighter. The best of the three for cheese. |
| **Pepperidge Farm Golden Butter** (9.75 oz) | Grocery, cracker aisle | Actually made with butter. The upgrade pick. |
| **Store brand round crackers** | Target, Aldi, Kroger | Genuinely close. This is not a category where the name matters much. |

**Label check:** if you want real butter flavor, read the ingredient list —
most of these use vegetable oil. Pepperidge Farm Golden Butter lists butter.

**Skip:** reduced-fat versions. The fat is the entire reason to use this
cracker. Without it you have a worse saltine.

## Quick FAQ

**Are Ritz crackers okay for a charcuterie board?**
Yes, for the right board. Put them on a cheddar-and-sausage board and they're
perfect. Put them on a brie-and-fig board and they're a mistake.

**How many do I need?**
About 4–5 per person alongside other bases. A sleeve holds roughly 30.

**Do butter crackers actually contain butter?**
Usually not. Most use vegetable oil and are named for the flavor.

**Why do mine always go soggy?**
The fat and the low bake mean they absorb moisture quickly. Keep them away
from cut fruit and soft cheese, and set them out last.
