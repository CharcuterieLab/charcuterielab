## The short version

The Spanish salami seasoned with black pepper instead of paprika — essentially
what chorizo would be without the pimentón. Milder, drier and more versatile.
If chorizo is already on the board, this is the second Spanish sausage to add.

## What it is

Salchichón is coarsely chopped pork seasoned with black peppercorns, salt,
garlic and sometimes nutmeg or clove, then cased and air-dried. Salchichón de
Vic (Catalonia) is the best-known protected version, made with coarse pepper and
cured in cool mountain air.

**Taste:** peppery, savory, mildly tangy, less sweet than Italian salami
**Texture:** firm and dry, coarse grind, slightly chewy
**Look:** pale pinkish-red with visible black peppercorns and irregular fat

Whole peppercorns are the mechanism and the reason salchichón eats differently
from a ground-pepper salami. Biting a whole corn releases a burst of piperine
in one spot rather than a uniform background heat, so the sausage tastes
intermittently sharp — which keeps a mild salami from getting monotonous.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego** | Spanish default. Buttery sheep's milk against black pepper. |
| **Membrillo** | Sweet against peppery salt. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Salt and fat with a lean, dry sausage. |
| **Green olives and pickled guindilla peppers** | Brine and acid cutting the fat. |
| **Pan con tomate** | Acid and oil under a dry sausage. |
| **Aged Mahón or Idiazábal** | Spanish cheeses with enough character to meet it. |
| **Rioja, Priorat, cold lager** | Spanish reds or something plain and cold. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses, which the garlic and pepper flatten.
Otherwise it's an easy, agreeable sausage.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thin and on the diagonal** for a larger oval face that shows the
   peppercorns.
2. **Temper 20 minutes.**
3. **Fan the slices** rather than folding — the pepper pattern is the look.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Pair it with chorizo, not instead of it.** Side by side, the
   pepper-versus-paprika contrast is the point of having both.
6. **Peel the casing.**
7. **Fix:** if it's crumbling as you slice, it's over-dried. Cut slightly
   thicker.

| **Chorizo** | Most grocery | The paprika counterpart — see its own page. |
| **Genoa salami** | Any grocery | The Italian equivalent if Spanish isn't available. |

**Label check:** visible whole peppercorns, and pork and pepper high in the
ingredients. *Ibérico* means Iberian pork and a noticeably better sausage.

**Skip:** buying salchichón and fuet and chorizo all at once unless the board is
explicitly Spanish. Two cured sausages is usually enough.

## Quick FAQ

**How is salchichón different from chorizo?**
Chorizo is seasoned with smoked paprika; salchichón with black pepper. Same
country, same technique, completely different flavour.

**Is it spicy?**
Peppery rather than hot. The whole peppercorns give sharp bursts, not chili
heat.

**Salchichón or fuet?**
Fuet is thinner, milder and softer, with a white mould bloom. Salchichón is
wider, drier and more peppery.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz as one of several meats.

**Can I eat the casing?**
Natural casing is edible but chewy. Most people peel it.
