## The short version

Thick, tangy, herby and familiar to every guest at the party. Ranch is what
makes people eat the vegetables on a board, and it's just as good with
pretzels, chips and cheese curds. Choose a thick dip rather than a pourable
dressing, and keep it cold.

## What it is

Ranch was created in the 1950s by Steve Henson, who served it at Hidden
Valley Ranch, his guest ranch near Santa Barbara, California. The base is
buttermilk or sour cream with dried herbs (dill, parsley, chives), garlic,
onion and black pepper. Dip versions use sour cream or Greek yogurt so they
cling.

**Taste:** tangy, creamy and savory with garlic, onion and dill
**Texture:** thick, smooth and cool
**Look:** off-white with flecks of green herbs and black pepper

Ranch works on raw vegetables because of fat and acid. Fat coats the
tongue and softens the bitter, grassy edge of raw broccoli or celery, while
lactic acid from the cultured dairy adds the bright tang that makes the
vegetables taste fresh. Garlic and onion bring savory depth, which is why
it also suits salty snacks.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Carrots, celery, cucumber and mini peppers** | Fat and tang soften raw, grassy crunch. |
| **Kettle chips and mini pretzels** | Salty crunch dragged through cool cream. |
| **Cheese curds** | A Wisconsin bar-snack move. |
| **Pepperoni** | Pizza-and-ranch flavor without the pizza. |
| **Pickled okra or dill pickles** | Sour, briny crunch with creamy herbs. |
| **Lager or iced tea** | Cold and easy, nothing fancy. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses on the same plate. Ranch's garlic
and dill overpower them. Keep the ranch in the snack-and-veggie corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Pick a thick dip,** not a pourable dressing. It should hold a carrot
   stick upright.
2. **Spoon it into a small bowl** and smooth the top with the back of a
   spoon.
3. **Sprinkle on fresh dill, chives or cracked pepper** so it looks
   homemade.
4. **Serve about 2 tablespoons per person.**
5. **Keep it cold** until guests arrive. Nest the bowl in ice for long
   parties.
6. **Fix:** if store dressing is too thin, stir in a few spoonfuls of sour
   cream or Greek yogurt.

## Quick FAQ

**Can you put ranch on a charcuterie board?**
Yes. Serve it in a small bowl with vegetables, pretzels and chips. It's the
most popular dip in America for a reason.

**What's the difference between ranch dip and ranch dressing?**
Ranch dip is thicker, usually made with sour cream, so it clings to
vegetables. Dressing is thinner and made with buttermilk and mayonnaise.

**How do you make ranch dip quickly?**
Stir a 1 oz packet of ranch seasoning into 16 oz of sour cream and chill it
for 30 minutes so the herbs hydrate.

**How long can ranch dip sit out?**
Up to 2 hours. Set the bowl in a larger bowl of ice for a long party.

**What do you dip in ranch on a board?**
Carrots, celery, cucumber, mini peppers, pretzels, kettle chips and even
cheese curds.
