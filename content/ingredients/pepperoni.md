## The short version

A fine-ground, paprika-and-chili spiced dry salami invented by Italian-Americans
in New York in the early 1900s. Ask for pepperoni in Italy and you'll get bell
peppers. Buy it as a whole stick from a deli and it's a genuinely good board
meat rather than a pizza topping.

## What it is

Pepperoni is pork and beef ground fine, seasoned heavily with paprika and
cayenne, cured with lactic cultures for tang, then smoked and dried. The name
comes from *peperoni*, Italian for bell peppers — the sausage itself is an
American creation with no Italian original.

**Taste:** spicy, smoky, tangy, paprika-sweet
**Texture:** firm and slightly greasy, fine grain, softer than most dry salami
**Look:** deep red-orange, fine white fat flecks, glossy

Fine grinding plus high paprika is the mechanism, and it's what separates
pepperoni from soppressata. Grinding fine distributes the fat and spice
uniformly so every bite is identical; the heavy paprika load carries colour into
the fat, which is why the surface goes orange and why the slices cup when
heated.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged provolone** | Sharp Italian-American cheese with an Italian-American sausage. |
| **Butter crackers** | The deli-plate base. Fat and salt on a sweet cracker. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Cool milk against chili heat. |
| **Pickled cherry peppers and pepperoncini** | Acid and more heat. The pizzeria condiment shelf. |
| **Hot honey** | Sweetness and heat against spicy fat. Very good. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against spicy. Works well on a casual board. |
| **Cold lager, Chianti, Sangiovese** | Nothing precious required. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses other than mozzarella. Paprika and
cayenne linger and flatten anything subtle after them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy a whole stick, not pizza slices.** Deli sticks are a different and
   much better product than the thin pre-sliced rounds.
2. **Slice thin on the diagonal** for larger ovals.
3. **Temper 20 minutes.**
4. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Consider crisping some.** Two minutes in a dry pan turns pepperoni into
   crisp cups — genuinely one of the better cheap board items.
6. **Peel the casing.**
7. **Fix:** greasy on the board? It's warm. Pepperoni renders fat above about
   72°F more than most dry salami.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Deli pepperoni stick** | Deli counters, Italian markets | The board version. Ask for it whole. |
| **Volpi or Boar's Head pepperoni** | Grocery, deli counters | Widely available and reliable. |
| **Ezzo or Hormel cup-and-char pepperoni** | Online, restaurant supply | The kind that cups and crisps. Excellent crisped on a board. |
| **Creminelli or Olli** | Whole Foods, Target | Cleaner ingredients, small premium. |
| **Soppressata piccante** | Grocery, Whole Foods | The coarse Italian spicy salami — see its own page. |
| **Calabrese salami** | Italian delis | The Italian chili salami pepperoni is loosely modelled on. |

**Label check:** pork and beef, paprika and cayenne. Avoid long filler lists and
"mechanically separated" anything.

**Skip:** thin pre-sliced pizza pepperoni for a board. It's engineered to melt,
not to eat cold.

## Quick FAQ

**Is pepperoni Italian?**
No — it's Italian-American, invented in New York in the early twentieth
century. In Italy, *peperoni* means bell peppers.

**Why does pizza pepperoni cup?**
Natural casing and a particular fat structure make the edges contract faster
than the middle when heated. Cup-and-char pepperoni is made for it.

**Is it very spicy?**
Moderately — paprika-warm rather than chili-hot for most brands.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.

**Should I crisp it?**
On a casual board, yes. Two minutes in a dry pan makes crisp cups that people
finish first.
