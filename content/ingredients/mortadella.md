## The short version

A large cooked pork sausage from Bologna, studded with cubes of back fat and
usually pistachios. Silky, delicately spiced and faintly sweet. American bologna
descends from it and bears almost no resemblance — this is a genuinely fine
product.

## What it is

Mortadella Bologna IGP is finely ground pork emulsified into a smooth paste,
studded with cubes of neck fat, seasoned with black pepper, myrtle berries and
often pistachios, then slow-cooked in dry-air ovens for hours. The huge
diameter is functional — it lets the sausage cook evenly over a long, gentle
period.

**Taste:** delicate, sweet, faintly peppery, subtly spiced
**Texture:** silky and smooth with soft fat cubes throughout
**Look:** pale pink, pearly, dotted with white fat squares and green pistachios

Emulsification is the mechanism. Grinding pork to a fine paste and binding fat
into it produces a texture no dry-cured meat has — soft, uniform and yielding.
That's why mortadella is the one charcuterie item that works cubed, and why it
melts into a bite instead of resisting it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Grissini, wrapped** | Soft silk around dry crunch. The Bolognese aperitivo bite. |
| **Focaccia** | Oily bread and silky pork — a Ligurian sandwich in board form. |
| **Burrata or stracciatella** | Creamy on creamy, which works because both are so mild. |
| **Pistachios** | The nut that's already inside it, scattered alongside. |
| **Aged provolone or parmesan** | Sharp and salty against sweet and soft. |
| **Pickled cherry peppers and giardiniera** | Acid and heat cutting a rich, mild sausage. |
| **Lambrusco** | The Emilian pairing. Fizzy, slightly sweet red against fat. |

**Skip it with:** smoked cheese, which overwhelms it. Mortadella is delicate
despite its size.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Ask for it thick-sliced or cubed**, not shaved. Paper-thin mortadella
   tears and reads as lunch meat; a ¼-inch slice or a ½-inch cube shows what it
   is.
2. **Cube it and skewer it with pistachios** for the easiest good-looking bite
   on any Italian board.
3. **Temper 20 minutes.** The fat cubes need to soften.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Fold thick slices into loose quarters** if you're not cubing.
6. **Note the pistachios as a nut allergen** — people don't expect nuts in a
   sausage.
7. **Fix:** slices tearing means they're cut too thin. Ask for a thicker cut.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortadella Bologna IGP** | Italian delis, Whole Foods, specialty | The protected original. Ask for it at the counter. |
| **Mortadella with pistachios** | Deli counters, Italian markets | The standard version and the best-looking on a board. |
| **Fiorucci or Levoni mortadella** | Grocery deli, Italian markets | Widely imported. Reliable. |
| **Boar's Head Mortadella** | Deli counters | The everyday version. Fine and cheap. |
| **Creminelli mortadella** | Whole Foods, specialty | American-made, good quality. |
| **Truffle mortadella** | Italian delis, specialty | A real variant. Excellent, and a step up in price. |

**Label check:** the IGP mark for the Bologna original, and visible whole fat
cubes rather than a uniformly pink paste.

**Skip:** American bologna as a substitute. Same ancestry, entirely different
product.

## Quick FAQ

**Is mortadella just fancy bologna?**
The reverse — American bologna is a cheap descendant of mortadella. The
original is finely emulsified pork with whole fat cubes and real spicing.

**Why the pistachios?**
Traditional in many regions, and they add crunch and color to an otherwise
uniform sausage. Not all mortadella has them.

**Should I slice it thin?**
No. Thick slices or cubes. Shaved mortadella tears and loses its texture.

**Is it cooked?**
Yes — slow-cooked for hours in dry-air ovens. Unlike salami, it isn't
fermented and dried.

**Does it contain nuts?**
The pistachio version does. Worth flagging, since nobody expects nuts in a
sausage.
