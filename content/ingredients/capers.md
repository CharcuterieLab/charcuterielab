## The short version

Unopened flower buds of the caper bush, preserved in salt or brine. They're
intensely salty and floral, and they work as a scatter rather than a serving — a
teaspoon over smoked salmon or burrata changes the whole plate. Salt-packed are
better than brined by a clear margin.

## What it is

Caper buds, graded by size: **nonpareil** are the smallest and most prized;
sizes go up through surfines, capucines and capotes. **Salt-packed** capers are
buried in coarse salt and must be rinsed; **brined** capers sit in vinegar and
taste more of the brine than the bud.

**Taste:** intensely salty, floral, faintly mustardy and lemony
**Texture:** firm little beads that burst
**Look:** tiny olive-green buds

The mechanism is mustard oil. Capers contain glucosinolates — the same family of
compounds as mustard and horseradish — which is why they taste pungent as well as
salty. That pungency is what lets a tiny quantity cut through fat, and why capers
work with oily fish and rich cream where a plain salty ingredient wouldn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Smoked salmon and cream cheese** | The bagel board. Salt and mustard pungency cutting oily fish and dairy fat. |
| **Burrata** | Scattered over with olive oil and black pepper — cream with sharp salty punctuation. |
| **Tapenade** | Capers are already in it. Scattering more on top doubles the effect. |
| **Anchovies** | Two salt-cured Mediterranean things from the same shelf. Sicilian cooking pairs them constantly. |
| **Fresh mozzarella and tomato** | Salt and acid on something very mild. |
| **Fried cheese and croquettes** | Capers fried until they open are a garnish worth knowing about. |

**Skip it with:** sweet things and delicate aged cheeses. Capers are pungent out
of proportion to their size.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Rinse salt-packed capers thoroughly** — two or three changes of water, or
   soak for ten minutes. They're inedible otherwise.
2. **Drain brined capers** and pat dry.
3. **Scatter, don't serve.** A small bowl of capers is odd; a scatter over
   burrata or salmon is right.
4. **Serve 0.25 oz per person.**
5. **Fry them** for the upgrade: drain, dry, and fry in hot oil for 30 seconds
   until they burst open and crisp. Spectacular.
6. **Use nonpareil** for scattering; larger capers for chopping.
7. **Fix:** too salty? Rinse again. Salt-packed capers need more rinsing than
   feels reasonable.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Salt-packed capers** | Italian delis, specialty | The better product. They taste of caper rather than vinegar. Rinse well. |
| **Nonpareil capers in brine** | Any grocery | The everyday standard. Small and convenient. |
| **Pantelleria capers** | Italian specialty | From the Sicilian island; the benchmark, salt-packed. |
| **Caperberries** | Grocery, olive bars | The fruit rather than the bud — larger, milder, with a stem. Its own page. |
| **Fried capers** | Make them | 30 seconds in hot oil. A different ingredient entirely. |

**Label check:** salt-packed will say so and come in a tub or bag. Brined capers
in small jars are fine but taste more of vinegar.

**Skip:** capers in a jar with a cloudy, sludgy brine.

## Quick FAQ

**Salt-packed or brined capers?**
Salt-packed taste more of the caper itself. They need thorough rinsing. Brined are
more convenient and taste more of vinegar.

**What are nonpareil capers?**
The smallest size grade, and generally the most prized — firmer and more
concentrated than larger capers.

**How do I use capers on a board?**
Scatter them over burrata, smoked salmon or fresh mozzarella. They're a garnish,
not a serving.

**Can I fry capers?**
Yes, and it's worth doing. Drain, dry, and fry in hot oil for 30 seconds until
they burst open and crisp.

**How much per person?**
About a quarter-ounce — a scatter, not a spoonful.
