## The short version

Every board needs one meat that asks nothing of the eater. Prosciutto cotto is it:
a whole pork leg brined and slowly steamed, sliced thin, mild and faintly sweet.
Where dry-cured ham is salt and intensity, this is tender and gentle — which makes
it the slice that children, cautious guests and anyone tired of salt will
actually finish.

## What it is

Italian cooked ham. A whole deboned leg is brined, sometimes with herbs or juniper,
then steamed slowly at low temperature. The best versions — alta qualità on the
label — are a single muscle with no added starch or protein.

**Taste:** mildly sweet, clean pork, gentle salt
**Texture:** tender, moist, silky when sliced thin
**Look:** pale rose-pink with a fine white fat edge

The mechanism is low-temperature cooking. Steaming a brined leg at a controlled low
heat sets the proteins without squeezing out water, so the ham stays moist and
tender rather than firm and dry. Dry-curing does the opposite — it removes water
over months to concentrate everything. Same animal, opposite processes, opposite
results.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fontina** | Nutty, mild, melting. The northern Italian pairing, and the two are almost never served apart. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Mild on mild. It sounds boring and it is what people eat first. |
| **Focaccia** | Oily, salty bread. Cotto and focaccia is a whole Italian lunch. |
| **Grissini** | Wrap a strip around a breadstick. It looks intentional and takes ten seconds. |
| **Dijon mustard** | The one sharp note it needs. A mild ham with no acid beside it is flat. |
| **Melon** | The prosciutto and melon idea, gentler. Better for anyone who finds the cured version too salty. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and anything strongly funky. Cotto has nothing to
defend itself with.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy it sliced thin at the counter**, not pre-packed.
2. **Fold or ruffle the slices** rather than laying them flat. Flat ham looks like
   a sandwich.
3. **Serve at room temperature** — 20 minutes out of the fridge.
4. **Put mustard next to it.** It needs the acid.
5. **Serve 1.5 to 2 oz per person.** People take more of it than the cured meats.
6. **Keep it away from the strongest cheeses** on the board.
7. **Fix:** tasting watery? That is an industrial ham with added water and starch.
   Look for alta qualità next time; the difference is large.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Prosciutto cotto alta qualità** | Italian delis, good counters | Whole muscle, no starch or added protein. The version worth buying. |
| **Prosciutto cotto alle erbe** | Italian delis | Herb-crusted. Slightly more interesting on a board. |
| **Praga or smoked cotto** | Italian delis | Lightly smoked. A good bridge to the stronger meats. |
| **Jambon de Paris** | French delis | The French equivalent. Slightly saltier, equally good. |
| **Supermarket Italian-style cooked ham** | Any grocery | Cheaper and wetter. Acceptable for a kids' board. |

**Label check:** alta qualità is a legally defined Italian standard and means no
added starch, milk protein or polyphosphates. It is worth looking for.

**Skip:** anything sliced days ago and sweating in a pack. Cotto goes slimy fast.

## Quick FAQ

**What is the difference between prosciutto cotto and prosciutto crudo?**
Cotto is cooked ham — mild, moist and pale pink. Crudo is the dry-cured ham most
people mean by prosciutto, and is salty and intense.

**Is prosciutto cotto just deli ham?**
It is better made. A whole muscle is brined and slowly steamed rather than pressed
from trimmings, so it slices cleanly and tastes of pork.

**Is it good for a kids' board?**
It is the single best meat for one. Mild, sweet, familiar and not salty.

**What cheese goes with it?**
Fontina above all, then fresh mozzarella. Mild with mild, which is the point.

**How much per person?**
An ounce and a half to two ounces. People eat more of it than they do of cured ham.
