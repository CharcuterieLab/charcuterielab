## The short version

A clear, set jelly made with chilli and bell pepper. Its most famous use is the
Southern standard: pour a jar over a block of cream cheese, put crackers beside
it, and walk away. On a proper board it's the sweet-heat element, and it's
cheaper than hot honey.

## What it is

Peppers — jalapeño, habanero, or sweet bell with chilli added — cooked with
sugar, vinegar and pectin into a firm translucent jelly. Green and red versions
are both common; the colour comes from the pepper, not from dye in the good jars.

**Taste:** sweet, tangy, with heat arriving late
**Texture:** firm set jelly, spoonable, holds its shape
**Look:** translucent red or green, flecked with pepper

The mechanism is heat suspended in sugar and acid. Because it's a set jelly rather
than a syrup, it sits in place on a cracker or a slab of cheese instead of running
— which is what makes the cream cheese trick work. Heat and sugar arrive together;
the vinegar keeps it from being cloying.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese** | The Southern classic. Cold bland fat as a platform for sweet heat, with the jelly holding its shape on top. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sweet, with the chilli filling in where the cheese's bite ends. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang, sugar and heat — three distinct things in one bite. |
| **Pimento cheese** | Southern on Southern. Both are already sweet-savoury; the jelly adds the heat. |
| **Water crackers** | A neutral base is essential; the jelly is the whole flavour. |
| **Grilled or fried things** | Its wider habitat. Anything hot and crisp. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses. Sweet heat covers subtlety entirely.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **The classic version:** set a block of cream cheese on a small plate, pour
   the jelly over it, serve with plain crackers.
2. **The board version:** decant into a small bowl with a spoon.
3. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
4. **Break the set** with a fork before serving so it spoons cleanly.
5. **Say it's spicy.** Heat level varies enormously between brands.
6. **Give it a neutral cracker.** Anything seeded competes.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Serve it over cream cheese rather than neat — the fat blunts
   the heat significantly.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Red pepper jelly** | Any grocery, Southern brands | The standard. Usually mild to moderate. |
| **Jalapeño jelly** | Grocery, Southern specialty | Green, brighter, moderately hot. |
| **Habanero pepper jelly** | Specialty, hot sauce shops | Genuinely hot and fruitier. Warn people. |
| **Braswell's or Tabasco pepper jelly** | Grocery, Southern | Reliable widely available brands. |
| **Farmers market pepper jelly** | Farmers markets | Often the best, and usually hotter than commercial. |

**Label check:** peppers and vinegar should be visible in the ingredients. Some
cheap jars are apple jelly with pepper flavour and food colouring.

**Skip:** anything with artificial colour listed.

## Quick FAQ

**What do you eat pepper jelly with?**
Cream cheese is the classic — pour the jar over a block and serve with crackers.
It also works with cheddar and goat cheese.

**Is pepper jelly spicy?**
Mildly, usually. Habanero versions are genuinely hot. Heat varies a lot by brand,
so taste before serving.

**Red or green pepper jelly?**
Red is usually milder and sweeter; green (jalapeño) is brighter and often hotter.
Both work.

**How do I serve it on a board?**
Either over a block of cream cheese with crackers, or broken up with a fork in a
small bowl.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce.
