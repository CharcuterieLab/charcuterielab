## The short version

Peppers charred, peeled and packed in their own juices. They're silky, sweet and
lightly smoky — a rare combination on a board that's mostly salt and fat. A jar
costs two dollars, and the upgrade is buying Spanish **piquillo** peppers, which
are woodfire-roasted and noticeably better.

## What it is

Red bell peppers or piquillos, roasted until the skin blisters, peeled, and
packed in brine, oil or their own juices. Jarred versions are genuinely good —
this is one of the few preserved vegetables that loses nothing to the fresh
version.

**Taste:** sweet, smoky, mildly acidic
**Texture:** silky and soft, no resistance at all
**Look:** deep glossy red strips or whole small peppers

The mechanism is roasted sugar. Charring converts the pepper's sugars and drives
off water, so a roasted pepper is several times sweeter and deeper than a raw
one, with smoke on top. That sweetness sits between a jam and a vegetable — it
adds sugar to a savoury board without reading as dessert.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Sweet and smoky against chalky lactic tang. The most reliable pairing in this whole category. |
| **Manchego** | Spanish on Spanish. Sheep-milk salt against roasted sugar. |
| **Burrata** | Cream, sweetness, smoke and olive oil — a restaurant plate for four dollars. |
| **Hummus and dips** | Chopped over the top. Colour, sweetness and the smoke that hummus lacks. |
| **Romesco** | Peppers in the sauce and peppers beside it. Doubling down works here. |
| **Anchovies** | The Catalan combination. Sweet, salty, oily. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese. Sweet smoke and blue funk don't reconcile.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain well and blot.** They carry a lot of liquid.
2. **Cut into strips** if they're whole, and fan them.
3. **Serve on a plate or in a bowl** — the juices run and stain.
4. **Dress with olive oil, garlic and parsley** for a two-minute upgrade.
5. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
6. **Stuff piquillos** with goat cheese if you want a made dish rather than an
   ingredient.
7. **Fix:** watery and bland? Drain properly, then dress with sherry vinegar and
   oil. Jarred peppers almost always need it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Piquillo peppers** | Spanish delis, Whole Foods | Woodfire-roasted, small, sweet and deep. The best version by far. |
| **Jarred roasted red peppers** | Any grocery | The cheap default. Fine after draining and dressing. |
| **Fire-roasted peppers in oil** | Italian delis, olive bars | Richer and smokier than brined. |
| **Ajvar** | Balkan grocers | Roasted pepper spread. A different form, same flavour. |
| **Home-roasted** | Make it | Char bell peppers over a flame or under a broiler until black, steam in a covered bowl, peel. |

**Label check:** packed in juices or oil rather than water and vinegar. Piquillo
peppers will say so and cost more.

**Skip:** anything pale orange rather than deep red — under-roasted and bland.

## Quick FAQ

**Are jarred roasted red peppers as good as homemade?**
Close, and for a board they're fine. Piquillo peppers in a jar are better than
most people's home-roasted bell peppers.

**What are piquillo peppers?**
Small Spanish peppers roasted over wood fire, then peeled and jarred. Sweeter and
deeper than roasted bell peppers.

**What cheese goes with roasted red peppers?**
Fresh goat cheese above all, plus manchego and burrata. Not blue cheese.

**How do I roast peppers at home?**
Char them whole over a gas flame or under a broiler until the skin is black,
then steam in a covered bowl for ten minutes and peel.

**How much per person?**
About three-quarters of an ounce.
