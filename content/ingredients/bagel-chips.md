## The short version

Bagels sliced thin and baked hard. Because bagel dough is boiled before it's
baked, the crumb is dense and closed, and a bagel chip resists moisture better
than almost anything else on a board. Great with dips and spreads, too loud for
fine cheese.

## What it is

A real bagel is boiled in water before it goes in the oven. That step
gelatinizes the starch on the surface and produces a tight, chewy interior with
very little air in it. Slice that thin and bake it dry and you get a chip with
about twice the density of a cracker.

**Taste:** wheaty, salty, often seasoned
**Texture:** hard, dense, a slight chew at the center
**Look:** small ovals or half-moons, sometimes seeded

Density is the mechanism. Porous crackers wick moisture up from underneath and
soften; a bagel chip has fewer places for that moisture to go. It's the reason
this is the chip that's still crisp in the dip an hour later.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Whipped cream cheese** | The original pairing, in a shape you can scoop with. Cool and fatty against dry and salty. |
| **Smoked salmon spread** | Bagel logic applied directly. Nothing about it is a stretch. |
| **Spinach-artichoke and other hot dips** | Hot dips destroy thin crackers. These hold. |
| **Hummus and white bean dip** | Thick enough to require a real scoop, and this is one. |
| **Chicken and egg salad** | Sturdy enough to carry a chunky salad without folding. |
| **Sharp cheddar and pepper jack** | Assertive cheeses that can meet a seasoned chip halfway. |
| **Beer, bloody marys, brunch drinks** | This is brunch food, and it knows it. |

**Skip it with:** brie, burrata, fresh chèvre and anything expensive. A bagel
chip is thick, salty and often garlicky — it takes over. Also skip it if the
board is already leaning Mediterranean; pita chips do the same job in a way
that fits better.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Build them around a dip, not across the board.** They're big and dark, and
   scattered around they read as clutter.
2. **Serve 4–5 per person**, more if dips are the main event.
3. **Warm them for 4 minutes at 300°F** before serving. They go from fine to
   noticeably good.
4. **Break the big ones in half.** A full chip is more than one bite, and
   double-dipping starts where bites end.
5. **Use the plain ones on a cheese board and the seasoned ones with dips.**
   Everything-seasoned chips fight cheese.
6. **Fix:** they barely stale. An open bag holds for a week, which makes them
   the most forgiving base here.

**Label check:** "baked," a simple oil, and seasoning you can see. Powdered
garlic and onion coatings taste dusty.

**Skip:** cinnamon-raisin and other sweet bagel chips on a savory board. Save
them for a dessert board, where they're actually good.

## Quick FAQ

**Bagel chips or pita chips?**
Bagel chips are denser and hold up longer; pita chips are lighter and fit a
Mediterranean board better. For a hot dip, bagel chips.

**Can I make them from real bagels?**
Yes, and it's the best version. Day-old bagels sliced thin, brushed with oil,
350°F for 12–15 minutes.

**How many per person?**
4–5, or more if the board is dip-focused.

**Do they belong on a cheese board?**
Only the plain ones, and only on a casual board. They overpower delicate
cheeses.
