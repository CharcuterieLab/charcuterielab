## The short version

Green plantains sliced thin and fried. Salty, sturdy and completely free of
gluten, grain, dairy and nuts — the most allergen-friendly scoop you can put on
a board. Just make sure you buy the green savory kind, not the sweet ripe ones.

## What it is

Plantains are a starchy cooking banana. Picked green, they contain almost no
sugar and behave like a root vegetable — slice, fry, salt, and you have a chip
that's closer to a potato chip than to fruit. Picked ripe (*maduros*), the
starch has converted to sugar and the chips come out sweet and caramelized.

**Taste (green):** savory, salty, faintly starchy
**Taste (ripe):** sweet, caramel, banana-forward
**Texture:** thick, hard, substantial crunch
**Look:** pale gold ovals or long strips, sometimes rippled

Structural strength is the mechanism. Plantain chips are among the thickest
things you can put on a board, which makes them the gluten-free answer to
"what do I scoop the dip with?" — a question almond flour crackers and
nut thins both fail.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Guacamole** | The pairing. Salt and crunch against cool fat, and the chip never snaps in it. |
| **Black bean dip and refried beans** | Dense dips need a dense scoop. Nothing here does it better. |
| **Queso fresco and cotija** | Salty crumbly cheese on a salty sturdy base, with lime to cut it. |
| **Chorizo and cured Spanish meats** | Fat and paprika against a plain starchy crunch. |
| **Mango or pineapple salsa** | Acid and sweetness are what a starchy chip needs most. |
| **Crema or sour cream with lime** | Cool and tangy on hot-fried starch. |
| **Beer, margaritas, dry cider** | Anything with bubbles or acid. |

**Skip it with:** brie, chèvre and delicate European cheeses — the chip is
salty, thick and greasy enough to flatten them. And don't put the sweet ripe
version on a savory board by accident; check the bag.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy green, not ripe,** unless you specifically want sweet. Bags say
   "plantain chips" for both — look for "green," "sea salt," or "*maduros*" to
   tell them apart.
2. **Build a dip zone.** Fan them around one bowl rather than scattering them.
3. **Serve 6–8 per person.** People eat these like chips, because they are.
4. **Warm them 4 minutes at 300°F.** They go from fine to genuinely good.
5. **Stand a few upright in the dip** for height — they're rigid enough to hold.
6. **Fix:** they barely stale, and an open bag holds for days. The most
   forgiving base in this tier.

## Quick FAQ

**Are plantain chips gluten-free?**
Yes — plantain, oil and salt. They're also grain-free, dairy-free and nut-free,
which makes them the most allergen-friendly base here.

**What's the difference between plantain chips and banana chips?**
Plantains are a starchy cooking banana, picked green and fried savory. Banana
chips are sweet fruit, usually sugared.

**How many per person?**
6–8. People treat them as chips rather than crackers.

**Can I make them?**
Yes — slice green plantains paper-thin on a mandoline, fry at 350°F for two
minutes, salt immediately. Much better than any bag.

**Are they healthy?**
They're fried, so they behave like a chip nutritionally. The advantage is the
allergen profile, not the calories.
