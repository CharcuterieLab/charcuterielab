## The short version

Salmon buried in salt, sugar and dill for a couple of days, then sliced thin.
No smoke at all — that's the whole difference from smoked salmon. It's brighter,
fresher and sweeter, and it's genuinely the easiest impressive thing you can
make for a board.

## What it is

*Gravlax* means buried salmon. Medieval Scandinavian fishermen salted salmon and
buried it in sand above the tideline to ferment. The modern version skips the
burial: a fillet is packed in equal salt and sugar with a heavy blanket of dill,
weighted, and left in the fridge for 48 to 72 hours while the cure draws out
water.

**Taste:** clean, sweet-salty, dill-forward, fresher than smoked salmon
**Texture:** silky and firm, slightly denser than cold-smoked
**Look:** bright coral-orange, often with a green dill crust on one edge

Osmosis is the mechanism, and it's why this works without heat or smoke. Salt
and sugar draw water out of the flesh and firm the proteins, which both
preserves the fish and changes its texture from raw to sliceable. Dill is
flavour only — the cure does the work.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Hovmästarsås (sweet mustard-dill sauce)** | The traditional Swedish partner, and it's what completes the dish. |
| **Rye crispbread and pumpernickel** | Sour dark bread against sweet-salty fish. |
| **Cucumber and dill** | Cool and clean, echoing the cure. |
| **Crème fraîche or cream cheese** | Cool fat against salt. |
| **Lemon and capers** | Acid and brine, which is what any cured fish needs to stay bright. |
| **Boiled new potatoes** | The Scandinavian service. Warm starch under cold fish. |
| **Aquavit, dry Riesling, Champagne** | Clean spirits or high-acid wine. |

**Skip it with:** strong cured meats and blue cheese, which bury it. It's more
delicate than smoked salmon because there's no smoke to give it weight.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thin on a shallow angle**, away from the dill crust, in long ribbons.
2. **Leave some dill on the edge of the slices** — it's the visual identity.
3. **Drape in loose folds**, not flat stacks.
4. **Temper 15 minutes.** Cool, not cold.
5. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
6. **Make it yourself if you have three days.** Equal parts salt and sugar, a
   large bunch of dill, a skin-on fillet, weighted in the fridge for 48–72
   hours, then rinsed and dried. It costs a third of the shop price and it's
   better.
7. **Fix:** too salty? Rinse the slices briefly and pat dry. Homemade gravlax
   that over-cured can be rescued this way.

isn't gravlax.

things that's dramatically cheaper and better made at home.

## Quick FAQ

**Is gravlax smoked?**
No. That's the entire difference from smoked salmon — gravlax is cured in salt,
sugar and dill only.

**Is it raw?**
Cured rather than cooked. Salt and sugar firm the flesh and preserve it. Use
previously-frozen fish if you're curing at home.

**Can I make it?**
Yes, easily. Equal salt and sugar, lots of dill, weighted in the fridge for two
to three days.

**What's the sauce?**
*Hovmästarsås* — a sweet mustard and dill sauce. It's the traditional partner
and worth making.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.
