## The short version

A firm sheep's-milk cheese from the French Basque country, aged three months to
a year. Buttery, nutty and remarkably easy to like — one of the few cheeses
that's genuinely gentle without being bland. It has a traditional pairing with
black cherry jam that's worth taking literally.

## What it is

Ossau-Iraty AOP is named for the Ossau valley in Béarn and the Iraty forest in
the Basque Country. It's made from raw milk of Manech and Basco-Béarnaise
sheep, in a tradition that goes back thousands of years — shepherds in these
valleys were making sheep cheese long before anyone wrote it down.

**Taste:** buttery, nutty, faintly sweet, gently savory
**Texture:** firm and dense, smooth, supple rather than crumbly
**Look:** ivory to pale gold paste, natural grey-brown rind

Sheep's-milk fat is the mechanism, as it is with Manchego, but Ossau-Iraty is
aged in a damper climate and comes out softer and rounder. Where Manchego goes
firm and slightly crumbly, this stays supple — which makes it one of the easiest
firm cheeses to eat in quantity.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Black cherry jam (*confiture de cerise noire*)** | The traditional Basque pairing, from Itxassou. Dark fruit against nutty sheep's milk. Do this one properly. |
| **Bayonne ham** | The regional cured ham. Salt and cure with buttery nut. |
| **Marcona almonds and walnuts** | Nut on nut, reinforcing. |
| **Quince paste** | If cherry jam isn't available, membrillo does the same job. |
| **Espelette pepper** | The Basque chili. A light dusting is traditional and excellent. |
| **Fresh pear** | Cool sweet water against dense fat. |
| **Irouléguy, Txakoli, Rioja** | Basque and Pyrenean wines. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses in the same corner, and very sharp
condiments — this is a gentle cheese and mustard or pickle overwhelms it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut into thin wedges or batons** with the rind on the outer edge.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** It's mild enough that people eat more of it
   than they expect.
4. **Put black cherry jam beside it.** This is a specific traditional pairing
   and it's better than the generic honey-with-everything approach.
5. **Use it as the approachable cheese** on a board that also has something
   challenging — it's the one that keeps cautious guests fed.
6. **Trim the rind.**
7. **Fix:** if it's tasting flat, it's cold or it's next to something too
   strong. Move it away from the blue.

| **Abbaye de Belloc** | Cheese counters, specialty | Monastery-made Basque sheep cheese. Excellent. |
| **Idiazábal** | Cheese counters | The Spanish Basque cousin, often smoked — see its own page. |
| **Manchego** | Most grocery | The widely available Spanish sheep cheese — see its own page. |

**Label check:** the AOP mark and the age. Longer-aged wheels are firmer and
nuttier; younger ones are softer and milkier.

**Skip:** pairing it with anything aggressive. Its whole quality is
gentleness, and a strong partner wastes it.

## Quick FAQ

**Is Ossau-Iraty like Manchego?**
Same idea — firm sheep's-milk cheese — but softer, butterier and less crumbly.
Ossau-Iraty is the gentler of the two.

**What's the traditional pairing?**
Black cherry jam from Itxassou. It's specific, regional, and much better than
a generic honey drizzle.

**Can you eat the rind?**
No, trim it.

**Is it strong?**
No. It's one of the most approachable aged cheeses there is.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
