## The short version

Pepper on a cheese board isn't seasoning, it's an aromatic. A few coarse cracks
over burrata or fresh strawberries adds a floral, piney heat that no other
ingredient here supplies. The rule is coarse and freshly cracked — pre-ground
pepper is dust and tastes of nothing.

## What it is

Dried unripe peppercorns, cracked coarsely. **Tellicherry** is the large,
late-picked Indian grade and the one worth buying. Sarawak, Kampot and Malabar are
the other names to know. Freshly cracked pepper retains volatile oils that
disappear within days of grinding.

**Taste:** hot, floral, piney, faintly citrusy
**Texture:** coarse gritty fragments
**Look:** black and grey flecks, visible on pale surfaces

The mechanism is piperine plus volatile aromatics. The heat comes from piperine,
which is slower and rounder than chilli heat; the aroma comes from terpenes that
evaporate quickly once the peppercorn is broken. That's why fresh cracking matters
so much more with pepper than with almost any other spice.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | The standard finish. Oil, flaky salt and coarse pepper on cream — three things that turn an ingredient into a dish. |
| **Pecorino romano** | *Cacio e pepe* is the whole argument. Sheep-milk salt and pepper are a fixed pairing. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Very mild cheese that needs aroma more than it needs flavour. |
| **Berries and strawberries** | Pepper on strawberries sounds wrong and is excellent — the aromatics amplify the fruit's own. |
| **Olive oil** | Pepper and good oil share the same peppery register; they reinforce each other. |
| **Fresh figs and honey** | Pepper cuts sweetness and adds a savoury edge. |

**Skip it with:** already spiced things and heavily seasoned cured meat.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Crack it coarse**, in a mill set as wide as it goes, or under the flat of a
   knife.
2. **Grind it fresh**, right over the food. Aroma lasts minutes, not days.
3. **Put the mill on the table** so guests can add their own.
4. **Use it on the pale things** — burrata, mozzarella, ricotta, fruit — where it
   shows.
5. **Add it last**, after the oil and salt.
6. **Buy Tellicherry** if you're buying anything. It's a small premium.
7. **Fix:** tastes like dust? That's pre-ground pepper. There's no fixing it —
   buy whole peppercorns.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tellicherry peppercorns** | Grocery, spice shops | Large, late-picked, aromatic. The best everyday choice. |
| **Kampot pepper** | Specialty, online | Cambodian, floral and complex. Expensive and remarkable. |
| **Sarawak or Malabar** | Spice shops | Good mid-range whole peppercorns. |
| **Pink peppercorns** | Specialty | Not true pepper. Sweet, floral, fragile. Beautiful on fresh cheese. |
| **A decent pepper mill** | Anywhere | The actual purchase that matters. It should crack coarse, not grind fine. |

**Label check:** buy whole peppercorns, always. Check the mill can be set coarse —
many cheap ones only grind fine.

**Skip:** pre-ground pepper in a tin, and disposable grinders with tiny
peppercorns.

## Quick FAQ

**Why use freshly cracked pepper?**
The aromatic compounds evaporate within days of grinding. Pre-ground pepper has
heat but almost no aroma.

**What is Tellicherry pepper?**
A grade of Indian black pepper picked late, so the berries are larger and more
aromatic. The best value upgrade in this category.

**What do you put pepper on for a cheese board?**
Burrata, fresh mozzarella, ricotta, pecorino, and — surprisingly — strawberries
and figs.

**Coarse or fine?**
Coarse. Fine pepper disappears into the food; coarse cracks give distinct bursts
of aroma and heat.

**Is pepper on fruit really good?**
Yes. Pepper's floral aromatics amplify the fruit's own. Try it on strawberries
with a little balsamic.
