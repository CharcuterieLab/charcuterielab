## The short version

Smoked almonds do something no other cheap ingredient does: they add smoke and
savoury depth to a board without meat. On a vegetarian board that's genuinely
useful — smoke is what people miss when the salami is gone. Most supermarket
brands use smoke flavouring rather than actual smoke, and honestly, it works.

## What it is

Almonds roasted with smoke flavouring, salt and usually a little sugar or
maltodextrin. Genuinely cold-smoked almonds exist from specialty producers and
taste noticeably cleaner, but the mass-market version is the one most people
know and it's good.

**Taste:** smoky, salty, faintly sweet, bacon-adjacent
**Texture:** hard and crisp, with a fine coating
**Look:** dark brown, matte, slightly dusty

The mechanism is smoke as a savoury shortcut. Smoke compounds read to the palate
as "cooked meat", which is why smoked almonds anchor a board the way salami does.
They also carry enough flavour to stand up to strong cheese, where a plain almond
would just be crunch.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Smoke and sharp are the flavour base of every good ploughman's plate. |
| **Aged gouda** | Both are caramel-and-smoke; they read as one deep flavour rather than two. |
| **Brie** | Smoke against soft fat — the contrast the cheese needs when there's no meat on the board. |
| **Apples** | Sweet crisp fruit is the standard counterweight to smoke. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue** | Salt and smoke against salt and funk. Aggressive, and it works. |
| **Beer boards** | Smoked almonds and a stout is a real pairing, not just a coincidence. |

**Skip it with:** burrata, fresh ricotta, mozzarella and other delicate fresh
cheeses. Smoke obliterates them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the tin.
2. **Serve in a small bowl.** The coating rubs off onto cheese and crackers and
   leaves brown dust.
3. **Use them as the savoury element** on a vegetarian board, where the meat
   would otherwise be.
4. **Serve 0.5–1 oz per person** — less than plain almonds, because they're
   stronger.
5. **Keep them away from delicate cheeses** physically, not just conceptually.
   Smoke transfers.
6. **Pick one strong nut**, not three. Smoked almonds plus spiced nuts plus
   candied pecans is chaos.
7. **Fix:** too salty? Blue Diamond's lightly salted version exists, or make your
   own by tossing raw almonds with smoked paprika and oil, then roasting.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue Diamond smokehouse almonds** | Any grocery | The standard. Cheap, consistent, and genuinely good. |
| **Trader Joe's smoked almonds** | Trader Joe's | Slightly less sweet than Blue Diamond. |
| **Cold-smoked almonds** | Specialty, farmers markets, online | Real smoke rather than flavouring. Cleaner and more expensive. |
| **Smoked paprika almonds** | Spanish delis, Whole Foods | Pimentón rather than smoke. Different and excellent with manchego. |
| **Smoked marcona almonds** | Cheese shops, specialty | The luxury version — soft, oily, smoky. |

**Label check:** "natural smoke flavour" is the mass-market approach and it's
fine. Cold-smoked will say so and cost more.

**Skip:** honey-smoked versions on a cheese board — they belong in the dessert
corner if anywhere.

## Quick FAQ

**Are smoked almonds actually smoked?**
Usually not — most mass-market versions use smoke flavouring. Cold-smoked
almonds from specialty producers are the real thing and taste cleaner.

**Why put smoked almonds on a board?**
They add smoke and savoury depth, which is what a vegetarian board is otherwise
missing.

**What cheese goes with smoked almonds?**
Aged cheddar, aged gouda, brie, and blue cheese. Avoid fresh cheeses like
burrata and ricotta.

**Should they go in a bowl?**
Yes. The smoky coating rubs off onto crackers and cheese and leaves brown dust.

**How much per person?**
Half to one ounce — less than plain almonds, since they're much stronger.
