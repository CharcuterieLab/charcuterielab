## The short version

A small Burgundian cheese washed repeatedly in *marc de Bourgogne* until the
rind turns sticky orange and the interior goes almost liquid. It smells
enormous and tastes rich, meaty and surprisingly sweet. Serve it with a spoon.

## What it is

Époisses AOP comes from a village in Burgundy and was reportedly Napoleon's
favorite cheese. During aging the rind is washed with increasingly strong
solutions of brine and marc — the pomace brandy of the region — over five to
eight weeks. That encourages *Brevibacterium linens*, the bacterium behind the
smell, and breaks the paste down until it's spoonable.

**Taste:** rich, meaty, savory, faintly sweet, less aggressive than the aroma
**Texture:** soft to genuinely liquid at room temperature
**Look:** small round in a wooden box, sticky orange-red rind, pale runny paste

Alcohol washing is the mechanism. Marc both feeds the surface bacteria and
keeps competing molds off, so *B. linens* colonizes the rind unchallenged. Its
enzymes then dissolve the paste from the outside in, which is why a ripe
Époisses collapses when you cut it and why the box is structural rather than
decorative.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette** | Plain bread and a spoon. This cheese needs a vehicle, not a partner. |
| **Marc de Bourgogne or Burgundy** | The regional pairing. Alcohol against fat and funk. |
| **Cornichons** | Sharp vinegar as a reset between rich bites. |
| **Boiled potatoes** | Spoon it over them warm. A genuine Burgundian dish. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin against a very rich paste. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness rounding the sulphur notes, as with any washed-rind. |
| **Champagne or Chablis** | Acid and bubbles against saturated fat. |

**Skip it with:** everything delicate, everything aged and hard, and anything
you want to taste afterwards. Époisses colonizes a board — and a refrigerator.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper a full hour.** Cold Époisses is firm and closed. At room
   temperature it should be visibly slumping.
2. **Serve it in its box.** Cut the top off the rind and let people spoon it
   out. The wooden box is holding the cheese together.
3. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** One small round serves four to six.
4. **Put it on its own plate, off the main board.** The aroma travels and the
   paste runs.
5. **Give it a small spoon**, not a knife.
6. **Warn people, then tell them it tastes milder than it smells** — which it
   does, considerably.
7. **Fix:** double-wrap it in the fridge and put it in a sealed container.
   Otherwise everything you own will smell of it within a day.

**Label check:** the AOP mark and a box. Époisses is always sold in a wooden
box; a washed-rind cheese without one is something else.

**Skip:** buying it more than two or three days ahead, and buying it at all for
a crowd you don't know. It's genuinely divisive.

## Quick FAQ

**Is Époisses really banned on French public transport?**
It's widely repeated and not actually a law. It is, however, a cheese that
would justify one.

**How strong is it?**
The aroma is among the strongest of any cheese. The taste is much milder than
the smell — rich, meaty and faintly sweet.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, though most people spoon the paste out and leave it.

**How do I serve it?**
In its box, top cut off, with a spoon and plain bread.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. One small round serves four to six.
