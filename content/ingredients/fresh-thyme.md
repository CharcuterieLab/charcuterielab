## The short version

Where rosemary is structural, thyme is edible. The leaves are small and soft
enough to scatter over goat cheese or into a pot of honey, and unlike rosemary
people will actually eat them. It's the herb that makes a plain cheese look
finished.

## What it is

Small soft leaves on thin woody stems. Common thyme is the standard; **lemon
thyme** is citrusy and excellent with fresh cheese. The leaves strip off the stem
by running your fingers down it against the direction of growth.

**Taste:** earthy, faintly floral, mildly peppery, less resinous than rosemary
**Texture:** tiny soft leaves, edible raw
**Look:** small grey-green leaves, sometimes with pale flowers

The mechanism is scale. Rosemary needles are too big and too tough to eat raw;
thyme leaves are small enough to disappear into a bite while still carrying aroma.
That means it's the one fresh herb that can be a flavour ingredient on a cold
board rather than only a decoration.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Scatter over a log with olive oil and pepper. Earthy herb against lactic tang, and it looks deliberate. |
| **Honey** | Drop a sprig into the pot. Thyme honey is a product in its own right, and it takes twenty minutes to imitate. |
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Bloomy rinds carry earthy mushroom notes that thyme extends. |
| **Marinated mushrooms** | Thyme and mushroom is a fixed pairing in cooking, and it holds cold. |
| **Baguette and warm bread** | Scattered with oil and salt on warm bread. |
| **Roasted vegetables and peppers** | Standard, and useful if the board leans vegetable. |

**Skip it with:** strongly spiced things. Thyme is subtle and gets buried.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Strip the leaves** by running your fingers down the stem against the growth
   direction.
2. **Scatter the leaves**, don't lay the stems — that's rosemary's job.
3. **Leave a few whole sprigs** for the honey pot or tucked beside a cheese.
4. **Rinse and dry well.** Wet herbs bead on cheese.
5. **Add it last.** The aroma fades within the hour.
6. **Use lemon thyme** with fresh goat cheese if you can find it — it's a real
   upgrade.
7. **Fix:** stems too woody to strip? Those are old. The stems should be flexible.

| **Fresh rosemary** | Grocery | The structural counterpart. Its own page. |

**Label check:** leaves should be firmly attached and the stems flexible. A
clamshell with loose leaves rattling at the bottom is old.

**Skip:** dried thyme for board use. It looks like dust and tastes like it.

## Quick FAQ

**Can you eat fresh thyme raw?**
Yes — the leaves are small and soft enough to eat, unlike rosemary needles.

**How do I strip thyme leaves?**
Hold the top of the stem and run your fingers down it against the direction the
leaves grow. They come off in one motion.

**What do I scatter thyme on?**
Fresh goat cheese, brie, marinated mushrooms, and warm bread with oil. Drop a
sprig into a honey pot too.

**Thyme or rosemary for a board?**
Both. Rosemary fills gaps and provides aroma; thyme is edible and gets scattered
over the food.

**What is lemon thyme?**
A citrusy variety. Noticeably better than common thyme with fresh cheese and
honey.
