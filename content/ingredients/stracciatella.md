## The short version

Shredded fresh mozzarella curd stirred into cream — the filling that makes
burrata burrata, sold on its own in a tub. It spoons, it spreads, and it
portions cleanly, which makes it the practical choice when burrata's one-cut
drama isn't worth the mess.

## What it is

*Stracciatella* means "little shreds." Fresh mozzarella curd is torn into
strands by hand and folded into heavy cream. It's from Puglia, like burrata,
and it's the same filling — burrata is just stracciatella inside a mozzarella
pouch.

**Taste:** milky, sweet, rich, very lightly salted
**Texture:** loose and stringy in thick cream, like torn silk
**Look:** white, glossy, visibly shredded

Fat plus fiber is the mechanism, and it's why stracciatella reads as more
luxurious than plain cream. The mozzarella strands give it structure and a
faint chew, so it isn't just cream on a spoon — there's something to catch on
the tongue, which is what separates it from mascarpone or crème fraîche.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Good olive oil, flaky salt, black pepper** | It arrives underseasoned by design. This is the finishing, not a pairing. |
| **Ripe tomato** | Acid and sweetness against milk fat. The Pugliese default. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and cure over sweet cream. |
| **Ciabatta or grilled bread** | Something to catch it. It won't sit on a thin cracker. |
| **Peaches, figs, nectarines** | Ripe summer fruit with unsalted cream. |
| **Aged balsamic** | Sharp syrup cutting fat. A few drops. |
| **Prosecco, Vermentino, dry rosé** | Cold, light, high-acid. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and other strong cheeses, and loud bases like
everything crackers. Like burrata, it's delicate on flavor as well as texture.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy it fresh and serve it the same day.** Same rule as burrata — a few
   days is the whole window.
2. **Temper 20 minutes** so the cream loosens.
3. **Spoon it into a shallow bowl on the board**, never directly on wood. It
   runs.
4. **Season after plating:** olive oil, flaky salt, cracked pepper. Unseasoned
   stracciatella tastes like nothing.
5. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** An 8 oz tub covers four to six.
6. **Put bread and crostini directly beside it.** It's a spoonable cheese and
   it needs a vehicle.
7. **Fix:** separated or watery? Stir it gently back together and drain any
   loose liquid. Don't whisk — you'll break the strands.

| **Italian deli stracciatella** | Italian markets, cheese counters | Usually made or delivered fresh. Noticeably better. |
| **Maplebrook Farm** | Whole Foods, natural grocers | Vermont-made, well regarded. |
| **Imported Pugliese stracciatella** | Eataly, specialty grocers | The real thing when the date is fresh. |
| **Burrata** | Grocery cheese case | Same filling, plus the pouch and the reveal. See its own page. |
| **Whipped ricotta** | Any grocery | The cheap substitute. Less rich, holds much longer. |

**Label check:** the date, and nothing else. Stracciatella has no aging to hide
behind — freshness is the entire quality signal.

**Skip:** anything with a long shelf life. Stabilized stracciatella loses the
loose, stringy texture that is the whole point.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between stracciatella and burrata?**
Burrata is stracciatella sealed inside a mozzarella pouch. Same filling, minus
the skin and the cutting moment.

**Isn't stracciatella a soup? Or a gelato?**
All three exist. *Stracciatella alla romana* is an egg-drop soup, and
stracciatella gelato is chocolate-shredded. Same word, different foods.

**How long does it keep?**
Two to four days. Buy it the day you serve it.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz. An 8 oz tub serves four to six.

**Does it need seasoning?**
Yes — olive oil, salt and pepper after plating. It's deliberately unsalted.
