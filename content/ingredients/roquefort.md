## The short version

A sheep's-milk blue from the south of France, aged in natural limestone caves.
Sharp, salty, wet and intensely aromatic — noticeably stronger than Stilton or
Gorgonzola. It needs something sweet beside it, and it needs its own corner.

## What it is

Roquefort AOP is made only from raw Lacaune sheep's milk and must be aged in
the caves of Combalou at Roquefort-sur-Soulzon. Natural fissures in the rock —
*fleurines* — draw humid air through the caves, and *Penicillium roqueforti*
originally grew there naturally. It's one of the oldest protected foods in
Europe, with a charter from 1411.

**Taste:** sharp, very salty, tangy, intensely blue, lingering
**Texture:** moist, crumbly and creamy at once, almost wet
**Look:** stark white paste heavily marbled with blue-green, no rind

Sheep's milk plus salt is the mechanism, and it explains the intensity. Sheep's
milk is roughly twice as fat and protein dense as cow's milk, so there's more
substrate for the mold to work on — and Roquefort is heavily salted, which the
sheep's-milk fat carries directly to the tongue. The result is a blue with
about twice the impact of a cow's-milk one.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Sauternes** | The great pairing. Botrytised sweet wine against extreme salt — sugar is the only thing that balances this cheese. |
| **Walnut raisin bread** | Sweet raisin, tannic walnut, salt. As good here as with any blue. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness, straightforwardly. |
| **Fresh pear and fig** | Cool sweet water against sharp salt. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin meeting blue bitterness. |
| **Endive leaves** | Bitter and crisp — the classic French salad pairing, taken apart. |
| **Port, Banyuls, Monbazillac** | Any sweet fortified wine works on the same principle. |

**Skip it with:** everything delicate, and any other strong aroma — smoked
cheese, washed-rind cheese, garlicky spreads. Roquefort takes over a board
unless it's isolated.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve less than you think** — three-quarters of an ounce per person. It's
   the most intense cheese most guests will encounter.
2. **Temper 45 minutes.** Cold Roquefort is pure salt.
3. **Cut with a wire or floss.** It's wet and crumbly and a blade smears it.
4. **Give it its own knife, its own corner, ideally its own small plate.**
5. **Put something sweet directly beside it** — honey, fig jam, or sweet wine.
   Without it, the cheese dominates everything.
6. **Serve it last in the tasting order** if you're sequencing. Nothing tastes
   the same after it.
7. **Fix:** wrap in foil, not plastic. Roquefort is traditionally sold in foil
   for a reason — it stops the cheese drying while letting it breathe.

**Label check:** the AOP mark and the red sheep logo. Roquefort is always
sheep's milk and always cave-aged; anything else is a different blue.

**Skip:** buying it for a crowd you don't know. It's genuinely intense and
about a third of people won't eat it.

## Quick FAQ

**Is Roquefort stronger than Stilton?**
Yes, considerably. Sheep's milk and heavy salting make it roughly twice as
intense.

**Why is it wrapped in foil?**
Roquefort has no rind, and foil keeps it from drying while allowing some
breathing. Store it that way at home too.

**What wine goes with Roquefort?**
Sauternes. The sweet-against-salt pairing is one of the most famous in food, and
it works.

**How much per person?**
Under an ounce. Less than any other cheese on a board.

**Is it made from sheep's milk?**
Yes, always — raw Lacaune sheep's milk, by regulation.
