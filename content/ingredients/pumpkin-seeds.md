## The short version

The nut-free board's crunch. Pepitas — hulled green pumpkin seeds — cost almost
nothing, toast in three minutes, and add both texture and colour. They're also the
right scatter for a goat cheese log when someone at the table has a nut allergy.

## What it is

Hulled seeds from specific pumpkin varieties (the shell-less Styrian types), sold
raw or roasted, salted or plain. **Pepitas** are the green hulled kind; whole
pumpkin seeds still in their white shell are a different, chewier thing.

**Taste:** mild, nutty, faintly grassy, salty when roasted
**Texture:** crisp and flat, snaps between the teeth
**Look:** flat oval seeds, deep green

The mechanism is crunch without an allergen. Nut allergy is the most common
constraint a board has to work around, and removing nuts usually removes the
board's only crisp element. A pepita replaces that exactly — same job, same size,
none of the risk.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a log in toasted pepitas. Green against white, crisp against chalky, and completely nut-free. |
| **Manchego** | Salt and sheep milk against grassy seed. Both Spanish-adjacent. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against mild; the seed provides crunch without competing. |
| **Feta** | Brine and salt against toasted green. This is a salad topping doing board duty. |
| **Dried cranberries** | Red and green, tart and toasty. The standard trail-mix pairing, and it looks good. |
| **Chilli-lime salt** | Pepitas take Tajín better than any nut does. |

**Skip it with:** nothing in particular — pepitas are mild and get along with
everything.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Toast in a dry pan** over medium heat, 3–4 minutes, until they puff and pop.
   Keep them moving.
2. **Salt while hot.** They take salt best straight out of the pan.
3. **Add spice if you want** — smoked paprika, chilli, cumin all work.
4. **Scatter over cheese** or serve in a small dish.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
6. **Say they're nut-free.** The whole reason they're there is the guest who
   needs to know.
7. **Fix:** chewy rather than crisp? Not toasted enough. Back in the pan until
   they audibly pop.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw pepitas** | Any grocery, bulk bins, Trader Joe's | Buy raw and toast them. Cheapest and best. |
| **Roasted salted pepitas** | Grocery, Trader Joe's | Ready to go. Toast them again briefly to wake them up. |
| **Styrian pumpkin seeds** | Specialty, Austrian and German markets | Darker, oilier, much more intense. Excellent. |
| **Chilli-lime pepitas** | Trader Joe's, Mexican markets | Ready-spiced and very good. |
| **Whole in-shell pumpkin seeds** | Grocery, autumn | Chewier and more work. A different ingredient. |

**Label check:** pepitas should be bright green and smell clean. Dull, greyish
seeds are old and will taste of oil.

**Skip:** anything that smells faintly of paint — that's rancidity.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between pepitas and pumpkin seeds?**
Pepitas are the hulled green seeds from shell-less pumpkin varieties. Whole
pumpkin seeds still have their white shell and are chewier.

**Are pumpkin seeds nut-free?**
Yes — they're a seed, not a tree nut, and they're the standard substitute on
nut-free boards.

**Do I need to toast them?**
Yes, for a board. Three to four minutes in a dry pan until they pop, salted hot.

**What cheese goes with pumpkin seeds?**
Fresh goat cheese above all, plus manchego, feta, and aged cheddar.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce — they're a scatter and a crunch element, not a serving.
