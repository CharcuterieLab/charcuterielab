## The short version

Green is the argument. Boards run cream, tan and brown, and a pistachio is the
only nut that breaks that. Decide early between in-shell (better looking, but
guests need somewhere to put the shells) and shelled (easier, and the only option
if you're scattering or coating anything).

## What it is

The seed of a small tree in the cashew family, sold in-shell (roasted and
salted, split open) or shelled. Iranian and Sicilian pistachios are greener and
more intense; Californian are larger, paler and milder.

**Taste:** sweet, resinous, faintly floral, salty
**Texture:** firm and slightly waxy, crisp rather than hard
**Look:** pale green kernels with a purple-tinged skin

The mechanism is colour plus a distinctive resinous aroma that pairs with fruit
rather than fighting it. Most nuts are savoury supporting players; a pistachio has
enough of its own perfume to work as a flavour in its own right, which is why it
appears in desserts, in mortadella, and rolled onto goat cheese.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a log in chopped pistachios. Green against white, crunch against chalk, and the tang cuts the nut's oil. |
| **Medjool dates** | Push one into a pitted date. Caramel, salt, crunch — the standard Middle Eastern bite. |
| **Blood oranges** | Sicilian pairing: green nut, red citrus, salt, oil. Also the best-looking plate you can build. |
| **Mortadella** | Pistachio is already in the meat. Serving it alongside amplifies what's there. |
| **Manchego** | Salt and sheep milk against resinous sweetness. |
| **Honey and ricotta** | The dessert corner. Chopped pistachios finish it. |

**Skip it with:** heavily smoked things. Smoke covers the pistachio's aroma
entirely, which wastes the ingredient.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Pick one format.** In-shell for a board people graze at; shelled for a board
   that's arranged and photographed.
2. **Put out a small empty bowl for shells** if you go in-shell. People will
   otherwise leave them on the board.
3. **Chop shelled pistachios** for rolling a goat cheese log or scattering over
   ricotta.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
5. **Buy roasted and salted** — raw pistachios are soft and bland.
6. **Rub off the papery skins** if you want maximum green; it's fiddly but real.
7. **Fix:** soft? 300°F for eight minutes. Pistachios go soft faster than most
   nuts because of the shell's humidity.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Roasted salted in-shell pistachios** | Any grocery, Costco | The default for grazing boards. Cheapest per ounce. |
| **Shelled roasted pistachios** | Trader Joe's, Whole Foods | Twice the price, half the work. Necessary for coating and scattering. |
| **Sicilian Bronte pistachios** | Italian delis, online | Intensely green and expensive. The best there is; use them where they show. |
| **Iranian pistachios** | Middle Eastern markets | Greener and more intense than Californian, at a moderate premium. |
| **Raw shelled pistachios** | Health stores, baking aisle | For coating cheese logs where you don't want extra salt. |

**Label check:** avoid dyed red pistachios — a mid-century trick to hide staining,
and they'll dye your fingers and the cheese.

**Skip:** anything where the shells are mostly closed; those kernels are
underdeveloped.

## Quick FAQ

**Shelled or in-shell pistachios for a board?**
In-shell look better and cost less, but guests need a bowl for the shells.
Shelled are essential if you're scattering or coating anything.

**Why are some pistachios greener?**
Variety and origin. Sicilian Bronte and Iranian pistachios are much greener than
Californian.

**What goes with pistachios?**
Fresh goat cheese, dates, blood oranges, mortadella, and manchego.

**Are red pistachios different?**
Only in dye. It's an old practice to mask shell staining, and the colour comes
off on your fingers.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce shelled, more in-shell since half the weight is shell.
