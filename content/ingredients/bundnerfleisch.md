## The short version

Air-dried beef from the Swiss canton of Graubünden, pressed flat during curing
and sliced almost transparent. Drier and more mineral than bresaola, with a
distinctive rectangular block shape. Serve it very thin and eat it quickly.

## What it is

Bündnerfleisch IGP is made from lean beef round, salted and seasoned with alpine
herbs, then dried in the cold dry air of the high Grisons valleys for several
months. It's pressed repeatedly during drying, which flattens the muscle into a
brick and drives out moisture evenly.

**Taste:** concentrated beef, mineral, herbal, moderately salty
**Texture:** very dry and firm, almost brittle at the edge
**Look:** dark red-brown rectangular block, marbled with fine white sinew lines

Altitude and cold are the mechanism. Grisons valleys sit high, dry and cold,
which draws moisture out slowly without ever letting the meat warm enough to
spoil. That produces a drier, denser result than Italian bresaola gets in the
milder Valtellina — more concentrated, and more fragile once cut.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gruyère or Emmental** | Swiss beef with Swiss cheese. The mountain plate. |
| **Rye crispbread and dark bread** | Dry and sour against dry and mineral. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | Acid against concentrated salt. |
| **Olive oil and cracked pepper** | Same treatment as bresaola — it needs the fat. |
| **Butter on dark bread** | The Swiss service, and it supplies what lean beef lacks. |
| **Apple and pear** | Cold sweet water against dry salt. |
| **Chasselas, Pinot Noir, Alpine whites** | Swiss wines, high-acid. |

**Skip it with:** smoked cheese and blue cheese, both of which bury it. It's
lean and delicate despite the intensity.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice as thin as your knife or the counter's machine allows.** Thick
   Bündnerfleisch is genuinely tough — this is not a cut you can be casual with.
2. **Lay slices flat and overlapping**, and serve within fifteen minutes. Lean
   dried beef curls fast.
3. **Dress it lightly** with olive oil and pepper, exactly as with bresaola.
4. **Temper only 15 minutes.** No fat to soften.
5. **Serve three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce per person.**
6. **Buy it cut to order** if you possibly can. Pre-sliced dries in the tray.
7. **Fix:** curling at the edges? A brush of olive oil recovers the surface.

**Label check:** the IGP mark and Graubünden or Grisons origin. It should be a
dense rectangular block, not a round.

**Skip:** buying it pre-sliced if the edges look dark and curled in the pack.
Lean dried beef shows its age immediately.

## Quick FAQ

**How is it different from bresaola?**
Bündnerfleisch is Swiss, drier, more concentrated and pressed into a rectangle.
Bresaola is Italian, softer and rounder. Same idea, different climate.

**Why is it rectangular?**
It's pressed repeatedly during drying, which flattens the muscle and drives
moisture out evenly.

**Does it need dressing?**
It benefits from olive oil and pepper, like bresaola. Lean cured beef has no fat
of its own.

**How much per person?**
Three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce. It's dense and intense.

**Why does it curl so fast?**
Almost no fat to protect the cut surface. Slice it late and serve it quickly.
