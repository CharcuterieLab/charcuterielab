## The short version

Double Gloucester with dried onion and chives mixed through it. Bright orange,
mild, savory and studded with green flecks. It's a pub cheese in the best
sense, and one of the few flavored cheeses that improves on its base rather
than hiding it.

## What it is

Double Gloucester is a firm English cow's-milk cheese, colored orange with
annatto and milder than cheddar. Cotswold is that cheese with dried onion and
chive worked into the curd before pressing. It's a modern product — 20th
century — but it sits in a long English tradition of blending herbs into hard
cheese.

**Taste:** mild, buttery, savory, oniony, gently tangy
**Texture:** firm but supple, smooth, slices cleanly
**Look:** vivid orange paste with green flecks throughout

Onion in the curd is the mechanism, and it's the reason this works where many
flavored cheeses don't. Dried onion is savory rather than sweet or perfumed, so
it deepens the cheese's own dairy savouriness instead of covering it. The base
is also deliberately mild — a flavored aged cheddar would be two strong things
fighting.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream crackers and water crackers** | Plain English bases under an already-seasoned cheese. |
| **Grainy mustard and chutney** | The Ploughman's plate. Sharp and sweet against savory onion. |
| **Pickled onions and cornichons** | Acid against a mild fatty cheese. Traditional and correct. |
| **Ham and roast beef** | The sandwich pairing, and it works on a casual board. |
| **Apple and grapes** | Fresh acid cutting the fat. |
| **Celery** | Crunch and bitterness. Very English. |
| **Ale, bitter, dry cider** | Pub drinking with pub cheese. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses, which pick up the onion, and Boursin
or any other allium-heavy item — one oniony cheese per board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut into thick batons or cubes.** It's firm and holds a clean shape, and
   the green flecks show best in a thick piece.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** It's mild and people eat plenty.
4. **Use it as the colour anchor** on an English board — the orange is stronger
   than anything else in the case except Mimolette.
5. **Put it beside the chutney and the pickled onions.**
6. **Give it its own knife.** Onion transfers.
7. **Fix:** if it's drying at the face, trim thinly. Flavored hard cheeses dry
   faster than plain ones.

**Label check:** visible green flecks distributed through the paste rather than
concentrated in streaks. Even distribution means it was worked into the curd
properly.

**Skip:** buying it alongside Boursin or another garlic-and-herb cheese.
They occupy the same slot and neither benefits.

## Quick FAQ

**What is Cotswold cheese?**
Double Gloucester — a mild orange English cheese — with dried onion and chives
worked through it.

**Is it strong?**
No. Mild and savory, with the onion adding depth rather than heat.

**Why is it orange?**
Annatto, added to the double Gloucester base. It's traditional and adds no
flavor.

**What's Huntsman?**
Double Gloucester layered with Stilton. Same family, and a striking cheese on a
board.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
