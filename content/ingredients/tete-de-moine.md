## The short version

A semi-hard Swiss cheese from the Jura mountains that's never sliced. It's
scraped with a girolle, a spinning blade on a spike, into thin ruffled
rosettes that look like flowers. It's the easiest way to put something that
looks hand-crafted on a board, and you can buy the rosettes ready-made.

## What it is

Tête de Moine AOP ("monk's head") has been made in the Bernese Jura since
monks at Bellelay Abbey started it over 800 years ago. It's a raw cow's milk
cheese, washed with brine during aging and aged at least 75 days. It comes as
a small drum that sits on the girolle's spike.

**Taste:** nutty, savory and fruity, with a strong, earthy aroma
**Texture:** firm, but the thin rosettes melt on the tongue
**Look:** pale gold ruffled rosettes, shaped like carnations

Shaving is the mechanism. A thin rosette exposes far more surface to the air
than a slice, so aroma compounds escape at once and reach the nose as you
eat. The result is a cheese that smells strong but tastes light. Physicists
have even studied the ruffles: the cheese is firmer near the rind than at the
center, so the blade drags unevenly and the shaving wrinkles as it curls.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Grapes** | Cold juicy sweetness against a salty, nutty cheese. |
| **Pears** | Soft and floral, a mild match for a strong aroma. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin and crunch echo the nutty notes. |
| **Rye crispbread** | Earthy and crisp, an Alpine-style base. |
| **Dried figs** | Dense fruit sugar to balance the salt. |
| **Dry white wine like Chasselas or a Pinot Gris** | Crisp acid cleans up after butterfat. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese next to it. Both are aromatic and they fight.
Let the rosettes be the one showpiece cheese.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Shave it cold.** Straight from the fridge, it curls into clean rosettes.
   Warm cheese tears.
2. **Trim the top rind**, push the drum onto the girolle spike, and turn the
   blade with light pressure.
3. **Serve 3 to 4 rosettes per person.** They're light but strongly
   flavored.
4. **Place them in a cluster or a line** like a row of flowers. They're the
   prettiest thing on the board.
5. **Shave just before guests arrive.** Rosettes dry out and flatten within a
   couple of hours.
6. **Fix:** store the cut drum with plastic wrap pressed on the top and the
   girolle dome on. The exposed face dries fast.

## Quick FAQ

**What is a girolle?**
A round board with a center spike and a rotating blade. The cheese sits on
the spike and the blade scrapes thin rosettes off the top as you turn it.

**Can I buy Tête de Moine already shaved?**
Yes. Costco and many cheese counters sell packs of ready-made rosettes.
They're less fresh-looking but save buying a girolle.

**Why is it shaved instead of sliced?**
Shaving exposes much more surface to the air, which releases more aroma and
makes a strong cheese taste lighter and nuttier.

**What does the name mean?**
Monk's head. It was first made by monks at Bellelay Abbey in the Swiss Jura.

**Do you eat the rind?**
No. Trim the rind off the top before shaving and leave the sides on to hold
the cheese together.
