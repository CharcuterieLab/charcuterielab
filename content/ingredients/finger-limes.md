## The short version

A small, finger-shaped Australian lime packed with hundreds of round juice
pearls. Squeeze one out and they spill like tiny green or pink caviar, then
pop in your mouth with a burst of lime. A few spoonfuls on a board brighten
everything near them and start conversations.

## What it is

Finger limes (Citrus australasica) grow wild in the rainforests of eastern
Australia, and California farms now grow them for the US market. The fruit is
about 3 inches long, with skin from green to deep purple. Inside, the juice
sits in separate round vesicles instead of the usual wedge-shaped segments,
and the pulp can be green, yellow or pink.

**Taste:** sharp lime, with grapefruit and herbal notes
**Texture:** tiny firm beads that burst when you bite
**Look:** caviar-like pearls spilling from a slim finger-shaped fruit

The vesicles are the mechanism. Each pearl holds its juice inside a thin
membrane, so the acid stays put until you bite. That means you can scatter
finger lime on cheese or fish and it won't soak in or make anything soggy,
unlike a squeeze of regular lime. The acid arrives in small, sharp bursts
that cut fat and reset the palate.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Smoked salmon** | Bursts of acid cut oily fish without making it wet. |
| **Burrata** | Sharp pops against soft cream. A restaurant favorite. |
| **Caviar or salmon roe** | Two kinds of pearls, salty and sour. |
| **Fresh chèvre** | Tangy on tangy, bright and clean. |
| **Blini with crème fraîche** | A finger-lime topping makes it a party bite. |
| **Champagne, gin and tonic or a crisp Sauvignon Blanc** | Bright acid and bubbles. |

**Skip it with:** chocolate and very sweet jams. The sharp acid clashes and
makes them taste flat.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut each lime in half crosswise** with a sharp knife.
2. **Squeeze from the closed end** to push the pearls out, like a tube of
   toothpaste.
3. **Serve about a quarter lime per person** as a topping.
4. **Spoon the pearls onto the cheese or salmon** just before guests arrive,
   or set out a small bowl with a tiny spoon.
5. **Leave one or two halved limes on the board** so people see where the
   pearls came from.
6. **Fix:** if the pearls are hard to push out, roll the whole lime on the
   counter for a few seconds first.

**Skip:** cutting them lengthwise. You'll slice through the pearls and lose

## Quick FAQ

**What is a finger lime?**
A small citrus native to Australia, about 3 inches long. Inside are hundreds
of tiny round juice vesicles that pop like caviar.

**What do finger limes taste like?**
Like lime with a hint of grapefruit and herbs. Very tart, and some varieties
are a little sweeter.

**How do you get the pearls out?**
Cut it in half crosswise and squeeze gently from the closed end, like a tube
of toothpaste.

**When are they in season?**
California-grown finger limes are harvested from late summer into winter.
Specialty grocers stock them in that window.

**How long do they keep?**
About two weeks in the fridge in a sealed bag. The pearls can also be frozen.
