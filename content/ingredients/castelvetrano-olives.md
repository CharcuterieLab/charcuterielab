## The short version

The one olive that belongs on every board. Castelvetranos are vividly green,
firm, buttery and only lightly salty — closer to a fresh fruit than to a
Kalamata. They're the olive people who "don't like olives" eat three of without
noticing.

## What it is

The Nocellara del Belice variety from Castelvetrano in western Sicily, cured
briefly in a mild brine rather than aged in salt or oil. The short cure is why
they keep their colour and their crunch. Sold with pits in, usually in brine.

**Taste:** buttery, mildly salty, faintly sweet, almost no bitterness
**Texture:** firm and meaty, snaps rather than yields
**Look:** bright grass-green, glossy, plump

The mechanism is a short cure. Long-cured olives develop the sharp, funky
compounds that make olives divisive; Castelvetranos are pulled early, so what's
left is fat and mild salt. On a board that means they add brine without adding
an argument, which is why they can sit next to a delicate cheese where a Kalamata
couldn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata and fresh mozzarella** | Mild salt and firm texture against cream with none. The only olive gentle enough not to bury them. |
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk fat and buttery olive; both are rich and neither is sharp. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat with a cold, firm, wet counterpoint. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Two buttery Mediterranean things, one salty and firm, one salty and crisp. |
| **Aged pecorino** | Salt against salt works here because the olive brings so much fat and so little bite. |
| **Dry white wine and vermouth** | The bar snack they were made for. |

**Skip it with:** nothing. Castelvetranos are the least confrontational olive
sold.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain them and pat dry.** Brine on the board soaks crackers.
2. **Serve in a small bowl**, not scattered — the brine transfers.
3. **Put out a pit bowl.** They're sold with pits and guests need somewhere to
   put them.
4. **Serve them cold or at room temperature** — both work, room temperature is
   better.
5. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
6. **Leave the pits in.** Pitted Castelvetranos are softer and less flavourful.
7. **Fix:** too mild? Warm them with olive oil, garlic and lemon zest for five
   minutes. It wakes them up.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Castelvetrano olives in brine** | Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, olive bars | The default. Buy from the olive bar if you can. |
| **Divina Castelvetrano** | Grocery, specialty | The most widely available jarred brand. Reliable. |
| **Nocellara del Belice** | Italian delis | The same olive under its variety name, sometimes cheaper. |
| **Pitted Castelvetrano** | Grocery | Convenient but softer and blander. Buy with pits if you can. |
| **Castelvetrano with citrus and herbs** | Olive bars | Marinated versions. Good, and they look better in a bowl. |

**Label check:** they should be bright green, not olive-drab. Dull colour means
they've been sitting or are a different variety sold under the name.

**Skip:** anything labelled "green olives" generically — a Castelvetrano is worth
paying for by name.

## Quick FAQ

**Why are Castelvetrano olives so green?**
They're cured briefly in mild brine rather than aged, which preserves the natural
green colour and the firm texture.

**Are Castelvetrano olives salty?**
Much less than most. They're buttery and mild, which is why they suit people who
usually avoid olives.

**Should I buy them pitted?**
No, if you can avoid it. Pitted versions are softer and less flavourful. Put out
a bowl for pits instead.

**What cheese goes with Castelvetrano olives?**
Burrata and fresh mozzarella, manchego, and aged pecorino.

**How much per person?**
About an ounce — four or five olives.
