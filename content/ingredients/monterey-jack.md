## The short version

A mild, buttery semi-soft cheese from California. On its own it's the least
interesting cheese in this section. Its aged version — Dry Jack — is one of the
best American cheeses made, and almost nobody outside California has tried it.

## What it is

Monterey Jack descends from cheese made by Franciscan friars in California and
was commercialized by a Monterey businessman named David Jacks in the 1800s.
Young Jack is a mild, high-moisture, semi-soft cheese. Dry Jack is the same
cheese aged seven months to two years, its rind rubbed with cocoa powder,
pepper and oil.

**Taste (young):** mild, buttery, milky, faintly tangy
**Taste (dry):** nutty, sharp, savory, deeply concentrated
**Texture:** soft and springy young; hard and granular aged
**Look:** pale ivory young; deep gold with a dark rubbed rind aged

Moisture loss is the mechanism, and the transformation is dramatic. Young Jack
is roughly 45% water. Dry Jack, after a year hanging in cloth, is far less —
and everything that was diluted in the young cheese concentrates. It's the
clearest example in this section of aging turning a dull cheese into a very
good one.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Tostada rounds and tortilla chips** | Sweet toasted corn under a mild California cheese. |
| **Apple and grapes** | Fresh acid against a plain, buttery paste. |
| **Chorizo and pickled jalapeño** | Fat, heat and acid — young Jack is the calm in that bite. |
| **Fig jam and honey** | Sweetness giving young Jack somewhere to go. |
| **Walnuts and almonds** | Especially with Dry Jack, which is nutty already. |
| **Dry Jack with aged balsamic** | It behaves like parmesan, and it takes the same pairing. |
| **Zinfandel, Sauvignon Blanc, cold lager** | Californian across the board. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and pecorino if you're serving the young version.
And skip putting young Jack next to Dry Jack — the comparison is unkind.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy Dry Jack if you can get it.** That's the recommendation on this page.
   Vella and Rumiano are the names to look for.
2. **Young Jack: cube or slice.** Dry Jack: break into shards like parmesan.
3. **Temper 30 minutes.**
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Use young Jack as a Latin-board cheese**, beside queso fresco, chorizo and
   pickled things — that's where its mildness works.
6. **Trim the rubbed rind on Dry Jack.** The cocoa-and-pepper coating is for
   protection, not eating.
7. **Fix:** young Jack tasting like nothing is young Jack working as intended.
   Give it a partner with flavor or buy the aged version.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vella Dry Jack** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, California grocery | The famous one. Aged, nutty, excellent. Buy this. |
| **Rumiano Dry Jack** | Whole Foods, natural grocers, West Coast | The other good producer. |
| **Young Monterey Jack** | Every grocery store | Mild and cheap. Fine as a filler. |
| **Pepper Jack** | Every grocery store | Young Jack with chilies. Good on a casual or Latin board. |
| **Queso fresco** | Latin dairy case | Similar mildness, more purpose — see its own page. |
| **Aged gouda** | Grocery, Whole Foods | If Dry Jack isn't available, this is the nearest sweet-nutty aged cheese. |

**Label check:** "Dry Jack" specifically, with an age. Plain "Monterey Jack" is
always the young version.

**Skip:** pre-shredded Jack for a board — it's coated in starch — and expecting
the young version to hold its own against anything assertive.

## Quick FAQ

**What is Dry Jack?**
Monterey Jack aged seven months to two years, with the rind rubbed in cocoa,
pepper and oil. Hard, nutty and genuinely excellent.

**Is Monterey Jack worth putting on a cheese board?**
Young Jack, only as a mild filler on a casual board. Dry Jack, absolutely.

**Is Pepper Jack the same cheese?**
Yes, young Monterey Jack with chili peppers added.

**Can you eat the Dry Jack rind?**
No. The cocoa and pepper rub is a protective coating.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
