## The short version

Mango cooked with vinegar, sugar, ginger and warm spice. On a cheese board it
does three things at once — sweet, sour, spiced — which is more than any jam
manages, and it's the traditional partner for a mature cheddar in Britain.
Major Grey's is the style you'll find everywhere and it's a good one.

## What it is

Chunks of underripe mango preserved in a sweet-sour spiced syrup with ginger,
garlic, chilli, mustard seed and vinegar. **Major Grey's** is the standard
Anglo-Indian sweet style; Indian *aam chunda* and hot mango pickle are much
sharper and saltier things.

**Taste:** sweet, sour, gingery, mildly hot
**Texture:** chunky with soft fruit pieces in a thick syrup
**Look:** amber-brown, glossy, visibly chunked

The mechanism is acid and spice carried in sugar. A cheese board's sweet elements
usually add nothing but sugar; chutney adds vinegar sharpness and warm spice as
well, which is why it can partner a very strong cheddar or a stilton where a fruit
jam would just sit there.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | The British pairing. A strong cheddar has salt, acid and crystalline crunch; the chutney answers all three with sweet, sour and spice. |
| **Stilton and blue cheese** | Ginger heat and vinegar cutting fat, with sugar handling the salt. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang plus warm spice. An easy upgrade on a plain log. |
| **Oatcakes** | The traditional Scottish base. Nutty, dry, and built for chutney. |
| **Coppa and spiced salami** | Chilli in both, sweetness bridging them. |
| **Curried nuts and poppadums** | If the board leans Indian, this ties it together. |

**Skip it with:** delicate bloomy cheeses and anything you want to taste on its
own. Chutney is loud.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small bowl** with a spoon — it's chunky, not spreadable.
2. **Chop any very large mango pieces** so it can be spooned onto a cracker.
3. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
4. **Put it beside the cheddar**, which is what it's for.
5. **Use a sturdy base.** Oatcakes and digestive biscuits hold it; water crackers
   snap.
6. **Buy Major Grey's style** for a cheese board rather than hot mango pickle,
   which is a different and much saltier thing.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? A spoonful of lime juice or cider vinegar sharpens it.

| **Geeta's Premium mango chutney** | British and Indian grocers | Better fruit, more ginger. A clear step up. |
| **Sharwood's mango chutney** | Grocery, British aisle | The British supermarket default. |
| **Hot mango chutney** | Indian grocers | Chilli-forward. Excellent with blue cheese. |
| **Ploughman's or apple chutney** | British delis | The other classic cheeseboard chutney. Sharper and less sweet. |

**Label check:** mango and vinegar should lead. Watch the sugar — some
supermarket versions are close to jam.

**Skip:** mango pickle in oil. Salty, sour and intensely spiced — a great
ingredient, but not this one.

## Quick FAQ

**What cheese goes with mango chutney?**
Mature cheddar above all, plus stilton and other blues, and fresh goat cheese.

**What is Major Grey's chutney?**
A style rather than a brand — the sweet, mild Anglo-Indian mango chutney sold in
most supermarkets.

**Is chutney the same as jam?**
No. Chutney has vinegar and spice as well as sugar, so it's sour and savoury as
well as sweet.

**What crackers go with chutney?**
Sturdy ones — oatcakes and digestive biscuits. Thin water crackers snap under it.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce, spooned rather than spread.
