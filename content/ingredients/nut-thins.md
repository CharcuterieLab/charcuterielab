## The short version

Thin, crisp crackers made from rice flour and ground nuts. They're the
gluten-free option stocked in essentially every American supermarket, which
makes them the practical answer when you find out about a dietary restriction
the day before. Light, mild, and fragile in dip.

## What it is

Rice flour gives the structure, nut meal gives the flavor and fat, and the
whole thing is rolled very thin and baked until it snaps. The result sits
somewhere between a water cracker and a rice cake — lighter than either almond
flour crackers or seed crackers.

**Taste:** mild, faintly nutty, lightly salted
**Texture:** very thin, brittle, clean snap
**Look:** small pale rectangles, smooth, slightly glossy

Availability is the mechanism here, and it's not a small thing. Every other
gluten-free base in this tier requires a trip to a natural grocer or an online
order. This one is on the cracker shelf at a regular supermarket, next to the
Triscuits, at a normal cracker price. For a host with a gluten-free guest and
one afternoon, that's what matters.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Sharp cheddar** | Mild base, loud cheese. It behaves almost exactly like a water cracker here. |
| **Brie and Camembert** | Thin and crisp against soft and rich — the standard contrast, gluten-free. |
| **Fresh goat cheese with honey** | The nut note bridges tangy cheese and sweet honey. |
| **Smoked salmon and cream cheese** | Light enough not to overwhelm the fish. |
| **Fig jam and quince** | Neutral enough for sweet spreads to lead. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt against a faintly nutty base. Simple and good. |
| **Sparkling wine, dry white** | The cracker adds so little that the wine pairing is really with the cheese. |

**Skip it with:** thick dips and hummus — they're very thin and snap
immediately. And be aware they contain almonds or pecans, so they aren't a
solution for a nut-allergic guest.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Treat them as your gluten-free water cracker.** Same role, same placement,
   same neutrality.
2. **Serve 5–6 per person.** They're small and light.
3. **Use a separate dish and a clean knife.** For a guest with celiac disease,
   a cracker that touched a wheat board isn't safe.
4. **Label them, and note the nuts.** "Gluten-free, contains almonds" covers
   both questions in one card.
5. **Keep them out of the dip corner.** Plantain chips or cassava chips are the
   gluten-free scoop; these aren't.
6. **Fix:** they soften in humidity and re-crisp at 300°F for 3 minutes.

| **Plain rice crackers** | International aisle, health food aisle | If a guest is nut-allergic as well, this is the swap. |

**Label check:** Nut-Thins are gluten-free but **not nut-free**. If you need
both, go to cassava or plain rice crackers instead.

**Skip:** the ranch, cheddar and barbecue flavors on a cheese board. They're
snack crackers and read like it.

## Quick FAQ

**Are Nut-Thins actually gluten-free?**
Yes — rice flour and nut meal, no wheat. They're certified on the box.

**Are they nut-free?**
No, the opposite. They're made with almonds or pecans.

**Which flavor for a cheese board?**
Hint of Sea Salt. It's the most neutral and works with everything.

**How many per person?**
5–6. They're thin and disappear quickly.

**Can I dip with them?**
No — they snap. Use plantain or cassava chips for dips.
