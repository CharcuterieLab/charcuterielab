## The short version

A German cheese that crosses a triple-crème brie with a mild blue: white bloomy
rind, rich buttery paste, and a light blue veining through the middle. It's the
easiest blue anyone will ever eat, and it's the right cheese for a guest who
says they don't like blue.

## What it is

Cambozola was created by the Champignon dairy in Bavaria in the 1970s — the
name is a portmanteau of Camembert and Gorgonzola, and it's a registered
trademark rather than a traditional cheese. Cream is added to the milk, the
cheese gets a bloomy white rind like a brie, and *Penicillium roqueforti* is
introduced lightly for the blue.

**Taste:** buttery, rich, mildly tangy, gentle blue note at the finish
**Texture:** soft, dense, spreadable, like a firm triple-crème
**Look:** ivory paste with faint blue-grey veining, white bloomy rind

Cream dilution is the mechanism. Added cream raises the fat content well above
a normal blue, and fat coats the palate — which physically blunts the sharp
compounds the mold produces. The blue is still there; it just arrives softened
and at the end of the bite rather than the start.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Water crackers** | Lean and dry against a very rich cheese. A butter cracker here is fat on fat. |
| **Fig jam and honey** | Sweetness with a gentle blue. Easy and always works. |
| **Pear and grapes** | Cool acidic fruit cutting a high-fat paste. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin against richness. |
| **Baguette** | Plain bread under a soft spreadable cheese. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt against cream — the blue is mild enough not to fight it. |
| **Sparkling wine, off-dry Riesling, port** | Acid or sweetness against fat and salt. |

**Skip it with:** Roquefort or Cabrales on the same board — Cambozola tastes
like nothing after either. And skip it if you want an actual blue cheese
experience; this is deliberately gentle.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.** It should be soft and spreadable, like a good brie.
2. **Cut wedges from the center out**, keeping rind on each piece.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.** People eat more of this than of a real blue.
4. **Use it as the bridge cheese** on a board that also has a serious blue —
   guests can work up to it.
5. **Give it a spreading knife**, not a hard blade. It spreads more than it
   slices.
6. **The bloomy rind is edible**, like brie's.
7. **Fix:** if it's still firm, it needs more time out. Cambozola is much better
   at full room temperature.

## Quick FAQ

**Is Cambozola a real cheese or a brand?**
Both — it's a genuine German cheese, invented in the 1970s, but the name is a
trademark rather than a traditional designation.

**Is it actually blue cheese?**
Yes, with *Penicillium roqueforti* veining. It's just very mild, because the
added cream blunts the sharpness.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, it's a bloomy rind like brie's.

**What if my guests don't like blue cheese?**
This is the cheese for them. Serve it with honey and don't mention the word
blue until after they've tried it.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz. It's rich but people take more of it than of a sharper blue.
