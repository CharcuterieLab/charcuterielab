## The short version

German liver sausage — smooth, spreadable and mineral, the everyday cousin of
French pâté at a fraction of the price. **Braunschweiger is smoked liverwurst**,
not a separate product. Serve it with raw onion and mustard on dark bread.

## What it is

Liverwurst is pork liver and pork, cooked and emulsified to a smooth paste with
onion, marjoram, allspice and pepper. *Braunschweiger* is the same thing after
cold smoking, named for the city of Braunschweig. Both are fully cooked and sold
ready to eat.

**Taste:** mineral and livery, sweet from onion, warm with allspice
**Texture:** smooth, soft, spreadable, sometimes coarse-flecked
**Look:** pale grey-pink, smooth, often in a chub or ring

Emulsification is the mechanism, and it's why liverwurst reads so differently
from a coarse country pâté. Grinding liver and fat to a uniform paste
distributes the mineral flavour evenly and removes all texture contrast — so
the liver arrives smooth and constant rather than in bursts. Whether that's
better or worse is taste; it's certainly milder.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Raw onion, thinly sliced** | The traditional partner and genuinely essential. Sharp allium against sweet mineral liver. |
| **Pumpernickel or cocktail rye** | Dark sour bread. The German plate. |
| **Grainy or brown mustard** | Vinegar and heat against a smooth mineral spread. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onion** | More acid. Liver spreads need it. |
| **Apple slices** | Sweet and tart, and better than it sounds. |
| **Butter under it on the bread** | The German way, and it softens the mineral edge. |
| **Dark beer, dry Riesling** | Malt sweetness or sharp acid, either of which lifts liver. |

**Skip it with:** smoked cheese, delicate fresh cheeses, and any other liver
product. One liver item per board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve it in a small bowl or as a short chub with a knife**, not
   pre-spread.
2. **Temper 20 minutes.**
3. **Put raw onion right beside it.** Thin half-moons of white or red onion.
   This is the pairing that makes liverwurst work, and leaving it off is the
   usual reason people say they dislike it.
4. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Dark bread, not crackers.**
6. **Scatter chives or cracked pepper over the top** so it doesn't look
   anonymous.
7. **Fix:** if it's tasting flat and pasty, it's too cold and there isn't
   enough acid nearby. Both are easy fixes.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **German deli liverwurst** | German butchers, delis, specialty | Fresher, coarser and much better than supermarket chubs. |
| **Boar's Head Liverwurst** | Deli counters | The widely available American version. Perfectly good. |
| **Braunschweiger** | Grocery deli, Midwest markets | The smoked version. Same product, more depth. |
| **Leberkäse** | German markets | A different German liver loaf — sliceable, often served hot. |
| **Pâté de campagne** | Whole Foods, French delis | The coarse French relative — see its own page. |
| **Teewurst** | German markets | The smoked pork spread without liver — see its own page. |

**Label check:** pork liver high in the ingredients. *Braunschweiger* means it's
been smoked. Coarse versions say *grob*; smooth ones say *fein*.

**Skip:** serving it without onion and mustard. It's a mineral spread and it
needs sharp partners.

## Quick FAQ

**Is braunschweiger the same as liverwurst?**
Effectively yes — braunschweiger is smoked liverwurst, named after the German
city. Same product, extra step.

**Is it cooked?**
Yes, fully cooked and ready to eat.

**Why does everyone serve it with onion?**
Sharp raw allium cuts the mineral richness of liver. It's the pairing that makes
the sausage work, and skipping it is why people think they dislike it.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.

**Liverwurst or pâté de campagne?**
Liverwurst is smooth, cheap and German. Pâté is coarse, pricier and French. The
pâté has more texture; the liverwurst is milder.
