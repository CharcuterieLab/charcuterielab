## The short version

Big, flat, golden beans sold in brine, like olives. They're firm, salty and
mild, and the fun is popping each bean out of its skin with your teeth.
They're cheap, high in protein and almost nobody puts them on a board, which
makes them a great surprise in the briny corner.

## What it is

Lupini (or tremoços in Portugal) are the seeds of the white lupin plant.
Raw, they're very bitter and inedible. They're soaked and boiled, then
rinsed in changes of water for days to leach out the bitter alkaloids, then
stored in salted brine. They're a classic Italian bar snack and a
Portuguese beer partner.

**Taste:** salty, mild and nutty, a little like a firm chickpea
**Texture:** firm and snappy, with a chewy skin
**Look:** flat, round, bright golden-yellow beans

Leaching is the mechanism. The bitter compounds in lupin are water-soluble,
so days of soaking and rinsing wash them out and leave a mild, protein-rich
bean. Brining then seasons it and keeps it. The skin stays intact, which is
why lupini keep their snap in a bowl for hours.

**Skip it with:** dessert items. They're pure savory brine.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain and rinse them.** The jar brine is very salty.
2. **Soak for 10 minutes in fresh water** if they still taste too salty.
3. **Toss with olive oil, lemon zest and chili flakes** for an easy upgrade.
4. **Serve about 1 oz per person.**
5. **Put them in a bowl with a second small bowl for skins,** and show guests
   how to pop them.
6. **Fix:** if they've gone soft in the fridge, a 30-minute soak in cold
   salted water firms them back up.

## Quick FAQ

**What are lupini beans?**
The seeds of the lupin plant, soaked and brined to remove their natural
bitterness. They're a traditional bar snack in Italy, Portugal and the
Middle East.

**Do you eat the skin?**
You can. Many people nick the skin with their teeth and pop the bean out,
which is half the fun. The skin is edible, just chewy.

**Can someone with a peanut allergy eat lupini?**
Be careful. Lupin is a legume related to peanuts, and some people with
peanut allergies react to it. It's a listed allergen in Europe.

**Why are they so salty?**
They're stored in brine. Rinse them and soak for 10 minutes in fresh water if
they're too salty.

**Are lupini beans keto?**
They're high in protein and fiber and very low in net carbs, so they fit most
keto boards.
