## The short version

A hard sheep's-milk cheese from Lazio and Sardinia, salted much more heavily
than any Italian cow's-milk cheese. Sharp, tangy, aggressively savory. Put a
little on the board and put honey next to it.

## What it is

*Pecora* means sheep. Pecorino Romano is DOP-protected, made from raw sheep's
milk, and dry-salted repeatedly over weeks rather than brined once — which is
why it's so much saltier than parmesan. Minimum aging is five months for table
use, eight for grating.

**Taste:** very salty, sharp, tangy, faintly gamey
**Texture:** hard, dry, granular, crumbles into shards
**Look:** white to pale straw, much whiter than Parmigiano

Sheep's milk plus repeated dry-salting is the mechanism. Sheep's milk has
roughly twice the fat and protein of cow's milk and a distinctive tangy,
slightly gamey character. Layer weeks of dry salt on top of that and you get a
cheese that reads as seasoning rather than as a mild snack — which is why it's
grated over pasta and eaten in small pieces on a board.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Honey — especially chestnut or wildflower** | Sweet against a very salty cheese. The traditional Roman pairing and the one that rescues it. |
| **Fresh fava beans, in spring** | The Roman May Day dish. Green, sweet and raw against sharp salt. |
| **Pears and figs** | Fruit sugar and water cutting the salt. |
| **Taralli and friselle** | Plain Southern Italian bread with a Southern Italian cheese. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin and oil against dry salt crystals. |
| **Cracked black pepper** | *Cacio e pepe* logic. Pepper's heat lifts the cheese. |
| **Big reds — Montepulciano, Primitivo, Barolo** | Tannin to stand up to the salt. |

**Skip it with:** feta and other salty cheeses — that's salt twice, and both
lose — and delicate fresh cheeses, which it obliterates. One aggressive salty
cheese per board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve less than you think.** Three-quarters of an ounce per person is
   plenty. It's the saltiest cheese most people will meet on a board.
2. **Break it into small shards** with a short parmesan knife.
3. **Temper 30 minutes.** Cold, it's just salt; warm, the tang and the sheep
   character come through.
4. **Put honey directly beside it** with a spoon. Without something sweet
   nearby it dominates the whole board.
5. **Place it away from the cured meats** — prosciutto and pecorino together is
   salt on salt.
6. **Trim the rind.** It's hard, dark and inedible.
7. **Fix:** if it's overwhelming everyone, move it to the edge and add more
   fruit around it. There's no way to make it milder.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pecorino Romano DOP** | Grocery cheese case, Whole Foods, Italian delis | The standard. Check for the DOP stamp on the rind. |
| **Locatelli Pecorino Romano** | Most grocery stores | The most widely distributed brand in the US. Reliable. |
| **Fulvi Pecorino Romano** | Cheese counters, specialty | Made in Lazio rather than Sardinia. Rounder and less harsh. |
| **Pecorino Sardo** | Cheese counters, Italian delis | Sardinian, milder and nuttier. A gentler alternative. |
| **Pecorino Toscano** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods | Much milder and softer — see its own page. |
| **Trader Joe's Pecorino Romano** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and serviceable. |

**Label check:** DOP on the rind, and note the production zone. Lazio-made
wheels are generally rounder and less harshly salty than Sardinian ones.

**Skip:** generic "Romano" without the DOP mark. Often a cow's-milk imitation
and much less interesting.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is pecorino so much saltier than parmesan?**
It's dry-salted repeatedly over weeks rather than brined once, and sheep's milk
carries salt differently than cow's milk.

**Pecorino Romano vs. Pecorino Toscano?**
Romano is hard, dry and very salty. Toscano is softer, milder and much more
approachable on a board.

**How much per person?**
Under an ounce. It's very intense.

**What's the one thing to serve with it?**
Honey. It's the traditional Roman pairing and it makes the cheese work on a
board.

**Can I use it instead of parmesan?**
For grating, often yes — but reduce the salt elsewhere in the dish. On a board
they're genuinely different cheeses.
