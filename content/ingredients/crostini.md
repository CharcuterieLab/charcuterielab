## The short version

Thin slices of bread brushed with olive oil and baked until crisp. They exist
for one reason: toasting drives the water out, so a crostino holds a wet
topping for an hour without collapsing. Bread can't. Crackers snap. This is the
in-between.

## What it is

*Crostini* means "little crusts." Traditionally Tuscan, traditionally made from
yesterday's bread, traditionally served with chicken liver pâté. Slices are cut
thin, oiled on both sides, and baked until they're dry all the way through
rather than just browned on top.

**Taste:** toasty, olive-oil rich, neutral underneath
**Texture:** crisp through, with a slight chew at the center
**Look:** small golden ovals, blistered and uneven

Three near-relatives get confused with it. **Bruschetta** is thicker, grilled,
and rubbed with raw garlic. **Melba toast** is sliced thinner and baked twice
until brittle. **Crackers** were never bread. Crostini sit in the middle, which
is exactly why they're useful.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Whipped ricotta and honey** | Soft, wet and mild needs a crisp, oiled base with a little salt to push against. |
| **Burrata and tomato** | The only base that survives tomato juice. A cracker would be paste in five minutes. |
| **Chicken liver pâté** | The original Tuscan pairing. Rich and soft against dry and crisp. |
| **Goat cheese and fig jam** | Olive oil in the toast bridges tangy cheese and sweet jam. |
| **Whipped feta with herbs** | Wet, salty spread that needs structure and gets it. |
| **Sautéed mushrooms** | Warm and moist. Toasting is what makes this possible on a board. |
| **Prosciutto and shaved parmesan** | The crisp snap does the work a cracker would, but tastes like bread. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, really — this is the most forgiving base on the
board. The only failure mode is topping them too early.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice a baguette on the diagonal, ¼ to ⅓ inch thick.** Thinner than that
   burns; thicker stays chewy in the middle.
2. **Brush both sides with olive oil.** Both sides matters — one-sided crostini
   go soft from the bare side.
3. **Bake at 375°F for 10–12 minutes, flipping once**, until pale gold and dry
   through. Color is not the goal; dryness is.
4. **Cool on a rack, never in the pan.** Trapped steam undoes the whole thing.
5. **Season while warm** with flaky salt, or rub a cut garlic clove over the
   surface — the residual heat releases the oils.
6. **Make up to 3 days ahead** and keep airtight at room temperature. Never
   refrigerate; the fridge makes them stale, not fresh.
7. **Top no more than 20 minutes before serving**, and put wet toppings in
   bowls beside the crostini so guests build their own.

**Yield:** one baguette makes roughly 24–30 crostini, which serves 6–8 people.

## What to buy

Homemade is better and costs almost nothing. But if you're short on time:

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A day-old baguette** | Any bakery, discount rack | The correct raw material. Stale bread makes better crostini than fresh. |
| **Isola Crostini with Olive Oil** (7 oz) | Grocery specialty aisle, Amazon | Closest store-bought to homemade. |
| **Whole Foods Market Rustic Crostini, Olive Oil & Sea Salt** (6 oz) | Whole Foods | Reliable, well-seasoned, good size. |
| **John Wm. Macy's Crostini** | Grocery specialty aisle, Amazon | Flavored varieties. Treat them as a flavored cracker, not a neutral base. |
| **Melba toast** | Any grocery, cracker aisle | Thinner and drier. Fine substitute when you want something crisper. |

**Label check:** olive oil, not "vegetable oil blend." The oil is most of the
flavor.

**Skip:** pre-toasted rounds that list sugar or corn syrup. They brown fast in
the factory and taste faintly sweet under savory toppings.

## Quick FAQ

**How far ahead can I make crostini?**
Three days, stored airtight at room temperature. This is the best make-ahead
item on a board.

**Crostini vs. bruschetta — what's the difference?**
Crostini are thin, oven-toasted and small. Bruschetta is thick, grilled and
rubbed with garlic. Bruschetta is a bigger, messier bite.

**How many per person?**
3–4. One baguette makes enough for six to eight people.

**Can I use sourdough instead of baguette?**
Yes, and it's excellent — just cut the slices in half so the pieces stay
bite-sized.

**Why did mine go soft?**
Either they cooled in the pan, or they weren't baked dry through. Golden on
the outside isn't the same as dry in the middle.
