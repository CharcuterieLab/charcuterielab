## The short version

Cranberries are sour enough that even loaded with sugar the sauce stays sharp,
which makes it the most useful condiment on a holiday board. Make it rather than
buy it — cranberries, sugar, orange zest, fifteen minutes — and make the
whole-berry version, not the jellied cylinder.

## What it is

Cranberries cooked with sugar and usually orange zest or juice until the berries
burst and the pectin sets the sauce. **Whole berry** keeps the fruit visible;
**jellied** is strained and firm enough to slice, which is a Thanksgiving
tradition rather than a board ingredient.

**Taste:** sharply tart, sweet, faintly bitter
**Texture:** loose and jammy with burst berries
**Look:** bright crimson, glossy

The mechanism is acidity that survives sugar. Cranberries are among the most
acidic fruits used in cooking, so even a heavily sweetened sauce reads as sharp.
On a board dominated by fat and salt that acid is the point — it's what lets a
sweet condiment work with brie, ham and cheddar all at once.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie** | The baked brie topping, served cold. Sharp acid slicing soft fat, with the colour making the plate. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sharp, with sugar as the bridge. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Two acids in different registers, and the crimson against white is the best colour contrast on a winter board. |
| **Ham and turkey** | The holiday logic. Sweet-tart fruit with salty roast meat. |
| **Stilton** | Sugar for the salt, acid to stop it cloying — better than fig jam on a Christmas board. |
| **Pecans and walnuts** | Cranberry and nut is the fixed American holiday pairing. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses like burrata. The acid is too sharp.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make it:** 12 oz cranberries, 3/4 cup sugar, 1/2 cup water or orange juice,
   the zest of an orange. Simmer 12–15 minutes until the berries pop and it
   thickens.
2. **Cool completely.** It sets as it cools.
3. **Decant into a small bowl** with a spoon.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Add orange zest, port or rosemary** if you want it to read as a board
   condiment rather than a dinner side.
6. **Make it a day ahead.** It improves overnight.
7. **Fix:** too tart? More sugar while it's still hot. Too sweet? A squeeze of
   lemon.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fresh or frozen cranberries** | Grocery, Nov–Dec; freezer year-round | Make it yourself. Fifteen minutes, and much better. |
| **Whole berry cranberry sauce** | Any grocery | The bought version to buy. Visible fruit, loose texture. |
| **Cranberry orange relish** | Grocery, seasonal | Uncooked and very sharp. Excellent with goat cheese. |
| **Cranberry chutney** | Specialty, seasonal | Spiced and vinegar-sharpened. The best bought option for a board. |
| **Jellied cranberry sauce** | Any grocery | Sliceable like membrillo, which is a legitimate board trick — but it's mostly sugar. |

**Label check:** whole berry over jellied, and check the sugar. Some brands are
sweeter than jam.

**Skip:** cranberry cocktail jelly and anything with corn syrup leading.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I use cranberry sauce on a cheese board?**
Yes — it's one of the sharpest sweet condiments available, which makes it very
useful on a rich winter board.

**Whole berry or jellied?**
Whole berry for a board. Jellied can be sliced like membrillo, but it's mostly
sugar.

**How do I make cranberry sauce?**
Simmer 12 oz cranberries with 3/4 cup sugar and 1/2 cup orange juice for about
15 minutes until the berries burst. Cool completely.

**What cheese goes with cranberry sauce?**
Brie, aged cheddar, fresh goat cheese, and stilton.

**Can I make it ahead?**
Yes, and it improves overnight. It keeps a week in the fridge.
