## The short version

A soft cow's-milk cheese with an edible white bloomy rind. Mild, buttery, and
the safest cheese on any board — which is exactly why it's usually served
wrong. Cold brie is chalky and tastes of almost nothing. Give it an hour on the
counter and it becomes a different cheese.

## What it is

Brie comes from the Île-de-France region east of Paris. The white rind is
*Penicillium candidum*, a mold sprayed on the surface that ripens the cheese
from the outside inward — which is why a cut wedge shows a firmer core and a
softer, runnier layer near the rind. The younger the wheel, the wider that
chalky center.

**Taste:** buttery, mild, faintly mushroomy, milky
**Texture:** soft and yielding, ranging from cakey to nearly liquid
**Look:** pale ivory paste under a fuzzy white rind

Temperature is the mechanism, and it's the single most important thing on this
page. Brie's fat is solid below about 60°F. Cold, that fat holds the aromatic
compounds locked in place and the paste stays firm. At room temperature the fat
softens, releases those aromatics, and the cheese goes from bland to nutty and
mushroomy. Nothing else you do to brie matters as much.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette** | Soft crumb, crisp crust, no competing flavor. The pairing brie was made for. |
| **Water crackers** | Dry snap against wet cream, and no fat-on-fat heaviness. |
| **Fig jam** | Sweet against mild and salty, and the jam's density holds on soft cheese. |
| **Honey** | Same logic, lighter. Especially good on a younger, chalkier wheel. |
| **Green grapes and pear** | Acid and water cut the fat and reset the palate between bites. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Salt and crunch against soft and mild — the texture the cheese can't provide. |
| **Champagne, dry cider, Chardonnay** | Bubbles and acid slice through butterfat. Big reds bulldoze it. |

**Skip it with:** everything crackers, pretzel crisps and anything garlicky or
heavily salted. Brie is quiet, and a loud base wins. Also skip pairing it next
to a strong blue on the board — put something between them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Take it out of the fridge 45–60 minutes before serving.** This is the
   whole page. A cold wheel is a wasted wheel.
2. **Cut wedges from the center out, like a pie**, so every guest gets both
   rind and center. Cutting the tip off is the one cheese-board faux pas people
   actually notice.
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person** when it's one of three cheeses. An 8 oz wheel
   covers six to eight people.
4. **Leave the rind on.** It's edible, it's most of the flavor, and cutting it
   off leaves you with butterfat. Say so out loud — people ask.
5. **Score the top and leave one wedge cut**, so nobody has to be the first to
   break into it.
6. **Give it its own knife** and place it away from the blue cheese and the
   cured meat, both of which will overpower it.
7. **Fix:** a wheel that's still chalky in the center is underripe, not broken.
   Bake it 10 minutes at 350°F and serve it warm and molten instead.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Président Brie** | Nearly every grocery store | The reliable default. Mild, consistent, cheap. |
| **Ile de France Brie** | Grocery cheese case | A step up in flavor for a dollar or two more. |
| **Brie de Meaux** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods, Murray's | The real French raw-milk original where legal to import. Dramatically better. |
| **Fromager d'Affinois** | Cheese counter, Whole Foods | Technically not brie, but softer and richer. Most people prefer it in a blind taste. |
| **Trader Joe's Double Crème Brie** | Trader Joe's | Best value in the category. |
| **Costco Kirkland Brie** | Costco | Large format, good price for a big board. |

**Label check:** look for a soft, giving feel when pressed gently through the
package, and a rind that's white with faint reddish-brown flecks — not grey,
and not shrunken away from the paste.

**Skip:** pre-cut brie wedges sold in plastic. They dry at the cut faces and
they've usually been sitting. Buy a small wheel instead.

## Quick FAQ

**Do you eat the rind on brie?**
Yes. It's edible and it carries most of the flavor. Cutting it off leaves you
with plain butterfat.

**Brie or Camembert — what's the difference?**
Camembert is smaller, stronger and more mushroomy; brie is larger, milder and
more buttery. Camembert is the one to buy if brie has ever bored you.

**How long before serving should I take it out?**
45 to 60 minutes. It matters more than anything else you do.

**How much brie per person?**
1–2 oz as one of three cheeses. An 8 oz wheel serves six to eight.

**How do I cut it?**
Wedges from the center out, like a pie, so every piece has rind and center.
