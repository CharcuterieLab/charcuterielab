## The short version

Not a prestige ingredient, and that's fine. Honey roasted peanuts cost a dollar,
deliver sweet-salty crunch, and work genuinely well on a beer board or beside
sharp cheddar. The one thing to be careful about is the allergen — peanuts are a
different allergy from tree nuts and a far more serious one for some guests.

## What it is

Peanuts (a legume, not a nut) roasted with a honey and sugar coating, usually
with salt and a little starch to keep the glaze dry. Planters is the archetype;
almost every brand tastes similar.

**Taste:** sweet, salty, roasted, unmistakably peanut
**Texture:** crisp, with a fine dry coating
**Look:** tan-brown, matte, slightly dusty

The mechanism is familiarity. Almost everything on a good board is unfamiliar to
somebody; honey roasted peanuts are the ingredient everyone already knows they
like. On a casual board that matters more than it does on a curated one — it
gives guests somewhere safe to start.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp, salty, crystalline against sweet and crunchy. The best pairing here by some distance. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | Sugar blunts salt. A cheaper version of what a candied pecan does. |
| **Pretzel crisps** | Both salty, both crunchy, both cheap. A beer board in two ingredients. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel meets honey. Two sweet things that agree. |
| **Apples** | Tart crisp fruit against sweet coated nut — peanut butter and apple, deconstructed. |
| **Beer, especially amber and brown ales** | The context these belong in. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses and anything expensive. The coating's
sweetness bulldozes subtlety.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the jar.
2. **Serve in a bowl.** The honey coating sheds dust that will coat cheese.
3. **Label them, or tell people.** Peanut allergy is common and serious, and a
   mixed nut bowl is where people get caught out.
4. **Keep them physically separate** from other nuts if any guest has an allergy.
5. **Serve 0.5–1 oz per person.**
6. **Use them on casual boards** — beer boards, game-day boards, kids' boards —
   rather than on a curated cheese plate.
7. **Fix:** soft? 300°F for six minutes. The coating re-crisps.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Planters honey roasted peanuts** | Any grocery | The standard. Cheap and consistent. |
| **Trader Joe's honey roasted peanuts** | Trader Joe's | Slightly less sweet. |
| **Honey roasted with sea salt** | Grocery | Better balance for a cheese board. |
| **Boiled peanuts** | Southern grocers, roadside | A completely different, softer, savoury ingredient — excellent on a Southern board. |
| **Honey roasted almonds or cashews** | Grocery | The tree-nut versions, if peanuts are off the table. |

**Label check:** many honey roasted peanuts contain wheat starch in the coating —
worth checking if anyone's avoiding gluten.

**Skip:** anything where sugar is the first ingredient.

## Quick FAQ

**Are peanuts okay on a charcuterie board?**
Yes, on casual boards. They're cheap, familiar, and pair well with sharp cheddar.
Just flag the allergen.

**Are peanut and tree nut allergies the same?**
No — they're separate allergies, and peanut allergy is more common and often more
severe. Keep peanuts in their own bowl and mention them.

**What cheese goes with honey roasted peanuts?**
Aged cheddar first, then blue cheese and aged gouda.

**Do they contain gluten?**
Often, yes — many brands use wheat starch in the coating. Check the label.

**How much per person?**
Half to one ounce. People graze on these more than on plain nuts.
