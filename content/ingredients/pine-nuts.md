## The short version

Pine nuts are a finishing ingredient, not a bowl of nuts. Toasted and scattered
over goat cheese, burrata or ricotta, they add a soft resinous crunch that
nothing else provides. Buy Mediterranean (Italian, Spanish, Turkish) rather than
Chinese if the budget allows — the flavour is much better and there's no risk of
"pine mouth".

## What it is

The seed of certain pine species, extracted from cones by hand — which is why
they're among the most expensive things by weight in any grocery store. The
Mediterranean stone pine gives long, slim, sweet seeds; Chinese and Korean pines
give shorter, fatter, more resinous ones.

**Taste:** sweet, buttery, resinous, faintly piney
**Texture:** soft-crisp, waxy, almost creamy when toasted
**Look:** small ivory teardrops

The mechanism is texture at small scale. A pine nut is too soft to be a crunch
element and too expensive to serve loose, but scattered over a soft cheese it
gives every spoonful a small point of resistance — the same job breadcrumbs do in
Italian cooking.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego and golden raisins** | The Spanish trio. Sheep cheese, sweet raisin, resinous nut — a fixed tapa. |
| **Burrata** | Scatter over the top with olive oil and salt. Texture in an otherwise texture-free ingredient. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | Same logic. With honey it becomes a dessert; with salt and oil it stays savoury. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll or scatter. The nut's sweetness balances the tang. |
| **Basil and pesto flavours** | If the board leans Italian, pine nuts tie it together. |
| **Raisins and currants** | Sicilian cooking pairs them constantly, for good reason. |

**Skip it with:** anything strongly smoked or aggressively salty. The flavour is
delicate and gets lost.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Toast in a dry pan**, not the oven. Two to three minutes over medium,
   shaking constantly. They burn in seconds.
2. **Tip them out of the pan immediately.** Residual heat will take them from
   golden to black.
3. **Scatter, don't serve loose.** They go over the cheese, not in a bowl.
4. **Use 0.25 oz per person** — a tablespoon covers a whole burrata.
5. **Buy small quantities.** The oil content means they spoil quickly.
6. **Store in the freezer.**
7. **Fix:** a lingering metallic taste days later is "pine mouth", associated with
   certain Chinese pine species. Harmless and temporary, but buy Mediterranean to
   avoid it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Italian or Spanish pine nuts** | Italian delis, specialty, online | Long, slim, sweet. The best flavour, and the highest price. |
| **Turkish pine nuts** | Middle Eastern markets | Very good and cheaper than Italian. The best value in the category. |
| **Chinese pine nuts** | Any grocery, Trader Joe's | Short and fat, more resinous, much cheaper. Occasionally cause pine mouth. |
| **Frozen pine nuts** | Trader Joe's, Costco | Sold frozen for a reason. Fresher than the shelf-stable ones. |

**Label check:** look at the shape. Long and slim means Mediterranean; short and
stubby means Asian. Country of origin is often on the back in small print.

**Skip:** anything that smells at all sharp, and bags that have been sitting in a
warm aisle.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need to toast pine nuts?**
Yes. Two to three minutes in a dry pan, shaking constantly. They burn faster than
any other nut.

**Why are pine nuts so expensive?**
They're extracted from pine cones largely by hand, and the trees take decades to
produce.

**What is "pine mouth"?**
A lingering metallic taste that some people get days after eating certain Chinese
pine nut species. Harmless and temporary. Mediterranean pine nuts don't cause it.

**How do I use pine nuts on a board?**
Toasted and scattered over soft cheese — burrata, ricotta, goat cheese — rather
than served loose in a bowl.

**How should I store them?**
In the freezer. They're very high in oil and spoil quickly.
