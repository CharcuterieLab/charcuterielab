## The short version

A semi-firm Catalan goat cheese covered in a soft grey-blue mold rind that
looks like velvet. It tastes of hazelnut, fresh herbs and damp forest floor.
Almost nobody outside Spain knows it, and it's one of the best-value cheeses at
any good counter.

## What it is

Garrotxa is named for a volcanic region of Catalonia. The traditional cheese
had effectively died out by the 1970s and was revived in the 1980s by a group
of young farmers returning to the land — the modern cheese is a deliberate
reconstruction. It's aged about two months under a natural *Penicillium* mold
rind.

**Taste:** hazelnut, fresh herbs, damp earth, mild goat tang
**Texture:** semi-firm, smooth, dense, slightly springy
**Look:** bright white paste under a thick velvet grey-blue rind

The mold rind is the mechanism. Unlike a bloomy white rind, which softens the
paste beneath, Garrotxa's grey mold sits on a firm cheese and contributes
aroma rather than texture — those herbal, forest-floor notes come from the rind
inward. It's why the cheese smells more complex than a plain aged goat cheese
tastes.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Membrillo (quince paste)** | Spanish, floral, sweet. Works with every Spanish cheese and especially well here. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Hazelnut in the cheese, almond beside it. Direct echo. |
| **Rosemary crackers** | Herbal against herbal. The cheese already tastes faintly of the hillside. |
| **Fresh figs and grapes** | Sweet and wet against dense and dry. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness under a nutty, mildly tangy cheese. |
| **Jamón serrano** | Salt and cure with a firm goat cheese — less obvious than with sheep's milk, and very good. |
| **Cava, Albariño, light Rioja** | Spanish and high-acid, cutting a dense goat's-milk paste. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and everything crackers. Garrotxa is subtle and
herbal, and both bury it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut wedges with the grey rind attached.** The velvet rind against stark
   white paste is a genuinely striking cross-section.
2. **Temper 30 minutes** — the herbal notes are muted cold.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
4. **Point out the rind.** People assume grey mold means spoilage; it's a
   deliberate natural rind and it's part of the flavor.
5. **Trim the rind before eating** — it's edible but chalky, and most people
   prefer it off.
6. **Put it beside the Spanish things** — membrillo, Marcona almonds, jamón.
7. **Fix:** if it seems bland, it's cold. Garrotxa is a subtle cheese and it
   needs full room temperature.

**Label check:** a thick, even, velvety grey rind. Patchy or cracked rind means
the wheel has dried out.

**Skip:** trimming the rind before it reaches the board. The grey-on-white
cross-section is half of why you bought it.

## Quick FAQ

**Is the grey rind safe to eat?**
Yes — it's a natural *Penicillium* mold rind, deliberately developed. It's
edible but chalky, and most people trim it.

**What does Garrotxa taste like?**
Hazelnut, fresh herbs and damp forest floor, with a mild goat tang. Subtler
than most goat cheeses.

**Is it a rare cheese?**
It nearly died out and was revived in the 1980s. It's now made by several
Catalan producers and reasonably available at good US counters.

**How is it different from French goat cheese?**
Firmer and less acidic, with herbal and nutty notes rather than lemony tang.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.
