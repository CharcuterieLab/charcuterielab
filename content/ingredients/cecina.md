## The short version

Spain's answer to jamón, made from beef instead of pork. A whole beef leg is
salted, smoked over oak and air-dried for months, then sliced paper-thin. It's
deep ruby red, lean and smoky, with a richer flavor than bresaola. It's also
the easiest luxury meat for guests who don't eat pork.

## What it is

The classic is Cecina de León IGP, from the province of León in northwest
Spain, made from the hindquarter of beef. The meat is salted, rinsed, smoked
over oak or holm oak, and dried in cool mountain air for at least seven
months. Real Cecina de León is hard to find in the US, but US-made
Spanish-style cecina is available online.

**Taste:** smoky, deeply beefy, savory, lightly salty
**Texture:** firm and lean, tender when sliced thin
**Look:** dark ruby-red slices with a darker, almost mahogany edge

Beef has less intramuscular fat than a ham, so it dries more slowly and
concentrates in a different way. Months of drying pull water out and let
enzymes break protein into savory amino acids, which is where the deep beefy
flavor comes from. Oak smoke adds aromatic compounds to the surface and helps
protect the meat while it dries.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Good olive oil** | The Spanish way. Fat carries flavor on a lean meat and adds silkiness. |
| **Manchego** | Rich sheep's milk fat balances the lean beef. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Buttery and salty, they add the fat cecina lacks. |
| **Piquillo peppers** | Sweet, smoky peppers echo the oak smoke. |
| **Membrillo** | A little fruit sweetness against the salt. |
| **Tempranillo, Mencía or an amber ale** | Smooth tannins and fruit, built for smoke and beef. |

**Skip it with:** smoked cheese and spicy chorizo right beside it. Smoke on
smoke gets muddy, and paprika heat hides the beef.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 15 minutes.** Cold, it tastes mostly of salt.
2. **Drizzle a little olive oil over the slices** just before serving. This is
   the classic Spanish service.
3. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** It's lean and rich in flavor.
4. **Fan the slices flat** rather than folding them. The color is the
   selling point.
5. **Place it near the Manchego** so guests take them together.
6. **Fix:** if the slices are stuck together, let the pack warm for a few
   minutes and peel them from one corner.

## Quick FAQ

**What is cecina?**
Cured, smoked and air-dried beef from Spain, sliced paper-thin. The
best-known is Cecina de León, made from the hind leg of beef.

**Is cecina the same as bresaola?**
They're cousins. Both are lean air-dried beef, but cecina is smoked over oak
and aged longer, so it's darker and smokier.

**Can I buy Cecina de León in the US?**
It's rare. Spanish-style cecina made in the US, sold by La Tienda, is the
easiest option.

**Is it raw?**
It's cured, smoked and dried, not cooked, like jamón or prosciutto. It's
eaten as is.

**How do the Spanish serve it?**
Sliced thin, drizzled with olive oil and sometimes a pinch of smoked paprika.
