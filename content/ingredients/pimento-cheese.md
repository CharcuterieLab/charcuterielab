## The short version

Sharp cheddar grated by hand, bound with mayonnaise, folded with chopped pimentos
and cayenne. It's a Southern institution and it does two jobs on a board — it's a
cheese, and it's a spread. Make it yourself; the tubs are gluey and mild.

## What it is

Grated sharp cheddar mixed with mayonnaise, diced pimentos (sweet roasted red
peppers), and seasoning — cayenne, black pepper, sometimes grated onion or
Worcestershire. The texture ranges from chunky to whipped depending on who made
it.

**Taste:** sharp, tangy, creamy, gently spicy
**Texture:** thick and chunky, spreadable
**Look:** orange-gold, flecked with red pepper

The mechanism is emulsified cheese. Cheddar on its own is dry and crumbly;
mayonnaise's oil-and-egg emulsion coats the grated shreds and turns them
spreadable without heat. That's why pimento cheese behaves like a dip while
tasting entirely like cheddar — and why the mayonnaise quality matters as much as
the cheese.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Butter crackers (Ritz)** | The correct base. Salty, buttery, and soft enough not to fight the spread. |
| **Pepper jelly** | Sweet heat over savoury fat — the two Southern staples, stacked. |
| **Celery sticks** | The traditional lighter service. Cold, wet, crunchy against thick and rich. |
| **Tomato jam** | Sweet-savoury against sharp and creamy. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Same flavour in a different form; the contrast in texture is the point. |
| **White bread, crusts off** | The sandwich it was invented for. Not board food, but worth knowing. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses and anything subtle. It's rich and sharp
and it takes over.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Grate the cheese yourself.** Pre-shredded cheddar is coated in anti-caking
   starch and makes the spread pasty.
2. **Make it:** 8 oz sharp cheddar grated on the large holes, 1/2 cup good
   mayonnaise, 4 oz diced pimentos drained, 1/4 tsp cayenne, black pepper.
3. **Mix by hand, not in a processor.** Texture is the whole point.
4. **Chill an hour** so it firms and the flavours settle.
5. **Serve at cool room temperature**, in a bowl with a spreader.
6. **Serve 1 oz per person.**
7. **Fix:** too stiff? A spoonful more mayonnaise. Too loose? More grated cheese.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sharp or extra-sharp cheddar block** | Any grocery | The base. Buy a block and grate it. |
| **Duke's mayonnaise** | Southern grocers, online | The traditional choice. No sugar, more tang. |
| **Jarred pimentos** | Any grocery, near the olives | Drain them well. |
| **Palmetto Cheese or Pawleys Island** | Southern grocers | The best-known bought brands. Decent. |
| **Homemade with smoked cheddar** | Make it | A real upgrade — smoke does what bacon would. |

**Label check:** in bought versions, cheese should lead, not mayonnaise or cream
cheese. Anything led by dairy blends will be gluey.

**Skip:** pre-shredded cheese and low-fat mayonnaise. Both wreck the texture.

## Quick FAQ

**What is pimento cheese?**
A Southern American spread of grated sharp cheddar, mayonnaise and diced roasted
red peppers, seasoned with cayenne.

**Why grate the cheese myself?**
Pre-shredded cheddar is coated in starch to stop it clumping, which makes the
spread pasty rather than creamy.

**What crackers go with pimento cheese?**
Butter crackers like Ritz are the traditional choice. Celery sticks are the
lighter option.

**Can I make it ahead?**
Yes — it's better after an hour in the fridge and keeps five days.

**What are pimentos?**
Sweet roasted red peppers, sold diced in jars. Mild, not spicy.
