## The short version

A sweet, silky, pale-green spread from Sicily, made from ground pistachios
and sugar. Think chocolate-hazelnut spread, but nuttier, less sweet and far
more fragrant. A spoonful on ricotta or a piece of biscotti makes the dessert
corner of a board feel like an Italian pastry shop.

## What it is

Crema di pistacchio is made by grinding pistachios to a paste and blending it
with sugar, oil and often milk powder. The famous versions use pistachios
from Bronte, on the slopes of Mount Etna, which carry a protected
designation. It's been around for decades in Sicily and became a global trend
thanks to pistachio-filled chocolate bars.

**Taste:** sweet, nutty, floral, with a buttery finish
**Texture:** smooth and silky, like a soft frosting
**Look:** pale to bright green, glossy

Pistachios are unusual nuts because they're green, thanks to chlorophyll in
the kernel, and they carry floral, almost marzipan-like aroma compounds.
Grinding releases the oil and those aromas at once. Sugar then rounds off the
slight bitterness of the skins, so the flavor reads as pure, sweet pistachio.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Ricotta** | Mild, milky cheese to carry a rich, sweet spread. A Sicilian classic. |
| **Mascarpone** | Doubles down on cream for a cannoli-style bite. |
| **Biscotti** | Crunchy and dry, perfect for dipping into the jar. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Bitter cocoa balances the sugar. |
| **Strawberries or raspberries** | Tart red fruit against sweet green spread. |
| **Espresso, Moscato d'Asti or Marsala** | Italian after-dinner drinks for an Italian dessert. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and cured meats. It's a dessert spread, and
salty, funky flavors make it taste sugary and odd.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Stir the jar well.** The oil separates.
2. **Spoon it into a small bowl** with a butter knife or a small spoon.
3. **Serve about 1 tablespoon per person.** It's rich.
4. **Swirl a spoonful into a bowl of ricotta** for an easy pistachio dip.
5. **Top with a few chopped pistachios** so guests know what it is.
6. **Fix:** if it's too stiff to spread, warm the jar in a bowl of hot water
   for 5 minutes.

**Label check:** find the pistachio percentage on the label. Under 20
percent is mostly sugar and oil; 40 percent or more tastes of real nuts.

**Skip:** green-dyed versions with artificial color. Real pistachio cream is
pale olive-green, not neon.

## Quick FAQ

**What is pistachio cream?**
A sweet spread made from ground pistachios, sugar and usually milk powder and
oil. The best-known versions come from Bronte in Sicily.

**Is it the same as pistachio butter?**
No. Pistachio butter is just ground nuts and tastes savory. Pistachio cream
is sweetened and silky, like a nut-butter version of Nutella.

**How much pistachio is in it?**
It varies a lot, from under 15 percent to over 40 percent. The percentage on
the label is the best guide to quality.

**Do I need to refrigerate it?**
Most jars are shelf-stable unopened. Check the label once it's open; many say
to keep it cool and use it within a few weeks.

**Is it dairy-free?**
Usually not. Most brands add milk powder. Some vegan versions use only nuts,
sugar and oil.
