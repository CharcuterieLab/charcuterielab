## The short version

Not a fruit you eat beside cheese — a fruit you spoon *onto* it. Cut a passion
fruit in half and you have two purple cups of intensely aromatic, seedy, sharp
pulp that works exactly like a sauce. It's the strongest aroma on any board, and
half a fruit goes a long way.

## What it is

A vine fruit, purple or yellow, with a hard wrinkled shell and a hollow full of
orange pulp suspended around crunchy black seeds. The pulp is very acidic and
extraordinarily fragrant. Purple passion fruit is smaller and sweeter; yellow
(maracuyá) is larger and sharper.

**Taste:** intensely tart, floral, tropical, faintly like guava
**Texture:** slippery gel with crunchy edible seeds
**Look:** wrinkled purple shells filled with glossy orange

The mechanism is aroma concentration. Passion fruit carries some of the highest
concentrations of volatile esters of any fruit — which is why a teaspoon changes
a whole bite, and why it's used as an accent everywhere rather than as a
component. On a board it does the job a vinaigrette does on a salad.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | Spoon it straight over the top. Acid and perfume against cream, with olive oil and salt. The best use of both. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | The pulp is the sauce; the ricotta is the base. Honey optional. |
| **Mascarpone** | Rich, sweet, and flat on its own — passion fruit gives it an edge. Basically a deconstructed dessert. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Two sharp things that don't compete: lactic tang against fruit acid. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Mild milk plus loud fruit. A cleaner version of tomato and mozzarella for winter. |
| **Prosecco** | Not on the board, but it's the drink that makes the pairing make sense. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and aged cheese. The aroma bulldozes both.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy them wrinkled.** A smooth passion fruit is underripe and mouth-puckering;
   wrinkled skin means the sugars have developed.
2. **Halve crosswise with a serrated knife.** The shell is tough.
3. **Serve the halves as cups** on the board, with a small spoon. That's the
   presentation, and it's better than anything you could arrange.
4. **Or scoop the pulp into a small dish** to spoon over cheese.
5. **Eat the seeds.** They're crunchy and edible and provide the texture.
6. **Serve half a fruit per two people.** It's an accent.
7. **Fix:** too sharp? Stir a little honey into the scooped pulp. Don't dilute it
   with anything watery.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Purple passion fruit** | Whole Foods, Latin and Asian markets | Smaller, sweeter, the one to buy. Look for heavily wrinkled skin. |
| **Yellow passion fruit (maracuyá)** | Latin markets | Larger and sharper. Better for sauces than for eating from the shell. |
| **Frozen passion fruit pulp** | Latin markets, freezer aisle | Excellent and much cheaper. Goya and Goya-type brands. Thaw and spoon. |
| **Passion fruit purée (Boiron, Perfect Purée)** | Specialty, online | Restaurant standard. Expensive, intensely concentrated, keeps in the freezer. |

**Label check:** wrinkles are ripeness, not age. Heavy for its size means full of
pulp; a light one has dried out.

**Skip:** smooth, taut, shiny passion fruit — it will be painfully sour.

## Quick FAQ

**Do you eat passion fruit seeds?**
Yes. They're crunchy, edible, and provide the texture. Straining them out leaves
a thin juice.

**How do I know when passion fruit is ripe?**
The skin should be deeply wrinkled. Smooth means underripe.

**How do I serve passion fruit on a board?**
Halve it and set the halves out as cups with a small spoon, or scoop the pulp
into a dish and spoon it over fresh cheese.

**What does passion fruit go with?**
Cream cheeses — burrata, ricotta, mascarpone, fresh goat cheese. It overwhelms
cured meat and aged cheese.

**Is frozen pulp as good?**
For spooning over cheese, close enough, and much cheaper. The halved shells are
better presentation.
