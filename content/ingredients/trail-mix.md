## The short version

Salted nuts, dried fruit and chocolate in one handful. On a board, trail mix
is the cheapest, easiest gap filler there is, and it gives every bite salty,
sweet and crunchy at once. It's a natural on kids', game-day, hiking and
budget boards.

## What it is

Trail mix is a mix of nuts, dried fruit and something sweet, packed for
energy on long hikes. The classic American version is peanuts, raisins and
candy-coated chocolate, nicknamed GORP. Store mixes now range from simple
peanut-raisin blends to cashews, almonds, cranberries, pretzels and coconut.

**Taste:** salty, sweet and nutty, with bursts of chocolate and fruit
**Texture:** crunchy nuts, chewy fruit and snappy candy shells
**Look:** a colorful jumble of tan nuts, dark raisins and bright candies

Trail mix works because of contrast and fat. Salt on the nuts makes the
fruit and chocolate taste sweeter, and sugar makes the salt pop. Nuts bring
fat and protein, which slow digestion and make the mix filling, which is why
hikers carry it. On a board, that same density means a little goes a long
way.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Beef jerky** | Savory chew next to sweet crunch. A hiking-board pair. |
| **Colby Jack or cheddar cubes** | Mild cheese with salty-sweet nuts. |
| **Dried cranberries** | Add more tart fruit to balance the sweetness. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Push it toward a dessert board. |
| **Apples** | Fresh crunch beside dried fruit. |
| **Hot cocoa, cold milk or a brown ale** | Easy, comforting drinks. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses like burrata in the same corner.
The candy and salt overwhelm them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Pour it into a small bowl** or use it to fill gaps between items.
2. **Keep it away from wet foods** like pickles and jam, which make it
   soggy.
3. **Serve about an ounce per person.**
4. **Pick out stale or broken pieces** from the bottom of the bag.
5. **Label it,** because it contains peanuts and tree nuts.
6. **Fix:** if the chocolate is melting in a warm room, swap in a mix with
   yogurt-covered raisins or no chocolate.

**Label check:** "mountain mix" and "GORP" mean the classic peanut-raisin
style. Check the allergen box: most mixes contain peanuts, tree nuts and
milk.

**Skip:** mixes heavy on banana chips. They're mostly fried and sugared,
and they go stale fast once opened.

## Quick FAQ

**Is trail mix good on a charcuterie board?**
Yes, especially on snack, kids', hiking and game-day boards. Use it to fill
the gaps between cheese and meat.

**What's in classic trail mix?**
Peanuts, raisins and candy-coated chocolate. That combination is often
called GORP, for good old raisins and peanuts.

**How much trail mix per person?**
About an ounce, or a small handful, when it's one of several snacks.

**Can I make my own trail mix for a board?**
Yes. Mix roughly equal parts salted nuts, dried fruit and chocolate, then
add one surprise like pretzels or coconut chips.

**Is trail mix safe for nut allergies?**
No. Classic trail mix contains peanuts and usually tree nuts. Use a
seed-based mix with sunflower and pumpkin seeds instead.
