## The short version

Half of *prosciutto e melone*, the most famous fruit-and-cured-meat pairing
there is. Cantaloupe is the traditional partner; honeydew is milder and greener.
Serve it cold and cut it into pieces a slice of ham can wrap around.

## What it is

Cantaloupe — properly muskmelon in the US — has orange flesh and netted skin.
Honeydew is smooth-skinned with pale green flesh, sweeter and less musky.
Galia, Charentais and Canary are other worthwhile varieties. Watermelon is a
different fruit for board purposes and belongs with feta rather than ham.

**Taste:** sweet, musky, faintly floral; honeydew is cleaner and greener
**Texture:** soft, juicy, yielding
**Look:** orange or pale green wedges and cubes

Sugar and water against salt is the mechanism, and it's the whole reason the
pairing exists. Prosciutto is concentrated salt and fat with essentially no
water. Melon is the opposite — mostly water, high sugar, no salt. Each supplies
exactly what the other lacks, which is why the combination reads as complete
rather than merely pleasant.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Prosciutto di Parma** | The canonical pairing. Wrap a strip around a wedge, or drape it over. |
| **Jamón serrano or ibérico** | The Spanish version, and just as good. |
| **Burrata and fresh mozzarella** | Milky and unsalted against sweet and cold. |
| **Feta, with watermelon** | The other melon pairing — salt, water and mint. |
| **Mint and basil** | Herbs lift melon considerably. |
| **Cracked black pepper and lime** | Both sharpen melon's sweetness. |
| **Prosecco, dry rosé, Vinho Verde** | Cold, light and acidic. |

**Skip it with:** strong cheeses and smoked items. Melon is delicate and it
loses to anything assertive.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut pieces a ham slice can wrap** — batons or thick wedges roughly two
   inches long. Cubes are harder to wrap and look less generous.
2. **Serve it cold.** This is the rule. Room-temperature melon is flat and
   faintly perfumed in an unpleasant way.
3. **Cut within two hours.** Melon weeps and the cut faces dull.
4. **Serve 2–3 oz per person.**
5. **Put it directly beside the prosciutto**, with a few pieces already
   wrapped so people see the idea.
6. **Drain it.** Set cut melon on a towel for a minute before it goes on the
   board.
7. **Fix:** bland melon? Salt and lime. A pinch of flaky salt on cantaloupe is a
   genuine improvement, not a rescue.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cantaloupe** | Any grocery, summer | The prosciutto partner. Look for a beige netted skin and a sweet smell at the stem end. |
| **Honeydew** | Any grocery | Milder and greener. Better on a lighter board. |
| **Charentais or Galia** | Farmers markets, specialty | Small French and Israeli varieties, far more perfumed. Worth seeking. |
| **Watermelon** | Any grocery, summer | For the feta-and-mint pairing rather than the ham one. |
| **Pre-cut melon** | Grocery refrigerated | Convenient and always less sweet. Acceptable in a hurry. |
| **Canary melon** | Specialty, farmers markets | Bright yellow, very sweet. Striking on a board. |

**Label check:** smell the stem end. A ripe melon is fragrant there; a
scentless one will taste of nothing. It should also feel heavy and give
slightly at the blossom end.

**Skip:** melon that isn't in season. Winter cantaloupe is watery and it makes
the pairing pointless.

## Quick FAQ

**Which melon goes with prosciutto?**
Cantaloupe, traditionally. Honeydew works and is milder.

**Does it have to be cold?**
Yes. Warm melon tastes flat and oddly perfumed.

**How do I pick a ripe one?**
Smell the stem end — it should be fragrant. It should feel heavy and give
slightly at the blossom end.

**How much per person?**
2–3 oz, cut into pieces a ham slice can wrap.

**Why does melon and ham work?**
Melon is water and sugar with no salt; prosciutto is salt and fat with no
water. Each supplies what the other lacks.
