## The short version

A soft, spreadable Calabrian salami loaded with red chili — brilliant scarlet,
genuinely hot, and unlike anything else in the case. Spread it on warm bread
with something creamy nearby and it converts people instantly.

## What it is

'Nduja is made from fatty cuts of pork — belly, jowl, shoulder — ground to a
paste with a large proportion of Calabrian chili, then cased and briefly aged.
The chili can run to a third of the total weight, which is why the color is so
intense and why it keeps without drying hard. The name traces back to French
*andouille*, brought south during Napoleonic rule.

**Taste:** hot, smoky-sweet from the chili, deeply porky, salty
**Texture:** soft and spreadable, like a coarse pâté
**Look:** vivid scarlet-orange paste, glossy with rendered fat

Fat plus capsaicin is the mechanism, and it's why 'nduja burns differently.
Capsaicin dissolves in fat rather than water, so a fat-rich paste carries heat
across the whole mouth and holds it there. That's a slower, broader burn than a
dry chili flake, and it's why dairy — also fat — is the thing that stops it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Warm crostini or grilled bread** | Heat loosens it to a spreadable glaze. This is the standard service. |
| **Fresh mozzarella, burrata or ricotta** | Cool milk fat quenching capsaicin. The essential partner. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness blunts heat and the combination is genuinely excellent. |
| **Eggs, if the board runs into brunch** | 'Nduja and scrambled eggs is one of the great uses. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweet and smoky, echoing the chili from a gentler angle. |
| **Pecorino or aged provolone** | Salt and sharpness standing up to the burn. |
| **Cold lager, Primitivo, Aglianico** | Cold and structured. Avoid delicate wines. |

**Skip it with:** prosciutto and other delicate cured meats — the chili wipes
them out, and a guest who eats 'nduja first will taste nothing else for
several minutes. Give it its own corner.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve it in a small bowl with its own knife.** It's a spread, not a slice,
   and it stains everything it touches.
2. **Warm it slightly** — 20 seconds in a pan or 30 in the oven — so it goes
   glossy and spreadable.
3. **Serve half an ounce to an ounce per person.** It's very hot and very rich.
4. **Put something creamy directly beside it.** Ricotta or mozzarella isn't a
   suggestion here; it's what makes the bite work.
5. **Place it at the far end of the board** from anything delicate.
6. **Warn people.** It looks like a pâté and it isn't.
7. **Fix:** too hot for the room? Stir a spoonful into ricotta to make a milder
   pink spread. That's a real Calabrian move.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Calabrian 'nduja, imported** | Italian delis, Whole Foods, specialty | The real thing. Look for Spilinga or Calabria on the label. |
| **Tempesta 'Nduja** | Whole Foods, specialty grocers | Chicago-made, widely available, excellent. |
| **Olli or Creminelli 'nduja** | Whole Foods, grocery | Easier to find, milder than imported. |
| **'Nduja in a jar** | Italian markets, online | Convenient and spreadable straight from the fridge. |
| **Sobrasada** | Spanish grocers, specialty | The Mallorcan equivalent — see its own page. |
| **Soppressata piccante** | Grocery, Whole Foods | If you want the heat in sliceable form. |

**Label check:** pork, chili, salt. The color should be deep red-orange all the
way through, not brown.

**Skip:** buying a large piece. An ounce per person goes a long way and it
keeps well but not indefinitely once opened.

## Quick FAQ

**How hot is 'nduja?**
Genuinely hot — the chili can be a third of the weight. Not painful for most
people, but far beyond a mild soppressata.

**Is it a salami or a pâté?**
Technically a salami — cured, fermented pork in a casing. It's just soft enough
to spread.

**What stops the heat?**
Dairy. Ricotta, mozzarella, burrata. Capsaicin dissolves in fat, and milk fat
carries it away.

**How much per person?**
Half an ounce to an ounce. It's rich and hot.

**Can I cook with it?**
It's superb in pasta, on pizza, and stirred into eggs. A jar goes a long way.
