## The short version

Cured pig cheek — fattier, more delicate and more expensive than pancetta, and
the cut Roman cooks insist on for carbonara and amatriciana. On a board it's a
small, rich curiosity rather than a volume meat. Half an ounce is a portion.

## What it is

Guanciale is the jowl, cured with salt, black pepper and sometimes herbs, then
air-dried for a few months. The jowl has a different fat structure from belly —
softer, more evenly distributed, and it renders at a lower temperature. That's
the reason Roman cooks won't substitute pancetta.

**Taste:** intensely porky, peppery, sweeter and more delicate than bacon
**Texture:** very soft, almost creamy fat with a thin seam of meat
**Look:** a mostly-white triangular slab with a thin pink muscle line, edged in
black pepper

Fat structure is the mechanism. Jowl fat is softer and lower-melting than belly
fat, so it renders into liquid faster in a pan and melts on the tongue faster
on a board. That's an advantage in cooking and a limit on a board — a large
piece is overwhelming in a way pancetta isn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pecorino Romano** | Salt and sheep's-milk tang against pure fat. Half of carbonara, uncooked. |
| **Cracked black pepper** | The other half. Guanciale is peppered already and it takes more. |
| **Crostini, warm** | Warmth softens the fat and the bread catches it. |
| **Cornichons and pickled things** | Vinegar cutting an extremely rich slice. |
| **Taralli** | Dry, plain, Italian. Nothing competing. |
| **Fresh fava beans or peas, in spring** | Green sweetness against fat. A Roman spring pairing. |
| **Frascati, Cesanese, Lambrusco** | Roman and Emilian wines with acid. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. And don't serve it alongside pancetta
and lardo — pick one cured fat per board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice it very thin.** Thinner than pancetta. This is the fattiest item in
   the section and the slice thickness is what makes it pleasant or not.
2. **Serve about half an ounce per person**, as a taste rather than a portion.
3. **Temper 20 minutes.**
4. **Lay it flat, slightly overlapping**, so the pepper-crusted edge shows.
5. **Put acid and something salty beside it** — cornichons and pecorino.
6. **Consider crisping some of it instead.** A few minutes in a dry pan turns
   it into crackling shards, which is a genuinely better board item than raw
   guanciale for most crowds.
7. **Fix:** if raw guanciale is too rich for the room, crisp the rest. Nobody
   dislikes it crisped.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Imported Italian guanciale** | Italian delis, specialty grocers | The real thing. Ask the counter to slice it thin. |
| **La Quercia Guanciale** | Whole Foods, specialty | Iowa-made and excellent. The easiest good one to find. |
| **Fra' Mani or Tempesta guanciale** | Specialty grocers, online | Small-batch American. Very good. |
| **Pancetta** | Grocery, Italian markets | The easier, less rich alternative — see its own page. |
| **Lardo** | Italian delis | Pure back fat, the next step in richness — see its own page. |
| **Any Italian butcher** | Local | Often cheaper than a specialty grocer and cut to order. |

**Label check:** guanciale should be mostly white fat with one thin muscle
seam, and a dark pepper crust. If it looks like streaky bacon, it's belly.

**Skip:** buying a large slab for a board. It's expensive and half an ounce per
person is genuinely the right amount.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between guanciale and pancetta?**
Guanciale is jowl, pancetta is belly. Guanciale is fattier, softer and more
delicate; Romans consider it non-substitutable in carbonara.

**Can I eat it raw?**
Yes, thinly sliced — it's a dry cure. Many people prefer it crisped.

**Why is it so expensive?**
There are only two jowls per pig, and the cure takes months.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce. It's the richest thing in this category.

**Should I crisp it instead?**
For most boards, yes. A few minutes in a dry pan makes shards that everyone
likes.
