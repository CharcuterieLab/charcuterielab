## The short version

Two kinds exist and only one works on a board. Buy **Fuyu** — squat, tomato-shaped,
eaten firm. Hachiya is acorn-shaped and astringent enough to dry your mouth out
unless it's collapsing-ripe. Slice a Fuyu crosswise and you get glowing orange
discs with a faint star pattern inside.

## What it is

An autumn fruit, in season roughly October through December. Fuyu persimmon
tastes like a cross between a mango and a firm apricot, with the texture of a
crisp pear. No peeling, no coring, almost no prep.

**Taste:** honeyed, mild, faintly cinnamon
**Texture:** crisp and dense, like an apple that gives slightly
**Look:** deep orange discs, translucent at the edges

The mechanism is sugar without acid. Most board fruit — grapes, apples, citrus —
brings acid to cut fat. Persimmon brings almost none, so it behaves like a soft
sweetener beside salty things instead of a palate scrubber. That makes it a
partner for cured meat rather than a competitor to it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Prosciutto** | Honey sweetness against clean salt, with no acid fighting the meat's delicacy. The autumn version of melon and ham. |
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk nuttiness picks up the persimmon's faint spice. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tang supplies the acid the fruit lacks; the fruit supplies the sugar the cheese lacks. |
| **Blue cheese** | Low-acid sweetness blunts salt without the sharp edge an apple would add. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin and crunch against dense soft flesh. |
| **Aged gouda** | Both carry caramel notes; they read as one flavour. |

**Skip it with:** very acidic pickles and cornichons nearby — the contrast makes
the persimmon taste flat and soapy.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy Fuyu.** Squat and flat-bottomed, like a small orange tomato. If it's
   acorn-shaped and pointed, that's Hachiya — wrong fruit for this.
2. **Choose firm with a little give.** Rock-hard is bland; soft is Hachiya
   territory.
3. **Don't peel.** The skin is thin and edible and holds the slice together.
4. **Slice crosswise, 1/4 inch.** Crosswise shows the faint star pattern;
   lengthwise wedges look like nothing.
5. **Brush with lemon** if the board will sit more than an hour. It browns
   slowly, but it browns.
6. **Fan the discs** in a short arc rather than stacking them.
7. **Fix:** mouth-drying astringency means you bought Hachiya. Let it ripen to
   jam-soft on the counter and spoon it instead.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fuyu persimmon** | Grocery, Oct–Dec | The board persimmon. Firm, sweet, sliceable. |
| **Sharon fruit** | UK and specialty grocers | Israeli Fuyu-type, bred non-astringent. Same use. |
| **Hachiya persimmon** | Farmers markets, autumn | Only when jam-soft. Spoon it onto crostini, don't slice it. |
| **Dried persimmon (hoshigaki)** | Japanese grocers, specialty | Expensive, spectacular. Slice thin — tastes like caramelised apricot. |

**Label check:** shape decides everything. Flat bottom = Fuyu = slice it.
Pointed bottom = Hachiya = wait.

**Skip:** persimmons with black spots spreading from the stem — that's the
inside going too.

## Quick FAQ

**Fuyu or Hachiya?**
Fuyu. It's squat and tomato-shaped and you eat it firm. Hachiya is pointed and
astringent until it's almost liquid.

**Do I peel it?**
No. The skin is thin, edible, and holds each slice together.

**When is persimmon in season?**
October through December in the northern hemisphere. Out of season they're
expensive and bland.

**Why does my persimmon dry my mouth out?**
It's an underripe Hachiya. Tannins. Let it get jam-soft and it disappears.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz — half a persimmon serves two or three people on a mixed board.
