## The short version

Duck breast salt-cured and air-dried until firm, then sliced thin with the fat
cap on. Darker, denser and more mineral than pork charcuterie, with a wide band
of fat that melts on contact. It's also, genuinely, the easiest cured meat to
make yourself.

## What it is

A whole duck breast — usually Moulard or Pekin — is buried in salt for a day,
rinsed, seasoned with pepper and herbs, then hung in a cool humid place for one
to three weeks until it loses about 30% of its weight. That's the entire
process. *Magret séché* is the French name.

**Taste:** rich, mineral, gamey-sweet, more intense than pork
**Texture:** dense and firm meat with a soft melting fat layer
**Look:** deep mahogany-red muscle under a thick pale fat cap

Duck fat's low melting point is the mechanism. It softens well below body
temperature — the same property that makes duck fat prized in cooking — so a
thin slice arrives with a band of fat that liquefies immediately. That
contrast, dense meat against melting fat, is the entire appeal.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fig jam or cherry preserve** | Duck and dark fruit is a permanent pairing. The sweetness cuts the richness. |
| **Fruit and nut crisps** | The cracker brings sweet and nut; the duck brings mineral fat. A complete bite. |
| **Brie or triple-crème** | Rich on rich, which works because the duck is savoury rather than fatty-sweet. |
| **Comté** | Nutty and firm against dense and mineral. |
| **Orange or pomegranate** | Acid and sweetness — the classic duck accompaniments. |
| **Pistachios and walnuts** | Tannin against mineral duck fat, and crunch against a dense slice. |
| **Pinot Noir, Madiran, Sauternes** | Duck's traditional wines, from light red to sweet. |

**Skip it with:** smoked cheese and everything crackers. It's expensive and
distinctive, and both flatten it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice very thin, across the grain**, keeping the fat cap on every slice.
   The fat is the point.
2. **Lay slices flat and slightly fanned** so the dark meat and pale fat read as
   a band.
3. **Temper 20 minutes** so the fat softens.
4. **Serve three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce per person.** It's rich and
   costly.
5. **Put something sweet and dark beside it** — fig jam, cherry preserve or
   pomegranate.
6. **Don't trim the fat.** It's half the product.
7. **Fix:** if the slices are chewy, they're cut too thick or with the grain.
   Slice across, thinner.

**Label check:** it should be a single whole breast, dark red with a thick pale
fat cap. Cured duck sold pre-sliced in a tray dries fast — check the edges.

**Skip:** trimming the fat to make it look leaner. That's where the flavour and
the texture live.

## Quick FAQ

**Is it actually prosciutto?**
No — the name is borrowed. It's cured duck breast, called *magret séché* in
France.

**Can I make it at home?**
Yes, and it's the standard first project for home curing. Salt for 24 hours,
rinse, season, hang for one to three weeks until it loses about 30% of its
weight.

**Is it cooked?**
No, cured — salt and drying, like prosciutto.

**How much per person?**
Three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce. It's rich and expensive.

**What's the best pairing?**
Fig jam or cherry preserve. Duck and dark fruit, as always.
