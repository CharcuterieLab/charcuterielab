## The short version

An honest ingredient: edible flowers are on a board because they look
extraordinary, not because they taste of much. Pansies, violas, nasturtiums and
borage against pale cheese are the single biggest visual upgrade available. The
one rule that matters is buying food-grade — florist flowers are sprayed with
things you should not eat.

## What it is

Flowers grown without pesticides and sold for eating. The common ones are
**pansies and violas** (mild, grassy), **nasturtiums** (genuinely peppery, like
watercress), **borage** (blue, faintly cucumber), **calendula** (petals, mildly
bitter), and **chive or thyme flowers** (properly savoury).

**Taste:** mild to nearly none, except nasturtium and chive flowers
**Texture:** delicate, papery, wilts fast
**Look:** vivid purple, yellow, orange and blue against cream and white

The mechanism is contrast. A board's palette runs cream, tan and brown, with red
fruit as the usual break. A violet or an orange nasturtium is a colour that
appears nowhere else in food, which is why a few flowers change a photograph more
than any amount of extra cheese does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Press flowers onto a log or scatter over. White is the best background for them, and the cheese's tang carries the flavour they lack. |
| **Burrata and ricotta** | Same logic — pale, soft, and improved by anything with colour. |
| **Berries** | Spring board logic. Flowers and fruit read as one arrangement. |
| **Honey** | A flower on a honey-drizzled cheese is the whole aesthetic in one bite. |
| **Nasturtium with anything savoury** | The one flower with real flavour — peppery enough to work with cured meat. |
| **Chive flowers with fresh cheese** | Purple, oniony, and actually a seasoning. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, but don't scatter them everywhere. Restraint is what
separates a board from a wedding cake.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy food-grade only.** Florist and garden-centre flowers are sprayed with
   pesticides not approved for food.
2. **Rinse gently in cold water** and dry on paper towel. They bruise instantly.
3. **Add them last**, right before serving. Most wilt within an hour out of water.
4. **Use a few, placed deliberately** — three to five on a standard board, not
   scattered like confetti.
5. **Put them on the pale things**, where the colour shows.
6. **Say they're edible.** Guests assume garnish means decoration and leave them.
7. **Fix:** wilting? Float them in ice water for ten minutes and dry gently. It
   revives most of them.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Food-grade edible flowers** | Whole Foods, specialty grocers, online | Sold in small clamshells near the herbs. Expensive and short-lived. |
| **Nasturtiums** | Farmers markets, grow them | Peppery and genuinely flavourful. The most useful edible flower. |
| **Pansies and violas** | Specialty, spring | The classic board flower. Mild and vividly coloured. |
| **Chive or thyme flowers** | Farmers markets, garden | Savoury and free if you grow the herb. |
| **Dried edible flowers** | Specialty, online | Longer-lasting, much less striking. Fine on a cheese log. |
| **Grow your own** | Seed packets | Nasturtiums and violas are among the easiest things to grow. |

**Label check:** the package should say edible or food-grade explicitly. If it
doesn't, don't.

**Skip:** anything from a florist, a garden centre, or the roadside. Also skip
lilies, foxglove, hydrangea and oleander — all toxic.

## Quick FAQ

**Are all flowers edible?**
No — many are toxic. Only use flowers sold specifically as food-grade, or ones you
grew yourself without pesticides and have positively identified.

**Do edible flowers taste of anything?**
Mostly very little. Nasturtiums are genuinely peppery and chive flowers are
oniony; pansies and violas are nearly neutral.

**Can I use flowers from a florist?**
No. Florist flowers are treated with pesticides and preservatives that aren't food
safe.

**How long do they last?**
A day or two refrigerated in their clamshell, and about an hour on a board before
they wilt.

**How many should I use?**
Three to five on a standard board. Restraint is what makes them look considered.
