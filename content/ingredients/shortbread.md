## The short version

The base that's also a dessert. Shortbread is roughly a third butter by weight,
which makes it the richest thing you can put under a cheese — and that richness
is exactly why it works with tart, salty and sharp things rather than sweet ones.
Buy Scottish, in thick fingers.

## What it is

Flour, butter and sugar in a 3:2:1 ratio, baked slowly so it stays pale.
Traditional Scottish shortbread contains nothing else — no eggs, no leavening,
no vanilla. Sold as fingers, petticoat tails (wedges), or rounds.

**Taste:** intensely buttery, mildly sweet, faintly salty
**Texture:** short and sandy, crumbling rather than snapping
**Look:** pale gold, matte, often fork-pricked

The mechanism is fat replacing gluten. So much butter coats the flour that gluten
strands can't form, which is why shortbread crumbles instead of snapping — the
literal meaning of "short". That crumb is why it carries soft cheese well: it
collapses under the topping rather than shattering.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Mascarpone** | Butter under cream. Very rich, and the shortbread's salt is what keeps it from being sickly. |
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Butter and bloomy-rind fat, with the sugar doing what a fruit jam usually does. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | The best pairing here. Sweet buttery crumb against salt and funk — the same logic as honey, with texture. |
| **Berries and fresh fruit** | Acid cutting butter. Shortbread with strawberries is a British summer fixture. |
| **Brandied cherries** | Spoon the syrup over. It soaks into the crumb. |
| **Tea and dessert wine** | Tannin in tea strips the butter from the palate, which is precisely why the pairing has lasted three centuries. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and savoury spreads. Shortbread is sweet and reads
as dessert.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Break fingers in half** if they're long — a full finger is more base than
   anyone needs.
2. **Fan them at the edge** of the board, near the sweet corner.
3. **Keep them dry.** Shortbread absorbs moisture from cheese faster than a
   cracker and goes soft.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person** — one or two pieces.
5. **Put them near the blue cheese**, which is where they do their best and least
   obvious work.
6. **Buy thick.** Thin shortbread rounds break under a wedge of cheese.
7. **Fix:** soft from humidity? Ten minutes in a 300°F oven, then cool completely.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Walkers pure butter shortbread** | Any grocery | The widely available standard. Thick fingers, real butter, reliable. |
| **Scottish artisan shortbread** | Specialty, British delis | Dean's, Shortbread House of Edinburgh. Noticeably better. |
| **Petticoat tails** | British delis | Round, scored into wedges. Good-looking on a board. |
| **Salted or sea-salt shortbread** | Specialty | Better with blue cheese. Worth seeking out. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Three ingredients, one bowl. Genuinely easy, and much better fresh. |

**Label check:** butter should be the second ingredient after flour. Anything
listing palm oil or "vegetable fat" is not shortbread.

**Skip:** thin shortbread cookies with added flavourings, and anything
chocolate-dipped for use as a base.

## Quick FAQ

**Does shortbread go with cheese?**
Yes — particularly blue cheese, brie and mascarpone. The butter and sugar do what
a fruit jam does, with texture added.

**Why is it called shortbread?**
"Short" describes the crumbly texture created when fat coats the flour and stops
gluten forming.

**Thick or thin shortbread for a board?**
Thick fingers. Thin rounds break under a wedge of cheese.

**Can I make it myself?**
Yes — flour, butter and sugar in a 3:2:1 ratio. It's one of the simplest bakes
there is.

**How much per person?**
One or two pieces, about an ounce.
