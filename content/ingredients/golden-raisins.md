## The short version

Dark raisins taste of prunes and disappear against dark wood. Golden raisins are
the same grape treated with sulphur instead of sun — pale amber, plumper, and
noticeably tarter. They cost about the same and look ten times better. Scatter
them, don't pile them.

## What it is

Thompson seedless grapes, dried with sulphur dioxide to preserve the pale colour
rather than sun-dried to brown. **Sultanas** are the same idea. Golden raisins
keep more acid and more moisture than dark raisins.

**Taste:** sweet with a real tart edge, faintly winey
**Texture:** plump and chewy, softer than dark raisins
**Look:** pale amber, translucent, glossy

The mechanism is retained acidity. Sun-drying caramelises grape sugar and drives
out acid, which is why dark raisins read as pure sweetness. Golden raisins keep
enough tartness to work like a very small dried apricot — sweet enough to blunt
salt, sharp enough not to cloy.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego** | The Spanish trio: sheep cheese, golden raisins, pine nuts. It's a classic tapa, and it works cold. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against tart sweetness, with the raisins filling in the cheese's dry crumble. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Scatter over a log. Sugar and lactic tang, and they stick to the surface. |
| **Pine nuts** | Sicilian logic — raisins and pine nuts appear together in every dish on that island. |
| **Coppa and soppressata** | Fatty spiced pork with something sweet and small to break it up. |
| **Curry-spiced nuts** | Raisins are what stops a spiced nut mix reading as one flat note. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, but don't run them alongside dates and dried figs.
Pick one or two sweet elements, not four.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the bag.
2. **Scatter into gaps** or fill a tiny dish. Never a large mound — they read as
   filler when piled.
3. **Sprinkle over a goat cheese log** with nuts and a drizzle of oil.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Plump them** in warm water, sherry or white wine for ten minutes if
   they've hardened, then dry them.
6. **Keep them in a sealed jar**, not the open bag — they harden fast.
7. **Fix:** clumped into a solid brick? Warm water, two minutes, drain, dry.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden raisins / sultanas** | Any grocery, Trader Joe's | The default. Cheap and consistently good. |
| **Jumbo golden raisins** | Whole Foods, bulk bins | Noticeably plumper. Worth the small premium for a board. |
| **Flame raisins** | Grocery, bulk | Large, reddish, sweet-tart. A good middle ground. |
| **Muscat raisins** | Specialty, Spanish delis | Large, seeded, intensely floral. Sold on the stem sometimes — spectacular. |
| **Zante currants** | Any grocery | Tiny and sharp. Best scattered over cheese rather than eaten alone. |

**Label check:** sulphur dioxide is what makes them golden — worth flagging for
sulphite-sensitive guests. Skip any with added oil-heavy coatings.

**Skip:** dark seedless raisins for board use. They're for baking.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the difference between golden and regular raisins?**
Golden raisins are treated with sulphur dioxide instead of sun-dried, so they
stay pale, plumper, and tarter.

**Are golden raisins the same as sultanas?**
Effectively yes — both are pale-dried Thompson seedless grapes.

**How do I serve raisins on a board?**
Scatter them into gaps or sprinkle them over a goat cheese log. Don't make a pile.

**Do they contain sulphites?**
Yes — that's what keeps them golden. Worth mentioning if a guest is sensitive.

**How do I soften hard raisins?**
Soak in warm water, sherry or white wine for ten minutes, then pat dry.
