## The short version

The pitted black olives from the can, the ones every kid has worn on their
fingers at Thanksgiving. They're mild, soft and buttery, with almost none of
the bitterness or salt of a Greek olive. Put them out for the holiday relish
tray crowd, and set a kalamata beside them for the olive lovers.

## What it is

These are California-style ripe olives, mostly the Manzanilla variety, and
much of the US supply is grown in California's Central Valley. The process was developed by
Freda Ehmann in the 1890s. Despite the name, they're picked green, cured in
a series of lye baths with air bubbled through the tanks, then canned. A
little iron salt sets the color.

**Taste:** mild, buttery and only lightly salty
**Texture:** soft, tender and smooth, almost creamy
**Look:** uniformly jet-black, glossy and round, usually pitted

The color and mildness come from fast oxidation. Lye breaks down the bitter
compound oleuropein in hours instead of the months a natural brine takes.
Bubbling air through the tanks oxidizes the olive's phenolic compounds,
which turn dark, and ferrous gluconate binds to them to fix the black.
Canning and sterilizing give the soft texture.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Kalamata olives** | Serve mild and bold side by side. Guests can choose. |
| **Colby Jack or cheddar cubes** | Mild cheese and mild olive for a kid-friendly corner. |
| **Pepperoni** | Pizza toppings on a board. |
| **Dill pickles and banana peppers** | A classic American relish tray. |
| **Cream cheese** | Stir chopped olives into it for a retro spread. |
| **Lager or a light red** | Easy, crisp drinks. |

**Skip it with:** dessert items. Even mild olives taste odd next to
chocolate and caramel.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain them well** and pat dry so the brine doesn't spread.
2. **Serve them in a small bowl;** they roll off a flat board.
3. **Serve 4 to 5 olives per person.**
4. **Toss them with a little olive oil, lemon zest and chili flakes** to
   make them taste homemade.
5. **Keep a few on the side for kids** to put on their fingertips. It's
   tradition.
6. **Fix:** if they taste of the can, rinse them and soak 10 minutes in cold
   water with a pinch of salt.

## Quick FAQ

**Why do canned black olives taste so mild?**
They're picked green, cured quickly in a lye solution, and darkened with air
instead of being fermented for months. That skips most of the bitter,
salty, fermented flavor of Greek olives.

**Are canned black olives really ripe?**
No, despite the name ripe olives. They're picked green and turned black by
oxidation during curing.

**What is the ferrous gluconate in black olives?**
An iron salt that fixes the black color so it doesn't fade. It's a safe,
common food additive.

**Are black olives good on a charcuterie board?**
Yes, especially on kids', holiday and budget boards. Serve them next to a
bolder olive like kalamata for contrast.

**What size black olives should I buy?**
Large or jumbo pitted olives look best on a board and fit nicely on a
fingertip.
