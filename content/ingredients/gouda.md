## The short version

The red-waxed Dutch cheese in every grocery store. Young Gouda is soft,
buttery and mildly sweet, nothing like the hard, crunchy aged kind. It's the
cheese you put out when you don't know the crowd, and it's always eaten.

## What it is

Gouda is a Dutch washed-curd cheese named for the town of Gouda in South
Holland. Young Gouda is aged roughly one to six months and coated in wax to
keep it moist. Most US grocery Gouda is young, imported from the Netherlands
or made in Wisconsin.

**Taste:** mild, buttery and sweet with a slight tang
**Texture:** smooth, supple and creamy; it slices cleanly
**Look:** pale yellow paste with a few small holes, under red or yellow wax

The sweetness comes from washing. Gouda makers replace some of the whey with
warm water, which rinses out lactose. Less lactose means less lactic acid,
so the cheese stays sweet and mellow instead of sour. Over years, that same
low-acid paste turns into the caramel-crunchy aged Gouda. Young, it's all
butter.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Apples** | Crisp acid against butter. The easiest pairing on the board. |
| **Honey ham** | Sweet and salty, soft and soft. |
| **Spicy brown mustard** | Sharp heat gives a mild cheese some backbone. |
| **Pecans or walnuts** | Toasty crunch echoes Gouda's nutty notes. |
| **Pears and grapes** | Juicy, gentle fruit that freshens the butterfat without shouting over a mild cheese. |
| **Summer sausage** | Smoky, salty meat against creamy cheese: the gift-box classic for a reason. |
| **Honey or fig jam** | A little sweetness brings out Gouda's faint caramel notes. |
| **Aged and smoked Gouda** | Serve all three for a Gouda flight. |
| **Amber ale, Beaujolais or dry cider** | Malt, juicy fruit or crisp apple meet the sweetness without burying it. |

**Skip it with:** strong blue cheese in the same bite. Blue buries a young
Gouda's gentle butteriness. Give them separate sides.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut off the wax.** Slice the wheel into wedges first, then peel or trim
   the wax from each piece.
2. **Slice wedges into thin triangles,** or cube them for kids.
3. **Serve 1 to 1.5 oz per person.**
4. **Let it sit out 20 to 30 minutes.** Warmth brings the sweetness out.
5. **Leave one wedge with a strip of red wax** on the back edge. It looks
   good and tells guests what it is.
6. **Fix:** if the cut faces look greasy, the room is too warm. Move the
   board out of the sun.

## Quick FAQ

**What is young Gouda?**
Gouda aged about one to six months. It's soft, mild and buttery, unlike aged
Gouda, which turns hard, caramel-sweet and crunchy.

**Do you eat the wax on Gouda?**
No. The red or yellow wax is a protective coating. Cut it off before serving.

**Is Gouda good on a charcuterie board?**
Yes. Young Gouda is one of the safest crowd-pleasers. Serve it beside a
bolder cheese so the board has contrast.

**What does Gouda pair with?**
Apples, ham, spicy brown mustard, pecans and amber beer. Fruit and mustard
both cut its butteriness.

**How do you pronounce Gouda?**
Most Americans say GOO-da. The Dutch say something closer to HOW-da.
