## The short version

A mix of nuts tossed with butter or oil, rosemary, cayenne, salt and often a
little brown sugar, then roasted. The reason to make them rather than buy them is
temperature: warm spiced nuts are the only hot thing on most boards, and the
smell alone does half the work.

## What it is

Any combination of nuts — almonds, pecans, cashews, walnuts, hazelnuts — roasted
with fat, herbs and spice. The canonical version is the Union Square Cafe bar nuts
recipe: rosemary, cayenne, brown sugar, butter, salt. Countless variations exist
and almost all of them work.

**Taste:** savoury, herby, warm with chilli heat, faintly sweet
**Texture:** crisp, lightly glazed, varied by nut
**Look:** glossy mixed browns flecked with dark green rosemary

The mechanism is heat and aroma. Rosemary and warm fat produce volatile compounds
that carry across a room, and a warm nut releases far more aroma than a cold one.
It's the same reason bread is served warm — the ingredient hasn't changed, but the
experience of it has.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Manchego** | Rosemary, salt and sheep milk are a Spanish combination that needs no explanation. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Heat and herb against sharp acid; the cayenne is what makes it interesting. |
| **Gorgonzola and blue cheese** | Savoury-sweet nuts against salt and funk, with heat cutting through the fat. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt on salt, with rosemary tying them together — the flavours of the same region. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against herb and heat. |
| **Cocktails, especially anything with gin** | The bar-nut context these come from. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. Rosemary and cayenne overwhelm them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make them:** 2 cups mixed nuts roasted at 350°F for 10 minutes.
2. **Toss the hot nuts** with 2 tbsp melted butter, 2 tbsp chopped fresh
   rosemary, 1/2 tsp cayenne, 2 tsp brown sugar, and 2 tsp flaky salt.
3. **Serve warm.** This is the entire point.
4. **Use fresh rosemary**, not dried. Dried rosemary tastes of dust here.
5. **Serve 1 oz per person** in a small bowl.
6. **Make them ahead** and reheat at 300°F for five minutes just before guests
   arrive.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Halve the cayenne. Too flat? More salt, and more rosemary
   than feels reasonable.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw mixed nuts** | Costco, bulk bins, Trader Joe's | Buy raw and season them yourself. This is the right answer. |
| **Fresh rosemary** | Produce section | Essential. Dried won't do. |
| **Trader Joe's rosemary marcona almonds** | Trader Joe's | The best ready-made shortcut in this category. |
| **Union Square Cafe bar nuts recipe** | Free online | The canonical version. Follow it exactly the first time. |
| **Za'atar or dukkah-spiced nuts** | Middle Eastern markets | A different and excellent direction. |

**Label check:** bought spiced nuts are usually stale and under-seasoned. If you
must buy, buy from somewhere with turnover and check the date.

**Skip:** anything with "seasoning" listed instead of actual herbs and spices.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the best spiced nut recipe for a cheese board?**
The Union Square Cafe bar nuts — rosemary, cayenne, brown sugar, butter and salt
on warm roasted nuts. It's the standard for a reason.

**Should spiced nuts be served warm?**
Yes. Warm nuts release far more aroma, and it's the only hot element on most
boards.

**Can I make them ahead?**
Yes — up to a week in an airtight container. Reheat at 300°F for five minutes
before serving.

**Fresh or dried rosemary?**
Fresh. Dried rosemary is woody and tastes dusty in this application.

**What cheese goes with spiced nuts?**
Manchego, aged cheddar, aged gouda and blue cheese.
