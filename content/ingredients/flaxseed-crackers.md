## The short version

A cracker made almost entirely of flax seed, held together by the seed's own
gel rather than flour. Gluten-free, very high in fat, and much more assertive
than it looks. Great with goat cheese and blue. Store it in the fridge once
it's open.

## What it is

Flax seeds release a mucilage when wet — a natural gel that binds without
gluten. Mix the seeds with water and a little seasoning, spread the slurry
thin, and dehydrate or bake it low until it's rigid. That's the entire product.
No flour is involved, which is why the category exists at all.

**Taste:** deeply nutty, earthy, faintly bitter
**Texture:** hard, brittle, snaps into rough shards
**Look:** dark brown, densely packed with whole seeds

Fat is the mechanism. Flax is roughly 40% oil, and that oil is what you taste —
rich, grassy, a little bitter at the finish. It also makes these crackers
substantially more filling than they look, and much shorter-lived than a
flour cracker.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Tangy and bright against earthy and oily. The pairing that sells this cracker. |
| **Blue cheese** | Both are bitter-edged. They meet rather than fight, and the cracker's fat softens the salt. |
| **Avocado and flaky salt** | The low-carb board's headline bite. Fat on fat, saved by the salt. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Oily fish and oily seed, with the cracker's bitterness cutting through. |
| **Sharp cheddar** | The cheese's acid slices the seed oil cleanly. |
| **Hummus and baba ganoush** | Sturdy enough to scoop and earthy enough to belong. |
| **Almond butter, honey** | The dessert direction. Bitter, sweet, rich. |

**Skip it with:** burrata, mozzarella and mild brie — the cracker buries them
completely. And be honest about the crowd: flaxseed crackers are a strong,
seedy, texture-heavy thing that not everyone enjoys. They're never the only
base.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Break them into rough shards.** They come as slabs, and jagged pieces suit
   them better than neat squares.
2. **Serve 2–3 per person.** They're heavy and filling; people rarely take a
   fourth.
3. **Always pair with a neutral cracker.** Consider this a specialty option,
   not a foundation.
4. **Set them beside the goat cheese and the avocado**, which is where they
   belong.
5. **Refrigerate the open package.** This is the one genuinely important tip on
   this page — flax oil oxidizes faster than almost any edible fat, and a
   rancid flax cracker tastes sharply bitter. The fridge roughly doubles its
   life.
6. **Fix:** if they taste harsh or paint-like, they've gone off. Toasting won't
   help. Throw the box out.

## Quick FAQ

**Are flaxseed crackers gluten-free?**
Most are, since they contain no flour. Check for a certification if a guest has
celiac disease.

**Why do they taste bitter?**
A little bitterness is normal — it's the seed oil. Sharp, harsh bitterness
means the box has gone rancid.

**Should I refrigerate them?**
After opening, yes. Flax oil oxidizes quickly, and the fridge roughly doubles
how long they stay good.

**How many per person?**
2–3. They're dense, oily and filling.

**Are they keto-friendly?**
Yes — very low net carbs and high fat. It's a large part of why the category
exists.
