## The short version

Small square slices of rye or pumpernickel sold specifically for parties.
They're soft bread, not a cracker, which makes them the only chewy base on most
boards — a genuine texture nobody else is providing. They also dry out within
the hour, so serve them late.

## What it is

Cocktail rye is ordinary deli rye baked in a narrow pan and sliced thin, giving
you roughly two-inch squares. It's usually sold in the bread aisle or the
refrigerated case, often alongside a pumpernickel version, and often frozen.

**Taste:** mildly sour, caraway-scented, faintly sweet
**Texture:** soft, moist, chewy
**Look:** small brown squares with a thin crust edge

Softness is the mechanism, and it's rarer on a board than you'd think. Look at
a typical spread: crackers, crisps, toasts, chips — all of them brittle. One
soft, chewy element changes how the whole board eats, in the same way a single
crunchy thing rescues a plate of soft food.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Corned beef, Swiss and mustard** | A Reuben in one bite. The bread is already the right bread. |
| **Cream cheese and smoked salmon** | Soft on soft, with the rye's sourness supplying the only edge. |
| **Hot crab or artichoke dip** | Warm dip on soft bread is a different pleasure from dip on a chip. |
| **Liverwurst and grainy mustard** | The traditional pairing. Rich, soft, sharp. |
| **Butter and radish** | Cultured butter, thin radish, flaky salt. Better on this than on a cracker. |
| **Pickled herring and dill** | Northern European board logic, exactly as intended. |
| **Sharp cheddar and horseradish** | The rye's caraway and the horseradish heat are old friends. |

**Skip it with:** wet dips and anything you'd need to scoop — it's soft bread
and it tears. And skip it on a board that has to sit out for two hours.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Put it out last, and only what you'll use in the first hour.** This is the
   fastest-drying item on any board.
2. **Cover the reserve with a damp paper towel** in a container. Bread stales
   by moisture loss, so a little humidity buys you an hour.
3. **Serve 2–3 slices per person.** A one-pound loaf gives you roughly 30
   slices — enough for ten to twelve people.
4. **Build a few open-faced bites and leave the rest plain.** Two or three
   assembled squares show guests what to do; the rest let them improvise.
5. **Toast option:** 5 minutes at 350°F turns it into a sturdy little crouton
   that behaves like a cracker. Useful if the party runs long.
6. **Fix:** dried-out slices toast beautifully. Don't try to revive them soft.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rubschlager Cocktail Rye** (16 oz) | Grocery bread aisle, deli case, sometimes frozen | The standard. Check three sections of the store — it moves around. |
| **Rubschlager Cocktail Pumpernickel** (16 oz) | Same | Darker and sweeter. Buy both and alternate them on the board. |
| **Store bakery rye, sliced thin and quartered** | Any grocery bakery | Ask the counter to slice it thin. Cheaper and fresher. |
| **Deli rye from a Jewish or German bakery** | Local bakery | Best flavor by a distance. Slice and quarter it yourself. |
| **Frozen cocktail bread** | Grocery freezer aisle | Keeps indefinitely. Thaw at room temperature the morning of. |

**Label check:** rye flour should appear high in the list. Many "rye" breads
are mostly wheat with caraway seeds and coloring.

**Skip:** anything sold as cocktail bread that's white and sweet. The rye is
the point.

## Quick FAQ

**Where do I find cocktail rye in the store?**
It hides. Check the bread aisle, the refrigerated deli case, and the freezer —
different stores shelve it in all three.

**How many slices per person?**
2–3. A 16 oz loaf holds around 30 slices.

**How do I keep it from drying out?**
Serve it late, keep the reserve covered with a damp towel, and put out only
what will be eaten in the first hour.

**Can I toast it?**
Yes — 5 minutes at 350°F. It becomes a sturdy cracker-like base, which is a
good rescue if the party is long.

**Rye or pumpernickel?**
Both. They look good alternated and they pair slightly differently — rye is
sharper, pumpernickel sweeter.
