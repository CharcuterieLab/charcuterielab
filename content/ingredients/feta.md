## The short version

Sheep's milk cheese aged in brine — salty, tangy, crumbly and much sharper than
the crumbled tubs suggest. Buy it as a block in its own brine, not pre-crumbled
and dry, and it becomes one of the best-value cheeses on any board.

## What it is

Real feta is a protected designation: made in Greece, from sheep's milk or a
sheep-and-goat blend, aged in brine for at least two months. Everything else is
"feta-style." The brine is not packaging — it's part of the aging, and it keeps
the cheese moist and salty right through.

**Taste:** salty, sharp, tangy, faintly barnyardy
**Texture:** firm and crumbly, creamy when good
**Look:** stark white blocks or slabs, no rind

Salt is the mechanism, and it works outward. Feta is salty enough to season
whatever it touches — tomatoes, watermelon, cucumber, olive oil. On a board
that means it functions less like a cheese to be tasted alone and more like a
seasoning with body, which is why almost every good feta pairing involves
something bland and wet.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Watermelon and mint** | Salt against water and sugar. The best summer bite in this section. |
| **Cucumber and tomato** | The Greek salad, taken apart. Feta does the seasoning. |
| **Olive oil and dried oregano** | Not a pairing so much as the traditional service. Pour it over a slab. |
| **Kalamata olives** | Brine on brine — both are aged in salt, and they read as one thing. |
| **Pita chips and sesame crackers** | Sturdy Mediterranean bases that can take a salty crumble. |
| **Honey and hot honey** | Sweet against sharp salt. Whipped feta with hot honey is a modern standard. |
| **Dry rosé, assyrtiko, retsina, ouzo** | Greek and high-acid. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses like burrata, which lose to the salt,
and rich sweet jams — feta wants clean sweetness like honey or fruit, not
preserve.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy the block in brine.** Pre-crumbled feta is dried and coated to stop
   clumping, and it's a noticeably worse cheese. This is the whole page.
2. **Serve it as a slab**, not crumbles — a rough rectangle with oil poured
   over it and oregano scattered on top looks like a dish.
3. **Or whip it:** feta, a splash of olive oil and a spoon of Greek yogurt in a
   food processor. Two minutes, and it becomes a dip.
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** An 8 oz block serves five to six.
5. **Rinse it briefly** if it's aggressively salty, then pat dry.
6. **Put it in a shallow dish** — it weeps brine and oil.
7. **Fix:** dry and squeaky? Sit it in a little olive oil for twenty minutes.
   It softens and takes on the oil.

| **Dodoni Feta** | Grocery cheese case, Greek markets | Widely imported Greek brand. Reliable. |
| **Mt Vikos Feta** | Whole Foods, grocery | Widely available and good. |
| **Bulgarian feta** | Cheese counters, Eastern European markets | Creamier and tangier than Greek. Many people prefer it. |
| **French feta** | Cheese counters | Milder and softer. The gentle entry point. |
| **Trader Joe's Feta in brine** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and decent. Buy the brined tub, not the crumbles. |

**Label check:** sheep's milk (or sheep and goat) and packed in brine. Cow's
milk "feta" is a different, blander cheese, and dry crumbles are a different
product again.

**Skip:** pre-crumbled feta in a plastic tub. It's dried, often anti-caked, and
it's the single biggest reason people think they don't like feta.

## Quick FAQ

**Is all feta made from sheep's milk?**
Real Greek PDO feta is sheep's milk, or sheep with up to 30% goat. Cow's milk
versions exist but can't legally be called feta in the EU.

**Block or crumbles?**
Block, in brine, always. Crumbles are dried and coated.

**Why is mine so salty?**
It's brine-aged — that's correct. Rinse it briefly and pat it dry if it's too
much.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz. An 8 oz block serves five to six.

**What's the easiest upgrade?**
Whip it with olive oil and yogurt, then pour hot honey over the top.
