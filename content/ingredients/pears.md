## The short version

Sweeter, softer and more floral than apples, and a better match for blue cheese
and hard aged cheese. The whole difficulty is timing: a pear ripens from the
core outward, so by the time the outside feels soft the middle is mush. Check
the neck.

## What it is

Bartlett is the classic yellow-when-ripe pear — juicy, floral, soft. Bosc is
brown, firmer and holds its shape when sliced, which makes it the best board
pear. Anjou is green, mild and slow-ripening. Comice is the sweetest and most
fragile. Concorde and Forelle are smaller and firmer.

**Taste:** honeyed, floral, mildly acidic, more perfumed than apple
**Texture:** soft and buttery when ripe, grainy near the core
**Look:** pale cream flesh with a green, brown or red skin edge

Ripening from the inside is the mechanism, and it's why pears are the trickiest
common fruit to buy. Ethylene works outward from the core, so a pear that feels
soft at the belly is already over. The neck — the narrow part near the stem —
firms up last and gives way first when ripe. Press there.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Gorgonzola, Stilton, Roquefort** | The great pairing. Sweet floral juice against salt and blue funk. Better than apple here. |
| **Parmigiano-Reggiano** | An Italian proverb tells farmers not to let on how good cheese and pears are together. |
| **Aged gouda and Comté** | Caramel and nut against honeyed sweetness. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat against soft sugar, as with melon. |
| **Walnuts and hazelnuts** | Tannin and crunch against soft flesh. |
| **Honey** | Sweet on sweet, which works because pears are floral rather than sugary. |
| **Sauternes, port, poire William** | Sweet wine and pear brandy. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, but be aware pears are soft and delicate — they lose
against a very sharp cheddar in a way apples don't.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Check ripeness at the neck.** Press gently just below the stem. It should
   give slightly. A soft belly means it's already overripe inside.
2. **Buy them firm and ripen at room temperature** for two to four days. Pears
   are one of the few fruits that genuinely ripen better off the tree.
3. **Slice thin lengthwise with the skin on**, and core as you go.
4. **Stop browning** the same way as apples — three minutes in lightly salted
   water, then drain and pat dry.
5. **Serve 3–4 slices per person.**
6. **Fan them beside the blue cheese.** That's the pairing worth making
   obvious.
7. **Fix:** grainy near the core? That's normal pear stone-cell texture. Cut
   around it.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bosc** | Any grocery, autumn–winter | Brown, firm, holds its shape when sliced. The best board pear. |
| **Bartlett** | Any grocery | Juicy and floral. Ripens fast — check daily. |
| **Anjou (green or red)** | Any grocery | Mild and slow to ripen. Forgiving. |
| **Comice** | Grocery, specialty, winter | The sweetest and most fragile. Superb, briefly. |
| **Forelle or Seckel** | Farmers markets, specialty | Small enough to halve and serve whole. Very pretty on a board. |
| **Farmers market pears** | Local, autumn | Almost always better — pears bruise in transit. |

**Label check:** press the neck, not the body. Bosc stay firm even when ripe,
so go by the neck and by smell — a ripe pear is faintly fragrant.

**Skip:** buying pears the same day you need them. They almost always need two
to four days on the counter.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I tell when a pear is ripe?**
Press the neck just below the stem. It should give slightly. Don't press the
belly — by the time that's soft, the core is mush.

**Which pear is best for a board?**
Bosc. It's firm enough to slice cleanly and holds its shape.

**Pear or apple with blue cheese?**
Pear. It's softer and more floral, and it suits blue better than apple's
sharpness.

**Do I peel them?**
No — the skin adds colour and it's thin enough to eat.

**How much per person?**
3–4 thin slices.
