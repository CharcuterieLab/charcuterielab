## The short version

The biggest nut in the bowl, and the one people pick up because of the size. On
their own they're creamy, mild and slightly waxy — pleasant rather than
memorable. The move that makes them worth buying is shaving them thin over cheese
with a vegetable peeler, which turns them into pale curls that look like truffle.

## What it is

The seed of a giant Amazonian tree, harvested from wild forests rather than
farms — genuinely a foraged crop. Sold shelled (whole or halves) or in shell.
Notably high in selenium, to the point that a couple a day is a full dose.

**Taste:** mild, creamy, faintly coconut, slightly waxy
**Texture:** dense and soft, oily, crumbly when shaved
**Look:** large pale-cream wedges, or thin translucent curls when shaved

The mechanism is scale and softness. Every other nut on a board is bite-sized;
a Brazil nut is a mouthful, which changes how people eat it. Shaved, that same
softness makes it behave like a hard cheese — thin curls that melt rather than
crunch, and read as a garnish rather than a nut.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp and crystalline against soft and creamy. The shaved version over cheddar is the best use of both. |
| **Stilton and blue cheese** | Mild fat absorbing salt, without the bitterness a walnut brings. |
| **Medjool dates** | Both dense and rich; the date supplies the sugar the nut lacks. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against cream. Shave the Brazil nut over it. |
| **Dried figs** | Sticky and seedy against smooth and oily. |
| **Dark chocolate** | The Christmas-bowl pairing, and it's correct. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. The nut's oil coats the palate and
buries them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Shave them with a vegetable peeler** over hard cheese. This is the whole
   argument for Brazil nuts on a board.
2. **Or toast whole** at 350°F for 8 minutes and salt — it sharpens a flat nut.
3. **Halve them lengthwise** if serving whole; a full Brazil nut is an awkward
   mouthful.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Buy in shell for a holiday board** — a bowl of mixed in-shell nuts with a
   cracker is a real thing, and Brazil nuts are the centrepiece.
6. **Keep them cold.** Very high oil content.
7. **Fix:** waxy and bland? Toasting fixes most of it. Salt while warm.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shelled Brazil nuts** | Grocery, bulk bins, Trader Joe's | The default. Buy whole, not pieces. |
| **In-shell Brazil nuts** | Grocery, autumn and Christmas | Freshest, and good board theatre with a nutcracker. |
| **Roasted salted Brazil nuts** | Specialty, bulk | Harder to find, and better than raw for a savoury board. |
| **Chocolate-covered Brazil nuts** | Confectioners | Dessert corner. Very good. |

**Label check:** rancidity is the main risk — the oil content is high and turnover
in shops is low. Smell before buying from bulk bins.

**Skip:** Brazil nut pieces, and any bag that has been on a warm shelf.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I serve Brazil nuts on a board?**
Shave them thin over hard cheese with a vegetable peeler, or toast and halve them
and serve whole.

**Do Brazil nuts need toasting?**
Not strictly, but 8 minutes at 350°F with salt makes a real difference — raw
ones are waxy and flat.

**Is it true you shouldn't eat too many?**
They're extremely high in selenium. A couple a day is a full dose; a large
handful daily over time is more than recommended. On a board it's a non-issue.

**What cheese goes with Brazil nuts?**
Aged cheddar, aged gouda, and blue cheeses like stilton.

**How do I store them?**
In the fridge or freezer. High oil content means they go rancid quickly.
