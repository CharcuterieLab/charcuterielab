## The short version

The thick caramel tub sold beside the apples every fall. It turns sliced
apples into a dessert board, kids love it and it costs a few dollars. The
grown-up move: put it next to sharp cheddar and pretzels and let guests
discover salty-sweet.

## What it is

Caramel dip is sugar and corn syrup cooked with milk, cream or sweetened
condensed milk and butter until thick and golden, then salted and sometimes
flavored with vanilla. It's thicker than the caramel sauce for ice cream so
it clings to an apple slice instead of dripping off.

**Taste:** rich, buttery and sweet with toasted-sugar notes
**Texture:** thick, smooth and sticky; it stretches as you pull away
**Look:** glossy golden brown

Two reactions make caramel. Heating sugar alone breaks it into hundreds of
new compounds, which is caramelization. Cooking sugar with milk proteins
adds the Maillard reaction, giving the deeper butterscotch and toffee
notes of dairy caramel. Corn syrup interferes with sugar crystals forming,
which keeps the dip smooth instead of grainy.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Apples** | Tart, crisp fruit against thick, sweet caramel. The original. |
| **Pears** | Softer and juicier, great in fall. |
| **Sharp aged cheddar** | Salty and sharp with sweet: the caramel apple with cheese. |
| **Mini pretzels** | Salted caramel in one dip. |
| **Graham crackers and chocolate-covered pretzels** | Build a dessert corner around it. |
| **Hot cider, coffee or a tawny port** | Warm, toasty drinks for toasty sugar. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and cured meats in the same corner. Caramel's
sweetness turns salami greasy and blue cheese harsh. Keep it on the sweet
side of the board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Scoop it into a small bowl** with a spoon; a tub on the board looks
   unfinished.
2. **Warm it 10 to 15 seconds** in the microwave and stir if you want it
   silkier.
3. **Top it with flaky salt,** chopped peanuts or mini chocolate chips.
4. **Serve 1 to 2 tablespoons per person.**
5. **Slice apples last** and keep them in lemon water until you plate them.
6. **Fix:** if it's gone too runny from heating, chill it 10 minutes.

**Label check:** "dip" means thick enough to cling. "Sauce" and "topping"
are thinner. "Salted caramel" versions pair even better with cheese.

**Skip:** caramel candies melted down at the last minute. They harden as
they cool and glue themselves to the board.

## Quick FAQ

**What is caramel dip?**
A thick, spoonable caramel made with sugar, corn syrup and milk or cream,
sold in tubs next to the apples. It's thicker than ice cream caramel sauce.

**Do you heat caramel dip?**
You can serve it cold or at room temperature. A few seconds in the
microwave makes it silkier, but it gets runny if overheated.

**What do you dip in caramel?**
Apples first, then pears, pretzels, graham crackers, bananas and sharp
cheddar.

**How do you stop apples from browning?**
Soak the slices for 5 minutes in water with a squeeze of lemon, or a
teaspoon of salt per cup, then pat dry.

**Does caramel dip need to be refrigerated?**
Most tubs from the produce section are refrigerated. Check the label; some
shelf-stable jars only need the fridge after opening.
