## The short version

Hummus does something no cheese or meat does — it makes the board filling. It's
also the reliable answer for vegan and vegetarian guests. The only mistake is
serving it flat in a tub: swirl it in a shallow bowl, pool olive oil in the
hollow, and put something on top.

## What it is

Chickpeas blended with tahini, lemon, garlic and olive oil. The ratio of tahini to
chickpea is what separates good hummus from paste — the best versions are close to
half tahini and taste nutty rather than beany.

**Taste:** nutty, lemony, garlicky, savoury
**Texture:** smooth and thick, ideally silky
**Look:** pale beige, swirled, with an oil pool and a topping

The mechanism is bulk with acid. Everything else on a board is intense and eaten
in small amounts; hummus is mild and eaten in quantity, which changes the board's
pacing. The lemon and tahini give it enough acid and bitterness that it still sits
correctly beside salty cheese rather than reading as filler.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pita chips and lavash** | The base it was designed for. Sturdy enough to scoop without snapping. |
| **Feta** | Brine and salt against mild nutty bulk — the standard mezze combination. |
| **Olives** | Two savoury things from the same table, and the brine cuts the tahini's fat. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweetness and smoke against lemon and garlic. Also the best-looking topping for it. |
| **Dukkah or za'atar** | Sprinkled on top. Turns a plain tub into something that looks made. |
| **Cucumber and raw vegetables** | The lighter half of a mezze board. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses and sweet jams. Garlic dominates a board's
neighbourhood.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Spread it in a shallow bowl** and swirl the back of a spoon through it to
   make a hollow rim.
2. **Pool good olive oil in the middle.** This is the difference between homemade-
   looking and tub-looking.
3. **Top it:** paprika, za'atar, dukkah, whole chickpeas, toasted pine nuts, or
   chopped parsley.
4. **Serve at room temperature.** Cold hummus is stiff and tastes of nothing.
5. **Serve 1 oz per person** — more than any other spread on the board.
6. **Give it its own corner** with its own scoops. Garlic transfers.
7. **Fix:** stiff and pasty? Beat in a tablespoon of cold water or lemon juice —
   it loosens and lightens immediately.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Local Middle Eastern deli hummus** | Middle Eastern grocers | Almost always the best. Higher tahini, better texture. |
| **Sabra or Cedar's** | Any grocery | The supermarket standard. Reliable, a bit thick. |
| **Trader Joe's hummus** | Trader Joe's | Good value, and their flavoured versions are decent. |
| **Roasted red pepper hummus** | Grocery | The most useful flavoured version on a board. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Canned chickpeas, good tahini, lemon, garlic, ice water. Ten minutes. |

**Label check:** tahini should be high in the ingredients. Anything led by
chickpeas and water with tahini near the end will taste flat.

**Skip:** hummus with visible separation in the tub, and anything with added
starch.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I make hummus look good on a board?**
Spread it in a shallow bowl, swirl a hollow into it with a spoon, pool olive oil
in the middle, and top with paprika, za'atar or toasted nuts.

**Is hummus vegan?**
Yes — chickpeas, tahini, lemon, garlic, oil. It's usually the safest thing on a
mixed board for vegan guests.

**Does hummus contain allergens?**
Sesame, from the tahini. That's a declared allergen in most countries and worth
mentioning.

**How much hummus per person?**
About an ounce — more than any other spread, because people eat it in quantity.

**Why is my hummus stiff?**
It's cold, or under-thinned. Bring it to room temperature and beat in a spoonful
of cold water or lemon juice.
