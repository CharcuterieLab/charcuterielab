## The short version

A semi-soft Spanish goat cheese bathed in red wine for three days, which dyes
the rind deep violet. The paste is mild, sweet and springy, with none of the
sharpness people expect from goat cheese. The purple rind is the most useful
color on any board.

## What it is

The real name is Queso de Murcia al Vino DOP, from Murcia in southeastern
Spain, made from Murciano-Granadina goat's milk. After pressing, the wheels are
soaked in Doble Pasta red wine — which colors the rind and lightly perfumes the
outer paste, but doesn't penetrate far.

**Taste:** mild, sweet, milky, faintly winey near the rind
**Texture:** semi-soft, smooth, springy, easy to slice
**Look:** stark white paste with a vivid purple-violet rind

Colour is the mechanism, and it's honest about being mostly visual. The wine
bath contributes a light fruity note at the very edge and dyes the rind
dramatically — but the cheese underneath is a mild pressed goat cheese. That's
not a criticism: a board needs one item that stops the eye, and white paste
against violet rind does it better than anything else available.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Membrillo (quince paste)** | Spanish and sweet against a mild, milky cheese. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Nut and salt with something soft and sweet. |
| **Fresh figs and grapes** | Fruit against a faintly winey rind. Also looks good beside the purple. |
| **Olive oil crackers** | Fruity and peppery, Mediterranean, and neutral enough not to compete. |
| **Honey** | Sweet on sweet works here because the cheese has so little acid. |
| **Jamón serrano** | Salt and cure giving a mild cheese an edge. |
| **Rioja, Tempranillo, Cava** | Spanish reds, echoing the wine in the rind. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese, which flattens it. It's a mild cheese and it
needs to be kept away from anything loud.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut thin wedges with the purple rind on the outer edge**, and fan them so
   the color reads as a band. This is the whole reason to buy it.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.**
3. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
4. **Put it where the board needs color** — usually next to pale cheeses and
   cream-colored crackers.
5. **Trim the rind before eating.** It's edible but it tastes mostly of dry
   wine tannin.
6. **Say what it is.** People assume purple means flavored, and it isn't — the
   paste is mild goat cheese.
7. **Fix:** if the rind is drying and flaking, trim it back. The paste
   underneath keeps well.

**Label check:** the DOP mark if you want the real Murcian cheese. Several
producers make wine-washed goat cheeses under similar names.

**Skip:** expecting it to taste strongly of wine. It doesn't, and buying it for
that reason leads to disappointment.

## Quick FAQ

**Does it taste like wine?**
Only faintly, right at the rind. The paste is mild, sweet goat cheese.

**Is the purple rind natural?**
Yes — it's stained by a three-day soak in red wine, not dyed.

**Can you eat the rind?**
It's edible but tastes of dry tannin. Most people trim it.

**Is it strong for a goat cheese?**
No, the opposite. It's one of the mildest goat cheeses available, with almost
none of the usual tang.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
