## The short version

The loudest fruit you can put on a board. Pineapple has enough acid to cut
through anything, which makes it excellent beside sharp aged cheese and cured
pork — and a bad idea next to cream, where its enzymes actively break protein
down and turn soft cheese slimy within the hour.

## What it is

A bromeliad, not a single fruit — each "eye" is a separate flower fused into one
body. Sold whole, pre-cut, or canned. **Golden / MD-2** is the sweet, low-acid
variety in most supermarkets; the older Smooth Cayenne is sharper.

**Taste:** very sweet, very acidic, faintly resinous
**Texture:** fibrous, extremely juicy
**Look:** gold wedges with a paler firm core

The mechanism is bromelain — a protease that breaks down protein. It's why fresh
pineapple stings your tongue after a few pieces, why it won't set in gelatine, and
why it should never sit against burrata. It's also why it cuts fatty cured pork so
cleanly: it's doing chemistry, not just contrast.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sharp. The cheddar's crystalline crunch survives the acid where a soft cheese wouldn't. |
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk fat and salt, cut hard by the acid. |
| **Prosciutto and coppa** | Fatty cured pork is what pineapple is best at. Pineapple and ham is a cliché because it works. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel notes plus tropical sugar; the cheese is dry enough to stand up. |
| **Chilli and lime** | Tajín on pineapple is a legitimate board move — salt and heat against sugar. |
| **Grilled, with blue cheese** | Grilling deactivates the enzyme and caramelises the sugar. Then it works with soft cheese. |

**Skip it with:** burrata, fresh mozzarella, ricotta, and brie. The enzyme
degrades them and the acid drowns them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut top and bottom flat**, stand it up, and slice the skin off in strips
   from top to bottom.
2. **Dig out the remaining eyes** with the tip of a knife, in diagonal rows.
3. **Quarter lengthwise and cut the core out.** The core is edible but woody.
4. **Cut small wedges, not chunks** — thin triangles about an inch across. Chunks
   are too much acid in one bite.
5. **Blot well.** Pineapple juice runs further than any other board fruit.
6. **Serve it in a small bowl or on its own plate**, not touching cheese.
7. **Fix:** underripe and mouth-burning? Grill or broil the wedges for a few
   minutes. Heat kills the bromelain and concentrates the sugar.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Whole golden pineapple** | Any grocery | Cheapest and best. Buy it gold-skinned, not green. |
| **Pre-cut pineapple spears** | Grocery refrigerated section | Fine, and a real time-saver. Cut the spears down into wedges. |
| **Grilled pineapple** | Make it | Different ingredient — caramel, no sting, safe next to soft cheese. |
| **Dried pineapple** | Bulk bins, Trader Joe's | Chewy and intense. Buy unsweetened; the sweetened kind is candy. |
| **Canned rings in juice** | Any grocery | Bromelain is destroyed by canning, so these are safe near soft cheese. Blot hard. |

**Label check:** smell the base. A ripe pineapple smells sweet at the bottom.
Pull test on the leaves is unreliable — go by colour and smell.

**Skip:** entirely green pineapples — they don't ripen further after picking,
they just soften.

## Quick FAQ

**Why does pineapple hurt my tongue?**
Bromelain, an enzyme that breaks down protein. Cooking or grilling destroys it.

**What cheese goes with pineapple?**
Aged cheddar, manchego, aged gouda — anything hard and sharp. Avoid soft fresh
cheeses; the enzyme degrades them.

**How do I cut pineapple for a board?**
Skin it, quarter it lengthwise, cut out the woody core, then cut small thin
wedges rather than chunks.

**Can I use canned pineapple?**
Yes, and it's safer next to soft cheese since canning destroys the enzyme. Buy
it in juice, not syrup, and blot it dry.

**How do I know if a pineapple is ripe?**
Gold rather than green skin, heavy for its size, and a sweet smell at the base.
