## The short version

Small rounds of soft leavened Indian flatbread, blistered from a hot oven. Warm
them for two minutes and you have the pillowy, chewy element that almost every
charcuterie board is missing. Widely stocked, cheap, and nearly impossible to
get wrong.

## What it is

Naan is a yeast-leavened flatbread, traditionally enriched with yogurt or milk
and slapped onto the wall of a tandoor, where fierce heat puffs it and chars it
in about a minute. The dairy and the leavening are what make it soft rather
than crisp — this is bread, not a flatbread cracker.

**Taste:** mild, faintly sweet and tangy from the yogurt
**Texture:** soft, pillowy, chewy, with a blistered surface
**Look:** small teardrop or oval rounds, puffed and charred in spots

Softness with structure is the mechanism. Focaccia is soft but rich; lefse is
soft but thin; cocktail rye is soft but dries out. Naan is soft, sturdy enough
to hold a topping, and forgiving about sitting on a board for an hour — which
makes it the most practical soft base in this whole section.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Hummus, raita, whipped feta** | Soft warm bread torn into a thick dip. It scoops where a cracker snaps. |
| **Mango chutney and sharp cheddar** | The chutney bridges the Indian bread and the British cheese. Genuinely excellent. |
| **Labneh with olive oil and za'atar** | Warm bread, cool thick yogurt cheese. Nothing else needed. |
| **Marinated paneer or halloumi** | Regional logic, and both are firm enough to sit on soft bread. |
| **Fresh goat cheese and honey** | Mild bread lets both lead. |
| **Cured meats** | Warm chewy bread against cold salty meat is a reliable contrast. |
| **Beer, riesling, gin and tonic** | Cold and slightly sweet against warm bread and spice. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses — burrata on warm bread turns to
liquid — and very dry aged cheeses, where soft-on-crumbly doesn't work. Naan is
also filling, so a little goes a long way on a board with a lot else on it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Warm it. Always.** 2 minutes at 400°F, or 30 seconds a side in a dry hot
   skillet. Cold naan is dense and slightly gluey; warm naan is excellent. This
   is the whole page.
2. **Brush with melted butter or ghee** while warm, and scatter flaky salt or
   nigella seeds. Ten seconds, large difference.
3. **Tear into strips or wedges**, don't cut. Torn edges hold dip better.
4. **Serve 1–2 mini naan per person**, torn.
5. **Put it in a napkin-lined bowl beside the board** to hold heat, rather than
   flat on the board where it cools and stiffens.
6. **Warm in batches** so there's a fresh one later in the party.
7. **Fix:** stiffened naan revives with a light spritz of water and 90 seconds
   in a hot pan under a lid.

| Product | Where | Notes |

| **Stonefire Original Naan** | Same | Full size. Tear into six pieces per round. |
| **Trader Joe's Mini Naan** | Trader Joe's (often frozen) | Cheap and reliable. Bakes from frozen in minutes. |
| **Deep Frozen Naan** | Indian groceries, Amazon | Closer to tandoor-baked. Better flavor if you can find it. |
| **Any Indian grocery** | Local | Fresh or frozen, better and cheaper than a supermarket. |
| **Indian restaurant takeaway** | Local | Genuinely an option — order a few naan the afternoon of and warm them at home. |

**Label check:** yogurt or milk in the ingredients means real naan. Versions
made without dairy are closer to a plain flatbread and taste flatter.

**Skip:** garlic naan on a cheese board. The garlic lingers and mutes every
cheese eaten after it — same problem as everything crackers.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I have to warm mini naan?**
Yes. It's the difference between a good board item and a dense, gluey one. Two
minutes at 400°F.

**How many per person?**
1–2 mini naan, torn into pieces.

**Naan or pita for a board?**
Naan is softer, richer and chewier; pita is drier and pocketed. For a soft warm
element, naan.

**Can I use garlic naan?**
On a dip board, yes. On a cheese board, no — the garlic overwhelms the cheese.

**Where do I find mini naan?**
The bakery or deli section of most supermarkets, or the freezer. Stonefire is
the most common brand.
