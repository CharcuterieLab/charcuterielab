## The short version

The pale wrinkled pepper from every Italian sub. Pepperoncini are barely spicy —
most of what you taste is vinegar and a mild vegetal sweetness — which makes them
useful as an acid without committing the board to heat. They cost about two
dollars and they last forever.

## What it is

A mild chilli (also called friggitello or Tuscan pepper) pickled in vinegar brine.
Greek versions tend to be slightly larger and milder; Italian ones are a little
sharper. Sold whole with stems, or sliced into rings.

**Taste:** tangy, mildly sweet, with very gentle heat
**Texture:** soft-crisp with thin walls
**Look:** pale yellow-green, wrinkled, two to three inches

The mechanism is acid with thin walls. Unlike a firm pickled vegetable, a
pepperoncini collapses easily and releases its brine into the bite — which is why
it works inside a sandwich and beside fatty salami rather than as a standalone
crunch. It's a delivery system for vinegar more than a texture ingredient.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Soppressata and Italian salami** | The sub, deconstructed. Fatty spiced pork cut by mild acid, with no competing heat. |
| **Provolone piccante and aged provolone** | Sharp piquant cheese and mild sharp pepper — the Italian deli pairing. |
| **Feta** | The Greek salad. Salt and brine against tang. |
| **Mortadella** | Soft, fatty, mild meat that needs an interruption. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Mild on mild, where the pepper's acid is the only sharp note. |
| **Italian sub and antipasto boards** | Where they belong, and where nobody has to be told what they're for. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses and French charcuterie. They're loud in a
vinegary way even if they're not hot.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain and blot.**
2. **Serve whole with stems** in a small bowl — the stems make them easy to pick
   up and look better than rings.
3. **Or use sliced rings** scattered over a meat board.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — two or three peppers.
5. **Warn nobody.** They're genuinely mild, which is the point.
6. **Stuff them** with provolone or cream cheese if you want them to do more.
7. **Fix:** too soft? An old jar. They should have some resistance.

| **Mezzetta pepperoncini** | Grocery | The widely available brand. Reliable. |
| **Greek pepperoncini** | Greek and Middle Eastern grocers | Slightly larger and milder. |
| **Sliced pepperoncini rings** | Grocery | Convenient for scattering. Softer. |
| **Stuffed pepperoncini** | Olive bars | Filled with provolone or cheese. Better than they sound. |

**Label check:** they should be pale yellow-green and reasonably firm. Anything
brown or collapsed in the jar is old.

**Skip:** nothing in particular. This is a hard ingredient to get wrong.

## Quick FAQ

**Are pepperoncini spicy?**
Barely. They're one of the mildest chillies used in pickling — mostly tangy and
vegetal.

**What's the difference between pepperoncini and banana peppers?**
Banana peppers are smoother, straighter and even milder. Pepperoncini are
wrinkled and slightly tangier. They're often used interchangeably.

**What do they go with?**
Italian salami, provolone, mortadella and feta — antipasto and Greek salad
ingredients.

**Whole or sliced?**
Whole with stems for a board; they look better and are easier to pick up. Rings
are for scattering.

**How much per person?**
Two or three peppers, about half an ounce.
