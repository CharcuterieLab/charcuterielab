## The short version

Small button or cremini mushrooms cooked and marinated in oil, vinegar, garlic and
herbs. They're meaty and savoury, they hold up for hours on a board, and they're
one of the few things a vegetarian guest can eat that still tastes substantial.
Almost nobody puts them out.

## What it is

Whole small mushrooms blanched or cooked, then packed in a marinade of olive oil,
white wine vinegar, garlic, bay, peppercorns and often parsley or oregano. Italian
*funghetti sott'olio* is the reference version.

**Taste:** savoury, tangy, garlicky, faintly earthy
**Texture:** firm and springy, denser than raw mushroom
**Look:** small pale brown caps, glossy with oil, flecked with herbs

The mechanism is glutamate plus acid. Mushrooms are one of the richest vegetable
sources of free glutamate, and the vinegar marinade adds the sharpness they lack
on their own. The combination gives you a savoury, acidic bite in one piece —
which is exactly what a cured meat does, without the meat.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Earthy savouriness against lactic tang, with the mushroom's firmness against the cheese's softness. |
| **Gruyère and comté** | Alpine cheeses and mushrooms are a fixed pairing in cooking; the same logic holds cold. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat with something savoury and acidic that isn't more meat. |
| **Baguette and crostini** | The oil is a dressing. Spoon a mushroom onto oiled bread. |
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Bloomy rinds carry mushroom notes already; this amplifies them. |
| **Antipasto boards** | Where they belong, alongside artichokes and peppers. |

**Skip it with:** sweet jams and fruit. Mushrooms want savoury company.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain**, keeping a little oil and some herbs clinging.
2. **Serve in a small bowl** with a pick or small spoon — they're slippery.
3. **Halve large ones** so they're a single bite.
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
5. **Serve at room temperature.** Cold oil goes cloudy and the flavour flattens.
6. **Dress with parsley and lemon zest** if they look dull from the jar.
7. **Fix:** rubbery? Over-cooked before marinating. Buy a better brand, or make
   your own — it takes fifteen minutes.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Funghetti sott'olio** | Italian delis, Eataly | Italian jarred mushrooms in oil. The best version. |
| **Olive bar marinated mushrooms** | Whole Foods, Italian delis | Fresh and well-dressed. Buy by weight. |
| **Jarred marinated mushrooms** | Any grocery | Cheap and reliable, if often over-vinegared. |
| **Marinated mixed mushrooms** | Specialty | Includes oyster and other varieties. More interesting texture. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Simmer button mushrooms 5 minutes in vinegar and water, drain, marinate in oil, garlic and herbs. |

**Label check:** olive oil over sunflower, and check the vinegar isn't the first
liquid listed — over-acidic jars taste harsh.

**Skip:** anything sold in water rather than oil or a proper marinade.

## Quick FAQ

**Are marinated mushrooms good on a charcuterie board?**
Yes — they're savoury, substantial and vegetarian, which makes them useful on a
board that would otherwise rely on cured meat for depth.

**What cheese goes with marinated mushrooms?**
Fresh goat cheese, gruyère and comté, and brie.

**Can I make them at home?**
Yes. Simmer button mushrooms in vinegar and water for five minutes, drain, then
marinate in olive oil with garlic and herbs. Ready the next day.

**Should they be served cold?**
Room temperature. Cold oil goes cloudy and mutes the flavour.

**How much per person?**
About three-quarters of an ounce — three or four small mushrooms.
