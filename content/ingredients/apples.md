## The short version

The cheapest acid on any board and the traditional partner for cheddar. Pick a
tart, firm variety, slice it thin, and stop it browning with a squeeze of lemon
or a brief salt-water soak. Mealy apples ruin the bite.

## What it is

Board apples need three things: acidity, firmness and a skin worth looking at.
Honeycrisp is crisp and balanced, Granny Smith is sharply tart and holds
colour, Pink Lady is dense and acidic, Braeburn is aromatic. Red Delicious has
none of those qualities and shouldn't be near a board.

**Taste:** sweet-tart, depending heavily on variety
**Texture:** crisp and snapping when good; mealy when old
**Look:** thin slices with a bright red or green skin edge

Malic acid is the mechanism, and it's the reason apple and cheddar is a
permanent pairing. Cheddar's own sharpness comes from lactic acid; apple brings
malic acid plus sugar and water. Together they scrub fat off the palate while
adding sweetness the cheese lacks.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | The oldest pairing in the category. Acid and sugar against salt and fat. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel notes in the cheese, fresh acid in the apple. |
| **Blue cheese** | Sweetness and acid blunting salt and funk. |
| **Camembert and Normandy cheeses** | Apples and Normandy cheese grow in the same place for a reason. |
| **Walnuts and pecans** | Tannin, crunch and acid — the whole autumn board. |
| **Cured ham and speck** | Salt against tart juice. |
| **Dry cider, Riesling, Calvados** | Apple with apple. It always works. |

**Skip it with:** nothing. Apples are as agreeable as grapes. The only mistake
is a bland mealy variety.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice thin — about ⅛ inch — with the skin on**, in wedges or half-moons.
   The coloured skin edge is what makes them look like fruit rather than filler.
2. **Stop the browning.** Toss slices in water with a squeeze of lemon, or a
   half-teaspoon of salt per cup of water for three minutes, then drain and pat
   dry. The salt method doesn't affect the taste and works better.
3. **Cut within an hour of serving** even with the soak.
4. **Serve 3–4 slices per person.**
5. **Fan them next to the cheddar.** Proximity is what gets people to try the
   pairing.
6. **Serve cold and crisp.**
7. **Fix:** already browning? A brief lemon-water dip stops it getting worse but
   won't reverse it. Slice a fresh apple instead.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Honeycrisp** | Any grocery, autumn | Crisp, balanced, holds up well. The safe choice. |
| **Granny Smith** | Any grocery, year-round | Sharply tart and slow to brown. Best with rich cheeses. |
| **Pink Lady / Cripps Pink** | Most grocery | Dense, acidic, excellent. Underrated on boards. |
| **Braeburn or Jazz** | Grocery | Aromatic and firm. Good all-rounders. |
| **Farmers market heirlooms** | Local, autumn | Ashmead's Kernel, Cox's Orange Pippin and similar are far better than supermarket varieties. |
| **Red Delicious** | Everywhere | Mealy, low-acid, thick-skinned. Avoid. |

**Label check:** heavy for its size and firm all over. Any give under your thumb
means mealy flesh underneath.

**Skip:** pre-sliced apples in a bag. They're treated to stop browning and they
taste of it.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I stop apple slices browning?**
Soak them for three minutes in salt water — half a teaspoon per cup — then
drain and pat dry. It works better than lemon and doesn't change the flavour.

**Which variety is best for a cheese board?**
Honeycrisp for balance, Granny Smith for tartness, Pink Lady for density. Avoid
Red Delicious.

**Do I peel them?**
No. The skin is the colour.

**How much per person?**
3–4 thin slices.

**Why do apple and cheddar work?**
Malic acid and sugar against lactic acid, salt and fat. The apple resets your
palate while adding the sweetness cheddar doesn't have.
