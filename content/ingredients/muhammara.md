## The short version

A Syrian dip of roasted red peppers and walnuts bound with breadcrumbs and
pomegranate molasses, with Aleppo pepper for warmth. It's sweet, sour, nutty and
mildly hot all at once, which is more than any other spread on this list manages —
and hardly anyone puts it on a board.

## What it is

Roasted red peppers blended with toasted walnuts, breadcrumbs, pomegranate
molasses, olive oil, cumin and Aleppo pepper. Thick, coarse, and deep red. Related
to romesco in construction — nuts and bread for body — but sweeter and sourer.

**Taste:** sweet roasted pepper, sour pomegranate, nutty, warmly spiced
**Texture:** coarse and thick, slightly grainy
**Look:** deep brick red, matte, coarse

The mechanism is pomegranate molasses. It's intensely sour and slightly bitter,
and it does what lemon can't — it adds acid with body and depth rather than
brightness. Combined with the pepper's sweetness and the walnut's tannin, the
result hits sweet, sour, bitter and savoury in one spoon.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pita chips and lavash** | The intended base. Thick enough to need something sturdy. |
| **Feta** | Salt and brine against sweet-sour and nutty. The mezze pairing that makes both better. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Lactic tang against fruit acid, with the walnut supplying texture neither has. |
| **Pomegranate seeds** | Scattered on top. The same flavour in fresh form, plus crunch and colour. |
| **Hummus and baba ganoush** | The three-dip mezze board. Pale, grey and red — they look right together. |
| **Grilled lamb and kebabs** | Pomegranate sourness cuts lamb fat, and the walnut echoes the char. The meal this dip was built around. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses and sweet jams. It's already sweet, sour and
spiced.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Spread in a shallow bowl** with a swirled hollow.
2. **Pool olive oil in the middle**, scatter walnuts and pomegranate seeds on top.
3. **Serve at room temperature.**
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
5. **Make it:** blend 2 jarred roasted red peppers, 1 cup toasted walnuts, 1/2 cup
   breadcrumbs, 2 tbsp pomegranate molasses, 1 tsp Aleppo pepper, cumin, olive oil
   and salt. Keep it coarse.
6. **Buy pomegranate molasses** — nothing substitutes for it, and one bottle
   lasts years.
7. **Fix:** flat? More pomegranate molasses. It's almost always the missing note.

| **Pomegranate molasses** | Middle Eastern grocers, Whole Foods | Essential for homemade. Cheap and keeps forever. |
| **Aleppo pepper** | Middle Eastern grocers, spice shops | Mild, fruity heat. Chilli flakes are a poor substitute but workable. |
| **Jarred roasted red peppers** | Any grocery | Fine for homemade. Drain them well. |
| **Ajvar** | Balkan grocers | A related pepper spread without nuts. Cheaper and widely available. |

**Label check:** walnuts and pomegranate molasses should both be in the
ingredients. Without either it's just a pepper dip.

**Skip:** anything smooth. Texture from the nuts and breadcrumbs is essential.

## Quick FAQ

**What is muhammara?**
A Syrian dip of roasted red peppers and walnuts with pomegranate molasses,
breadcrumbs and Aleppo pepper. Sweet, sour, nutty and mildly hot.

**Is muhammara spicy?**
Mildly. Aleppo pepper gives fruity warmth rather than sharp heat.

**What's the difference between muhammara and romesco?**
Both are pepper-and-nut dips, but muhammara uses walnuts and pomegranate molasses
and is sweeter and sourer; romesco uses almonds and smoked paprika.

**Does it contain nuts?**
Yes — walnuts. It's a tree nut allergen worth flagging on a board.

**What do you serve it with?**
Pita chips, flatbread, feta, fresh goat cheese, and raw vegetables.
