## The short version

A soft, pale pink, cold-smoked German sausage that spreads like butter. Mild,
smoky and faintly tangy. The name comes from the afternoon tea table, where it
was served on bread — which is still exactly the right way to eat it.

## What it is

Teewurst is finely ground pork and bacon — often around a third fat — mixed
with salt and spices, cased, cold-smoked over beech, and then briefly ripened.
That short fermentation gives it a gentle tang. Rügenwalder Teewurst is the
best-known style, and the sausage carries a German PGI.

**Taste:** mildly smoky, savoury, faintly tangy, gently spiced
**Texture:** very soft and smooth, spreadable straight from the fridge
**Look:** pale salmon-pink paste, sometimes in a small cloth-tied casing

High fat plus fine grinding is the mechanism. Grinding pork and bacon to a
paste and keeping the fat content high produces a spread that never firms up,
so the sausage stays soft without needing to be cooked or emulsified. Cold
smoke then flavours it without setting it.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pumpernickel or cocktail rye** | Dark sour bread against a mild smoky spread. The German service. |
| **Pickled onions and gherkins** | Acid cutting a fatty paste. Essential. |
| **Grainy mustard and horseradish** | Sharpness and heat against a soft, mild, fatty spread. |
| **Radishes and butter** | The German bread plate, and the pepper of the radish helps. |
| **Emmental** | Mild cheese alongside a mild spread. |
| **Chives and cracked pepper** | Scatter on top — it needs a little lift. |
| **Dark beer, dry Riesling** | Malt sweetness or acid, both of which cut smoked pork fat. |

**Skip it with:** smoked gouda or any second smoked item, and delicate fresh
cheeses. It's mild but it's still a smoked pork paste.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve it in a small bowl or in its casing with a knife.** It's a spread,
   not a slice.
2. **Temper 20 minutes** so it loosens.
3. **Scatter chives and cracked pepper over the top.** Plain teewurst looks and
   tastes underfinished.
4. **Serve three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce per person.**
5. **Dark bread directly beside it**, not crackers — the sourness matters.
6. **Peel the casing** and scoop the paste out.
7. **Fix:** too soft and greasy at room temperature? Serve it cooler; unlike
   sobrasada it doesn't need warming.

**Label check:** pork and bacon high in the list, and a smooth pale pink paste.
*Streichfähig* means spreadable.

**Skip:** buying a large amount. It's rich and an ounce per person is generous.

## Quick FAQ

**Why is it called tea sausage?**
It was traditionally served on bread at the afternoon tea table.

**Is it raw?**
It's cold-smoked and briefly fermented rather than cooked — a raw cured sausage,
like a very soft salami.

**How is it different from liverwurst?**
No liver. Teewurst is pork and bacon; liverwurst is liver-based and much more
mineral.

**How much per person?**
Three-quarters of an ounce to an ounce.

**What do I serve it on?**
Dark rye or pumpernickel, with pickled onions and mustard alongside.
