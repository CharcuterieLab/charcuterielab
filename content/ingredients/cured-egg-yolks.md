## The short version

Raw egg yolks buried in a mix of salt and sugar for about a week, then dried
until they're firm, translucent and bright gold. Grated with a microplane,
they fall like golden snow and taste rich, salty and savory. It's a
chef's trick that costs pennies and makes a plain bowl of burrata look like
a restaurant dish.

## What it is

Curing egg yolks is an old technique from Asian and European kitchens that
has become a modern chef favorite. Yolks are nestled into a bed of salt and
sugar, covered and refrigerated for four to seven days. Then they're rinsed
and dried in a low oven or in the fridge until firm. Many people call them
"egg yolk bottarga."

**Taste:** rich, salty, savory, like an eggy aged parmesan
**Texture:** firm and fudgy when sliced; fine and fluffy when grated
**Look:** translucent amber-gold spheres, bright orange-gold when grated

Osmosis is the mechanism. Salt and sugar draw water out of the yolk through
its membrane, while salt moves in. As the yolk loses water, its proteins and
fats concentrate and set into a firm gel. What's left is the yolk's natural
richness plus a lot of salt, packed into a form you can grate.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | Golden shavings on white cream. Rich on rich, with a salty finish. |
| **Crostini with olive oil** | A crunchy, plain base that lets the yolk shine. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Mild cheese that needs seasoning. |
| **Asparagus or crudités** | The classic egg partner, for a spring board. |
| **Good olive oil** | Fat carries the savory flavor. |
| **Champagne, Chablis or a dry sherry** | Bright acid against rich, salty yolk. |

**Skip it with:** dessert items. It's salty and savory, and the sugar in the
cure isn't enough to make it sweet.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Start a week ahead.** Mix equal parts kosher salt and sugar, make
   wells, slide in the yolks and cover with more cure.
2. **Refrigerate 4 to 7 days** until the yolks feel firm, like gummy candy.
3. **Rinse, pat dry and dry them in the oven at its lowest setting** (150 to 170°F) for about an hour, or
   uncovered in the fridge for a day.
4. **Serve about half a yolk per person,** grated.
5. **Grate it over burrata or crostini at the table** with a microplane.
6. **Fix:** if a yolk is still soft in the center, return it to the fridge
   uncovered for another day.

**Label check:** the freshest eggs you can find. Older yolks are more
likely to break when you handle them.

**Skip:** cracking the yolks straight into the cure. Separate each into a
small bowl first so a broken one doesn't ruin the batch.

## Quick FAQ

**What are cured egg yolks?**
Raw egg yolks buried in salt and sugar for several days. The cure draws out
moisture until the yolks are firm enough to grate, like a hard cheese.

**What do they taste like?**
Rich, salty and savory, a bit like aged parmesan with an eggy richness.
They're often compared to bottarga.

**Are cured egg yolks safe to eat?**
They're raw yolks preserved by salt and drying. Use very fresh eggs,
pasteurized if you want extra safety, keep them in the fridge and avoid them
for pregnant guests.

**How long do they keep?**
About a month in an airtight container in the fridge.

**Can I buy them?**
They're rarely sold. They're easy to make at home with eggs, salt and sugar.
