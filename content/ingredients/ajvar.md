## The short version

Red peppers roasted until the skins blister, peeled, then cooked down with
aubergine, oil and vinegar until thick. Every Balkan household makes it in autumn
and eats it all winter. On a board it is the cheapest way to get sweetness and
smoke into the same spoonful, and a jar costs about three dollars.

## What it is

A relish rather than a dip. The peppers are roasted whole over fire, which is
where the smoke comes from, then peeled and milled with aubergine, sunflower or
olive oil, vinegar and salt. Good ajvar is thick enough to hold a spoon mark.

**Taste:** sweet roasted pepper, smoke, and a gentle vinegar tang at the end
**Texture:** thick and slightly coarse, spreadable
**Look:** deep orange-red, matte, holds its shape

The mechanism is roasted sugar plus reduction. Charring converts the pepper's
sugars and drives off water; cooking it down does it again. The result is several
times sweeter and deeper than a raw pepper, with smoke layered on — which is why a
spoonful of ajvar reads as a whole cooked dish rather than a condiment.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Chalky lactic tang against sweet smoke. The single most reliable thing to do with ajvar. |
| **Feta** | Salt against sugar, with the brine cutting the oil. The Balkan plate, essentially. |
| **Kielbasa and smoked sausage** | Smoked meat and smoked pepper are the same idea twice. It works because both are savoury rather than sweet. |
| **Sourdough and rustic bread** | Ajvar was invented to go on bread. A thick slice is still the best delivery. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Doubling down. The whole pepper beside the spread version reads as deliberate. |
| **Halloumi** | Squeaky salt against sweetness, and the oil in the ajvar keeps the cheese from feeling dry. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and anything sweet. Ajvar already carries the sugar.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Open it an hour ahead** if it has been refrigerated. Cold ajvar is stiff and
   tastes of nothing.
2. **Stir it.** The oil rises.
3. **Serve in a shallow bowl** with a spoon. It is too thick to scoop with a
   cracker without breaking it.
4. **Swirl olive oil over the top** and add cracked pepper.
5. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
6. **Put sturdy bread beside it**, not thin crackers.
7. **Fix:** tastes flat? A squeeze of lemon or a splash of red wine vinegar. Most
   jarred ajvar is a little under-acidified for a board.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild ajvar (blago)** | Balkan and Eastern European grocers | The default. Sweet, smoky, no heat. |
| **Hot ajvar (ljuta)** | Balkan grocers | Chili added. Clearly labelled and genuinely warm. |
| **Homemade / market ajvar** | Farmers markets, delis | Coarser and much better. Buy it when you see it. |
| **Pindjur** | Balkan grocers | Ajvar's chunkier cousin with tomato. A different spread, worth knowing. |
| **Supermarket own-brand** | Larger grocery chains | Thinner and sweeter. Fine, but drain the oil off the top. |

**Label check:** peppers first, aubergine second. If tomato paste or sugar is high
in the list you are buying a sweeter, thinner product.

**Skip:** anything watery enough to run on the plate. Good ajvar sits still.

## Quick FAQ

**What does ajvar taste like?**
Sweet roasted pepper with smoke and a little acid. Closer to a thick pepper relish
than to a tomato sauce.

**Is ajvar spicy?**
Mild ajvar is the default and is not spicy at all. Hot ajvar — ljuta — has chili
added and is clearly labelled.

**What is the difference between ajvar and pindjur?**
Pindjur adds tomato and is usually chunkier. Ajvar is peppers and aubergine, and
smoother.

**What cheese goes with ajvar?**
Fresh goat cheese and feta. Salty white cheese against sweet roasted pepper is the
whole idea.

**How much per person?**
About three-quarters of an ounce. It is a spread, so people take more of it than
they think.
