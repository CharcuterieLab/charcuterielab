## The short version

Thick, hard, extra-crunchy potato chips. Kettle chips don't snap in a thick
dip, they stand up to smoked fish and caviar, and they turn French onion dip
into the most popular corner of the board. A plain, sea-salt bag is all you
need.

## What it is

Kettle chips are potato chips fried in small batches in a kettle of oil
rather than on a continuous conveyor. Cape Cod Potato Chips started making
them in 1980 in Hyannis, Massachusetts, and the style spread across the
country. Most are made from thick slices of russet or chipping potatoes
with the starch left on.

**Taste:** salty, toasty and deeply potato-y
**Texture:** thick, hard and shattering, with folded, curled edges
**Look:** golden with darker brown edges and lots of curls

The crunch comes from the batch. When a load of cold potato slices drops
into the kettle, the oil temperature falls, then slowly climbs back up. The
slices cook longer at a lower temperature, losing water gradually, so the
starch sets harder and the slices curl and stick together. Continuous fryers
hold one high temperature and make thinner, lighter chips.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **French onion or ranch dip** | Thick chips scoop thick dips without breaking. |
| **Pimento cheese** | Southern spread on a sturdy chip. |
| **Smoked salmon or caviar** | Salty crunch and rich fish. The high-low classic. |
| **Beef jerky** | Salt and smoke for a game-day board. |
| **Dill pickles** | Sour crunch with salty crunch. |
| **Champagne, lager or a dry rosé** | Bubbles and acid cut through fried fat. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses. Chips are too salty and loud for
burrata or fresh mozzarella.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Open the bag right before guests arrive.** Chips go stale fast.
2. **Pick out the biggest, flattest chips** for the board and save broken
   ones for snacking.
3. **Serve about an ounce per person,** in a bowl or a loose pile beside the
   dip.
4. **Keep them away from wet foods** like pickles and olives.
5. **Refill from the bag** instead of piling everything out at once.
6. **Fix:** if they've lost their crunch, bake them at 350°F for 3 to 5
   minutes, then cool.

## Quick FAQ

**What makes kettle chips different from regular chips?**
They're fried in small batches, so the oil temperature drops when the
potatoes go in and the chips cook longer. That makes them thicker, harder
and crunchier.

**Are potato chips okay on a charcuterie board?**
Yes. Kettle chips are sturdy enough for thick dips and pair especially well
with smoked fish, caviar and onion dip.

**Are kettle chips gluten-free?**
Plain kettle chips are usually potatoes, oil and salt. Flavored ones can
contain wheat or malt vinegar, so check the label.

**Do potato chips go with caviar?**
Yes. Salty, crisp, plain kettle chips with a spoon of crème fraîche and
caviar is a popular high-low pairing.

**How do you keep chips crisp on a board?**
Add them right before serving, keep them away from wet foods, and refill
from the bag rather than piling them all out at once.
