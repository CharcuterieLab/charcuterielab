## The short version

Aubergine burnt black over a flame, scooped out, and blended with tahini, lemon
and garlic. It's lighter than hummus and much smokier, and on a board it does the
job smoked meat does for carnivores — it's the vegan element that carries real
depth rather than just bulk.

## What it is

Whole aubergines charred until the skin blackens and the flesh collapses, then
mashed or blended with tahini, lemon juice, garlic and olive oil. The Levantine
*mutabbal* is the same dish with more tahini and yoghurt.

**Taste:** smoky, nutty, lemony, faintly bitter
**Texture:** looser and stringier than hummus, sometimes chunky
**Look:** pale grey-beige, glossy with oil, swirled

The mechanism is char. Burning the skin drives smoke compounds into the flesh —
the same phenolic compounds that make smoked meat taste smoked. On a vegetarian
board that's the missing element: not just savoury, but the specific smoky depth
that otherwise only comes from cured meat.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pita chips and lavash** | Warm flatbread is the intended base. It's looser than hummus, so it needs something with a rim. |
| **Feta** | Salt and brine against smoke and tahini — the mezze pairing. |
| **Olives** | Same table, and the brine cuts the tahini's fat. |
| **Pomegranate seeds** | Scattered on top. Acid and crunch against something soft and smoky, plus the only colour it has. |
| **Whipped feta** | Two dips that go together: one salty and white, one smoky and grey. |
| **Grilled lamb and kofta** | Its native meal. Relevant if the board is a mezze board. |

**Skip it with:** delicate cheeses and sweet jams. Smoke and garlic dominate
their neighbours.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Spread in a shallow bowl** with a swirled hollow.
2. **Pool olive oil in the middle** and scatter parsley, sumac or pomegranate
   seeds on top.
3. **Serve at room temperature.**
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person.**
5. **Make it:** char whole aubergines directly on a gas flame or under a broiler
   until the skin is black and the flesh is collapsed — 15 minutes, turning.
   Scoop out the flesh, drain it, then blend with tahini, lemon and garlic.
6. **Drain the flesh properly.** Watery baba ganoush is the most common failure.
7. **Fix:** bland? Not charred enough. The blackened skin is where the flavour
   comes from, so take it further than feels right.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Middle Eastern deli baba ganoush** | Middle Eastern grocers | Almost always better than supermarket versions. Properly smoky. |
| **Sabra or Cedar's baba ganoush** | Grocery | Widely available. Milder and less smoky than the real thing. |
| **Mutabbal** | Middle Eastern grocers | The yoghurt-and-tahini version. Creamier and richer. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Two aubergines, tahini, lemon, garlic. The char is the only step that matters. |

**Label check:** aubergine should be first, tahini second. Anything led by oil or
yoghurt will taste flat.

**Skip:** versions with mayonnaise, and anything that looks pale and pasty rather
than grey and loose.

## Quick FAQ

**What is baba ganoush?**
Charred aubergine blended with tahini, lemon and garlic. Smokier and lighter than
hummus.

**Is baba ganoush vegan?**
Traditionally yes. The Levantine variant *mutabbal* often includes yoghurt, so
check if it matters.

**Why does mine taste bland?**
Not charred enough. The aubergine skin should be completely blackened and the
flesh fully collapsed before you scoop it.

**Why is it watery?**
Aubergine holds a lot of liquid. Drain the scooped flesh in a sieve for ten
minutes before blending.

**What do you serve it with?**
Pita chips, warm flatbread, feta, olives, and raw vegetables.
