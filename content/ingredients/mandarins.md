## The short version

The cheap, reliable winter fruit. No knife, no browning, no juice on the board —
peel and separate into segments and you're done. Clementines are the sturdiest;
satsumas peel most easily but fall apart faster.

## What it is

An umbrella name for small, loose-skinned, easy-peel citrus: clementines,
satsumas, tangerines, and the branded hybrids sold as Cuties and Halos. All are
sweeter and less acidic than an orange, with thinner membranes.

**Taste:** sweet, mildly acidic, floral
**Texture:** tender segments that separate cleanly
**Look:** small orange crescents, arranged in a loose fan

The mechanism is portability. A mandarin segment is the only citrus you can pick
up with your fingers and eat in one bite without dripping — the membrane holds
the juice in until you bite. That makes citrus available to a board where slices
of orange would be too wet.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie** | Sweet-acid juice against soft fat; the classic winter version of grapes and brie. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | Mild curd plus sweet citrus — this is basically dessert. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and sugar, with the segment doing the same job as melon in July. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against bright citrus; the contrast is bigger than it sounds. |
| **Almonds and marcona almonds** | Citrus and almond are a Mediterranean fixture; salt links them. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Orange and chocolate, on a board that runs to dessert. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and washed-rind cheeses. Sweet citrus makes both
taste metallic.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Peel and separate into segments.** Don't slice mandarins crosswise —
   the membranes are too thin to hold.
2. **Pull off the white pith strings.** Thirty seconds, and it's the difference
   between tidy and scruffy.
3. **Fan the segments** in a curve, or pile them into a small bowl.
4. **Leave one whole, unpeeled, on the board** for colour and to signal what
   the segments are.
5. **Serve cold.** Citrus is better straight from the fridge.
6. **Prep within an hour** — segments dry at the edges after that.
7. **Fix:** seedy? You've got tangerines rather than clementines. Buy Halos,
   Cuties, or anything labelled seedless.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Clementines (Cuties, Halos)** | Any grocery, Nov–Mar | The default. Seedless, sturdy segments, cheap by the bag. |
| **Satsumas** | Grocery, farmers markets, winter | Sweetest and easiest to peel. Delicate — handle gently. |
| **Sumo Citrus / Dekopon** | Grocery, Jan–Apr | Large, bumpy-topped, exceptionally sweet. Expensive and worth it once. |
| **Tangerines** | Grocery, winter | More acidic and often seedy. Better flavour, more work. |
| **Kishu mandarins** | Farmers markets, specialty | Tiny, seedless, intensely sweet. Beautiful on a board. |

**Label check:** buy loose rather than bagged when you can — bags hide soft
fruit at the bottom. Heavy for size, tight skin, no soft spots.

**Skip:** anything with skin puffed away from the flesh; that fruit is drying out.

## Quick FAQ

**Clementines or satsumas?**
Clementines hold their segments better on a board. Satsumas taste sweeter and
peel more easily but fall apart faster.

**Do I slice mandarins?**
No — peel and separate into segments. The membranes are too thin to slice.

**Are Cuties and Halos different fruit?**
They're brand names for clementines, and sometimes murcott mandarins later in
the season.

**When are mandarins in season?**
November through March, with Sumo Citrus arriving January through April.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz — one mandarin covers two or three people on a mixed board.
