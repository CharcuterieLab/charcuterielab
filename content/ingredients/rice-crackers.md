## The short version

Japanese rice crackers — senbei and arare — glazed with soy and often wrapped
in seaweed. Light, savory and usually gluten-free, with one important
exception: traditional soy sauce contains wheat, so plenty of rice crackers do
too. Read the box before you promise a guest they're safe.

## What it is

Senbei are the large flat rice crackers, arare the small ones. Both are made
from steamed and pounded rice, dried, then baked or fried and brushed with a
soy-based glaze. Some are wrapped in a band of nori; some are dusted with
sesame or chili.

**Taste:** savory, salty-sweet, toasted, sometimes marine
**Texture:** very light, airy, sharp snap
**Look:** small and glossy, often amber-glazed, sometimes nori-wrapped

Glutamates are the mechanism. Soy sauce and nori are both loaded with them,
which means a rice cracker arrives with umami already in place. On a board
that's a real asset next to mild things and a liability next to a cheese that
was going to supply the savoury depth itself.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese and cucumber** | Cool, mild and wet against light, salty and dry. The easiest fusion bite there is. |
| **Smoked salmon and avocado** | Soy, sesame and salmon is an established combination; the cracker just supplies the crunch. |
| **Aged gouda** | Surprising and excellent. Both have deep caramel-savory notes from long processing. |
| **Wasabi or yuzu kosho, in tiny amounts** | Heat that clears rather than lingers, on a base that won't muddy it. |
| **Pickled ginger and daikon** | Acid and crunch against salt. A palate reset in one bite. |
| **Edamame hummus or miso dip** | Same flavor family, no translation required. |
| **Sake, dry riesling, sparkling wine** | Clean and high-acid drinks handle the soy glaze well. |

**Skip it with:** fruit jams and honey — soy glaze and sweet preserve is a
confused bite — and rich European cheeses like triple-crème or blue, where the
soy fights the dairy rather than supporting it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Give them their own bowl.** They're small, they're a different cuisine,
   and mixing them into a European cracker fan reads as a mistake.
2. **Serve 5–6 per person.** They're tiny and light — people take a handful.
3. **Put out the nori-wrapped ones last.** Seaweed pulls moisture from the air
   and softens within an hour of opening.
4. **Anchor a fusion corner** with them — cucumber, smoked salmon, sesame — so
   the board reads as two deliberate halves rather than one confused middle.
5. **Don't top them ahead.** The glaze goes tacky under anything wet.
6. **Fix:** softened rice crackers re-crisp at 300°F for 3 minutes, nori and
   all.

**Label check:** this is the important one. Traditional soy sauce is brewed
with wheat, so a rice cracker glazed with it is **not gluten-free**. Look for
tamari, an explicit gluten-free mark, or a plain unglazed cracker.

**Skip:** the heavily seasoned snack mixes with peanuts and dried peas. Fine
snacks, wrong shape and flavor for a board base.

## Quick FAQ

**Are rice crackers gluten-free?**
Often, but not reliably. The soy glaze usually contains wheat. Check for a
gluten-free label rather than assuming.

**What cheese goes with rice crackers?**
Cream cheese and aged gouda. Skip the rich European cheeses.

**How many per person?**
5–6. They're small and light.

**Why did the seaweed ones go soft?**
Nori is hygroscopic — it pulls moisture from the air fast. Open that bag last.

**Are senbei and arare the same thing?**
Same family, different size. Senbei are the large flat ones; arare are the
small bite-size pieces.
