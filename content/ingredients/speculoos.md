## The short version

Belgian caramelised biscuits — Biscoff is the brand everyone knows. Thin, very
crisp, and flavoured with caramelised sugar and cinnamon rather than ginger.
They're the friendliest sweet base on a board: everybody has had one, everybody
likes them, and they cost almost nothing.

## What it is

A thin spiced shortcrust biscuit made with brown or caramelised sugar and a warm
spice blend — cinnamon dominant, with nutmeg, clove and cardamom. Traditionally
Belgian and Dutch, baked for St Nicholas. Also sold as a spread, which is a
different ingredient.

**Taste:** caramelised sugar, cinnamon, warm spice, faintly toffee
**Texture:** thin and very crisp, snaps sharply
**Look:** deep caramel brown, often stamped with a pattern

The mechanism is caramelisation rather than added flavour. The sugar is cooked to
a caramel stage in the dough, which produces the toffee note that makes speculoos
recognisable. Because that's a browning flavour rather than a sweet one, it sits
next to aged cheese — which develops the same kind of compounds — more naturally
than a plain sugary biscuit does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged gouda** | The standout. Both taste of butterscotch and caramel — they're the same flavour in two textures. |
| **Mascarpone** | Crisp caramel biscuit under soft sweet cream. Effectively tiramisu components. |
| **Gorgonzola** | Sweet spice against salt and funk, with a snap that blue cheese lacks. |
| **Brie** | Soft fat with a thin crisp sweet base, where the spice does what a jam would. |
| **Apple butter** | Cinnamon on cinnamon, and the caramel deepens it. |
| **Coffee** | Its actual purpose. Useful if the board runs late. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and sharp savoury spreads.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve them whole.** They're thin and already the right size.
2. **Fan them near the sweet corner** and beside the aged gouda.
3. **Unwrap them** if they came individually packaged.
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person** — two or three biscuits.
5. **Keep them dry.** Thin biscuits soften fast.
6. **Put the spread out too** if the board runs to dessert — speculoos spread on a
   plain cracker is a legitimate move.
7. **Fix:** soft? 300°F for four minutes, then cool completely. They're thin, so
   watch them.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lotus Biscoff** | Any grocery | The one everyone knows. Cheap, consistent, individually wrapped. |
| **Belgian speculoos** | Belgian and European delis | Thicker and more spiced. Better. |
| **Dutch speculaas** | Dutch delis, IKEA | Often with almonds, and more heavily spiced. |
| **Speculoos spread (cookie butter)** | Grocery, Trader Joe's | The spreadable version. A dessert-corner ingredient in its own right. |
| **Pepparkakor** | IKEA, Scandinavian delis | Thinner and ginger-forward. See gingersnaps. |

**Label check:** caramelised sugar should be in the ingredients — that's what
makes it speculoos rather than a plain spiced biscuit.

**Skip:** chocolate-coated versions for use as a base.

## Quick FAQ

**What is speculoos?**
A thin, crisp Belgian biscuit made with caramelised sugar and warm spice. Biscoff
is the best-known brand.

**Is Biscoff the same as speculoos?**
Biscoff is a brand of speculoos. Traditional Belgian and Dutch versions are often
thicker and more heavily spiced.

**What's the difference from gingersnaps?**
Speculoos leads with cinnamon and caramelised sugar; gingersnaps lead with ginger
and molasses and have more bite.

**What cheese goes with speculoos?**
Aged gouda above all — they share butterscotch notes — plus mascarpone, brie and
blue cheese.

**How many per person?**
Two or three, about three-quarters of an ounce.
