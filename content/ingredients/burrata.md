## The short version

A skin of fresh mozzarella wrapped around a filling of cream and torn curd.
Cut it and the middle runs out. It's the best centerpiece on a summer board and
the shortest-lived thing you'll buy — days, not weeks.

## What it is

Burrata was invented in Puglia in the early 1900s as a way to use up mozzarella
scraps: leftover curd shreds mixed with cream and sealed inside a pouch of
fresh mozzarella. The name means "buttered." The outer skin is firm and
squeaky; the inside is *stracciatella*, which is also sold on its own.

**Taste:** milky, sweet, faintly lactic, lightly salted
**Texture:** firm skin over a loose, creamy, shredded interior
**Look:** a white ball, often tied at the top, that collapses when cut

Contrast inside one ingredient is the mechanism, and no other cheese does it.
Everything else on a board has one texture. Burrata has two, and the moment of
cutting — when the cream runs — is the reason it works as a centerpiece rather
than just another fresh cheese.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Ripe tomato and flaky salt** | Acid and sweetness against milk fat. The pairing everything else is measured against. |
| **Good olive oil and cracked pepper** | It needs seasoning more than it needs a partner. Oil and pepper is a complete dish. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and cure against sweet milk. The Puglian classic. |
| **Peaches or figs** | Late-summer fruit sugar with a milky, unsalted cheese. |
| **Ciabatta or olive oil crackers** | Something to catch the cream. This is functional as much as flavor. |
| **Basil and aged balsamic** | Herbal and sharp, cutting through the fat. |
| **Vermentino, Prosecco, dry rosé** | Light, high-acid, cold. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and other strong cheeses, which annihilate it,
and loud bases like everything crackers. Burrata is delicate to the point of
fragility on flavor as well as texture.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy it the day you serve it.** This is the rule. Burrata is at its best
   within 48 hours of being made and noticeably worse after four days.
2. **Take it out of the fridge 30 minutes ahead** and drain it from its liquid.
3. **Put it in a shallow bowl on the board**, not directly on the wood. It
   releases liquid and it will run.
4. **Cut it open at the table**, in front of people. The reveal is half of what
   you're serving.
5. **Season it after cutting** — olive oil, flaky salt, cracked pepper. Unsalted
   burrata tastes flat.
6. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.** One 4 oz ball serves two to four as part of a
   board.
7. **Fix:** don't refrigerate leftovers expecting them to hold. Use them that
   night on pasta or toast.

| **Local Italian deli burrata** | Italian markets, cheese counters | If it's made in-house or delivered weekly, buy this instead. |

| **Maplebrook Farm Burrata** | Whole Foods, natural grocers | Vermont-made, well regarded. |
| **Imported Pugliese burrata** | Eataly, specialty grocers | The real thing, when the date is fresh. Check it. |
| **Stracciatella** | Cheese counters, Italian delis | Just the filling, sold in a tub. Easier to spoon onto a board. |

**Label check:** the date is the only thing that matters here. Buy the freshest
package on the shelf, and don't buy burrata that has more than a few days left
on its life.

**Skip:** anything with a long shelf date. Burrata engineered to last two weeks
has been stabilized, and it eats like firm mozzarella.

## Quick FAQ

**What's inside burrata?**
Stracciatella — shredded mozzarella curd mixed with cream — inside a fresh
mozzarella skin.

**How long does it keep?**
Two to four days at best. Buy it the day you serve it.

**Burrata or fresh mozzarella?**
Burrata for a centerpiece moment; mozzarella when you need something that
slices and holds.

**Do I season it?**
Yes. Olive oil, flaky salt and pepper after cutting. Unseasoned burrata tastes
flat.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz. A 4 oz ball serves two to four on a mixed board.
