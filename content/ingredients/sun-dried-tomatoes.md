## The short version

Tomatoes dried until they're a fraction of their size, which concentrates
everything — sugar, acid and glutamate. On a board they're the most savoury
vegetable available, and they behave more like a cured meat than a salad. Buy
them packed in oil; the dry ones in bags need rehydrating and are rarely worth it.

## What it is

Plum tomatoes halved, salted and dried in the sun or in a dehydrator, then either
packed in olive oil with herbs or sold dry. Oil-packed versions are soft and ready
to eat; dry ones are leathery and need soaking.

**Taste:** intensely sweet, acidic and savoury, with a salty edge
**Texture:** chewy and dense, softer when oil-packed
**Look:** deep brick red, wrinkled, glossy with oil

The mechanism is glutamate concentration. Tomatoes are naturally high in free
glutamate — the compound behind savouriness — and drying multiplies it several
times over. That's why a sun-dried tomato reads as meaty rather than vegetal, and
why it works on a vegetarian board where you'd otherwise miss the salami.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Chalky lactic tang against dense sweet-savoury chew. The classic, and it works because neither has any moisture in common. |
| **Fresh mozzarella** | Very mild milk with a very intense tomato — the caprese logic, concentrated. |
| **Manchego** | Salt and nuttiness against sugar and umami. |
| **Prosciutto** | Two concentrated, chewy, salty things. Surprisingly good. |
| **Baguette and focaccia** | The oil they're packed in is a dressing; bread is what carries it. |
| **Pesto and basil** | The Italian flavour set. Relevant if the board leans that way. |

**Skip it with:** delicate bloomy cheeses. Sun-dried tomatoes are loud and sweet.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy oil-packed.** Dry ones need 20 minutes in hot water and are still
   leathery.
2. **Drain and blot**, keeping a little oil.
3. **Cut large halves into strips** — a whole one is a lot of chew.
4. **Serve in a small bowl.** The oil marks a wooden board.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.** They're intense.
6. **Use the oil.** It's tomato-infused and worth drizzling over cheese or bread.
7. **Fix:** too chewy? Warm them in their oil for a few minutes; they soften
   noticeably.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun-dried tomatoes in olive oil** | Grocery, Italian delis, olive bars | The default. Ready to eat, soft, well-seasoned. |
| **Italian pomodori secchi sott'olio** | Italian delis | Better tomatoes and better oil. Worth the premium. |
| **Semi-dried / sunblush tomatoes** | Olive bars, specialty | Softer, juicier, less intense. Excellent on a board and easier to eat. |
| **Dry sun-dried tomatoes** | Grocery, bulk bins | Cheapest. Need soaking, and never get as soft. |
| **Sun-dried tomato pesto** | Grocery | The spreadable version. A different but related ingredient. |

**Label check:** olive oil rather than sunflower oil, and check for added sulphur
dioxide if that matters to a guest.

**Skip:** dry-packed for board use unless you're going to soak them properly.

## Quick FAQ

**Oil-packed or dry sun-dried tomatoes?**
Oil-packed for a board — they're soft and ready to eat. Dry ones need soaking and
stay leathery.

**What are semi-dried tomatoes?**
Also sold as sunblush. Dried less far, so they're juicier and milder. Often better
on a board than fully dried.

**What cheese goes with sun-dried tomatoes?**
Fresh goat cheese above all, plus fresh mozzarella and manchego.

**Can I use the oil?**
Yes — it's infused with tomato and herbs and is worth drizzling over bread or
cheese.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce. They're very concentrated.
