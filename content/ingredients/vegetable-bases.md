## The short version

Endive leaves, cucumber rounds and mini sweet peppers, used as the base rather
than the garnish. Free of gluten, grain, nuts and dairy, crisp and cold, and
they solve the low-carb guest problem without a single specialty product. Some
hold for hours; some weep in twenty minutes.

## What it is

Not a product — a technique. Certain vegetables have the right combination of
rigidity, shape and mild flavor to hold a topping the way a cracker does. The
good ones are firm, slightly concave, and taste of very little.

**Endive:** naturally boat-shaped, faintly bitter, holds for hours
**Cucumber:** cool and neutral, weeps within about 30 minutes once cut
**Mini sweet peppers:** sweet, sturdy, halve into perfect cups
**Celery:** classic, stringy, best cut short
**Jicama:** crisp, sweet, stays rigid a long time

Water is the mechanism, and it works both directions. The high water content is
why these feel refreshing next to salty cured meat and rich cheese — and it's
also why the wrong choice puddles on your board. Endive and jicama hold;
cucumber and tomato do not.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Whipped feta or labneh** | Thick, salty and creamy scooped into a bitter endive leaf. The best bite on this page. |
| **Fresh goat cheese with herbs** | Tangy dairy against cool crunch, no carb in between. |
| **Blue cheese and walnut** | Endive's bitterness is the traditional partner for blue — this is a classic salad taken apart. |
| **Chicken or tuna salad** | The retro use, and mini peppers hold it better than celery does. |
| **Hummus and white bean dip** | Sturdy vegetables scoop as well as any chip. |
| **Smoked salmon and crème fraîche** | On cucumber rounds. Cool, clean, and it looks expensive. |
| **Crisp white wine, dry rosé** | Everything here is fresh and cold; the wine should be too. |

**Skip it with:** dry aged cheeses — a crumbly aged gouda on a wet cucumber is
a bad texture. And skip anything that needs to sit: dressed toppings turn these
soft fast.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Use endive as your workhorse.** Separate the leaves, trim the base, and
   they hold a filling for two hours without weeping. Nothing else here does.
2. **Cut cucumber thick — at least ⅓ inch — and cut it last.** Thin rounds go
   limp almost immediately.
3. **Halve mini peppers lengthwise and remove the seeds.** You get two natural
   cups from each one, and they're the sturdiest option.
4. **Salt nothing in advance.** Salt draws water out; that's the whole problem.
   Season at the moment of serving.
5. **Serve 3–4 pieces per person**, and keep a damp paper towel over prepped
   vegetables in the fridge until the last minute.
6. **Fill only half of them.** Filled vegetables look great for the first
   hour and tired after; leave the rest plain and refill.
7. **Fix:** limp cucumber can't be saved. Endive and jicama revive in ice water
   for ten minutes.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Belgian endive** | Most grocery produce sections | The best vegetable base there is. Buy pale, tight heads. |
| **Persian or English cucumbers** | Produce | Fewer seeds and thinner skin than standard cucumbers. Worth the small premium. |
| **Mini sweet peppers** | Produce, Costco, Trader Joe's | Sold in bags. The sturdiest and the most colorful. |
| **Jicama** | Produce, Latin markets | Peel and cut into planks. Stays crisp longer than anything else. |
| **Celery hearts** | Produce | The pale inner ribs, not the tough outer ones. Cut into 2-inch lengths. |
| **Radishes and watermelon radish** | Produce, farmers market | Sliced thick. Peppery, and watermelon radish is stunning. |

**Label check:** none — this is produce. Buy firm, heavy-for-its-size, and as
close to serving day as you can.

**Skip:** pre-cut vegetable trays. They've been sitting in moisture for days
and they're limp before they reach the board.

## Quick FAQ

**Which vegetable holds up best on a board?**
Endive, by a wide margin. Then jicama and mini peppers. Cucumber is last.

**How far ahead can I prep them?**
Cut them the morning of, store under a damp towel in the fridge, and assemble
at the last minute. Cucumber should be cut just before serving.

**How many per person?**
3–4 pieces alongside other bases.

**Won't they make the board wet?**
Only if you choose badly or salt early. Endive and peppers stay dry; cucumber
and tomato don't.

**Is this actually a base or just a garnish?**
A base. Endive filled with whipped feta is a complete bite — it just happens to
be a leaf instead of a cracker.
