## The short version

A firm, salty Cypriot cheese with an unusually high melting point — you can put
it straight in a hot pan and it browns instead of running. Two minutes a side
and you have a hot, squeaky, golden-crusted thing that nothing else on a board
resembles.

## What it is

Halloumi is made from sheep's and goat's milk, sometimes with cow's milk added.
The curd is heated to a high temperature before shaping, which denatures the
proteins and locks the structure in place. That's why it holds its shape in a
pan. It's then folded with mint and stored in brine.

**Taste:** salty, milky, faintly minty, savory
**Texture:** firm and squeaky raw; crisp-crusted and chewy when seared
**Look:** white folded slabs, browning to deep gold when cooked

Protein denaturation is the mechanism, and it's what makes halloumi unique on a
board. Most cheeses melt because their protein network relaxes with heat.
Halloumi's was already set by high-temperature curd cooking, so heat browns the
surface without collapsing the interior — grilling is the intended use, not a
trick.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Lemon, squeezed over hot** | Acid on salt and fat, immediately off the pan. This is the traditional service and it's non-negotiable. |
| **Hot honey** | Sweet and heat over salty crust. The pairing that converts people. |
| **Watermelon and mint** | Cool and sweet against hot and salty. Summer's best contrast. |
| **Za'atar and olive oil** | Herbal, sesame, sumac — the regional seasoning. |
| **Pita chips and warm flatbread** | Something to hold a hot slab. |
| **Roasted red peppers and olives** | Sweet, smoky, briny neighbors. |
| **Cold beer, dry rosé, assyrtiko** | Cold and acidic against hot and salty. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses, and anything you want to stay cold —
halloumi is served hot and it dominates the corner of the board it sits in.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice about ⅓ inch thick.** Thinner burns before the inside warms;
   thicker stays squeaky in the middle.
2. **Pat it completely dry.** Surface brine steams instead of browning, and
   that's the usual reason halloumi comes out pale.
3. **Dry pan, medium-high, 2 minutes a side** until deep gold. It has enough fat
   — added oil mostly spatters.
4. **Squeeze lemon over it the second it leaves the pan.**
5. **Serve it immediately, in a small warm dish beside the board.** Halloumi
   cools into rubber within about ten minutes.
6. **Serve 1–2 oz per person**, cooked in batches so there's a hot one later.
7. **Fix:** gone rubbery? Back in the pan for thirty seconds. It recovers once,
   not twice.

| **Bread cheese (juustoleipä)** | Grocery cheese case, Wisconsin brands | Finnish relative. Milder, also pan-fries. |

**Label check:** sheep's and goat's milk means a more traditional, saltier
cheese. All-cow versions are milder and browner but less distinctive.

**Skip:** pre-sliced halloumi in dry packaging if a brined block is available.
Brine keeps it from drying at the edges.

## Quick FAQ

**Why doesn't halloumi melt?**
Its curd is heated to a high temperature during making, which sets the protein
structure before it's ever shaped. Heat browns it rather than collapsing it.

**Do I need oil in the pan?**
No. It has enough fat of its own, and added oil mostly spatters.

**Can I serve it cold?**
You can, but it's squeaky and very salty. Searing is the point.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz, cooked in batches so it stays hot.

**What if it goes rubbery?**
Back in the pan for thirty seconds. It recovers once. Cook less at a time
instead.
