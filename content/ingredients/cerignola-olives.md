## The short version

Cerignolas are the size of a small plum — two or three fill a hand. They're mild,
crisp and only lightly salty, and they come in three colours: bright green,
purple-black, and a startling red that's dyed. On a board they're a visual
ingredient first and a flavour ingredient second, and there's nothing wrong with
that.

## What it is

The Bella di Cerignola variety from Puglia, harvested large and cured briefly in
mild brine. **Green** is the standard and the best; **black** is the same olive
ripened; **red** is dyed with vegetable colouring and sold mostly for looks. All
are sold with pits.

**Taste:** mild, buttery, faintly sweet, lightly salty
**Texture:** crisp and firm, with a clean snap
**Look:** very large, glossy, in vivid green, black or red

The mechanism is scale. An olive this size changes how the bowl reads — it looks
generous rather than fiddly, and a guest takes one rather than a handful. The mild
cure means the flavour doesn't have to carry the ingredient; the size does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata and fresh mozzarella** | Mild enough not to overpower cream, and the size contrast against a soft white ball is the plate. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and fat with a large cold crisp counterweight. |
| **Manchego** | Both mild and rich; nothing here is fighting. |
| **Marcona almonds** | Two Mediterranean bar snacks, one crisp and wet, one crisp and dry. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | Very mild things together, where the olive's brine is the only sharp note. |
| **Vermouth and aperitivo drinks** | Its actual purpose in Italy. |

**Skip it with:** strongly flavoured blue cheeses. A Cerignola has nothing to
fight back with.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain and pat dry.**
2. **Serve in a bowl with a pit bowl beside it.** These are too big to pit
   discreetly.
3. **Use the green ones** for a cheese board. The red are a novelty and the black
   are milder still.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person** — that's only two or three olives.
5. **Marinate them** with lemon zest, chilli and rosemary if you want more from
   them.
6. **Serve at room temperature.**
7. **Fix:** bland? They are mild by design. Dress them, or serve a Kalamata
   alongside for contrast.

**Label check:** the red ones are coloured — check the ingredients if that
matters to you or a guest.

**Skip:** pitted Cerignolas. They lose their crispness, which is the whole point.

## Quick FAQ

**Why are Cerignola olives so big?**
It's the variety — Bella di Cerignola from Puglia grows to plum size naturally.

**Are the red ones natural?**
No. Red Cerignolas are dyed with vegetable colouring. Green and black are natural.

**Are they salty?**
Only mildly. They're cured briefly, so they're crisp and buttery rather than
briny.

**What cheese goes with Cerignola olives?**
Burrata, fresh mozzarella, manchego and fresh ricotta — mild cheeses that suit a
mild olive.

**How many per person?**
Two or three. They're large enough that an ounce is a small number.
