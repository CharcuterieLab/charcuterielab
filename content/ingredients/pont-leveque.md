## The short version

A square Normandy cheese with a washed golden rind and a supple, rich paste.
It's been made since at least the 12th century, which makes it one of the
oldest cheeses still in production. Funkier than Camembert, gentler than
Époisses.

## What it is

Pont-l'Évêque AOP comes from Normandy, named for a town between Caen and
Deauville. It was originally called *angelot* and monks were making it in the
1100s. The square shape comes from the wooden molds; the rind is brine-washed
and brushed during a six-week aging, developing a golden-orange color and a
light barnyard aroma.

**Taste:** rich, savory, faintly sweet and fruity, mild barnyard funk
**Texture:** supple and soft, springy rather than runny
**Look:** small square, straw-gold washed rind with fine ridges from the mold

Restrained washing is the mechanism. Pont-l'Évêque is washed and brushed rather
than soaked repeatedly in brine and spirit, so surface bacteria develop enough
to add savory depth without dissolving the paste. The result sits between a
bloomy-rind and a full washed-rind cheese — the aroma of the latter with the
structure of the former.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Dry cider** | The Normandy pairing. Acid and light sweetness against a rich washed-rind. |
| **Baguette** | Plain bread under a soft aromatic cheese. |
| **Apple** | Normandy grows apples. Acid against fat, and it's regionally correct. |
| **Calvados** | The local apple brandy. Strong enough to stand up. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin cutting a rich paste. |
| **Cornichons** | Sharp vinegar as a reset. |
| **Honey** | Sweetness rounding off the funk. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses in the same corner. It's mild for a
washed-rind but still travels.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Temper 45 minutes.**
2. **Cut it into squares or rectangles** from the square block, keeping rind on
   each piece. The shape is distinctive — don't cut it into wedges.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.** One small square serves four to six.
4. **Put cider or apple beside it.** The Normandy pairing is genuinely the best
   one.
5. **Place it in the middle of the board's intensity range.**
6. **The rind is edible** and part of the flavor, though some trim it.
7. **Fix:** an ammonia smell that doesn't fade means it's over-ripe. A light
   barnyard note is correct.

| **Taleggio** | Whole Foods | The easiest washed-rind to find — see its own page. |
| **Saint-Nectaire** | Cheese counters, Whole Foods | Earthy, supple, Auvergne. A good relative. |

**Label check:** the AOP mark and a square shape with fine ridge marks from the
mold. Round "Pont-l'Évêque-style" cheeses are something else.

**Skip:** buying it far ahead. Like all washed-rinds it ripens on and tips into
ammonia.

## Quick FAQ

**How old is Pont-l'Évêque?**
Monks were making a version of it in the 12th century, which makes it one of
the oldest French cheeses still produced.

**Is it as strong as Époisses?**
No. It's a gentle washed-rind — aromatic, but supple and mild on the palate.

**Why is it square?**
The traditional wooden molds. The fine ridges on the rind come from the same
molds.

**Can you eat the rind?**
Yes, and it's where much of the flavor is. Some people trim it.

**What's the traditional pairing?**
Dry Normandy cider, or Calvados. Apples in any form.
