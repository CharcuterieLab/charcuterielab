## The short version

Thin, twice-baked bread crisps with dried fruit, nuts and seeds baked right in.
They're expensive and they're worth it, because the cracker already contains
the fruit-and-nut pairing you'd otherwise have to arrange. Put one next to blue
cheese and you're done.

## What it is

They're made like biscotti: bake a quick bread loaf full of fruit, nuts and
seeds, chill it hard, slice it paper-thin, then bake the slices again until
they're dry and brittle. That second bake is why they shatter instead of bend.

**Taste:** sweet, nutty, herbal, salted
**Texture:** brittle, glassy, snaps into shards
**Look:** thin ovals with visible fruit and nut cross-sections

The mechanism is compression. A good board bite is usually three things —
cheese, something sweet, something with crunch. This cracker supplies two of
them, which is why it makes a two-item board look composed.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Blue cheese** | Raisin sweetness against salt and funk, pecan bitterness meeting the blue's. The single best use of this cracker. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | The crisp brings everything; the cheese just brings tang. Nothing competes. |
| **Triple-crème and brie** | Rich and mild needs a base with opinions. This one has several. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Fruit and sharp cheddar is the apple pairing in cracker form. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel in the cheese, dried fruit in the crisp. Same flavor family, doubled. |
| **A drizzle of honey and nothing else** | On a rushed board, this is a complete bite with two ingredients. |
| **Port, sherry, dessert wine** | Sweet fortified wine and a sweet-savory cracker is a classic cheese-course close. |

**Skip it with:** jam, chutney and honey *together* — the crisp is already
sweet, and three sweet things is dessert. Delicate fresh cheeses like burrata
disappear underneath entirely.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Lay them flat and slightly separated.** They're the prettiest thing in the
   cracker aisle and stacking hides the fruit and nut cross-sections.
2. **Serve 2–3 per person.** A 5.3 oz box holds roughly 20 crisps. They're rich
   and expensive — this is not the volume cracker.
3. **Always put a neutral cracker out too.** These are too flavored to carry a
   whole board.
4. **Place them beside the strongest cheese**, not the mildest. They win any
   fight with a mild one.
5. **Open the box gently.** They break in transit, and broken shards look like
   crumbs rather than a choice.
6. **Fix:** the broken pieces aren't waste — scatter them over a soft cheese as
   a topping and nobody knows.

**Label check:** you should be able to *see* whole nuts and fruit pieces in the
cross-section. If it's uniformly beige with flecks, the fruit was ground in for
color.

**Skip:** the sweeter dessert-leaning flavors if this is your only specialty
cracker. Rosemary raisin pecan is the versatile one.

## Quick FAQ

**Why are these so expensive?**
Two bakes, real nuts and dried fruit, and a slow slicing step. About $7–9 for
20 crisps is normal.

**What cheese goes best with Raincoast Crisps?**
Blue cheese, then fresh goat cheese. Both are on the label's own pairing
suggestions for a reason.

**Can I make them at home?**
Yes — a quick bread with fruit and nuts, frozen, sliced thin, baked again at
300°F for 15 minutes a side. It's a project, but a cheap one.

**How many per person?**
2–3. Put out a neutral cracker alongside for volume.
