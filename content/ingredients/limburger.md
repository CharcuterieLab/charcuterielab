## The short version

The cheese that became a punchline. It smells powerfully of feet — for a real
biological reason — and tastes mild, beefy and faintly sweet. The gap between
the reputation and the cheese is the widest of anything in this section.

## What it is

Limburger originated in the Duchy of Limburg, in what's now Belgium, and is
now made mostly in Germany and Wisconsin. The rind is washed with brine
containing *Brevibacterium linens*, which is the same bacterium that lives on
human skin and produces foot odour. That's not a metaphor — it's the identical
organism producing identical compounds.

**Taste:** mild, beefy, savory, faintly sweet, much gentler than the aroma
**Texture:** firm and chalky when young; soft and spreadable when ripe
**Look:** pale ivory paste, sticky reddish-brown rind, sold in foil bricks

The aroma-flavor gap is the mechanism, and it's larger here than anywhere else.
*B. linens* produces sulphur and short-chain fatty acid compounds that are
extraordinarily potent to the nose but contribute relatively little to taste.
Limburger is the clearest demonstration that a cheese's smell and its flavor
are two different things.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cocktail rye or pumpernickel** | The traditional sandwich base. Dark bread carries it. |
| **Raw onion and mustard** | The classic Limburger sandwich, and the sharpness is essential. |
| **Dark beer** | Malt against funk. The Wisconsin pairing, and it works. |
| **Pickled onions and cornichons** | Acid resetting the palate. |
| **Apple** | Sweet, cold and clean against a warm savory cheese. |
| **Boiled potatoes** | Bland starch absorbs the funk and makes it a meal rather than a dare. |
| **Gewürztraminer** | Aromatic and off-dry, which is what strong washed-rinds need in a wine. |

**Skip it with:** every delicate cheese, every aged hard cheese, and anything
you want to eat afterwards. Limburger dominates completely.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decide whether the board is the right venue.** Limburger works best as its
   own thing — a plate with rye bread, onion and mustard — rather than mixed
   into a cheese selection.
2. **Temper 45 minutes.**
3. **Cut thin slices** from the brick and lay them on the bread rather than
   free on the board.
4. **Serve about 1 oz per person.**
5. **Isolate it** — own plate, own knife, and ideally a different table.
6. **Tell people it tastes milder than it smells.** With this cheese it's true
   by a wide margin and it's the only way anyone will try it.
7. **Fix:** double-bag it in the fridge inside a sealed container. There is no
   half-measure here.

| **Liederkranz** | Specialty shops, online | The American cheese created as a milder Limburger. Hard to find and worth it. |

**Label check:** age matters. Young Limburger is firm and mild; a ripe one is
soft, sticky and much stronger. The foil brick doesn't tell you which.

**Skip:** putting it on a mixed board for a general crowd. It's not a cheese to
spring on people.

## Quick FAQ

**Why does Limburger smell like feet?**
Because it shares its bacterium. *Brevibacterium linens* lives on human skin
and produces the same compounds — the connection is literal, not a comparison.

**Does it taste as bad as it smells?**
No. It's mild, beefy and faintly sweet. The gap between aroma and flavor is
the largest of any cheese.

**How is it traditionally eaten?**
On dark rye with raw onion and mustard, with a dark beer.

**Is it still made in America?**
Yes — the Chalet Cheese Cooperative in Monroe, Wisconsin is the last US
producer.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz, and only for a crowd that's up for it.
