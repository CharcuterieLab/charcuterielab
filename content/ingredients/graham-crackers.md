## The short version

A dollar a box, sturdy enough to hold anything, and quietly excellent under soft
cheese. Graham crackers are barely sweet, faintly honeyed and wholemeal-tasting —
much closer to a British digestive biscuit than to a cookie. Break them along the
scored lines rather than serving full sheets.

## What it is

A wholemeal wheat cracker sweetened with honey or molasses, named for Sylvester
Graham. Sold in scored rectangular sheets. Cinnamon and chocolate versions exist;
plain honey is the one for a board.

**Taste:** mildly sweet, honeyed, toasty and wheaty
**Texture:** crisp and dry, snaps cleanly along the score
**Look:** tan rectangles with docking holes and a scored line

The mechanism is restraint. Most dessert bases are sweet enough to dominate what
sits on them; a graham cracker is only faintly sweet, so it reads as a wholemeal
cracker with a hint of honey. That leaves room for the cheese to lead, which is
why it works where a cookie wouldn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese** | Cheesecake, deconstructed. Faint honey and wholemeal under mild tangy fat. |
| **Brie and soft-ripened cheese** | Sturdy enough to carry a soft wedge, sweet enough to replace the jam. |
| **Dark chocolate** | S'mores logic without the fire. Bitter chocolate on a honeyed base. |
| **Gorgonzola** | Honey and blue cheese, with the cracker supplying the honey. Better than it sounds. |
| **Apple butter** | Spiced apple on wholemeal honey — an autumn board in two ingredients. |
| **Marshmallow and chocolate, on a kids' board** | The obvious one, and worth knowing. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat and sharp savoury spreads. The honey clashes.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Break them along the scored lines.** A full sheet is four crackers.
2. **Fan them near the sweet corner** and near the cream cheese.
3. **Keep them dry.** They go soft faster than a water cracker.
4. **Serve 1 oz per person** — two or three broken pieces.
5. **Buy plain honey**, not cinnamon or chocolate, for a cheese board.
6. **Use them for the cheesecake corner** — cream cheese, berries, honey.
7. **Fix:** soft? 300°F for five minutes and cool completely. They re-crisp well.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Honey graham crackers** | Any grocery | The default. Cheap and consistent. |
| **Annie's or organic grahams** | Grocery, natural section | Less sweet and more wheaty. Better with cheese. |
| **Cinnamon graham crackers** | Grocery | Good with apple butter, wrong with most cheese. |
| **Digestive biscuits** | British section, grocery | The British equivalent. Less sweet, more crumbly, excellent with cheddar. |
| **Chocolate graham crackers** | Grocery | Dessert corner only. |

**Label check:** whole wheat or graham flour should be listed. Some brands are
mostly white flour with molasses colour.

**Skip:** graham cracker crumbs, which are for crusts, not boards.

## Quick FAQ

**Do graham crackers work on a cheese board?**
Yes — they're only faintly sweet, sturdy, and work well under cream cheese, brie
and blue cheese.

**Are graham crackers the same as digestive biscuits?**
Close cousins. Digestives are less sweet and more crumbly; grahams are honeyed and
crisper.

**Which kind should I buy?**
Plain honey. Cinnamon and chocolate versions compete with the cheese.

**How do I serve them?**
Break the sheets along the scored lines and fan the pieces near the sweet corner.

**How much per person?**
Two or three broken pieces, about an ounce.
