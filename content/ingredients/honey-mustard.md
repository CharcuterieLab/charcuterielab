## The short version

Mustard with the edges taken off. On a board its real job is being the condiment
guests who avoid mustard will actually use — and it's the correct partner for
pretzels, ham and sausage. You can make it in thirty seconds: equal parts Dijon
and honey.

## What it is

Mustard blended with honey, sometimes with mayonnaise (which makes it a dressing
rather than a condiment). The board version should be mustard-forward — Dijon or
whole grain with honey stirred in, not a creamy dipping sauce.

**Taste:** sweet first, mild mustard heat behind
**Texture:** smooth and glossy, or grainy if whole-grain-based
**Look:** golden, thicker than plain mustard

The mechanism is sugar suppressing pungency. Honey blunts the sharp volatile heat
of mustard while leaving the vinegar acidity intact, so you keep the fat-cutting
function and lose the burn. That's a genuinely useful trade on a board with a
mixed crowd.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Black forest ham and cooked ham** | Sweet and salty pork with sweet-sharp mustard is the oldest sandwich in the world, on a board. |
| **Pretzel crisps and soft pretzels** | The German bar pairing. Salt, sweet, sharp, crunch. |
| **Kielbasa and cooked sausage** | Fatty smoked sausage cut by acid, with sugar carrying it. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp cheese, sweet mustard — the ploughman's arrangement. |
| **Soppressata** | Spiced fatty salami with sweetness to round it out. |
| **Beer boards** | Its natural home, alongside pretzels and sausage. |

**Skip it with:** pâté and delicate cured meats. They want the clean sharpness of
Dijon, not sugar.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Make it:** stir together two parts Dijon or whole grain mustard to one part
   honey. Adjust to taste. That's the whole recipe.
2. **Decant into a small bowl** with a spoon.
3. **Serve 0.25 oz per person.**
4. **Put it on the sausage-and-pretzel side** of the board.
5. **Use a mustard-forward ratio.** If it tastes like sweet mayonnaise, it's a
   dressing, not a board condiment.
6. **Put a plain mustard out too** if the board has pâté or good salami.
7. **Fix:** too sweet? More Dijon. Too sharp? More honey. It's a two-ingredient
   dial.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Homemade (Dijon + honey)** | Make it | Thirty seconds, better than anything bought, and you control the ratio. |
| **Maille honey Dijon** | Grocery | Mustard-forward and sharp. The best bought version. |
| **Inglehoffer honey mustard** | Grocery | Sweeter. Popular and reliable. |
| **Bavarian sweet mustard (süßer senf)** | German delis | Much sweeter and coarser. The traditional partner for weisswurst and pretzels. |
| **Hot honey mustard** | Specialty | Sweet, sharp and spicy. Good with sausage. |

**Label check:** avoid anything with mayonnaise or soybean oil high in the
ingredients — that's a salad dressing, not a board condiment.

**Skip:** squeeze-bottle honey mustard dipping sauce.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I make honey mustard?**
Stir two parts Dijon or whole grain mustard into one part honey. Adjust to taste.

**What goes with honey mustard on a board?**
Ham, sausage, pretzels, and aged cheddar. It's the beer-board condiment.

**Is honey mustard the same as the dipping sauce?**
No. Dipping sauces include mayonnaise, which makes them creamy dressings. A board
version should be mustard-forward.

**Why put it on a board at all?**
It's the condiment guests who dislike mustard will still use, and it's the right
match for pretzels and ham.

**How much per person?**
About a quarter-ounce.
