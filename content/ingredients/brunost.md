## The short version

Norway's brown cheese. It's whey, the liquid left over from making regular
cheese, boiled for hours until the milk sugar caramelizes into a dense, tan
block. It tastes like salted caramel with a tangy edge. Shave it paper-thin
and it's the most surprising thing on a winter board.

## What it is

Most cheese is made from curds and throws the whey away. Brunost goes the
other way: the whey is mixed with milk or cream and cooked down for hours
until most of the water is gone and the lactose browns. The thick paste is
poured into molds and cooled. The US staple is Ski Queen, a Gudbrandsdalsost
that mixes goat's and cow's milk. Ekte Geitost is the all-goat version.

**Taste:** salted caramel, dulce de leche, a tangy goat's milk finish
**Texture:** dense and fudgy, a little sticky
**Look:** a smooth tan to light-brown block with no rind

The color and flavor come from the Maillard reaction and caramelization of
lactose during the long boil. Whey is rich in lactose, so a lot of sugar
browns in a small space, which is why brunost is sweeter than any other
cheese. The goat's milk adds a sharp, slightly sour note that keeps it from
tasting like candy.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Lefse or rye crispbread** | The Norwegian way. Plain starch carries the sweetness. |
| **Tart apples** | Acid and crunch cut through the dense, sweet paste. |
| **Pears** | Juicy and mild, a gentler version of the apple trick. |
| **Gingersnaps** | Spice and snap turn it into a dessert bite. |
| **Lingonberry jam** | Tart berries against caramel, a Scandinavian classic. |
| **Coffee, aquavit or a malty brown ale** | Roast and malt match the caramel notes. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and sharp pickles side by side. Brunost is
sweet and mild, and strong salt or vinegar next to it makes it taste flat and
sugary.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Serve it straight from the fridge.** Brunost slices best cold.
2. **Shave it with a cheese plane** into paper-thin sheets. This is the
   traditional tool and it matters: thick cubes are cloying.
3. **Serve about 0.5 oz per person.** It's rich and sweet.
4. **Curl the shavings** and pile them loosely so they look like ribbons.
5. **Place it in the sweet corner** near fruit and cookies, away from the
   cured meat.
6. **Fix:** if the slices stick together, lay them on parchment and chill for
   10 minutes before plating.

**Label check:** whey listed as the first ingredient. That's the sign of real
brunost rather than a flavored cheese.

**Skip:** cubing it for a crowd. Thick pieces are too sweet and too sticky.

## Quick FAQ

**Is brunost really cheese?**
It's a whey cheese. It's made from the whey left over from cheesemaking,
boiled down with milk or cream until the sugars caramelize, rather than from
curds.

**What does brunost taste like?**
Like salted caramel or dulce de leche, with a tangy finish. Versions made with
goat's milk taste sharper.

**What is the difference between gjetost and brunost?**
Brunost means brown cheese. Gjetost or geitost is the goat's milk kind.
Gudbrandsdalsost, sold in the US as Ski Queen, mixes goat's and cow's milk.

**How do you cut brunost?**
With a cheese plane, into paper-thin sheets. Thick slices are too sweet and
too sticky.

**How long does it keep?**
Weeks in the fridge once opened, wrapped tightly. It's very low in moisture.
