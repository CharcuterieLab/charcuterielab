## The short version

Tuna loin cured in salt and dried in the sea air of southern Spain until it's
firm and deep red. Sliced paper-thin and dressed with olive oil, it tastes
like a cross between tuna and jamón. It's one of the oldest cured foods in
the Mediterranean, and almost nobody outside Spain has tried it.

## What it is

Mojama comes from the Andalusian coast, around Barbate and Isla Cristina,
where it has been made since the Phoenicians and Romans fished bluefin tuna
there. The loin is buried in sea salt for a day or two, rinsed, then
air-dried for two to three weeks. Mojama de Barbate has its own protected
geographical indication.

**Taste:** savory, meaty, salty, with a clean, concentrated tuna flavor
**Texture:** firm and dense, tender when sliced very thin
**Look:** a dark mahogany-red block; thin slices glow ruby against the light

Salt and drying concentrate the fish the same way they concentrate ham.
Water leaves, the flesh tightens, and enzymes break proteins into savory
amino acids. Tuna is naturally rich in inosinate, a compound that boosts the
savory taste of glutamate, so mojama tastes far more savory per bite than
fresh tuna.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Good olive oil** | Fat softens the dense texture and carries the flavor. Essential. |
| **Marcona almonds** | The classic Spanish partner. Buttery crunch with salty fish. |
| **Roasted red peppers** | Sweet and soft against salt. |
| **Manzanilla olives** | Briny and buttery, from the same part of Spain. |
| **Plain tomato on toast** | Fresh acid and juice balance the concentrated salt. |
| **Manzanilla or fino sherry, Albariño** | Dry, salty-edged wines from the same coast. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and smoked salmon. Blue cheese overpowers it,
and two oily fish on the board compete.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice it as thin as you possibly can.** Thick pieces are tough and
   salty. A very sharp knife or a mandoline works.
2. **Lay the slices flat and drizzle with olive oil** 10 minutes before
   serving.
3. **Serve about 0.5 oz per person.** It's concentrated.
4. **Scatter toasted Marcona almonds on top** or right beside it.
5. **Place it with the olives and peppers** to make a Spanish corner.
6. **Fix:** if it tastes too salty, let the slices sit in olive oil a few
   minutes longer. The oil mellows it.

## Quick FAQ

**What is mojama?**
Tuna loin that's salted and then air-dried for weeks until it's firm and
dark red. It's a specialty of the Andalusian coast in Spain.

**What does mojama taste like?**
Concentrated tuna: savory, meaty and salty, with a clean sea flavor. It's
much milder than anchovies.

**How do you serve it?**
Sliced as thin as possible and dressed with olive oil, often with toasted
almonds on the side.

**Is mojama the same as bottarga?**
No. Bottarga is cured fish roe. Mojama is the cured loin, the meat of the
fish.

**How long does it keep?**
Unopened, vacuum-packed mojama keeps for months. Once opened, wrap it
tightly and use it within a couple of weeks.
