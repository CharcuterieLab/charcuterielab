## The short version

Kiwi earns its place on colour. Nothing else available in February is that
green, and the seeded cross-section is one of the better-looking things you can
slice. Cut crosswise into wheels, not wedges. Like pineapple, it carries a
protein-digesting enzyme — keep it away from cream cheeses and off the board
until the last minute.

## What it is

A berry, technically, with fuzzy brown skin and emerald flesh flecked with tiny
black seeds. **Green (Hayward)** is the standard, tart and bright.
**Gold (SunGold)** is smooth-skinned, yellow, sweeter and much lower in acid.

**Taste:** tart, sweet, faintly like strawberry and gooseberry
**Texture:** soft, juicy, with crunchy seeds
**Look:** green wheels with a white centre and a black seed ring

The mechanism is actinidin — kiwi's version of pineapple's bromelain. It breaks
down protein, which is why kiwi tingles and why a slice left on burrata for an
hour will leave it slack. Against a hard aged cheese there's nothing to break
down, so the acid does clean work instead.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp acid against sharp cheese, and the cheese is dry enough to resist the enzyme. |
| **Manchego** | Nutty and salty against bright green acid. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Works if you plate it right before serving — tang plus tang, colour plus white. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against tart; a bigger contrast than it sounds. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and acid. Less obvious than melon and just as good. |
| **Gold kiwi with honey and ricotta** | Low-acid gold kiwi is safe with mild cheese in a way green kiwi isn't. |

**Skip it with:** burrata, fresh mozzarella, and anything creamy that will sit
on the board for an hour.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy them slightly firm.** A soft kiwi collapses under the knife.
2. **Ripen on the counter with a banana** if they're hard — two days, no more.
3. **Peel with a vegetable peeler** — faster and neater than a knife. Or halve
   and scoop with a spoon if you're serving in cups.
4. **Slice crosswise into 1/4-inch wheels.** The seed ring is the whole reason
   kiwi is on the board.
5. **Blot and plate late.** Cut kiwi weeps and, with the enzyme, degrades
   anything creamy it touches.
6. **Serve 1 oz per person** — it's a colour accent, not a centrepiece.
7. **Fix:** hard and sour? Two days on the counter next to a banana. There's no
   quick fix.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Green kiwi (Hayward)** | Any grocery, year-round | The default. Tart, cheap, reliably green. |
| **Gold kiwi (SunGold / Zespri)** | Grocery, spring–autumn | Sweeter, smooth-skinned, much lower acid. Safer near soft cheese. |
| **Kiwi berries** | Farmers markets, Whole Foods, autumn | Grape-sized, fuzzless, eaten whole. Excellent and rarely seen. |
| **Dried kiwi** | Bulk bins | Chewy and very tart. A niche option. |

**Label check:** press the shoulder near the stem. A little give means ready;
rock hard means a week away.

**Skip:** kiwis with wet or wrinkled patches — the inside is already mushy.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I peel kiwi?**
Yes, for a board — a vegetable peeler is fastest. The skin is edible but fuzzy
and nobody expects it.

**Why does kiwi make my mouth tingle?**
Actinidin, an enzyme that breaks down protein — the same effect as pineapple.

**Green or gold kiwi?**
Green is tarter and more striking. Gold is sweeter and works better next to mild
cheeses.

**How do I slice kiwi for a board?**
Crosswise into 1/4-inch wheels, so the seed ring shows.

**Will kiwi ruin soft cheese?**
If it sits against it, yes — the enzyme degrades the protein and the cheese goes
slack. Plate it separately and late.
