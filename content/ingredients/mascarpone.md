## The short version

Cream thickened with a little acid and nothing else — no rennet, no cultures,
barely a cheese by most definitions. It tastes like lightly sweetened whipped
butter. Belongs on the dessert half of a board, not the savory half.

## What it is

Mascarpone comes from Lombardy and is made by heating cream and adding an acid
— tartaric or citric — until it thickens. That's the whole process. There's no
fermentation, so there's no tang, and the fat content sits around 60–75%.

**Taste:** sweet, buttery, milky, almost no salt or tang
**Texture:** dense, smooth, spoonable, silkier than cream cheese
**Look:** pale ivory, thick, glossy

Absence of acid is the mechanism. American cream cheese is cultured, which
makes it tangy; mascarpone is set with acid and then stopped, so it stays
sweet. That single difference is why one belongs with smoked salmon and the
other belongs with berries.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Berries** | Tart fruit against sweet cream. The most direct and reliable pairing here. |
| **Honey and honeycomb** | Sweet on sweet works because mascarpone has no salt or tang to fight. |
| **Dark chocolate and cocoa nibs** | Bitterness is what mascarpone lacks entirely. Add it. |
| **Espresso or coffee** | Tiramisu logic. Bitter and hot against sweet and cold. |
| **Shortbread and biscotti** | A dessert base under a dessert cheese. |
| **Figs and stone fruit** | Ripe fruit sugar and acid on a plain sweet cream. |
| **Moscato, port, dessert wine** | Sweet with sweet, which works here as it wouldn't with a savory cheese. |

**Skip it with:** cured meat, blue cheese and anything savory. Mascarpone has
no salt and no tang, so it can't hold its own — it just tastes like cream that
wandered onto the wrong board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Put it on the dessert end**, or use it to build a dessert board of its
   own. Don't scatter it among the savory cheeses.
2. **Temper 20 minutes** so it's spoonable rather than solid.
3. **Whip it with a little vanilla or lemon zest** if you want it to read as
   finished rather than as an ingredient.
4. **Spread it in a shallow bowl** and dent the top for honey to pool in.
5. **Serve about 1 oz per person.** It's very rich and mostly fat.
6. **Give it a small spoon**, not a cheese knife.
7. **Fix:** too stiff to spread? Beat in a tablespoon of cream. Too loose? It
   was over-tempered — chill it fifteen minutes.

**Label check:** cream and an acid. If you see gums, starches or added sugar,
it's a dessert filling rather than mascarpone.

**Skip:** buying a large tub. It doesn't keep long once opened and you'll use
an ounce per person.

## Quick FAQ

**Is mascarpone the same as cream cheese?**
No. Cream cheese is cultured and tangy; mascarpone is acid-set and sweet.
They're not interchangeable on a board.

**Can I use it on a savory board?**
It won't hold up. Use cream cheese, boursin or labneh instead.

**How much per person?**
About 1 oz. It's mostly butterfat.

**How long does it keep once open?**
Under a week refrigerated, and it goes sour rather than moldy.

**What's the easiest thing to do with it on a board?**
Whip it, spread it in a bowl, pool honey in the middle, and put berries and
shortbread beside it.
