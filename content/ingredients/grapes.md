## The short version

The fruit on more charcuterie boards than any other, and the one most often
served badly. Loose grapes roll everywhere and look like a garnish; small
clusters snipped with scissors look deliberate and give people something to
pick up.

## What it is

Table grapes are bred for eating rather than wine — thicker skins, lower acid,
and usually seedless. The board-relevant ones are red globe and crimson (sweet,
firm), green Thompson (crisp, tart), black Concord-type (musky, slip-skin), and
the tiny champagne grapes that come in dense little clusters.

**Taste:** sweet, mildly acidic, faintly floral
**Texture:** firm, snapping skin over juicy flesh
**Look:** clusters on woody green-brown stems

Water is the mechanism, and it's why grapes appear on every board. Cured meat
and aged cheese are salt and fat with almost no moisture. A grape is 80% water
with a little acid — it resets the palate physically as well as by flavour,
which is why one between bites makes the next bite of cheese taste new.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Brie and soft cheeses** | Cool wet fruit against warm soft fat. The most common bite on any board, and it earns it. |
| **Manchego and aged sheep cheese** | Sweet juice cutting nutty salt. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Acid and sugar against sharp acid. |
| **Prosciutto and cured ham** | Salt and fat, water and sugar. The same logic as melon. |
| **Walnuts and almonds** | Crunch and tannin against soft juice. |
| **Blue cheese** | Sweetness blunting salt and funk. |
| **Almost anything** | Grapes are the least fussy thing on a board. That's the point of them. |

**Skip it with:** nothing, really. The only failure is presentation — loose
grapes scattered across a board look like an afterthought.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Snip into small clusters with scissors** — three to six grapes each. This
   is the whole page. Clusters look intentional and give people a handle.
2. **Leave the stems on.** A woody stem is what makes it read as fruit rather
   than as candy.
3. **Rinse and dry thoroughly.** Wet grapes bead water onto the board and
   soften whatever they touch.
4. **Serve 2–3 oz per person.**
5. **Use them to fill gaps.** Small clusters tucked into empty spaces are the
   standard way boards get their "abundant" look.
6. **Serve cold.** Unlike cheese, grapes are better straight from the fridge.
7. **Fix:** shrivelled at the stem end? Those go first. Pull them off before
   the cluster goes out.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Red or black seedless** | Any grocery | The default. Sweeter and better-looking than green on a board. |
| **Champagne grapes** | Whole Foods, farmers markets, seasonal | Tiny, dense, spectacular clusters. The best-looking grape you can buy. |
| **Cotton candy grapes** | Grocery, seasonal | Genuinely taste of spun sugar. A talking point. |
| **Concord grapes** | Farmers markets, autumn | Slip-skin and musky. Strong flavour — better beside blue cheese than brie. |
| **Green Thompson seedless** | Any grocery | Tarter and crisper. Good with rich cheeses. |
| **Grapes on the vine, boxed** | Costco, Whole Foods | Sold as intact clusters. Saves you the scissors. |

**Label check:** look at the stems, not the fruit. Green pliable stems mean
fresh; brown brittle stems mean the bunch has been sitting. A grape that falls
off when you lift the cluster is past it.

**Skip:** pre-loose grapes in a tub. No stems, no clusters, and they're
already softening.

## Quick FAQ

**How do I stop grapes rolling everywhere?**
Snip the bunch into small clusters with kitchen scissors. Never put loose
grapes on a board.

**Red or green?**
Red and black look better and taste sweeter on a board. Green are tarter and
work well with very rich cheeses.

**How much per person?**
2–3 oz — roughly one small cluster each.

**Should they be cold?**
Yes. Unlike cheese, fruit is better straight from the fridge.

**What are champagne grapes?**
Tiny seedless grapes in dense clusters, also called Zante currants. The most
striking grape you can put on a board.
