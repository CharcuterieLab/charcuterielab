## The short version

Salt-cured sturgeon roe, served very cold, in a small quantity, with almost nothing
done to it. It is the most expensive thing that will ever go on your board and the
easiest to ruin: a metal spoon, a warm room or a strong ingredient beside it will
all undo it. If the budget will not stretch, salmon or trout roe delivers most of
the visual and textural effect for a tenth of the price.

## What it is

Roe from sturgeon, screened, lightly salted and tinned. Beluga, ossetra and sevruga
are the classic species; almost everything sold today is farmed, which is both
legal and, increasingly, very good. Grain size, colour and firmness vary by species
and grade.

**Taste:** clean salinity, butter, hazelnut, a long mineral finish
**Texture:** firm beads that pop, then dissolve
**Look:** glossy grey to olive-black spheres, uniform

The mechanism is a burst of concentrated marine salt. Each egg is a sealed membrane
holding fat and salt, so nothing is released until it breaks against the palate.
That delay is why caviar tastes like an event rather than a seasoning, and why it
must never be spread or crushed — crushing releases everything at once and it just
tastes salty.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Blini** | The canonical base. Warm, soft, faintly yeasty, and structurally sound under a spoonful. |
| **Crème fraîche or mascarpone** | Cool acid and fat. It buffers the salt and makes a small portion feel generous. |
| **Smoked salmon** | Two cured fish, one smoked and one salted. A luxury board can hold both. |
| **Brioche toast points** | Butter and a little sweetness. The classier alternative to a cracker. |
| **Cream cheese** | The bagel-board version. Less traditional, entirely reasonable. |
| **Boquerones** | If you are building a seafood board, the vinegar-cured anchovy is the low-cost partner that makes the caviar look deliberate. |

**Skip it with:** anything strong. Cheese, cured meat, pickles and chili all
obliterate it. Caviar wants an almost empty corner of the board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Keep it cold until the last moment.** Nest the tin in crushed ice.
2. **Use a mother-of-pearl, bone or plastic spoon.** Metal makes it taste metallic.
3. **Never stir or spread it.** Lift, place, stop.
4. **Give it its own space** at one end of the board.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** and open the tin at the table.
6. **Put the accompaniments beside it, not on it** — blini, crème fraîche, chives.
7. **Fix:** tasting flat? It is too cold or too warm. Straight from the freezer it
   is mute; above about 10C the fat goes greasy. Ice, not the freezer.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Farmed ossetra** | Specialty fishmongers, online | The usual entry point. Nutty and firm. |
| **Kaluga hybrid** | Specialty, online | Large grain, close to beluga in style, considerably cheaper. |
| **Farmed sevruga** | Specialty | Smaller grain, more intense. Good value. |
| **Salmon or trout roe (ikura)** | Any good fishmonger, Japanese and Russian grocers | Large orange beads, bright and briny. A tenth of the price and excellent on a board. |
| **Lumpfish or capelin roe** | Supermarkets | Dyed and much saltier. Use it for colour, not for flavour. |

**Label check:** malossol means lightly salted. A packing date should be on the tin,
and the roe should be glossy rather than dull.

**Skip:** wild-caught beluga, which is protected and largely illegal to import.
Farmed hybrids are legal, ethical and good.

## Quick FAQ

**What is the difference between caviar and roe?**
Strictly, caviar is salt-cured sturgeon roe. Salmon, trout and lumpfish roe are
roe, and are a tenth of the price.

**Why should you not use a metal spoon?**
Metal reacts with the roe and adds a faint metallic taste. Mother-of-pearl, bone or
plastic does not.

**What does malossol mean?**
Lightly salted, in Russian. It is the standard for good caviar — less salt, shorter
shelf life, better flavour.

**What is a good caviar to start with?**
Farmed ossetra is the usual entry point. For a board on a budget, trout or salmon
roe delivers most of the effect for a fraction of the cost.

**How much per person?**
Half an ounce is generous. An ounce shared between two people is a real serving.
