## The short version

A walnut without the bitterness. Pecans are sweeter, softer, oilier and more
buttery, which makes them easier to like and less useful as a contrast. Toast
them. They're also the correct nut for anything candied — the shape holds sugar
better than any other.

## What it is

The kernel of a North American hickory relative, sold as halves or pieces.
Georgia and Texas produce most of them. There's no meaningful variety choice at
retail; freshness and size are what matter.

**Taste:** sweet, buttery, faintly maple
**Texture:** soft-crisp, oily, snaps cleanly
**Look:** long ridged halves, reddish-brown

The mechanism is fat without bitterness. Pecans are among the highest-fat nuts,
and they're low in the tannins that make walnuts astringent. That means they add
richness rather than contrast — which is why they work best against sharp,
acidic or salty things that supply the edge themselves.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp acid and crystalline salt against soft sweet fat. The pecan supplies what the cheddar lacks. |
| **Stilton and blue cheese** | Sweetness blunting salt. Gentler than a walnut for people who find that pairing too aggressive. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a log in chopped pecans and cranberries. Tang, sugar, crunch. |
| **Dried cranberries** | The fixed American pairing — tart red fruit and sweet buttery nut. |
| **Apples and pears** | Autumn logic, and the pecan's maple note suits both. |
| **Bourbon and maple** | Not on the board, but it's the flavour family a pecan belongs to. |

**Skip it with:** heavily smoked meats. Pecan sweetness and smoke both push in
the same direction and the result is muddy.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Toast them.** 350°F for 7–8 minutes — less than walnuts, since they're
   oilier and burn faster.
2. **Salt lightly while warm** if the board is savoury.
3. **Use halves.** Pecan halves are the best-looking nut on any board.
4. **Chop them** only for rolling cheese logs.
5. **Serve 0.5–1 oz per person.**
6. **Candy them** if you want the sweet version — see the candied pecans page.
7. **Fix:** rancid? Pecans spoil almost as fast as walnuts. Keep them in the
   freezer and buy in small amounts.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw pecan halves** | Any grocery, Costco | Buy raw and toast yourself. Mammoth halves look best. |
| **Georgia or Texas pecans** | Farmers markets, online, autumn | Fresher and noticeably sweeter than supermarket stock. |
| **In-shell pecans** | Grocery, autumn | Always fresh, and a bowl with a cracker is good board theatre. |
| **Candied / praline pecans** | Grocery, Trader Joe's | Sweet version. Belongs in the dessert corner. |
| **Smoked pecans** | Specialty, online | Underrated. Excellent with cheddar. |

**Label check:** pecans should smell sweet and faintly of butter. Any sharpness
means rancid. Buy in autumn if you can — that's when the crop lands.

**Skip:** pecan pieces for board use, and any bag past its date.

## Quick FAQ

**Pecans or walnuts on a cheese board?**
Walnuts if you want bitterness and contrast; pecans if you want richness and
sweetness. Pecans are the easier crowd-pleaser.

**Should I toast pecans?**
Yes — 350°F for 7–8 minutes. They burn faster than walnuts, so watch them.

**What cheese goes with pecans?**
Aged cheddar, stilton and other blues, and fresh goat cheese.

**How do I store pecans?**
In the freezer. They're high in oil and go rancid quickly at room temperature.

**Halves or pieces?**
Halves for the board. Pieces only for coating a cheese log.
