## The short version

Two very different sausages under one name. **French andouille** is made from
tripe and chitterlings, pale grey, sliced cold, and genuinely pungent.
**Louisiana andouille** is coarse smoked pork with cayenne and garlic, and it
wants a hot pan. Most Americans mean the second.

## What it is

The French original, *andouille de Vire* or *de Guémené*, is a cold-eaten
sausage of layered pork tripe and intestine, smoked and dried, sold in slices
that show concentric rings. Louisiana andouille descends from it via Acadian
settlers but diverged completely: coarse-chopped pork shoulder, heavy garlic,
cayenne and black pepper, double-smoked over pecan or sugarcane.

**French:** pungent, offaly, smoky, firm and rubbery — concentric ringed slices
**Louisiana:** smoky, garlicky, peppery, coarse and meaty — deep red-brown

Shared ancestry and divergent ingredients are the mechanism, and the reason for
the confusion. Both are smoked pork sausages named andouille; only one is made
from offal, and only one is eaten cold. Buying the wrong one is the mistake this
page exists to prevent.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Grainy mustard** | Both versions want sharp acid. Non-negotiable with the French one. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp cheese against smoke and pepper. Good with the Louisiana style. |
| **Pickled okra and hot pickles** | The Southern accompaniment, and the acid cuts the fat. |
| **Tostada rounds or crackers** | Something sturdy under a coarse sausage. |
| **Apple and pickled onion** | Sweet-sour against smoke. |
| **Cornichons** | For the French version specifically. |
| **Cold lager, dry cider, Beaujolais** | Cold and acidic against smoke and cayenne. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses, and smoked gouda — one smoked item
per board. The French version in particular travels across a board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Know which one you have.** Grey with visible concentric rings is French,
   eaten cold. Deep red-brown and coarse is Louisiana, and it wants heat.
2. **Louisiana: slice into coins and sear** two minutes a side in a dry pan
   until the edges char. Serve warm.
3. **French: slice thin and serve cold**, with mustard and cornichons.
4. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
5. **Warn people about the French version.** It's offal and it's pungent — an
   honest word beforehand goes a long way.
6. **Put the acid right beside it** either way.
7. **Fix:** Louisiana andouille that tastes flat cold is behaving normally. Sear
   it.

| **Jacob's World Famous Andouille** | Online, Louisiana | The benchmark Cajun producer. Worth ordering. |
| **Andouille de Guémené** | French delis, specialty importers | The tripe original, rings and all. Rare in the US. |
| **Andouillette** | French specialty | The smaller, even more pungent relative. For the committed only. |
| **Kielbasa** | Any grocery | The milder smoked sausage — see its own page. |
| **Chorizo** | Most grocery | If you want the paprika version instead — see its own page. |

**Label check:** French andouille lists tripe or chitterlings. Louisiana
andouille lists pork shoulder, garlic and cayenne. The ingredient list settles
it immediately.

**Skip:** buying French andouille for a general crowd without warning them. It
is genuinely divisive, and springing it on people rarely goes well.

## Quick FAQ

**Why are there two different andouilles?**
Louisiana andouille descends from the French one via Acadian settlers, but
changed completely — losing the offal and gaining smoke, garlic and cayenne.

**Is French andouille made from intestines?**
Yes — layered pork tripe and chitterlings. That's the traditional product and
it's why it's pungent.

**Do I cook Louisiana andouille?**
It's fully smoked and safe to eat, but it's much better seared. Cold, it's
rubbery.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.

**What's andouillette?**
A smaller, fresher, even more strongly flavoured French tripe sausage. Not a
board item unless you know your crowd very well.
