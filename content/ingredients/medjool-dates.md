## The short version

Medjools are the large soft ones — caramel-textured, glossy, and sweet enough to
read as candy. They do two jobs: sit on the board as fruit, and act as an edible
cup for cheese. A split, pitted Medjool filled with goat cheese and wrapped in
prosciutto is the most reliably popular single bite on any board.

## What it is

A large date variety, semi-soft, with a thin skin over dense caramel-coloured
flesh and a single long pit. Deglet Noor is the other common date — smaller,
drier, chewier, and better for chopping than for stuffing.

**Taste:** caramel, brown sugar, faint molasses
**Texture:** soft, sticky, slightly fibrous
**Look:** deep brown, glossy, wrinkled

The mechanism is concentrated sugar with almost no water. Fresh fruit resets the
palate by rinsing; a date does the opposite — it coats. That's why it works
against very salty, very funky things: blue cheese and cured pork have nothing
that a date can't cover. It's also why one or two per person is enough.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Blue cheese (gorgonzola, stilton)** | Sugar blunting salt and funk directly. The strongest sweet-savoury pairing on any board. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Stuff the date with it. Lactic tang inside caramel sweetness, and the textures are opposites. |
| **Prosciutto** | Wrap a stuffed date in a half-slice. Salt, fat, sugar, tang — a complete bite. |
| **Manchego** | Nutty sheep milk against molasses. A Spanish standard. |
| **Pistachios and almonds** | Push a nut into the pitted cavity. Crunch is what a date is missing. |
| **Parmigiano-Reggiano** | Crystalline salt against sticky sweet — the best cheap version of this pairing. |

**Skip it with:** other sweet things. A date beside honey, jam and dried figs
makes a board that reads as one flat sugary note.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy soft, glossy dates.** Dull, hard, crystallised ones are old.
2. **Split lengthwise on one side only** — not all the way through. The date
   should open like a book and stay in one piece.
3. **Pull the pit out.** It comes cleanly from a soft Medjool.
4. **Stuff, if you're stuffing:** goat cheese, mascarpone, or blue, piped or
   spooned in. A nut on top.
5. **Wrap in half a slice of prosciutto** for the full version.
6. **Serve 1–2 per person.** They're very rich.
7. **Fix:** hard and dry? Steam them for two minutes or soak in warm water for
   ten, then pat dry. They come back most of the way.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Medjool dates** | Any grocery, Costco, Middle Eastern markets | The board date. Buy the largest grade you can — "jumbo" or "large". |
| **Costco Medjools** | Costco | Best value by a distance. Two pounds, consistently soft. |
| **Deglet Noor** | Grocery | Smaller, drier, less sweet. Fine chopped into a cheese board's nut mix, wrong for stuffing. |
| **Barhi dates** | Middle Eastern markets, farmers markets | Soft, honeyed, sometimes sold fresh on the stem. Exceptional and rarely seen. |
| **Date paste / date syrup (silan)** | Middle Eastern markets | Drizzle over blue cheese. Different ingredient, same logic. |

**Label check:** look for a soft, plump body and a glossy skin. White crystals on
the surface are sugar bloom — harmless, but a sign they've been sitting.

**Skip:** pitted dates in vacuum packs that feel hard through the plastic, and
anything sold as "date pieces" coated in oat flour.

## Quick FAQ

**Which dates are best for a charcuterie board?**
Medjool — large, soft, and sturdy enough to split and stuff.

**How do I pit a date?**
Split it lengthwise along one side and lift the pit out. Don't cut all the way
through.

**What do you stuff dates with?**
Goat cheese, blue cheese, or mascarpone, usually topped with a pistachio or
almond and sometimes wrapped in prosciutto.

**How many per person?**
One or two. They're very sweet and very rich.

**My dates are hard — can I fix them?**
Steam them for two minutes or soak in warm water for ten, then pat dry.
