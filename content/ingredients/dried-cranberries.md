## The short version

The workhorse. Every supermarket has them, they cost almost nothing, they keep
forever, and a scatter of red across a pale board does more visual work than any
other cheap ingredient. They're always sweetened — raw cranberries are too sour
to dry palatably — so the choice is what they're sweetened with.

## What it is

Cranberries, sliced or whole, sweetened and dried. Standard versions use cane
sugar or corn syrup; better versions use apple or fruit juice concentrate. Almost
all carry a light coating of sunflower oil to stop them clumping.

**Taste:** sweet-tart, bright, slightly astringent
**Texture:** chewy and slightly leathery
**Look:** small deep-red pieces, glossy

The mechanism is colour more than flavour. Cranberries are the only cheap dried
fruit that stays genuinely red, and on a board dominated by cream, beige and tan,
red is what makes a photograph work. The tartness is real but modest once
sweetened — it supports rather than leads.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sweet-tart. The pairing behind every cranberry-studded cheddar sold at holidays. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a log in them with pistachios. Colour, texture, and acid, in one move. |
| **Brie** | Cool tart fruit against warm soft fat; the standard baked-brie topping, served cold. |
| **Stilton** | Sweet blunting salt, with the tartness keeping it from cloying. |
| **Pecans and walnuts** | The base of every holiday nut mix — cranberry and pecan is a fixed pair. |
| **Turkey and holiday boards** | The obvious contextual pairing, and it earns its place. |

**Skip it with:** anything already sweet. Cranberries are sweeter than they
taste, and stacking them with jam and honey flattens a board.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **No prep.** Straight from the bag.
2. **Scatter widely.** They're at their best distributed across the board as
   colour, not gathered in one pile.
3. **Roll a goat cheese log in them**, chopped, with nuts — one of the highest
   return-per-effort things you can do.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Look for juice-sweetened** versions if you want less sugar and more tartness.
6. **Keep them sealed** — the oil coating goes stale and they harden.
7. **Fix:** hard and clumped? Warm water for two minutes, drain, pat very dry.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ocean Spray Craisins** | Any grocery | The default. Cane sugar, consistent, cheap. |
| **Apple-juice-sweetened cranberries** | Trader Joe's, Whole Foods | Tarter and less candied. The better board option. |
| **Unsweetened dried cranberries** | Health stores, online | Genuinely sour, and hard going for most guests. Good with strong blue. |
| **Cranberries with orange** | Grocery, seasonal | Flavoured version. Works well with brie and holiday boards. |
| **Freeze-dried cranberries** | Health stores | Crisp and very tart. A texture change rather than a substitute. |

**Label check:** read what they're sweetened with. Apple or fruit juice
concentrate beats corn syrup. Sunflower oil in the ingredients is normal.

**Skip:** anything with added flavourings beyond fruit juice.

## Quick FAQ

**Are dried cranberries always sweetened?**
Almost always — raw cranberries are too sour to dry palatably. Apple-juice-
sweetened versions are the tartest widely available option.

**What's the difference between Craisins and dried cranberries?**
Craisins is Ocean Spray's brand name for sweetened dried cranberries. Same thing.

**How do I use them on a board?**
Scatter them across gaps for colour, or roll a goat cheese log in them with
chopped nuts.

**What cheese goes with dried cranberries?**
Aged cheddar, brie, stilton, and fresh goat cheese.

**How much per person?**
About half an ounce — they're a scatter, not a serving.
