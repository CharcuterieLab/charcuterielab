## The short version

A short summer season, the deepest red on any board, and the only fruit people
eat slowly because of the pits. Leave the stems on, put a small empty bowl
beside them, and don't try to pit them in advance.

## What it is

Sweet cherries are the board variety — Bing is the dark red standard, Rainier
the yellow-blushed sweet one, Lapins and Skeena are large and firm. Sour
cherries (Montmorency, Morello) are for cooking and preserving, not for eating
raw.

**Taste:** sweet with a wine-like depth, faintly almondy at the pit
**Texture:** firm skin, dense juicy flesh
**Look:** deep red-black to gold-blush, glossy, on green stems

Benzaldehyde is the mechanism behind the flavour people can't name. It's the
compound that gives almonds their scent, and cherries carry it — which is why
cherry and almond taste related, and why cherries pair unusually well with
marzipan, amaretto and nuts.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh chèvre and goat cheese** | Tart white cheese against dark sweet fruit. The cleanest cherry pairing. |
| **Duck prosciutto** | Duck and dark fruit is permanent. Cherries do what a preserve would. |
| **Blue cheese** | Sweetness and depth against salt and funk. |
| **Almonds and marzipan** | The benzaldehyde link. Cherry and almond taste designed for each other. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Obvious and correct. |
| **Aged gouda** | Caramel against wine-dark fruit. |
| **Pinot Noir, kirsch, port** | Cherry-forward wines and spirits. |

**Skip it with:** smoked fish and anything briny. Cherries belong on the sweet
half.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Leave the stems on.** They're the handle, they look better, and they keep
   the fruit from bleeding onto the board.
2. **Put a small empty bowl beside them for pits.** This is the practical detail
   people forget, and without it the pits end up on the board.
3. **Don't pit them in advance.** A pitted cherry weeps and collapses within
   the hour.
4. **Rinse and dry thoroughly.**
5. **Serve 2–3 oz per person**, piled loosely rather than arranged.
6. **Serve cold.** Cherries lose their snap at room temperature faster than
   most fruit.
7. **Fix:** out of season? Brandied cherries do the same job year-round — see
   their own page.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bing cherries** | Any grocery, May–August | The dark red standard. Sweet, firm, reliable. |
| **Rainier cherries** | Grocery, farmers markets, summer | Yellow with a red blush. Sweeter and more delicate. Striking on a board. |
| **Lapins or Skeena** | Grocery, summer | Large and very firm. Good travellers. |
| **Farmers market cherries** | Local, in season | Noticeably better — cherries lose sugar and firmness in transit. |
| **Brandied cherries** | Specialty, bar suppliers | The year-round substitute — see their own page. |
| **Dried cherries** | Any grocery | The other off-season answer — see their own page. |

**Label check:** green pliable stems and firm glossy fruit. Brown dry stems mean
the cherries have been sitting, even if the fruit still looks fine.

**Skip:** buying cherries out of season. Imported winter cherries are expensive
and disappointing.

## Quick FAQ

**Should I pit cherries for a board?**
No. Pitted cherries weep and collapse. Leave the stems and pits and put out a
small bowl for the pits.

**Bing or Rainier?**
Bing for deep colour and reliability, Rainier for sweetness and a striking
gold-and-red look.

**When are cherries in season?**
Roughly May through August in the US. Outside that, use dried or brandied.

**How much per person?**
2–3 oz.

**Why do cherry and almond go together?**
Both contain benzaldehyde — the compound behind almond scent. The link is
chemical, not just traditional.
