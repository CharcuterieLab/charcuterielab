## The short version

Honey infused with chilli — usually chilli flakes and a little vinegar. Heat
arrives a second after the sweetness, which is exactly the right order on a
board full of fat and salt. It's also trivially easy to make: warm honey, chilli
flakes, a splash of vinegar, twenty minutes.

## What it is

Honey steeped with hot peppers. Mike's Hot Honey is the brand that created the
category; most versions use chilli flakes with cider vinegar to keep the honey
from being cloying. Heat level ranges from barely noticeable to genuinely hot.

**Taste:** sweet first, then chilli heat, with a vinegar edge
**Texture:** thinner than plain honey, sometimes flecked with chilli
**Look:** amber, slightly red-tinged, flecked

The mechanism is delayed heat. Capsaicin registers a beat after sugar does, so a
drizzle of hot honey gives one bite two separate movements — sweetness that
handles the salt, then heat that clears the fat. Nothing else on a board does two
things in sequence like that, which is why it works on so many different
ingredients.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata and fresh mozzarella** | Cream with nothing to cut it, meeting sugar and heat. The best single use of hot honey on a board. |
| **Soppressata and spicy salami** | Doubling the heat and adding sugar to carry it — the pizza combination, on a board. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp against sweet, with heat where the cheese's own bite fades. |
| **Brie** | Soft fat needs sweetness; the chilli stops it being just dessert. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt, fat, sugar, heat. Four things in one bite. |
| **Fried and crispy things** | Cheese crisps, fried chicken, anything with a crust. Its native habitat. |

**Skip it with:** delicate aged cheeses you paid a lot for. The heat will bury
them.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Decant into a small pot with a dipper**, same as honey.
2. **Drizzle over the burrata** in front of people. It's a visual as much as a
   flavour.
3. **Keep it off the crackers** — it soaks in and softens them.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
5. **Make it:** warm 1 cup honey with 2 tsp chilli flakes and 1 tbsp cider
   vinegar over low heat for 20 minutes. Don't boil. Strain or leave the flakes in.
6. **Say it's spicy.** Guests should know before they drizzle.
7. **Fix:** too hot? Cut it half-and-half with plain honey.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mike's Hot Honey** | Any grocery | The original and the benchmark. Moderate heat, cider vinegar edge. |
| **Trader Joe's hot honey** | Trader Joe's | Cheaper and slightly milder. |
| **Habanero or ghost pepper honey** | Specialty, hot sauce shops | Much hotter. Use sparingly and warn people. |
| **Calabrian chilli honey** | Italian delis | Fruitier heat. Excellent with cured meat. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Twenty minutes, and you control the heat exactly. |

**Label check:** look for real chilli in the ingredients rather than "natural
flavour", and check whether vinegar is included — it's what keeps it from being
sickly.

**Skip:** anything where sugar or corn syrup outranks honey.

## Quick FAQ

**What is hot honey?**
Honey infused with chilli peppers, usually with a little vinegar. Sweet first,
then heat.

**What goes best with hot honey?**
Burrata and fresh mozzarella, spicy salami, aged cheddar, and anything fried or
crispy.

**Can I make hot honey at home?**
Yes — warm a cup of honey with two teaspoons of chilli flakes and a tablespoon of
cider vinegar for twenty minutes over low heat.

**How spicy is it?**
Mike's Hot Honey and supermarket versions are mild to moderate. Habanero versions
are genuinely hot — warn guests.

**Does it replace regular honey on a board?**
It can, but if you have a delicate aged cheese, put out plain honey too.
