## The short version

Cherry-sized red peppers, pickled sweet with a mild chilli heat, sold seeded and
hollow. The hollow is what makes them special — stuff one with goat cheese and
you have a two-ingredient appetizer that looks like real work. Peppadew is a
trademark; the pepper is a Piquanté.

## What it is

The Piquanté pepper, grown in South Africa and pickled in a sweet-sour brine.
Sold whole and deseeded in jars, in mild and hot versions. Similar peppers are
sold as "sweet piquanté", "goldew", or generically as sweet cherry peppers.

**Taste:** sweet, tangy, with warm chilli heat behind
**Texture:** firm and crisp, with a hollow centre
**Look:** small bright red globes, glossy

The mechanism is sweetness carrying heat. Sugar in the brine blunts the capsaicin
enough that even people who avoid spice will eat them, while the heat itself
still does the palate-clearing work. Combined with a hollow shape, that makes them
the rare board ingredient that's both a condiment and a vessel.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Stuff them. Tangy chalky cheese inside sweet-hot pepper is the best two-ingredient bite on any board. |
| **Cream cheese** | Milder stuffing for a milder crowd, and the white against red is the visual. |
| **Prosciutto** | Wrap a stuffed pepper in half a slice — salt, fat, sweet, heat, tang. |
| **Manchego** | Sheep-milk salt against sweet-sour brine and gentle chilli. |
| **Burrata** | Whole peppers scattered around a ball of burrata, with oil and salt. |
| **Antipasto boards generally** | They fill the gap between olives and roasted peppers. |

**Skip it with:** nothing. Peppadews are unusually easy to place.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Drain and pat dry**, especially if you're stuffing them.
2. **Stuff them:** pipe or spoon goat cheese, cream cheese, or whipped feta into
   the hollow. A piping bag or a zip bag with the corner cut works best.
3. **Or serve them whole** in a small bowl.
4. **Serve 0.75 oz per person** — three or four peppers.
5. **Stuff them up to a day ahead** and keep them covered in the fridge.
6. **Buy mild** unless you know your crowd.
7. **Fix:** too sweet on their own? Stuffed with a tangy cheese, the sweetness
   becomes an asset.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Peppadew mild** | Grocery, olive bars, Whole Foods | The default. Sweet with gentle heat. |
| **Peppadew hot** | Grocery, specialty | Noticeably hotter. Still sweet. |
| **Pre-stuffed peppadews** | Olive bars, deli counters | Filled with goat cheese or cream cheese. A real time-saver and usually good. |
| **Sweet piquanté peppers** | Trader Joe's, generic brands | The same pepper without the trademark. Cheaper. |
| **Sweet cherry peppers** | Italian delis | Similar and often stuffed with provolone and prosciutto. |

**Label check:** they should be sold whole and deseeded. Sliced versions can't be
stuffed and lose the point.

**Skip:** anything packed in oil rather than brine if you plan to stuff them —
they're slippery.

## Quick FAQ

**What is a Peppadew?**
A trademarked brand of pickled Piquanté pepper from South Africa — small, red,
sweet-sour, and mildly hot.

**Are Peppadews spicy?**
Mildly. The sugar in the brine blunts the heat considerably. Hot versions are
available.

**What do you stuff Peppadews with?**
Fresh goat cheese, cream cheese, or whipped feta. Wrap them in prosciutto for the
full version.

**Can I stuff them ahead?**
Yes — up to a day, covered in the fridge.

**How much per person?**
Three or four peppers, about three-quarters of an ounce.
