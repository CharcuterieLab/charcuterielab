## The short version

Crackers made from chickpea or lentil flour instead of wheat. They're
gluten-free, high in protein, and taste faintly of the legume they're made
from — savory and earthy rather than neutral. Best on a Mediterranean or
plant-based board.

## What it is

Legume flours behave differently from grain flours: more protein, less starch,
and a distinct flavor that doesn't bake out. Chickpea flour in particular
carries a nutty, slightly beany taste that's a feature in socca and falafel and
a surprise in a cracker if you're expecting neutral.

**Taste:** savory, earthy, nutty, faintly beany
**Texture:** firm, dense, satisfying crunch
**Look:** pale gold to tan, often thicker than a wheat cracker

Protein is the mechanism, in two senses. It makes the cracker genuinely filling
in a way a rice cracker isn't, and it browns differently in the oven, giving a
deeper roasted note. That savory depth is why these work with dips and struggle
next to a delicate cheese.

**Fair warning:** this is the most volatile shelf in the cracker aisle. Brands
launch and disappear constantly, and the chickpea *snack* category (puffs,
roasted chickpeas) is much larger than the chickpea *cracker* one. Buy what's
in front of you rather than hunting a specific box.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Hummus** | Chickpea on chickpea, deliberately. Same ingredient, two textures. |
| **Whipped feta and labneh** | Salty, tangy dairy against earthy legume. The Mediterranean board's core bite. |
| **Baba ganoush and muhammara** | Smoke and sweetness need a savory, substantial base. |
| **Marinated olives and sun-dried tomato** | Everything here is already savory and briny; the cracker fits the register. |
| **Aged cheddar and Manchego** | Assertive cheeses that can meet a flavored cracker halfway. |
| **Harissa or zhoug** | Heat and herbs on a base with enough body to carry them. |
| **Dry rosé, Sauvignon Blanc** | High-acid wine cuts a dense, savory cracker. |

**Skip it with:** burrata, fresh mozzarella and mild brie — the legume flavor
buries them. And skip sweet pairings; chickpea and fruit jam is a bad bite.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Build the plant-based corner around them.** Hummus, olives, roasted
   peppers, dates. They anchor that half of the board.
2. **Serve 4–5 per person.** They're filling — the protein is doing that.
3. **They're sturdy enough to scoop**, which puts them ahead of almond flour
   crackers for a dip-heavy board.
4. **Pair with something salty.** Legume flour on its own reads flat; salt is
   what wakes it up.
5. **Buy what's stocked.** The specific brand matters less here than in most
   categories, because the shelf turns over so fast.
6. **Fix:** re-crisp at 300°F for 3 minutes. They hold up well otherwise.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Whatever chickpea or lentil cracker your store stocks** | Whole Foods, Sprouts, Target, natural grocers | Genuinely the honest advice. The brands rotate; the category behaves consistently. |
| **Mary's Gone Crackers Real Thin, legume varieties** | Whole Foods, most grocery | When stocked, one of the more reliable options. |
| **Hippeas and roasted chickpea snacks** | Target, Whole Foods, grocery | Not crackers — puffs and roasted chickpeas. Good *on* a board, not a base. |
| **Lentil chips** | Natural grocers, Amazon | Sturdier and better for dip. Same flavor logic. |
| **Chickpea flour** | Bob's Red Mill, Anthony's, Indian groceries (as *besan*) | The DIY route, and the cheapest by far. See the socca page for the flatbread version. |

**Label check:** chickpea or lentil flour should be first. Many "chickpea"
snacks are mostly potato or rice starch with a little legume for the label.

**Skip:** sweetened or heavily seasoned legume snacks on a cheese board. They
read as a health-food snack rather than a base.

## Quick FAQ

**Are chickpea crackers gluten-free?**
Usually yes, but check — some blends add wheat. Look for a certification.

**Do they taste like chickpeas?**
A little, yes. Earthy and savory rather than neutral. That's why they suit
hummus and struggle with mild cheese.

**Why is this shelf so inconsistent?**
It's a young category with a lot of small brands. Buy on what's available
rather than seeking one product.

**How many per person?**
4–5. The protein makes them more filling than a wheat cracker.

**Cheaper alternative?**
Make socca from chickpea flour. Same flavor, a fraction of the cost.
