## The short version

Winter's answer to the empty fruit slot. Blood oranges run December through
April, taste like orange with a raspberry edge, and cut into crimson-and-orange
wheels that no other ingredient on a board can compete with visually. Slice
crosswise — the cross-section is the whole point.

## What it is

A pigmented sweet orange. The red comes from anthocyanins, the same compounds in
red wine and berries, which develop when the fruit sees cold nights. Three
varieties matter: **Moro** (darkest, most berry-like), **Tarocco** (sweetest,
partly red), **Sanguinello** (in between).

**Taste:** orange with raspberry and a faint bitterness
**Texture:** juicy, more tender-membraned than a navel
**Look:** crimson-streaked wheels, sometimes solid red

The mechanism is acid plus bitterness. A regular orange is mostly sugar and would
sit oddly beside cheese. A blood orange carries enough acid and pith bitterness to
work like a dressing — which is why the standard use isn't as fruit alongside but
as fruit *on top of* something creamy.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Burrata** | The defining winter pairing. Acid and bitterness cutting straight through cream, with olive oil and salt. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | Mild sweet curd needs an acid; the blood orange supplies it and the colour. |
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Two acids, different registers — citric against lactic. |
| **Prosciutto** | Salt and citrus, the way orange works in a Sicilian salad. |
| **Pistachios** | Sicilian logic: green, red, salt, sweet. Also the strongest colour combination on the board. |
| **Fennel and olives** | Anise and brine against citrus — this is a Sicilian salad on a board. |

**Skip it with:** aged hard cheeses and blue. Citrus acid clashes with the
ammoniated edge in both.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Slice crosswise into wheels**, 1/4 inch. Segments waste the colour.
2. **Cut the peel off first** if the skin is thick — top and bottom flat, then
   knife down the sides. Thin-skinned ones can be left unpeeled.
3. **Blot the slices** on paper towel. Citrus juice will run across a board and
   soak crackers.
4. **Set them on a small plate or in a shallow bowl**, not directly on wood.
5. **Finish with flaky salt and olive oil** if they're going next to cheese.
6. **Serve cold**, sliced within an hour.
7. **Fix:** pale and disappointing inside? Colour depends on how cold the growing
   season was. Sicilian imports are more reliably dark than Californian.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Moro blood oranges** | Grocery, Dec–Apr | Darkest flesh, strongest berry note. The one you want. |
| **Tarocco** | Italian grocers, specialty | Sweetest and least acidic. Partly red, not solid. |
| **Sicilian imports** | Whole Foods, Italian delis | More reliably pigmented than domestic. |
| **Cara Cara oranges** | Any grocery, winter | Pink-fleshed navel, not a true blood orange, but sweet and low-acid with good colour. |

**Label check:** heavy for its size means juicy. A blotchy red blush on the peel
often signals deeper colour inside, though it isn't a guarantee.

**Skip:** blood oranges out of season — from May onward they're storage fruit,
pale and dry.

## Quick FAQ

**When are blood oranges in season?**
December through April. Outside that window they're pale and expensive.

**Do I peel blood oranges for a board?**
Usually yes, cutting the peel and pith off with a knife, then slicing crosswise
into wheels.

**Which variety is reddest?**
Moro. Tarocco is sweeter but less uniformly red.

**Why is my blood orange barely red inside?**
The pigment develops with cold nights. Warm-winter fruit stays pale — Sicilian
imports are the safer buy.

**How do I stop juice running over the board?**
Blot the slices and serve them on a small plate or in a shallow dish.
