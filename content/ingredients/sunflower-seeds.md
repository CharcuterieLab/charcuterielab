## The short version

Almost free, entirely nut-free, and useful as a scatter rather than a serving.
Buy hulled kernels — the in-shell ones belong at a baseball game, not on a board.
Toast them and they go from filler to something people actually pick at.

## What it is

The hulled kernel of the sunflower, sold raw or roasted, salted or plain. There's
no meaningful variety choice; freshness and whether they're toasted are what
matter.

**Taste:** mild, nutty, faintly bitter, grassy
**Texture:** small and crisp, softer than a pepita
**Look:** small pale grey-green teardrops

The mechanism is scale. Sunflower seeds are the smallest crunch you can add,
which means they distribute across a surface rather than sitting on it. Rolled
onto a cheese log or scattered over a soft cheese they give texture in every bite
instead of every third one — a different effect from adding a nut.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Fresh goat cheese** | Roll a log in toasted seeds. Fine even crunch against chalky texture, and nut-free. |
| **Aged cheddar** | Sharp and crystalline against mild and crisp. |
| **Feta** | Brine and salt with a grassy seed — a Mediterranean salad, in board form. |
| **Golden raisins** | Sweet and small against savoury and small. The base of every seed mix. |
| **Brie** | Scattered over a wedge, they add the texture the cheese lacks. |
| **Sunflower seed butter** | If a guest can't have nuts, this is the spread that replaces peanut butter. |

**Skip it with:** strongly flavoured cheeses that will bury them. Sunflower seeds
are mild and need something to sit against, not something to fight.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy hulled kernels.** In-shell sunflower seeds are a spectator sport, not a
   board ingredient.
2. **Toast in a dry pan**, 3 minutes over medium, shaking. They colour fast.
3. **Salt while hot.**
4. **Scatter over cheese** or use them to coat a log. Don't serve a bowl of them
   on their own — they look like birdseed.
5. **Serve 0.5 oz per person.**
6. **Combine them with pepitas** for a nut-free seed scatter with better colour.
7. **Fix:** bitter? Old seeds. Sunflower kernels go rancid quickly because of
   their oil content — buy small and store cold.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw hulled sunflower kernels** | Any grocery, bulk bins | Cheapest. Toast them yourself. |
| **Roasted salted kernels** | Grocery, Trader Joe's | Ready to go. Toast briefly to refresh. |
| **Seed mix with pepitas** | Trader Joe's, health stores | Better colour and texture than sunflower alone. |
| **Everything-seasoned seeds** | Trader Joe's, specialty | Savoury and good with cream cheese. |
| **In-shell sunflower seeds** | Grocery | Not for boards. |

**Label check:** smell them. Sunflower kernels go rancid faster than most seeds,
and bulk bins are where that happens.

**Skip:** anything that smells sharp or tastes bitter — that's oxidised oil.

## Quick FAQ

**Are sunflower seeds nut-free?**
Yes. They're a seed and a standard substitute on nut-free boards.

**Hulled or in-shell?**
Hulled kernels. In-shell seeds mean guests spitting shells, which no board wants.

**Do I need to toast them?**
It's a big improvement — three minutes in a dry pan, salted hot.

**How do I use them on a board?**
Scatter over soft cheese or roll a goat cheese log in them. A bowl of them alone
looks like birdseed.

**Why do mine taste bitter?**
They're old. Sunflower kernels are high in oil and go rancid quickly — buy small
quantities and refrigerate.
