## The short version

Small Italian almond macaroons — crisp, cracked on top, and intensely of
marzipan. They're naturally gluten-free, they come individually wrapped in
twisted paper that looks beautiful in a bowl, and they're the sweet thing most
guests haven't had before.

## What it is

Ground almonds, sugar and egg white, baked. Two styles: **amaretti secchi** are
hard and crisp (the Saronno tin); **amaretti morbidi** are soft and chewy. The
bitter almond note traditionally comes from apricot kernels, which is where the
name — *amaro*, bitter — comes from.

**Taste:** intensely almond, sweet, faintly bitter
**Texture:** crisp and light, or soft and chewy
**Look:** small domed biscuits with a cracked top, often in twisted paper wrappers

The mechanism is benzaldehyde — the compound behind almond and apricot-kernel
aroma. It's one of the most recognisable flavours in food, so a small biscuit
registers strongly. That intensity plus small size is why one or two per person is
enough, and why they work as a punctuation mark rather than a serving.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Mascarpone** | Crumble amaretti over it. The Italian dessert combination, and the texture contrast is the point. |
| **Gorgonzola** | Sweet almond against salt and funk. Unexpected and one of the better blue cheese pairings. |
| **Brandied cherries** | Cherry and almond are botanically related and taste it. Spoon syrup over a crumbled amaretti. |
| **Fresh ricotta** | Crumbled on top with honey. A three-ingredient dessert. |
| **Dark chocolate** | The dessert corner. Bitter almond and bitter cocoa. |
| **Vin santo and dessert wine** | The traditional service. |

**Skip it with:** savoury cheese and cured meat. This is unambiguously dessert.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Leave a few in their paper wrappers** in the bowl — the twisted paper is
   half the appeal.
2. **Unwrap the rest** so guests know they can eat them.
3. **Crumble some over mascarpone or ricotta.** That's the best use.
4. **Serve 0.5 oz per person** — one or two biscuits.
5. **Put them in the dessert corner.**
6. **Keep the tin sealed.** They soften in humid air.
7. **Fix:** stale and soft? Five minutes in a 300°F oven crisps them again.

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lazzaroni Amaretti di Saronno** | Italian delis, grocery | The red tin. The reference, and the paper wrappers are the look. |
| **Amaretti morbidi** | Italian delis | Soft and chewy. Different texture, same flavour. |
| **Amaretti di Sassello** | Italian specialty | Soft Ligurian style. Excellent. |
| **Ricciarelli** | Italian delis, Christmas | Sienese soft almond biscuits, dusted with sugar. Related and superb. |
| **Macarons** | Patisseries | Different biscuit, similar role. More fragile and more expensive. |

**Label check:** almonds should lead the ingredients. Traditional amaretti use
apricot kernels for the bitter note — worth knowing if a guest has stone-fruit
concerns.

**Skip:** anything with wheat flour listed, if the gluten-free aspect matters.

## Quick FAQ

**What are amaretti?**
Small Italian almond macaroons made from ground almonds, sugar and egg white.
Crisp or chewy depending on style.

**Are amaretti gluten-free?**
Traditional ones are — they contain no flour. Check the label, as some modern
versions add wheat.

**What's the difference between crisp and soft amaretti?**
*Secchi* are hard and crisp; *morbidi* are soft and chewy. Both taste the same.

**What do they go with?**
Mascarpone and ricotta above all, plus brandied cherries, dark chocolate, and —
surprisingly — gorgonzola.

**How many per person?**
One or two. They're small but intensely flavoured.
