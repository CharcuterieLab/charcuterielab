## The short version

The Dutch cheese in the red wax ball. Young Edam is mild, springy and
forgettable; aged Edam is firm, salty and genuinely good. Almost every shop
sells the young one, which is why the cheese has the reputation it has.

## What it is

Edam is made from partly skimmed milk, which is why it's lower in fat than
gouda and firmer at the same age. It travelled well — the wax coating and low
moisture made it a staple of ships and colonial trade for centuries. Young Edam
is sold at four to six weeks; aged Edam runs a year or more.

**Taste (young):** mild, milky, faintly salty, slightly nutty
**Taste (aged):** salty, sharp, nutty, crystalline
**Texture:** springy and semi-firm young; hard and crumbly aged
**Look:** pale yellow paste, red or yellow wax coating

Reduced fat is the mechanism, and it explains both the texture and the
reputation. Less fat means a firmer, springier paste and less of the round
richness that makes young gouda pleasant. Age it and the developing salt and
crystals compensate — which is why aged Edam is worth seeking and young Edam
mostly isn't.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Apple and pear** | Fresh acid against a mild springy cheese. The Dutch pairing. |
| **Whole wheat crackers** | Toasted grain giving a plain cheese something to lean on. |
| **Grainy mustard** | Sharpness the cheese doesn't supply itself. |
| **Cured ham and salami** | Salt and fat with something mild. |
| **Dried apricots and dates** | Fruit sugar against salt, especially with aged Edam. |
| **Cumin seed** | Dutch cheeses are traditionally spiced with cumin — a small dish beside it works. |
| **Light lager, dry cider, Pinot Noir** | Nothing heavy required. |

**Skip it with:** blue cheese and pecorino, which erase it. And don't put it on
the same board as aged gouda — Edam reads as the lesser version of a very
similar idea.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Buy aged if you can find it.** This is the whole recommendation. Young
   Edam is a fine sandwich cheese and a dull board one.
2. **Cut into thin wedges or cubes.** Young Edam slices cleanly; aged Edam
   should be broken into shards.
3. **Temper 30 minutes.**
4. **Serve 1–2 oz per person.**
5. **Remove the wax entirely.** It's inedible and it's not decorative once
   the cheese is cut.
6. **Put fruit or mustard beside it** — a mild cheese needs a partner with an
   opinion.
7. **Fix:** rubbery and flat? That's young Edam behaving normally. Serve it
   with something sharp or swap to aged gouda.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aged Edam (12+ months)** | Cheese counters, Dutch specialty shops | The version worth buying. Salty, crystalline, nutty. |
| **Young Edam, red wax ball** | Most grocery stores | Widely available, mild, unremarkable. Fine for a mixed crowd. |
| **Cumin Edam (Leyden-style)** | Cheese counters, Dutch shops | Spiced with cumin seed. A real Dutch tradition and genuinely good. |
| **Aged gouda** | Grocery, Whole Foods | The richer, sweeter Dutch alternative — see its own page. |
| **Mimolette** | Cheese counters | The French cheese Edam inspired. More dramatic — see its own page. |
| **Trader Joe's Edam** | Trader Joe's | Cheap and mild. |

**Label check:** an age statement. Without one, assume it's the young version.

**Skip:** serving the wax ball whole as a centerpiece. It looks charming and
eats badly — cut and unwax it before it goes out.

## Quick FAQ

**Edam or gouda?**
Gouda is made from whole milk and is richer and sweeter. Edam is partly skimmed
and firmer and milder. Aged gouda beats aged Edam for most boards.

**Why is it in red wax?**
Preservation. The wax coating let Edam travel and keep, which made it a staple
of shipping and trade for centuries.

**Can you eat the wax?**
No. Remove it completely.

**Is aged Edam worth seeking out?**
Yes — it's salty, nutty and crystalline, and a genuinely different cheese from
the young version.

**How much per person?**
1–2 oz.
