## The short version

A semi-soft cheese from the Jura with a black horizontal line of ash running
through the center. Mild, creamy and faintly funky from a washed rind. It's the
best-looking sliced cheese you can put on a board, and the line comes with a
story worth telling.

## What it is

Morbier was made by Comté producers from leftover curd. Evening milk went into
the mold and was covered with a layer of soot from the bottom of the cauldron to
protect it overnight; morning milk went on top. The ash line is now vegetable
ash added deliberately, but the two-layer structure is real and visible.

**Taste:** mild, creamy, nutty, faintly barnyard from the rind
**Texture:** semi-soft, supple, slightly elastic
**Look:** pale ivory paste split by a distinct black horizontal line, orange
washed rind

The visible seam is the mechanism on a board, and it's the rare case where a
cheese's history is legible in the slice. Every other cheese here looks like a
solid color. Morbier looks like a cross-section of something, which makes it
the piece guests ask about.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Baguette** | Plain bread under a mild, faintly funky cheese. |
| **Cornichons and pickled onions** | Sharp vinegar against a creamy washed-rind paste. |
| **Cured ham and saucisson** | Salt and cure with a gentle Jura cheese. |
| **Walnuts** | Tannin and texture. The Jura pairing. |
| **Apple and pear** | Fresh acid cutting a supple, fatty cheese. |
| **Comté, side by side** | Same region, same producers, completely different textures. A good comparison. |
| **Vin Jaune, Chardonnay, dry cider** | Jura wines, or something with acid. |

**Skip it with:** delicate fresh cheeses — the washed rind gives Morbier more
presence than its mildness suggests. And don't bury the line under a garnish;
it's the reason you bought it.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Cut wedges that show the ash line clearly**, and lay them so the line
   reads across the board. This is the whole visual argument.
2. **Temper 30 minutes.** It's noticeably creamier warm.
3. **Serve 1–1.5 oz per person.**
4. **Tell the story.** Evening milk, soot from the cauldron, morning milk on
   top. It takes ten seconds and it's the most interesting thing on most
   boards.
5. **Put it next to Comté** if you have both — same region, same producers,
   entirely different cheeses.
6. **Trim the rind.** It's washed and edible but not pleasant.
7. **Fix:** an ammonia smell means it's over-ripe. A mild barnyard smell is
   correct.

| **Trader Joe's Morbier** | Trader Joe's | Available intermittently. Cheap and decent. |

**Label check:** the AOP mark, a clean straight ash line, and a paste with no
chalky core. Raw-milk versions have more character.

**Skip:** buying it far ahead. Morbier ripens on and tips into ammonia within a
week or two of being cut.

## Quick FAQ

**What's the black line in Morbier?**
Vegetable ash. Historically it was soot from the cheese cauldron, used to
protect the evening curd overnight before the morning milk was added on top.

**Does it taste of anything?**
The ash itself, no. The cheese is mild, creamy and faintly barnyard from its
washed rind.

**Can you eat the rind?**
It's edible but tough. Most people trim it.

**Is it a strong cheese?**
No — mild with a light funk. Far gentler than Époisses or Munster.

**How much per person?**
1–1.5 oz.
