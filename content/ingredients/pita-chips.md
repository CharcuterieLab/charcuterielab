## The short version

Baked wedges of pita bread — thick, salty, and strong enough to go into a dense
dip without snapping. That structural strength is the only reason to buy them,
and it's a very good reason. Keep them to one corner of the board.

## What it is

Pita chips are just pita bread split, cut into wedges, brushed with oil and
baked hard. Because pita is a bread rather than a cracker dough, the chip is
thicker and denser than anything else in the cracker aisle, with a slight chew
left at the center.

**Taste:** salty, wheaty, olive-oil savory
**Texture:** thick, hard, substantial crunch
**Look:** triangular, blistered and bubbled, uneven browning

Structure is the mechanism. Every other base on a board is designed to break
cleanly. This one is designed not to. Load-bearing capacity is what you're
buying, not flavor.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Hummus** | The pairing this chip exists for. Thick enough to scoop without leaving half the chip in the bowl. |
| **Baba ganoush and muhammara** | Same structural argument, plus the smoke and the chip's char are a natural echo. |
| **Whipped feta** | Salty on salty, but the dip is soft enough that the contrast carries it. |
| **Tzatziki and labneh** | Cool, wet and creamy against dry and hard. A wet dip needs a chip that won't soften. |
| **Marinated olives and feta cubes** | Assembles into a bite. The chip is the plate. |
| **Warm spinach-artichoke dip** | Hot dips destroy crackers. Pita chips hold. |
| **Roasted red pepper spread** | Sweet, oily and loose — the chip's salt gives it an edge. |

**Skip it with:** brie, fresh chèvre and other delicate cheeses (too salty and
too aggressive), and caviar or smoked fish. Also be careful about volume: pita
chips are big and loud-looking, and a handful of them can make a European-style
board look like a party platter.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Give them one corner and stop there.** They're the largest item on most
   boards. Spread out, they dominate the composition.
2. **Build a dip zone.** Put the bowl of hummus on the board and fan the chips
   around only that bowl — it reads as deliberate.
3. **Serve 5–6 per person**, more if dips are the main event.
4. **Warm them for 4–5 minutes at 300°F** before serving. It's a small thing
   that people notice.
5. **Stand a few upright in the dip** rather than laying all of them flat. It
   adds height, which most boards need.
6. **Fix:** softened chips re-crisp completely at 325°F for 5 minutes. Unlike
   thin crackers, they hold up to reheating more than once.

| **Stacy's Simply Naked Organic** | Grocery, Whole Foods | Same chip, organic. |
| **Stacy's Sea Salt** | Grocery | Slightly saltier. Good with mild dips, too much with feta. |
| **Trader Joe's Pita Chips** | Trader Joe's | Thinner and cheaper. Snap more easily in dense dip. |
| **Toufayan Pita Chips** | Grocery, Middle Eastern markets | Closer to real bakery pita. Worth seeking out. |
| **Store brand pita chips** | Target, Kroger, Aldi | Perfectly fine. This is a hard category to get wrong. |

**Label check:** "baked," not fried, and a short oil list. Flavored versions —
parmesan garlic, cinnamon sugar — are snacks, not board bases.

**Skip:** everything-flavored and cheese-flavored pita chips on a cheese board.
You already have cheese; you don't need cheese powder.

## Quick FAQ

**How many pita chips per person?**
5–6 alongside other bases. Double that if dips are the centerpiece.

**Can I make them myself?**
Yes. Split pitas, cut into wedges, toss with olive oil and salt, bake at 400°F
for 10–12 minutes. Better than store-bought and cheaper.

**Are pita chips gluten-free?**
No — they're wheat. For a gluten-free scoop, use tostada rounds or plantain
chips.

**Do they belong on a cheese board or a dip board?**
Both, but they're happiest with dips. On a cheese-forward board, put out a few
and lean on crackers instead.
