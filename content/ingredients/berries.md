## The short version

The four board berries — strawberry, raspberry, blackberry, blueberry — do the
same job: sharp acid and strong colour against pale fat. Rinse them at the last
possible moment and dry them properly, or they'll leak onto everything.

## What it is

**Strawberries** are the largest and sweetest, and the only one worth halving.
**Raspberries** are the tartest and most fragile — hollow, and they collapse if
stacked. **Blackberries** are the most tannic, with a faint winey depth.
**Blueberries** are the firmest and the least acidic, and they roll.

**Taste:** tart to sweet depending on type, all with real acidity
**Texture:** soft and juicy; blueberries are firm and snapping
**Look:** the strongest reds, purples and blacks available on any board

Anthocyanins are the mechanism, and they do two jobs. They're the pigments that
give berries their intensity — the deepest colours you can put next to cream and
tan cheese — and they carry a mild astringency that, with the acid, cuts fat
more sharply than a grape does.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Triple-crème and brie** | Sharp fruit acid against saturated butterfat. The single most effective use of berries on a board. |
| **Mascarpone or whipped ricotta** | Berries and sweet cream. The dessert corner. |
| **Fresh chèvre** | Tangy white cheese with tart red fruit. |
| **Dark chocolate** | Raspberry and dark chocolate especially. |
| **Honey and mint** | Sweet and herbal on tart fruit. |
| **Prosciutto** | Strawberries and prosciutto is less obvious than melon and just as good. |
| **Champagne, prosecco, rosé** | Bubbles and berries. |

**Skip it with:** strongly savoury or smoked items — berries are a sweet-corner
fruit and they clash with smoked fish or spiced sausage.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Rinse at the last minute and dry thoroughly.** This is the whole page. Wet
   berries bleed onto cheese and turn crackers soggy. Lay them on a towel and
   pat.
2. **Never rinse in advance and store wet** — they go mouldy within hours.
3. **Halve strawberries lengthwise**, leaving the green top on. Leave the other
   three whole.
4. **Group by type in small clusters** rather than mixing them into a scatter.
   Four separate colour blocks read as composed; a mixed pile reads as trail
   mix.
5. **Serve 2–3 oz per person.**
6. **Put them at the sweet end**, near the soft cheeses and the honey.
7. **Fix:** one mouldy berry contaminates the punnet fast — sort them before
   they go anywhere near the board.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Strawberries** | Any grocery, best in spring–summer | Look for full red to the shoulders and green caps. White shoulders mean picked early. |
| **Raspberries** | Any grocery | The most fragile. Check the bottom of the punnet for crushed fruit. |
| **Blackberries** | Any grocery | Deep matte black, not shiny red-black. Shiny means underripe and sour. |
| **Blueberries** | Any grocery, year-round | The most robust and the easiest to buy. Look for a pale silvery bloom. |
| **Farmers market berries, in season** | Local, summer | A different food from supermarket berries. Worth the trip. |
| **Frozen berries** | Any grocery | Not for a board — they weep as they thaw. |

**Label check:** turn the punnet over. Juice stains or crushed fruit on the
bottom means the whole box is close to going.

**Skip:** rinsing them the night before. It's the single fastest way to ruin
berries.

## Quick FAQ

**When should I wash berries?**
Right before serving, then dry them thoroughly on a towel. Washing early makes
them mould.

**Which berries are best for a cheese board?**
Blackberries and raspberries for tartness and colour, strawberries for size,
blueberries for durability. A mix of two looks better than all four.

**Do I halve strawberries?**
Yes — lengthwise, with the green top left on.

**How much per person?**
2–3 oz across all types.

**How do I stop them bleeding onto the cheese?**
Dry them properly, and give them their own small dish or a clear space rather
than nestling them against soft cheese.
