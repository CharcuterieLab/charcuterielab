## The short version

A spice blend that was a bagel topping until someone put it in a jar. Sesame,
poppy, dried garlic, dried onion and salt. On a board it does one job extremely
well: it turns a plain white spread into something that looks composed. It costs
about three dollars and requires no skill at all.

## What it is

A dry blend, sold everywhere now. The two seeds provide texture and toasted oil
flavour, the dried alliums provide savouriness, and the flaked salt does what salt
does. Good versions use larger salt flakes and whole seeds rather than dust.

**Taste:** toasted sesame, sharp dried garlic and onion, and a lot of salt
**Texture:** crunchy and granular
**Look:** black, white and beige speckle

The mechanism is contrast on a flat surface. Soft spreads have no texture and no
visual interest, and the tongue reads them as one continuous thing. Scattering
something crunchy and savoury across the top gives every bite two textures and a
sharp savoury edge, which is why a spoonful of seasoned cream cheese tastes like
more work than it was.

## What it pairs with — and why

| Pair it with | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Cream cheese** | The obvious one, and it is obvious because it works. Neutral fat carrying salt and toasted seed. |
| **Smoked salmon** | The bagel board without the bagel. Salt, smoke, allium and seed is a complete idea. |
| **Labneh** | Cool sour dairy under a savoury crust. Better than the cream cheese version, honestly. |
| **Whipped feta** | Already salty, so use a lighter hand. The seeds are what it is missing. |
| **Bagel chips** | Circular, and it works. Doubles the seasoning without adding a new flavour. |
| **Cucumber and vegetable bases** | Water and crunch under salt and oil. The lightest thing on the board. |

**Skip it with:** anything sweet, and any cheese with its own strong flavour. Blue
cheese with everything seasoning is just noise.

## How to prep it for the board

1. **Scatter it at the last minute.** The salt draws moisture and the seeds go
   soft within an hour.
2. **Season the base less than usual.** The blend brings its own salt.
3. **Put it in a small dish beside the spreads** as well as on them, so people can
   add more.
4. **Use it on one or two items only.** It is distinctive enough that four items
   wearing it looks lazy.
5. **A pinch per serving** is the whole measure.
6. **Flag it to guests** with sesame allergies.
7. **Fix:** tastes harsh and dusty? A cheap blend with powdered rather than whole
   garlic. Toast it in a dry pan for 30 seconds and it improves noticeably.

## What to buy

| Product | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Trader Joe's Everything But The Bagel** | Trader Joe's | The one that started the category. Cheap and good. |
| **Supermarket own-brand** | Any grocery spice aisle | Universally available now. Check for whole seeds. |
| **Artisan everything blends** | Spice shops | Larger salt flakes, better sesame. Worth it if you use a lot. |
| **Everything blend with sea salt flakes** | Specialty | The crunch is better and it looks better on a board. |
| **Homemade** | Make it | Equal sesame and poppy, half as much dried garlic and onion, flaky salt to taste. |

**Label check:** whole seeds visible through the jar, and salt that looks flaked
rather than fine.

**Skip:** blends where salt is the first ingredient by a wide margin. You are
buying seasoned salt, not seasoning.

## Quick FAQ

**What is in everything bagel seasoning?**
Sesame seeds, poppy seeds, dried garlic, dried onion and flaked salt. Proportions
vary by brand.

**What do you put it on for a board?**
Cream cheese, labneh, whipped feta, smoked salmon and avocado. Anything soft, pale
and mild.

**Is it salty?**
Yes — the salt is a main ingredient, so season whatever it goes on more lightly
than usual.

**Does it contain allergens?**
Sesame, which is a declared allergen in most countries. Poppy seed is also worth
flagging to guests.

**How much per person?**
A pinch. It is a finish, not a component.
